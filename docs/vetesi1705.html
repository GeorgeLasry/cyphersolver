Vetési Kökényesdi to Rákóczi, 9 January 1706

A newsletter to Rákóczi from his agent abroad on the post from France, glossed at the time, deciphered again with Rákóczi's own key, which was found filed elsewhere in the same archive.

Daniel Bourdeau

At a glanceExternal key matched · the whole letter

The letter
László Vetési Kökényesdi, Rákóczi's agent abroad, signing as “Cassimirus Miloftzki”, to Ferenc Rákóczi II, dated 9 January 1706: clear Hungarian with numerical cipher runs.
Where
Ráday Archives, C64-4d2-10, ff. 92–95; DECODE R473 (three images), catalogued 1 Jan 1705–1706 with Pál Ráday as receiver.
The cipher
A one-part Hungarian syllabic nomenclator: letters and syllables in two- and three-figure groups, names and words in larger ones (276 Franciaország, 1190 Kegyelmes Uram), 9 and 8 as nulls.
How it was deciphered
A hand of the time glossed nearly every group. On 23 Oct 2026 the gloss was transcribed afresh and Rákóczi's key DECODE R446 was found by testing the neighbouring key records against 18 glossed groups (16 agree); applied to all 380 groups it agrees with the gloss at 253 of 304 where both give a value.
What it says
The post from France: Nice castle surrendered to the French king, Valencia in revolt, the coming war in Italy and Spain, Holland tiring of the war, and Vetési's anxious report on an order of Rákóczi's he never received.
Still open
About a dozen clear words in a faded part of ff. 93–94, read with doubt; the printed note in Ráday Pál iratai I (1955) not compared (not online).

01 The letter and its date

The letter opens “Méltóságos Fejedelem” (Most Illustrious Prince), part of it in cipher, calls the addressee Nagyságod (Your Highness) and Kegyelmes Uram, names “Rádai uram” in the third person and is signed Cassimirus Miloftzki, a cover name of László Vetési Kökényesdi. The addressee is therefore Rákóczi; DECODE's receiver, Pál Ráday, is the secretary in whose papers it survives. The closing reads Költ azon, Kilenczedik Januarii, the place in unglossed cipher, and the archive's endorsement at the head of f. 92 gives 1706 I. 9; DECODE's “1 Jan 1705–1706” is a placeholder.

A pencil note on f. 92 cites Ráday Pál iratai I (1703–1706), ed. Benda Kálmán and others (Budapest 1955), item 102, footnote 1, p. 494. The volume is not online, so whether it prints the letter has not been checked. The images of the Ráday Archives are not in the public domain and are not reproduced here.

02 The cipher and the key

A hand of the time wrote the plaintext over almost every cipher group: Francziaország, Olaszországban, Spaniolországban, Holandia és Anglia, Ráday uram. DECODE's transcription renders this gloss as garbled syllables (“Fejedehd”, “ttolendia”), and an attempt in September 2026 to rebuild the key from it by alignment gave a noisy table.

The key turned out to be in the same archive. The DOC files of the DECODE key records R380–R520 were scanned against 18 glossed groups of R473; only R446 (Ráday Archives C64-4d2-25 no. 6, Rákóczi's Hungarian key, original) fits, with 16 of 18, and no other record scores above 1. DECODE's transcription of R446 was then corrected on its image: 69 is al, 71–74 an, ar, as, at, the ö/ü groups are distinct, 35 is ly and 296 Belgium; the last column, headed x, holds the nulls 9 and 8. Applied to the 380 groups of the letter, the key and the gloss both give a value at 304, and agree at 253. The rest are spelling variants, misalignments of the gloss over long runs and a few writer's slips. Where the gloss is garbled the key gives the word: Niszevárat ... az Franciaországi király kezében adta, az király táborba, Belgiummal, hogy Herbaville ujjobban gyözidelmeskedet.

03 What the letter says

Tegnap előtt Franciaországbul érkezett posta ad okot Nagyságodnak alázatoson udvarlanom. [...] Kegyelmes Uram, Niszevárat az Franciaországi király kezében adta, mellyért örömet itten tegnap estve az öreg templomban solenniter [?celebraltak].

“The post that arrived from France the day before yesterday gives me occasion to pay my humble respects to Your Highness. [...] Most Gracious Lord, [it] gave the castle of Nice into the hands of the King of France, for which they [?celebrated] the rejoicing solemnly here yesterday evening in the old church.” Nice castle surrendered to the French on 4 January 1706. Vetési goes on to Spain, where the court is discontented, Valencia has rebelled and Aragon wavers, and the Queen will not take up the administration; the King of France must send money there. Next year's war, he expects, will be in Italy and Spain, with the Empire on the defensive; taking Antwerp would not suit Holland's trade with Belgium, and people say Holland, tired of the useless expense, will leave the House of Austria. He has heard that Herbeville has won a victory, and he reports anxiously that he never received an order of Rákóczi's of 30 November, of which Ráday's December letter says nothing.
