Méry de Vic to Henri IV, 28 August 1589

Méry de Vic writes after the assassination of Henri III and the accession of Henri IV. The opening cleartext reports the arrival of the news and local allegiances. The cipher reports troops near Limoges and divided loyalties; later cleartext discusses expenses, elections, intercepted dispatches and cannon for rebel forts. Deciphered by George Lasry, working with ChatGPT Astra 6 and Anthropic Opus 5.5, in his BnF cipher research of October 2026: 545 elements, 523 identified (96.0%). His report calls the letter partly deciphered; the two excerpts in this repository have holes in every sentence, so it counts here as decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026).

05 Notes and corrections

Not read here. Lasry’s report calls the letter partly deciphered, and only his two excerpts are in this repository; both have holes in every sentence. Under the reading bar the letter counts as decoded, not read (reading audit, 22 October 2026), and no key recovery is claimed for this project.

The f. 43r cipher decoded sign by sign (23 October 2026). The letter is ff. 40r–43r (Gallica views 85–100), all clear except one 16-line cipher block on f. 43r (view 95); there is no decipherment, gloss or clear copy nearby, and no DECODE record. The block was decoded sign by sign with Lasry’s key table. The decode reproduces both of his excerpts and adds stretches such as “qui a tousiours parle en […] servit[e]ur de roy se change, come on m’a asseure”. The 13 October disagreement noted above was two different passages, not an error by Lasry. The cipher passage alone still has holes in most sentences (four signs with no value in his table, about twelve unread groups, seven letters on look-alike signs, two values from sense only), so it stays decoded, not read. The partial reading is texts/f43r_reading.md; Lasry’s own decryption file may close the holes.

## Notes

- The clear text: "Maté" expanded to Majesté; "s.rs" to sieurs; "Mrs" to messieurs; otherwise as written.
- "come on ma asseure" (clear) follows the cipher "... de roy se change": Lasry's excerpt "s'est déclaré comme
  plusieurs du ? m'ont asseuré" belongs to the earlier cipher passage (l. B), which the decode confirms; the 13 Oct
  spot-check's disagreement was a mismatch of passages, not an error in his excerpt.

Translation of `f43r_reading.md`. Same holes. Not a reading: the cipher passage stays decoded, not read.

The lords of Pompadour and of Clermont-Vertillac, holding for the other party, have written to the consuls and
inhabitants of this town offering them every assistance provided they throw off the just obedience they owe Your
Majesty. The Duke of Mayenne has likewise written to them, together with the leading men of the companies and to
private inhabitants, to dispose them to receive his orders; all his letters, twelve in number, are on the same subject
as the copy of one of them that I send Your Majesty. Although the wills of several are shaken, I believe that with God's
help all these rebel tricks will in no way shake the loyalty of this town or of the neighbourhood. And yet, to keep
everyone to his duty, I think it would be useful if Your Majesty were pleased to write several letters following the
memorandum I send, from which I hope some fruit will come.

[P], who had kept the late King in hope of serving him, has declared himself, as several of the […] have assured me. He

servant of the King, is changing, as I have been assured. [ʃ] his [?lieutenant] is also gathering […] men […] houses,
