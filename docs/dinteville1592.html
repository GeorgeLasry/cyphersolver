auoir veu d’ascendre a Geneve … (f. 128, interlinear)

Langres → Nevers · letters, digits and graphic signs, apparently with code symbols · 3 July 1592

Dinteville to the Duke of Nevers, 1592

BnF Français 3621, no. 116, f. 130 (DECODE R9451; Gallica btv1b52524472n, view 269). A clear letter from the royalist lieutenant in Champagne with two cipher passages, about ten lines in all.

The catalogue listed it as a transcription job, because DECODE says the leaf has a decipherment. It has none. The one crib, the interlinear decipherment of a sibling letter at f. 128, did not yield a key, so the cipher is not deciphered. No earlier decipherment was found.

Summary. DECODE describes R9451 as a “letter, with cipher and decryption”, and the catalogue therefore marked it as deciphered at the time. The image shows no decipherment. f. 130r has a clear letter dated at Langres on 3 July 1592 with two passages in cipher (about 3 and 7 lines), then an autograph postscript in clear French. The volume turns out to be on Gallica in full resolution, and two leaves earlier, f. 128r is another Dinteville letter of July 1592 in the same symbols, with a contemporary decipherment written above each cipher line: m’a dict avoir veu d’ascendre a Geneve deux millions d’or d’Espaigne … quarante cinq + mulets chargez … prendre le chemin de Besançon. That is a crib of about 150 symbols. A test for a key of one symbol per letter (homophones allowed, up to three null types) finds none: the same symbol sits under o in auoir and under d a few symbols later. The decipherer also left some symbols as crosses, which suggests code symbols for words. The cipher is not deciphered. The next step is a careful symbol-by-symbol transcription of f. 128 against its interlinear, then of f. 130.

01 The leaf

The letter is in clear French, and the cipher breaks into it twice. The symbols are a mixed set: digits (1 3 4 9 0), letters (o v w m z c a) and marks (# ψ α Δ □ ¢ ∇ π ƒ +), some barred or crossed, many followed by a dot. There are roughly 400 to 450 symbols. The paragraph after the date, beginning Monseigneur ce matin j’ay sceu … l’armée lorraine, is Dinteville’s own postscript in clear. The verso carries the address. Nothing on the leaf glosses the cipher.

02 The crib on f. 128

This leaf is probably no. 114, which the BnF notice lists as a Dinteville letter “avec chiffre et déchiffrement”. It uses the same symbols as f. 130. The decipherment is written above the cipher, roughly word by word, and runs: … m’a dict / avoir veu d’ascendre a Geneve deux millions d’or d’Espaigne / … quarante cinq + mulets chargez qui doibt aussi passer dans trois / jours et prendre le chemin de Besançon …, news of Spanish money moving through Geneva and Franche-Comté towards Flanders.

03 What was tried

I transcribed the second cipher line of f. 128 as 50 symbols against its 48-letter gloss. A search for a consistent key (each symbol one letter, several symbols per letter, up to three symbol types as nulls) finds no solution. The first five symbols fit auoir cleanly, but α then has to be both o and d. Either the cipher is not a plain homophonic alphabet (code symbols for words such as Geneve and Espaigne, or symbols worth more than one letter), or my reading of the glyphs by eye is wrong in places. With about 150 aligned symbols and no second crib, the two cannot be separated from here.

04 What remains

  • Both cipher passages of f. 130: not deciphered.
  • The f. 128 system: undetermined. The crib exists, but no key has been rebuilt from it.
  • Next steps: a verified hand transcription of f. 128’s three and a half lines; a sweep of the rest of fr. 3621 (and fr. 4716 no. 31, Dinteville to Nevers, 1590) for more Dinteville letters with decipherments; then the nomenclator-plus-language-model method used for Lorraine 1592 and Pélissier 1592.

f. 130 (no. 116), Dinteville to Nevers, Langres, 4 July 1592

Dinteville, at Langres, writes to the Duke of Nevers that the enemy means to return and take Châteauvillain, that he is left alone in Langres with the townspeople while the Lorraine army fortifies itself, and that without troops no taille can be raised; he asks to withdraw if the King does not provide for it. The cipher passages give the reasons he did not write in clear; 8 signs are open.

## f. 130 (no. 116, "IIIIe juillet 1592")

The cipher is given in bold. The clear text is from `clear_f130.txt`, a separate full transcription of the leaf; [?]
marks words uncertain there. Original spelling is kept; ? in the cipher marks open tokens. The clear text says Langres
asks for 200 horse and 200 foot, and Châteauvillain is blockaded. The cipher gives the reasons Dinteville did not
write in clear.

**Passage 1 (ll. 4–8).** …ce que J'ay eu auiourdhuy aduis de plusieurs endroitz que lennemy[?] faict estat dy
retourner et **l'emporter, ???? il n'y estoit pourveu, qui me faict les y renvoier avec de la pouldre.** vous
Iugerez[?] / aussi si vous plaist Monseigneur que **je demeure seul ici avec les habitans et** que la[rmée] … Lorrains[?]
se fortiffie de ce qui estoit du coste de Strasbourg **et, retournant a moi, il me seroit malaisé d'y conserver ceste
place en l'obeissance qu'[?]elle y est.** Messieurs de la Ville supplient tres humblement le Roy et vous d'y placer
deux cens chevaulx et deux cens hommes de pied…

**Passage 2 (ll. 11–16).** …Il y a plus de cinq cens chevaulx de garnison ennemye et le pais est […] soubz
contributions sur ce qu[i …] **si les forces n'y sont placées, oultre le hasart que court la ville, il ne se leuera un
denier de tailles. D'aultre costé, l'en y voi[t] le dedans si bien basti qu'il ne soit plus a craindre que le dehors;
les forces serviront tant a les contenir a leur devoir que a la deffence de la ville. Le Roi et vous m'excuserez si je
pass[?] de mot, que [?] s'il ne plaist a Sa Maiesté d'y pourvoir, de m'en retirer, d'aultant qu'il y a a perdre la vie
et l'honneur** comme[?] luy. / Je la doibs a sa Ma(jes)te…

**Joins.** Every cipher run continues the grammar of the clear text on each side, as transcribed independently:
- "et | l'emporter"
- "que | je demeure"
- "habitans et | que la[rmée]"
- "Strasbourg | et retournant"
- "qu'elle est | Messieurs de la Ville"
- "l'honneur | comme luy. Je la doibs a sa Majesté"

The request in clear for "deux cens chevaulx et deux cens hommes de pied" is picked up in cipher as "si les forces n'y
sont placées".

**English.**

Passage 1: …[the enemy] means to come back and carry the place, [unless?] it were provided for, which is why I am
sending them back there with powder. … if you please, my Lord, [consider] that I am left alone here with the
townspeople, that the Lorraine army is fortifying itself on the Strasbourg side, and that if it turns back on me, it
would be hard for me to keep this place in the obedience it is in now.

Passage 2: If troops are not stationed here, then besides the danger the town runs, not a penny of taille will be
raised. On the other hand, one sees the inside so well built that only the outside is to be feared; the troops will
serve as much to keep them to their duty as to defend the town. The King and you will forgive me if I speak bluntly:
if it does not please His Majesty to provide for it, [I ask] to withdraw, since there is life and honour to be lost…

f. 128 (no. 114), Dinteville to Nevers, Langres, 1 July 1592

A gentleman of M. de Gondy coming from Florence reports two million in Spanish gold landed at Genoa, with forty-five laden mules left at Besançon to take the road to Vesoul under an escort of a hundred horse. The interlinear decipherment on this leaf is the key source; the plaintext is printed in Revue de Champagne et de Brie t. XII, p. 340.
