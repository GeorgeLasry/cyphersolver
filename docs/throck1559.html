The letter
Sir Nicholas Throckmorton, ambassador in France, to Elizabeth I, Paris, 10 July 1559: his holograph, sixteen lines of cipher, signed.
Where
British Library, Add MS 4136 f. 32 (old f. 9), in the Forbes Papers; DECODE R2988 (with catalogue item 97, which DECODE listed as an unknown sender).
The cipher
Throckmorton’s first cipher: a letter alphabet of digits, letters and invented signs, with homophones, nulls and dotted signs for words and names.
How it was deciphered
With Satoshi Tomokiyo’s key for Cipher 1, attached to the DECODE record, after re-cutting the lines from the scan; seven values were corrected and four added from the text.
What it says
Throckmorton has already written twice on the 8th; he now sends a gentleman to report the French King’s state by word of mouth, asks the Queen to give him credit and to reward his “diverse and sundry” service, and signs off “at Paris the tenth of Julii”.
Still open
Twelve code signs not on the key, about 80 small signs in lines 5–14, and a starred note keyed to line 13 that is written in a different code.
Other ciphers
The marginal text below the letter is a word-sign code with its own page; John Wood’s 1568 line on the next leaf is another cipher, also with its own page.

01 The leaf

f. 32 (old 9) is Throckmorton’s holograph letter to Elizabeth, signed, sixteen lines of cipher in his first alphabet. Tomokiyo identifies the cipher and attaches his key to the DECODE record, but no decipherment of this letter was found in print: Forbes’s Full View does not print it, and the Calendar of State Papers Foreign has only the despatches of 8 July. Below and beside the subscription is a separate text in another system, the subject of its own page.

02 The letter of 10 July 1559

Key: Tomokiyo’s reconstruction of Cipher 1 (DECODE R2988 attachment), with these corrections: x and υ = i; y = f or h; z = m; ρ = d or t; ∫t and a flat-topped 6 = w; the looped Q = be; ξ and dotted q = your Majesty; a doubled sign and ζ inside a word are nulls. Code words are in square brackets; [..] marks not yet deciphered signs.

It may please [your Majesty] to be advertised that whereas I sent [letters] to the same by Maister Gray of the eight of [this] [month] [at] five of the clock [in] the afternoon, and [by] Carew [letters] of the same date written [at] midnight touching the [French King]’s state, I judge [your Majesty] will be desirous to be further enformed thereof. [..] convenient to put in to writing the whole discourse of [..] [which is known?] to [my] knowledge; of considering the present [..], [I judged it best?] to dispatch [this] gentleman, to whom [..] enforme the same [..] of the state [..] length, to whom it [may please your Majesty] to give credit. [..] Since [his] coming on [this] side [he hath] done [the] diverse and sundry ways [good] and painful service, [whereby he hath] deserved to be well considered and rewarded; beseeching [your Majesty] to be much [..] and gracious [lord] to him when [your Majesty] perceive [his] service [..] accepted, whereof I [have had] experience, as I shall be able to declare more plainly* and particularly to [..] when [it] shall please the same to command me to attend [..]. And [so I] humbly beseech [God] to preserve [your Majesty in] health, wealth and [all] felicity. [At] Paris the tenth of Julii.

Grades (README Conventions): lines 1–4 H; lines 5–15 C where the key gives the letters, M for the bracketed guesses. About 470 of 550 signs in lines 5–15 are deciphered with confidence. The two despatches of the 8th that it mentions are CSP Foreign I nos. 947 and 950; the bearer is not named in the deciphered part.

03 What remains open

  • Twelve code signs (1k̲, ρ̲, g̲, δ̄, δ̲, ō, ⊓̄, q̄, r̄, π̄, ƒ̇, ʋ̲): not on Tomokiyo’s key image, and none of the Add MS 4136 key leaves (R9258–R9262) gives them; the bracketed values are guesses from context.
  • About 80 of the 550 signs in lines 5–8 and 11–14, marked [..]: small signs at the resolution of the DECODE scan.
  • The starred note keyed to the star in line 13: it is written in the marginal word code, which has no key (the margin page).

R2988, Add MS 4136 f. 32, Throckmorton to Elizabeth I, Paris, 10 July 1559

Throckmorton sends a gentleman, the bearer, to report the French King's state by word of mouth after his two despatches of 8 July (by Gray and, at midnight, by Carew); he asks the Queen to give the bearer credit, praises his diverse and sundry service and asks that it be rewarded. An unpublished letter, not in Forbes or CSP Foreign.
