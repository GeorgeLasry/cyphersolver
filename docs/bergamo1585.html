Paris → Rome · homophonic cipher of two-digit figures with nomenclator · 1585–1586

The Paris nunciature to the Secretariat, Francia 18

Archivio Apostolico Vaticano, Segreteria di Stato, Francia 18, ciphered despatches in the F18 key (DECODE R15, R16), catalogued as the bishop of Bergamo and the archbishop of Nazareth to the Secretariat, “partially decrypted”.

Lasry had deciphered the first. The second looked deciphered on DECODE, but its decryption file is the first letter’s; it was decoded here with Lasry’s key and Meister’s nomenclator, into a run-on text with a hole in nearly every clause: decoded, not read.

Summary. Catalogue entry 242 grouped three DECODE records from the Paris nunciature volume Francia 18; two of them share a key and are on this page. R15 (ff. 7r–v) was deciphered by George Lasry with a key he reconstructed, also printed by Meister (1906, p. 324). R16 (f. 206, dated 3 March 1586) carries a “decryption” on DECODE, but that file is byte for byte the R15 decryption, filed on the wrong record, so DECODE held no decryption of R16. Lasry’s key, applied to the DECODE volunteer transcription of R16, gives Italian at once. The nomenclator comes from Meister’s printing of the 1583 key of Girolamo Ragazzoni, bishop of Bergamo, and brings the unknown groups from 66 to 18. The decode is one run-on block with an open group or slip in nearly every clause, so R16 is decoded, not read; its gist is that the Duke of Guise will not let the King sell church property under the papal bull unless he is given Metz and money, and that the clergy mean to appeal to a future council. Two name codes of R16 are not confirmed. The third record, R17 (ff. 233–235), is in Meister’s key no. 40, made for Morosini in June 1587: a different key, with its own page among Morosini’s despatches.

01 The documents and the keys

The three records share the shelfmark Francia 18 and a catalogue line, but not a date or a key: R15 and R16 are in the F18 key, R17 is not.

  • R15, Francia 18/1 ff. 7r–v. Deciphered by George Lasry (DECODE document D3102), with his reconstruction of the key (D3103): two-digit homophones with the second digit odd, 8 a null, marked two- and three-digit nomenclator codes. Nothing is added here.
  • R16, Francia 18/2 f. 206r–v, headed in clear 3. di Marzo 1586. DECODE shows a decryption (D3104). Its MD5 is that of R15’s decryption, and its text is R15’s. The same key file (D3105) is attached, so the letter was one run of a decoder away from a decryption.
  • R17, Francia 18/3 ff. 233r–235v, is in another key, Meister p. 393 no. 40, and is written up with Morosini’s despatches (moved there on 14 October 2026).

02 R16: Guise, Metz and the sale of church property, 3 March 1586

The despatch belongs to the quarrel over the bull of 1586 that let Henri III raise money by selling church property. The nuncio reports what the Duke of Guise has sent to tell him, what a well-placed informant heard, and the mood of the clergy’s assembly. Decoded text, word breaks added, doubled consonants as the writer spelled them:

hoggi il [Duca di Guisa] mi ha mandato a dire … che egli hora tiene per fermo che il [Re Christianissimo] debba fare la […] … prima havera riscosso quanti si deve vendere per la bolla, ma che esso [Duca di Guisa] non vi acconsentira mai … che esso [Duca di Guisa] non poteva ne voleva acconsentire a detta […] … mi viene … riferito da personaggio di molta autorita havere inteso da un gentilhuomo … che egli si potrebbe contentar … si facesse quando se gli desse Metz, e una buona somma di […] … che con li […] che ricevessero hora potrebbero poi prepararsi a nuova impresa … nondimeno il cuore di […] non corrisponde all’estrinseco … li ecclesiastici della citta … restano molto mal contenti della concessione della bolla per l’alienatione … detta congregatione inclina ad appellarsi ad futurum concilium, se vedera di non potere ottenere … che essa congregatione habbia molta speranza di essere esaudita da [Nostro Signore] …

In paraphrase, Guise tells the nuncio he will never consent to the King’s sale of church goods under the bull; others say he would consent if he were given Metz and a large sum, money he could turn to a new enterprise; the King shows him confidence but his heart does not match; the Paris clergy are angry at the concession and their assembly leans towards an appeal to a future council, while hoping the Pope will hear them.

03 Method

DECODE’s transcription D1610 (volunteer TimB) was decoded with Lasry’s key file as it stands, reading pairs and treating 8 as the null. That left 66 unknown groups, most of them marked nomenclator codes. Meister p. 324 no. 2 prints Ragazzoni’s key of 1583 for the Paris nunciature: the same letter alphabet, with a table of names and of marked words. In the OCR the names sit in a clean two-column list; the marked words are in one run with each code after its word, an alignment checked against R16 (26¸ che, 36¸ per, 28+ questi, 98+ essere, 20− non, 0̇ con). Where one digit is missing in the transcription the pairing slips; the decoder drops one digit when that restores known pairs. Unknowns fell to 18.

The decryption rests on the volunteer transcription; the scans were not checked figure by figure.

04 What remains uncertain

  • R16, name codes. 58 Duca di Guisa and 34 Re Christianissimo are H (they fit every occurrence; Guise asks for Metz); 14 Nostro Signore C. Meister’s 18 Duca di Ferrara and 48 Duca di Parma occur where no duke fits and are marked I: the 1586 table may have moved them.
  • R16, open: 70 (three times, a person), 90− (twice, the thing the King must do, probably the sale or the alienatione), 4+ (twice, “una buona somma di …”, probably denari), 22+, 8+, 12+, 30−, 60−, 0−, and about ten single slips of the transcription.

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.
