Adrianople and Constantinople → Hetman Sieniawski · three-digit syllabary in alphabetical bands · 1713–1714 · existing decipherment, partial

Stanisław Chomentowski at the Porte to Hetman Sieniawski, 1713–1714

→

Three letters of the Polish envoy to the Sultan, partly in a syllabic code that a second hand deciphered between the lines; the decipherment was transcribed and checked, and gaps in the faintest letter filled from the code’s own order.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

At a glanceExisting decipherment · partial · 2,129 of 2,230 tokens (95.5%); R7476 94.9%

The letters
Stanisław Chomentowski, voivode of Mazovia and Polish envoy at the Porte, to Hetman Adam Mikołaj Sieniawski: Adrianople 9 May 1713, an undated letter of 1713, and (probably his) a letter from Constantinople of May–August 1714.
Where
Archiwum Narodowe w Krakowie, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 24/11 no. 116 (DECODE R7484, R7485, catalogued “Krakow_UNK_3/4” with no shelfmark) and 26/32 nr 13 pp. 31–34 (R7476).
The cipher
Three-digit numbers for syllables, in alphabetical bands (a 6–8, b 14–21 … z 320–333), names heading their band (Cesarz 42, Moskwa 160, Turcy 271), mixed with clear Polish.
How it was deciphered
A second hand glossed the numbers syllable by syllable between the lines, and on R7485 also wrote the passage out in clear. The glosses were transcribed and checked number by number; the faint ones of R7476 were re-read at high contrast.
What it says
The Seraskier and the Khan, the road to Bender, the hospodar of Wallachia, an intercepted letter: “Seraskierowi Turcy wierzyć nie chcą i Hanowi”.
Still open
83 tokens of R7476 and 18 of R7484: syllables known but no word found, a few names and one-off codes, three illegible signs.

DECODE catalogues these records with letters in other keys, which have their own pages (a different cipher in each): Brâncoveanu and Cantacuzino (sieniawski1708), news from Bender in 1709 (turculet1709), an adviser’s letter (r7475) and a Vienna agent to Baron Schenck (schenck1706).

01 The letters

R7484 and R7485 are one archival unit, Zbiór Dzieduszyckich 24/11 no. 116 (the archive wrapper gives it; DECODE has “Krakow_UNK_3/4”). R7484 is dated Adrianople, 9 May 1713. R7485 is undated; its cipher passage is written out in clear at the head of the page by the hand that glossed it: “Seraskierowi Turcy wierzyć nie chcą i Hanowi, i dlatego mnie prosto wołają. Seraskier pisał do hospodara wołoskiego, aby mi perswadował, żebym na Bender jechał”.

R7476 (nr 13 pp. 31–34) was bound with the Brâncoveanu letters. Code XVIII is glossed Hospodar Multański, Brâncoveanu, who was held in Yedikule from April 1714, so the letter is of May–August 1714, from Constantinople. New passages include “on z tąd nie ruszy, póki mu Porta nie da takiego konwoju”, “z listu przejętego”, “sołtan gałga uciekł do Czerkies” and “donieść Jmci Panu Kanclerzowi Koronnemu”.

02 The syllabary, and the checks

The syllabary was rebuilt from the glosses of the three letters. Its numbers run in alphabetical bands, and the names of persons and powers stand at the head of their letter’s band (Cesarz 42 among the c’s, Moskwa 160, Turcy 271), so a number whose gloss is lost can be bounded by its neighbours.

  • R7485: 96 of 96 tokens give sense.
  • R7484: 495 of 513 (96.5%; three clear date figures taken out of the count). Code 76 is ferman (two glosses, the f band, and R7476’s “dano ferman”); slips settled (191 for 181 in Hanowi; 160 is Moskwa, not 169).
  • R7476: 1,538 of 1,621 (94.9%). Its glosses are faint. They were re-read at high contrast, the bad stretches re-transcribed digit by digit at 2.5–3 times (this hand closes its 7 like a 4), candidate words scored against a Polish model and checked against the code’s bands, and names checked against Perłakowski’s study of the embassy (Jan Spiegel, “Pan Szpigieł”; Franciszek Goltz). Codes 135 Kanclerz, 215 Porta, 280 u and 310 x were added.

03 What remains uncertain

  • R7476: 83 of 1,621 tokens give no word: 67 whose syllables are known from gloss or code but form no word after seven passes, 8 in names not identified (code 216, glossed “Pułta”), five one-off codes with no gloss (2, 224, 266, 299 twice), three illegible.
  • R7484: 18 of 513, in three short passages whose syllables are certain but whose words are not recovered (“umowi-ia t-r-ze-y s-za psuje”), and code 112, whose gloss is a flourish.
  • R7476 is ascribed to Chomentowski by its content and code, not by a signature.

R7484, Adrianople, 9 May 1713

Chomentowski, voivode of Mazovia and Polish envoy at the Porte, to Hetman Sieniawski: the Kapudan Pasha, made vizier, began to change course; a public divan on 24 April declared war sub ignoto nomine, with fear of a march on Chocim and the envoys sent to Yedikule; on the 26th the vizier was deposed and strangled, so things are in confusion.

Summary: Chomentowski, on his way to or at Bender/Adrianople, reports that the Turks will not believe the seraskier
or the Khan and therefore summon him directly; the seraskier wrote to the hospodar of Wallachia to persuade him
to go to Bender; he has escaped the first trap; the hospodar is friendly to the Poles but the Khan denounces him to
the Porte. Undated, but on the 1712-13 embassy (Chomentowski entered Turkey 1712; R7484, the same code, is dated
Adrianople 9 May 1713).
