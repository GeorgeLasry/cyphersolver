01 The letters

Scipion de Fiesque, a Genoese in French service, was Charles IX’s envoy at the court of Maximilian II from the autumn of 1567, when the King’s marriage to the Emperor’s daughter Elisabeth was being negotiated. BnF Français 15919 (formerly Harlay 238) holds 174 folios of his letters to the King and to the Queen Mother. Most open and close in clear and carry the sensitive part in cipher. Satoshi Tomokiyo noted that many are partly in cipher and reconstructed the key so that the passages with no decipherment of the time could be deciphered.

Harumi-Matsuba’s page list (44 pages) runs from October 1567 to 4 November 1569: eighteen letters to the Queen Mother and five to the King (15 February 1568, 4 September 1568, 10 September, 20 October and 29 October 1569). One is dated from Speyer (2 December 1568), the rest from Vienna. The per-page table, with view numbers, sign counts and a one-line summary, is in contributed/docs/PAGES.en.md in the repository folder.

02 The key

Tomokiyo’s table (Cryptiana, “French Ciphers for German (and Eastern) Affairs”) gives each letter two to five signs: digits (4 = b, 1 and 8 = i, 9 = n), letters used as signs (a and o = e, x = f, p = l, u = o), and invented forms (Δ = i, ϕ = m, ⊢ = q, ⊣ = r, ß = r). There are signs for doubled letters (ee, ff, ll, mm, nn, rr, ss), word codes (que, qu’il, et, vous, pour, le Roy, l’Empereur, and Φ for Saxe) and groups of clear-looking letters (par, moy, bro) that are nulls. The contributor named the signs (key.tsv, 95 entries) and inferred 23 values the table lacks, among them 16 = ce, ρ = e and the pair ꝑꝑ = ff. All 44 pages are in this one key.

03 What was contributed

Harumi-Matsuba opened issue #52 on 20 October 2026 and extended it twice the same day, with the work in a public repository (data CC BY 4.0, code MIT). The account was created on 19 October. Their own description of the method:

  • First transcription by Anthropic’s Claude, from line crops of the BnF’s full-size images.
  • Disagreements with OpenAI Codex transcriptions shown on enlarged crops to Codex and Claude Sonnet; a sign was corrected only where both agreed.
  • Afterwards, fresh Codex transcriptions made without sight of the first: 85.0–93.6% agreement by value per page (the lower run where there are two).
  • For every page, 1,000 keys with the letter values shuffled: none scores as French-like as the true key.
  • Line-by-line readings of five letters, most lines marked as inferred (word division, supplied spelling, the choice between s and p for one looped sign); mechanical decodes of all 44 pages.

A person chose the target and decided to publish; no person checked the transcription. Agreement between two AI readers measures their consistency, and a shuffled-key control shows the key is right, but neither shows how much of the text reads as sense. This project’s bar for a complete reading is 95% of the cipher read as sense, measured. So the result is recorded here as a contributed result with a partial extent.

04 Measured here

The contributed key was re-applied to every transcription with this project’s own decoder (measure.py):

  • 29,627 of 29,707 signs take a value (99.7%). That is the share of signs with a value, not of text read: see the note after this list.
  • Under the project’s period French model (fr-1530-despatches) the decodes score −2.37 to −3.42 per character; the best of 200 shuffled keys per page scores −4.38 to −5.31, and none of the 8,800 reaches the true key. Clean French scores −1.64 on the same model.
  • Split into words of a period French lexicon, 81.0% of the decoded letters fall in words, against 27.6% for shuffled keys and 90.3% for clean modern French with the spaces removed. Pages range from 68% (view 8 left) to 90% (view 155 right).

The key is right and the decodes are mostly French, but between a fifth and a tenth of the text is not yet words, which matches the contributor’s own marking of most lines as inferred.

Reading audit, 22 October 2026. Read as sentences (George Lasry’s test: can the text be built into sentences and translated?), only two pages hold: f. 79r and f. 94r in the contributed readings, on Archduke Charles’s journey to Spain, with scattered holes. The readings of ff. 39r–v, 100r and 158r have holes in most lines, and the 44 mechanical decodes are unspaced with wrong letters (0–17% of sentences clean). So that day 21 letters, and the other pages of the two, were decoded, not read: about 6% of the signs read, against the 99.7% that take a value. The re-reading that followed is in section 06.

05 A check against 1567

The volume holds one decipherment made at the time: view 23 right, headed “deschiffré de la precedente”, is a clear copy of the letter of 15 November 1567. The contributor found it but did not use it, because AI readers of its cursive agreed only 51–55% with their decode. It was transcribed here by eye from a 2,600-pixel image, as far as the end of the cipher on view 22 right (calib_v023r.txt).

Of its 1,193 letters, the contributed decode of view 22 right matches 1,000 (83.8%), and 98 of its 182 words of three or more letters come out whole (calib.py). The sense runs the same throughout: Lignerolles has left for the Duke of Saxony, it is rumoured that horse and foot are being raised in Germany for those who have taken arms against the King, the Emperor expects his mandates to stop it, he has written “de bonne encre noire” to two princes. Two places differ in content. Where the copy has “au pays du duc de Wirtemberg et du conte palatin”, the decode has the Φ code for Saxe and no palatin. And the looped sign that serves for both s and p gives desuis, sarteiment for depuis, partement. This reading of the cursive is itself not checked by a second reader.

The same check found a gap: the cipher of this letter continues on view 23 left (f. 18v), which is not in the contributed transcription. Its text is on the decipherment page, from “de son pays d’Austriche” to the signature. View 23 left was transcribed and decoded here on 23 October 2026 (13 lines, 446 signs) and checked against the decipherment, which abridges it.

06 The reading

On 22 October 2026 every letter was built here into French sentences from the key decode and translated (read/<date>_reading.md and _en.md); four letters (15 November 1567, 4 June, 21 August and 6 November 1568) follow decipherments of the time found in the volume, on view 23 right and in the margins of views 65, 103 and 125 right, three of them not recorded before. On 23 October view 6 right (the first cipher page of 11 October 1567) and view 23 left were transcribed from native-resolution crops, the sign Dz was settled as con, and the single-word holes of seventeen letters were worked through on the images (161 bracketed holes down to 90): this hand writes the number codes with z for 2 and i for 1 (“zo” = 20 pour, “i4” = 14 estre), which closes many of them. Twenty-two letters now build sentences. The letter of 10 September 1569 stays decoded, not read: its reading clears the sentence test, but 4.1% of its letters rest on look-alike sign choices that nothing outside confirms. Square brackets mark supplied letters or open codes, [?word] a doubtful word, [...] an unread place.

6 March 1568, to the Queen Mother (f. 39r): j'ay entendu que le duc Jehan Guillaume [de] Saxe, qui est allé en France pour vostre service, a amené avec luy six prescheurs qui sont d'une secte qui s'appelle Flacciani.

4 September 1568, to the Queen Mother (f. 100r; docketed “au Roy”, but the cipher addresses “Madame”), on the marriage: Il m'a dict qu'a ceste heure il n'y a plus aucune difficulté que le mariaige [ne se] face, car le prince d'Espaigne estoict mort; mais que [pp] desiroict d'estre mediateur [de] ce mariaige. The lone code pp is the King of Spain by sense.

29 October 1569, to the King (f. 158r), after Moncontour: Lequel m'a dict qu'il ne s'est meu a mettre en avant ce party que pour une sincere affection et grand desir qu'il a de [vous] veoir delivré de ceste guerre intestine.

The mechanical decodes of all 44 pages are in contributed/data/decoded_*.txt, the contributor’s line readings of five letters in contributed/docs/READING.en.md.

07 What remains uncertain

  • 10 September 1569: decoded, not read, for its look-alike sign choices (the y-shaped s/p signs and others); the partial reading is in read/1569-09-10_reading.md.
  • Phrase holes after the image checks, each re-read on native crops: f. 79v lines 1–3 and f. 80 lines 1 and 8 (17 July 1568); f. 100r line 18 and three words in lines 24–26 (4 September 1568); view 38 left line 17 (15 February 1568); f. 39r lines 8, 22–23 and 29 (6 March 1568). No decipherment of the time covers them.
  • About 90 single-word holes in the seventeen letters checked on 23 October, each with its figures in the reading logs.
  • View 6 right (11 October 1567): two words in lines 5–6 and about ten doubtful words; view 23 left (15 November 1567): the bearer’s name (nine signs) and about seventeen signs that the decipherment of the time abridges.
  • Lone signs and codes whose values are not settled: ϖ (the Emperor or the King, eleven places), ∞ alone, =ii, pp (the King of Spain by sense), the group ffx-3y and a few more; 16 is ce or vostre.
  • Transcription: the contributed transcription is AI-only and no person has checked it; the pages transcribed here are single AI transcriptions checked against the key. Untranscribed cipher was found on views 7 left, 110 right, 34 and 49.
