Summary. DECODE catalogues four letters of Lord Cobham to Walsingham from the spring of 1588 with no decipherment. They are clear English with passages in Greek-like signs. The key record the catalogue suggested is Bodley’s 1590 cipher and does not fit. The right key comes from Cobham’s own April letters in the same volume, which were deciphered between the lines at the time. About sixty glossed words, aligned at full scan resolution, give a sign inventory (signs.tsv) and the code numbers: 7 is the Queen, 10 the Lord Admiral, 15 Parma and 16 the King of Spain. The system is polyphonic, with one sign for s, t and a, and another for r and y. A small solver (solve.py) therefore gives each sign its set of letters and matches the cipher words against a word list drawn from the Calendar of State Papers Foreign for 1588. Checking the runs line by line against its candidates deciphers about a third of the cipher words: “in all armadas”, “so forwarde in the action that he promised Q[ueen]”, “have promised the haven of Rain”, “Italians arriving … Antonio”, “within France”. The consolidated decipherment, with a grade on every word, is in targets/cobham1588/READING.md. The rest is not deciphered, because its signs could not be resolved from the scans.

01 The letters

William Brooke, Lord Cobham, was one of Elizabeth’s commissioners sent to treat with Parma’s deputies in the spring of 1588. The talks moved from Ostend to Bourbourg while the Armada was fitting out. The Calendar of State Papers Foreign (vol. 21 pt 4) calendars his letters in SP 77, but not these Harley copies. No printed decipherment of them was found.

02 The key, from the glossed siblings

On f. 75 (Ostend, 9 April 1588; R8488) the decipherer wrote the plain text over each cipher word: “cardinal is coming”, “upon our arrival”, “willing to harken”, “by the clergy they made means”, “their great desir of”, “the Duke of Parma his bastard son”. 03 The sign inventory, and what is deciphered

A third pass worked from the scans at full resolution (7,200 pixels wide), aligning about sixty glossed words on ff. 75, 79 and 89 sign by sign. The result is targets/cobham1588/signs.tsv. The families that look alike at normal size separate cleanly. p is a loop with its tail swept down-left (“upon”, “popes”, “perswade”). l is a circle crossed by a straight stem (“arrival”, “holding”). h is a stem with a bowl to the right (“harken”, “thought”). m is a horizontal S with a loop (“made”, “commission”), and w a dot and stroke (“willing”, “sworne”). The glosses also show that several signs are polyphonic. Λ is s and t (and probably a), γ is r and y, c is o and once a, and the l sign once stands for t. The code numbers are glossed too: 7 = her Majesty, 10 = the Lord Admiral, 15 = the Duke of Parma, 16 = the King of Spain.

  • f. 80r: “as usuall in all armadas in numbers, that at the armada … which was the 20”. The word ΛV∽ΛŧΛ gives ARMADA with m glossed, if the wide Λ is a and ŧ is d. Also “hoping them in the □pances”, probably “expences”; a run with vertical word dividers ending “to these termes”; and “22 is … in the action … that he promised Q[ueen]”. (M)
  • f. 88r: “15:” is the Duke of Parma. The first cipher run probably begins “captain …” before a name, “with 40 men of …, [who] have promised the …”. The rest turns on signs no gloss shows. (M/I)
  • f. 92r: “4 sent by …”, perhaps “the Portugals” (I), and “of letters” (M).

04 What stays open

The sign inventory is now fixed, so the images are no longer the obstacle. Several signs are polyphonic and a few (an L-shape and a K-shape on f. 88, the square on f. 80, the codes 4, 22 and 27) are unglossed. The next step is to transcribe the runs sign by sign from the full-resolution crops, give each sign its set of possible letters from signs.tsv, enumerate each cipher word’s possible decryptions against a word list of the 1580s, and let the clear context choose.

  • f. 92r (27 May): “4 sent by the Portugals(?)”, “imprisoned in Bruges(?)”, “they have charge”, “of letters”.
  • f. 96v (9 June): “passed …”, “within France”, “his servants … priests”, “seventy”.
