Venice → Charles V · homophonic alphabet with a three-letter nomenclator · May–August 1523

Alonso Sánchez at Venice to Charles V, May–August 1523

Real Academia de la Historia, Madrid, Colección Salazar y Castro 9/28 (A-28), ff. 18–574; DECODE R9670, R9671, R9672, R9674, R9677, R9689, R9690, R9767, R9769, R9770. The catalogue listed these as ten ciphertexts with no decipherment, to an unknown recipient. They are the imperial ambassador’s despatches to Charles V from the months when the Venetian league was negotiated. Their key is Tomokiyo’s reconstruction of Sánchez’s 1522 cipher, applied unchanged. Bergenroth calendared seven of them in 1866 from the contemporary decipherings; the two August letters not in the Calendar were transcribed here in full from contemporary decipherments found on neighbouring leaves (23 October 2026).

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 21 September 2026 · updated 22 October 2026

Method: read with known key · Extent: complete

Summary. The records sit in the Salazar volume that follows the 1522 despatches (Sánchez 1522), and they use the same cipher in the same hand. DECODE flags every one of them “Inline Cleartext: Yes”, but that means passages written in the clear inside the letters; no decipherment was imaged with them. The decipherings made at court do exist, filed elsewhere in the volume. G. A. Bergenroth used them in 1866 for his Calendar of State Papers, Spain, vol. 2. Seven of the ten records are the despatches he calendared as nos. 549 (8 May), 552 (20 May), 554 (5 June) and 558 (13 June), or duplicates of them. The folio numbers match. The other three are dated 16 August 1523, the day after the league was published. They are not in the Calendar, and they are mostly in the clear. The 1522 key (Tomokiyo’s, with this project’s additions) decodes the code groups of every page examined, without change. Sánchez’s own letter of 16 August (R9767) was first deciphered here in part, and the reading audit of 22 October 2026 set its cipher passages back to decoded, not read. On 23 October the neighbouring records were fetched: DECODE R9768 (ff. 565–568, never opened) is the second copy of R9767, and its f. 567r is the court decipherment of R9767’s two cipher passages; R9770 f. 574r is the decipherment of the joint letter’s cipher paragraph, which stands on R9769 f. 570v after the date (nine lines, missed in September, so that letter is not all clear). Both were aligned group by group with the figures: the 1522 key holds, and one new code appears, pem = -mos. All ten records now build sentences.

01 The ten records

Every letter is headed Sacra cesarea y catholica magestad, so the “unknown recipient” is the Emperor. The dates come from the date lines and from internal references. The matches are by folio and content against Bergenroth’s abstracts, which carry the Salazar A-28 folio numbers.

02 The key

The cipher is the one Sánchez used in 1522. Satoshi Tomokiyo reconstructed it in 2025 from a letter with its decipherment attached, and the 1522 write-up extended it. It has an alphabet of cursive signs whose shapes are not their values, three nulls opening each paragraph, and a nomenclator of consonant–vowel–consonant groups assigned in alphabetical order of the plaintext. On R9670, R9671, R9672, R9677 and R9767 the groups decode coherently with that key and nothing contradicts it: xig de, vog en, ho que, nuf no, dom pud vuestra magestad, vum … lal esta [re]publica, gub rac señor infante, tum frances. The ambassador did not change his key between the two volumes.

03 Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 August 1523 (R9767)

From the reading of 23 October 2026 (R9767_reading.md, English R9767_en.md): the clear text, and the two cipher passages read from the court decipherment on f. 567r (DECODE R9768), checked group by group against the figures of f. 563r (R9767_cipher_align.md):

Pues por la que en comun escrivimos el prothonotario Caraciolo e yo vera vuestra magestad todo lo que occorre, no dire en esta salvo que recebi la de vuestra magestad de xv del passado, y quanto al entretener a este nuevo Duce hare lo que pudiere, ahunque a dezir a vuestra magestad verdad en el se conoce que no es mal frances; pero espero en dios que poco podra danyar, ahunque es bien toda via entretenerle.

Vuestra magestad vera por la capitulacion que no se ha expremido ninguna causa porque dan los desta republica el dinero a vuestra magestad; antes [?diversas] vezes en el tractar con ellos les diximos que vuestra magestad le pedia porque les dava una paz perpetua y no por otra causa, mostrando siempre que ni por el Imperio ni por la casa de Austria no se les havia de dar mas drecho del que tenian, y assi lo vera vuestra magestad en todas nuestras escrituras, pareciendonos que assi sera mas servicio de vuestra magestad.

Pareciendo nos lo mismo que vuestra magestad escrive, no quisimos obligar al señor Infante a la liga defensiva, pues estos no querian obligarse contra el turquo. Lo de la navegacion para los subditos del archiducado de Austria no se ha podido acabar con estos; estarse han como hasta aqui. Nuestro señor la imperial vida y estados de vuestra magestad prosperamente guarde con cumplimiento de la monarchia. De Venecia a xvj de agosto de mil D xxiij.

Sánchez refers the Emperor to the joint letter and acknowledges the imperial letter of 15 July. The new Doge “no es mal frances”, but can do little harm and should still be humoured. The capitulation expresses no reason why the Signoria pays the Emperor money: the ambassadors always said it was for a perpetual peace, not for any right of the Empire or the house of Austria. The earlier partial reading had the gist right but not this argument. The clear postscript on the defensive league and the Turk, and on shipping for the Archduke’s subjects, is not in the Calendar abstracts. The decipherment also corrects two code readings: the group read as yol is yoh causa, and mal is spelled out, not a code.

The joint letter, R9769/R9770. Caracciolo and Sánchez’s letter of the same day is transcribed in full, with its cipher paragraph read from the decipherment on f. 574r (R9769_reading.md, English R9769_en.md): the news of the peace and league concluded with Venice, the departure of the English ambassador and of the Infante’s ambassador, and prisoners released by the Signoria.

04 What remains open

  • R9767 and R9769/R9770: closed on 23 October 2026 from the court decipherments on ff. 567r and 574r; a few uncertain words in the joint letter ([?xxix], [?possessiones]).
  • R9670–R9690: not edited. Their content is in Bergenroth’s English abstracts, and a full Spanish edition would be transcription against the contemporary decipherings, not decipherment.

CSP Spain vol. 2 no. 549
8 May. M. Re. Ac. d. Hist. Salazar. A. 28. ff. 40-44. 549. Alonso Sanchez, Imperial Ambassador in Venice, to the Emperor. His negotiations with the Republic have been somewhat interrupted by the illness of Georgio Cornaro, who is one of the commissioners and a good Imperialist. The Doge, who was ninety years of age, died on the 7th, and it was necessary to wait for the election of another Doge. That was another cause of delay. On the 8th he and the ambassador of the King of England went to the Signory, and said they knew that the Doge had died, but as in Venice affairs of state are not interrupted by such an event, they begged them to give their definite answer about the alliance (between Venice, the Emperor, and the King of England). They answered that they would send them their commissioners. Fears that ... (fn. 1) Knows very well that until a new Doge is elected nothing will be done. Fears that the Venetians, under the pretext that they must first elect a new Doge, will delay their answer until they know whether the French will invade Italy, and until the Infante has sent his answer. The Pope has exhorted the Republic to conclude peace with him, and the English ambassador has done all he can to bring the affair to a speedy conclusion. Has had letters from the Infante. Has replied to them. Does not know what decision the Infante will make. Does not think that the exiles will pay the 18,000 ducats in one instalment, as they have promised. The Venetians answered the French in general words about their aid in case a French army should invade Italy. The French were not satisfied with the answer. Cardinal of Auch, &c.—Venice, the 8th of May 1523. Addressed : "To his Sacred, Imperial, and Catholic Majesty the Emperor and King, our Lord." Indorsed : "To the King. 1523. Venice. Alonso Sanchez. The 8th of May. Answered." Spanish. Autograph in cipher. Contemporary deciphering. pp . 6.

CSP Spain vol. 2 no. 552

The Venetians have elected a new Doge, Andrea Gritti, who is the most ardent partisan of France in Venice. Thinks, nevertheless, that the negotiations with the Signory are so far advanced that the Republic would conclude the treaty of alliance (with the Emperor and the King of England) if the Infante would only send his power.—Venice, the 20th of May 1523. CSP Spain vol. 2 no. 558
13 June. M. Re. Ac. d. Hist. Salazar. A. 28. ff. 224-228. 558. Alonso Sanchez, Imperial Ambassador in Venice, to the Emperor. The demands of the Infante place him in great difficulties. The Venetians refuse to continue the negotiations, although he (Alonso Sanchez), the nuncio, and the ambassador of the King of England have done all that is possible to persuade them to make peace with him (the Emperor). The Signory have sent to tell him that they still wish to conclude a league (with the Emperor and the King of England), but that the Infante must send his power and be a party to the treaty. They say they will entirely justify their behaviour to the Pope, to him (the Emperor), and to the King of England. Hopes the Prothonotary (Caracciolo) will soon arrive. Does not insist that the peace with Venice shall be expressly declared to be an eternal peace. It is said that the French will soon be in Italy, as neither he (the Emperor) nor the King of England occupy them much in the north—Venice, the 13th of June 1523. Indorsed : "To the King. 1523. Venice. Alonso Sanchez. The 13th of June. Answered." Spanish. Autograph in cipher. Contemporary deciphering. pp . 3.

# R9767 — Alonso Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 August 1523 (RAH 9/28, ff. 563–564)

Read from the DECODE images (IMG_R9767_I45759_P1–P2) with the 1522 key in `../sanchez1522/key_codes.tsv`
(Tomokiyo's table plus this project's additions) and `../sanchez1522/decode_tokens.py`. The letter is mostly
in the clear; only two passages on f. 563r are in cipher (about 18 lines). Code groups are resolved; the spelled
letter runs between them are only partly read. **Read in part.**

Notation: clear text in roman; `[code]` = code group read from the key; `‹…›` = spelled run, tentative;
`…` = not read. Grades as in the README conventions: H high, C confident, M moderate, I insecure.

## f. 563r, first paragraph (clear, then cipher from line 3)

> Pues por la que en comun escrivimos el prothonotario Caraciolo e yo vera vuestra magestad todo lo que
> occorre, no dire en esta salvo que recebi la de vuestra magestad de xv del passado, y quanto … [vog en] …
> [pac lo] [ho que] … aunque a dezir a vuestra magestad verdad [vog en] [veg el] [gad se] [xa con] …
> [ho que] [nuf no] [ta es] … [tum frances]. Pero espero en dios que poco podra danyar, aunque es bien toda via
> [vog en] … — then the three nulls.

Gist (M): having received the Emperor's letter of 15 July, Sánchez refers him to the joint letter with
Caracciolo; the cipher sentence concerns something French-leaning in Venice ("…que no es … frances"), which he
hopes "can do little harm", though it is well to stay watchful. The clear words around it carry the sense.

## f. 563r, second paragraph (cipher after the opening clear words)

> Vuestra magestad vera por la capitulacion [ho que] [nuf no] [gad se] [suf ha] ‹expre›‹ssa›[vuc do] ninguna
> cula (*sic*, clear) [lu por] [ho que] [xof da] … [pac lo] [xig de] [vum esta] ‹re›[lal publica] [veg el]
> [vu di]‹nero› a [dom vuestra] [pud magestad] … [vog en] [veg el] … [xa con] [veg el] … [pem ?] [ho que]
> [dom vuestra] [pud magestad] … [lu por] [ho que] … [xof da] … [dal un] … [nuf no] [lu por] … [ho que]
> [nef ni] [lu por] [veg el] … [nef ni] … [xig de] … [nuf no] [gad se] … [sam haui]… [xig de] [xof da]‹r›
> [ped mas] … [xel del] [ho que] … [pac lo] [dib ver]‹e› [dom vuestra] [pud magestad] [vog en] …
> [vog en] [vuc do] [nuf no] … [gad se] … [ped mas] [gad se] … [xig de] [dom vuestra] [pud magestad] — nulls.

Gist (M/I): on the capitulation of the Venetian league (signed 29 July, published 15 August): nothing was
expressed in it about … ; the money of this Republic to be given to Your Majesty …; "no se havia de dar mas";
"lo vera vuestra magestad". The subject is plainly the financial clause of the treaty (the Venetian payment the
ambassadors had failed to raise; CSP 566, 577), but the spelled runs that carry the particulars are not read.

## f. 564r (clear)

> Pareciendo nos lo mismo que vuestra magestad escrive, no quisimos obligar al señor Infante a la liga
> defensiva, pues estos no querian obligarse contra el turco. Lo de la navegacion para los subditos del
> archiducado de Austria no se ha podido acabar con estos; estarse han como hasta aqui. Nuestro Señor la
> imperial vida y estados de vuestra magestad prospere … De Venecia a xvj de agosto de mil D xxiij.
> Humillimos siervos y vasallos que sus imperiales pies y manos besan … alonso sanchez.

(The subscription is in the plural although only Sánchez signs; the leaf is written in the same hand as the
joint letter R9769/R9770 of the same day.)

Almost every code group on f. 563r is in the 1522 key (not counted exactly). The exceptions seen are `pem`
and `yol`; in the alphabetical order `yol` falls between *causa* and *cierto*. What remains open is the spelled
letter runs, not the codes.
