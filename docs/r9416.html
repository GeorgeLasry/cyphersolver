The Pressburg report and a servant’s letter, c. 1533–34

A Bavarian agent’s report on the Pressburg talks between the two kings of Hungary was broken from its ciphertext alone; the servant’s letter filed with it was deciphered at the time.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 15 October 2026

At a glanceCiphertext-only break · partial · f. 263 a working decipherment, six lines clean

The documents
Two leaves of one DECODE record. f. 262: a Bavarian servant to his duke, “eritags nach Jacobi” (the Tuesday after 25 July). f. 263: a report on the Pressburg negotiations between Ferdinand and John Zápolya, about 1533–34, probably its enclosure.
Where
Bayerisches Hauptstaatsarchiv, Munich, Kurbayern Äußeres Archiv 4591 ff. 262–263; DECODE R9416, part of catalogue entry 161, “partially decrypted”.
The ciphers
Two different sets of invented signs. f. 263 is a homophonic substitution with about 23 frequent signs and word division, and a few codes for persons.
How it was deciphered
f. 263: a solver (simulated annealing) with a language model built for it from German prints of 1472–1609 gave running German on its first run; a faint gloss of the time over the first lines, read afterwards, agrees word for word. f. 262: the clerk’s decipherment over every line, transcribed in part.
What it says
The diet at Pressburg, Ferdinand’s party arriving “mit grossem Pomp”, a Turkish embassy on Shrove Tuesday, Imre Czibak offering the Sultan tribute, Alvise Gritti dispatched. The servant asks for seven years’ pension and the office of Oberrichter at Straubing.
Still open
A clean decipherment of f. 263 beyond six lines; three person codes; faint words of the f. 262 gloss.

Until 15 October 2026 this record was filed on one page with thirteen other ciphertexts of the same key volume. They are different ciphers, each with its own page; the largest group is System A′ of the dukes’ agents in Hungary. The two leaves here are in two ciphers of their own and are kept together as one record, with a part for each.

01 f. 263: the report on the Pressburg talks

3,150 signs, word-separated. The solver, held to the frequency limits of a homophonic cipher, gave German at once with the new model: “…uon beden kunigen zu entlicher handlung gen Pressburg gesetzt… der tag zu Pressburg erfolgt ist… mit grossem pomp… dem turkischen kaiser… tribut… zu geben…”. The faint gloss on the first lines (“nunmen tag uon beden kunigen zu entlicher handlung gen bresburg gesetzt”) agrees with it word for word, which confirms the break independently. The report concerns the negotiations between “beden kunigen”, Ferdinand and John Zápolya, and their commissaries; a Turkish embassy; “der Emrich beeche” (Imre Czibak) offering the Sultan tribute and “tausent tucaten”; and “den hern Griti abgefertigt” (Alvise Gritti, killed in September 1534), so about 1533–34.

Lines 11–16 of the recto were deciphered word by word at the image (30 September 2026). One transcription code covers two shapes: the zig-zag with one bar is e, the dagger with two bars is s; another covers d and l. In normalised spelling:

… daraus der Tag zu Pressburg erfolgt ist, dahin auch des [Ferd.] Tail mit grossem Pomp kummen sint, auch volmechtigen Gwalt anzaigt, und sich zu beden Tailn entliche Vertrage versehen. Es hat sich aber zutragen, das am Ertag in der Fasnacht von dem turkischen Kaiser ain treffliche Botschaft ankummen …

(“… from which the diet at Pressburg followed, whither also [Ferdinand’s] party came with great pomp and showed full powers, and on both sides a final treaty was expected. But it came about that on Shrove Tuesday a distinguished embassy arrived from the Turkish emperor …”) [Ferd.] is a code sign identified only by the faint gloss; three signs after “Tail”, passed over by the gloss, are not rendered.

02 f. 262: a servant’s letter to his duke

The letter is glossed over every line in German by a hand of the time, so it was deciphered when it arrived; the gloss is faint and has been transcribed in part here. The servant writes of troops in Austria and Styria and of the Italian and Spanish foot (“walischen und spanischen knechten”) to move through Styria and by the Danube into Hungary, and asks for seven years’ outstanding pension and the office of Oberrichter at Straubing. It is dated “eritags nach Jacobi”. Its signs are a different set from f. 263 and were not transcribed.

03 What stays open

  • f. 263 beyond lines 11–16 is a working decipherment, running German with local errors; it needs the same hand re-keying. One sign serves ch where the solver gives s.
  • f. 263: the person codes “der d”, “der g”, “der h” (probably Ferdinand, Gritti’s party and the Hungarian king; g is read as Ferdinand once, from the gloss).
  • f. 262: the faint words of the gloss, and three lines where none is visible.

f.263 (pp. 3-4), report on the Pressburg talks, c. 1533-34

A Bavarian agent's report on the Pressburg negotiations between 'beden kunigen' (Ferdinand and John Zápolya), a Turkish embassy, Imre Czibak's offer of tribute to the Sultan and Alvise Gritti's dispatch, so c. 1533-34. Broken ciphertext-only by constrained annealing: decrypt_f263.txt is the solver's working decrypt of the whole leaf (local errors, before the look-alike split); recto lines 11-16 are re-keyed by hand and checked on the image (reading_p3_11-16.txt).

f.262 (pp. 1-2), servant's letter to his duke, 'eritags nach Jacobi'

A Bavarian servant to his duke, Tuesday after 25 July: troops in Austria and Styria, the Italian and Spanish foot to move through Styria and by the Danube into Hungary; he asks for seven years' pension and the Oberrichter post at Straubing. Read at the time: the faint contemporary interlinear gloss is transcribed in part; the cipher signs of this leaf were never transcribed.

Gracious lord: after I set out on my journey (on your business), I went through ... to the Count of Schauenburg(?);
the passage was impossible, because it is not only in Austria but also in Styria that (troops) are quartered, and
recently ... lay down ... [three lines unglossed] ... with the Walloon and Spanish soldiers, who are to go through
Styria and along the Danube, the other force into Hungary. Therefore my humble view is that Your Princely Grace (send)
... to give a full report of everything, and I would also write what is needful; ... some four thousand strong(?);
they have come out under cover of an embassy ...; serve neither friend nor foe ...; as it pleases Your Princely
Grace ... it is not safe to pass through ... I have therefore undertaken ... [page B] to set certain practices
begun through Herr ... going further, and to claim my outstanding pension of seven years. I ask therefore that Your
Princely Grace, in part payment of my salary, appoint me with fifty gulden as Oberrichter at Straubing, if Your
Grace sees fit, since I otherwise lack means to live; I also have a letter of recommendation from Your Princely
Grace, or where that is not convenient, from his embassy. Consider my long service, for which neither the
innkeepers nor the time have given me anything. I commend myself humbly to Your Princely Grace in all this.
