Bienvenut to the duc de Nevers, Paris, February 1590

A news-letter from League Paris, copied in clear except for nine names and provinces coded by small numbers; every code is resolved by the key slip bound after it, but only a sample of the letter is transcribed here so far.

The letter
Bienvenut to “Vostre Altesse”, Paris, the last day of February 1590: a copy, in clear except for code numbers for persons and provinces. The style and the content suggest the duc de Nevers.
Where
BnF français 3623 no. 23, ff. 35–36 (DECODE R9453, catalogued there as a key record); catalogue item 30, with the Champagne news-letters.
The cipher
A name code of nine numbers between 1 and 53: the King of Spain, the Pope, the King, Mayenne, Madame and M. de Nemours, Saint-Sorlin, the towns of the Union, the Duke of Parma.
How it was deciphered
The key is bound with the copy on f. 36r, and every number in the letter is on it.
What it says
Madame de Nemours walks Bienvenut through the Queen’s gallery and shows him Saint-Sorlin’s letters; the Pope and the King of Spain are close allies, and the League waits for papal troops to keep the war going.
Still open
Only a sample of ff. 35r–v (about 90 words) is transcribed in the repository; the rest of the clear copy is still to be transcribed from the Gallica images (reading audit, 22 October 2026). Which reading of the two dual entries (15, 17) applies in each place, and the recipient, are taken from context.

This letter was filed with three news-letters of June 1590 that use a two-digit alphabet, a different cipher with its own page (Champagne news letters, June 1590); their numbers conflict with this key (there 35 is a, here Mayenne).

01 The letter and its key

Every number in the letter is on the slip, so every code decodes (grade H). It is addressed to “Vostre Altesse” and reports the court of the League in Paris. Only a sample of the clear copy is transcribed in the repository so far (texts/n23_decryption.md); the codes are resolved in brackets:

[Mme de Nemours] me pourmena hyer par la gallerie du logis de la Reyne ... elle me monstra plus[ieu]rs avis qu'elle avoit eu de [St Sorlin] ... qu'il y a une tres estroicte intelligence entre [le Pape] & [le Roy d'Espagne] ... le courrier que Mr le Legat despescha a Rome ... pour entretenir la guerre jusques a ce que [le Pape] puysse envoyer 8 ou 10000 hommes de guerre avec lesquelz il baste l'ame a [Mayenne] & les porte sans qu'il a d'accabler [le Roy]

The rest of ff. 35r–v is a clear copy still to be transcribed from the Gallica images (canvases 79–80); nothing in it needs the key beyond the codes on the slip.

02 What stays open

  • Numbers 15 (the towns of the Union, or Provence and Dauphiné) and 17 (the Duke of Parma, or Picardy) each carry two readings on the slip; the one used in each place is chosen from the context.
  • The recipient, “Vostre Altesse”, is probably the duc de Nevers (the volume is his correspondence); the letter does not name him.
  • The copy has 26 code numbers (15 on the recto, 11 on the verso), all nine values on the slip.

Full texts

The transcription and the decryption of every document on this page, so that the reading can be used and checked. Short files are shown here; all are in the repository.

Paris, 28 Feb 1590, BnF fr. 3623 no. 23, ff. 35r-v (key f. 36r)

Bienvenut reports from Paris to 'Vostre Altesse' (probably the duc de Nevers, not proven) that Madame de Nemours showed him advices from Saint-Sorlin of a close understanding between the Pope and the King of Spain, and that the Legate's courier went to Rome to keep the war going until the Pope can send 8 or 10,000 men to stiffen Mayenne. The copy is in clear except nine persons and provinces coded by small numbers, all on the key slip bound with it.

# Bienvenut to "Vostre Altesse", Paris, 28 February 1590 (BnF fr. 3623 no. 23, ff. 35r-v): English translation of the excerpt

"[Madame de Nemours] walked me yesterday through the gallery of the Queen's lodging ... she showed me several reports
she had had from [Saint-Sorlin] ... that there is a very close understanding between [the Pope] and [the King of Spain]
... the courier whom the Legate despatched to Rome ... to keep the war going until [the Pope] can send 8 or 10,000 men
of war, with whom he may put heart into [Mayenne] and carry them on, without his having to crush [the King]."
