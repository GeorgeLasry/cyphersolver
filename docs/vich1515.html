Pedrezuela → Rome · code groups and signs, unidentified system · 26 October 1515

Ferdinand the Catholic to Jerónimo de Vich, Pedrezuela, 1515

The last ciphered letter of Ferdinand to his Rome ambassador in the Vich papers uses a code that no deciphered letter shares. Michael Brackx recovered its symbol alphabet from the ciphertext alone; only part of the code groups could be identified, so the letter is decoded, not read.

The letter
Ferdinand the Catholic to Jerónimo de Vich, his ambassador at Rome, Pedrezuela, 26 October 1515. Three months before the King’s death; almost all in cipher.
Where
Archivo Histórico Nacional, Estado 8715 N.73 (PARES 12751418), in the Vich family papers. No decipherment on the leaves or in PARES.
The cipher
Homophonic letter signs mixed with three-letter code groups that stand for whole words and also for syllables and single letters inside spelled words.
How it was broken
By Michael Brackx, from the ciphertext alone: a joint annealer for sign and group values under a Spanish five-gram, then the groups read by context in three passes.

A matched control on Lazarillo de Tormes recovers every sign; the statistical validation here passes.
Still open
144 unread group tokens, 192 tentative ones, and two sign splits the PARES images are too small to check.

Ferdinand’s letters to Vich of 1511–12 are in another cipher and have their own page, Ferdinand the Catholic to Jerónimo de Vich, 1511–12: a different cipher, with its own page. This letter was listed with them until 14 October 2026.

01 The letter

The Vich papers in the Archivo Histórico Nacional hold Ferdinand’s letters to Jerónimo de Vich, his ambassador at Rome from 1507. Most of the ciphered ones were filed with a clerk’s decipherment. N.73 was not. Its only clear text is the docket a 26 de octubre 1515, the opening words videlicet iterum, and the dateline en Pedrezuela a xxvj de otubre de dxv. Pedrezuela lies north of Madrid. Ferdinand’s next letter to Vich, of November 1515 (calendared in CSP Spain II, no. 240), refers back to this one of “the 26th of the previous month”.

02 The cipher

The transcription (n73_transcription.txt) has 124 lines and 3,177 tokens of 197 kinds. 1,326 are lower-case code groups as written, the commonest sin (151 times), sud (97), no (93), zre (89) and xed (68). 1,851 are signs, the commonest labelled X (171), TH (170), PHI (163) and RH (151). None of the 1511–12 values carries over, and N.74 of 1519, the nearest in date, is a third system.

Brackx’s solution shows how it works. The signs are a homophonic alphabet of 37 kinds, a few of them letter pairs: PHI is s, RH e, TH o, both O and Z a. The code groups stand for common words (zre que, sud de, yob por, yem papa, teb Italia, xif liga), but also for syllables and letters in the middle of words spelled with signs: tok is ha in haueros, gso is l, no is t. The September 2026 attempt here annealed the groups either as letters or as whole words, never as both, which is why it failed.

03 How the key was recovered

Michael Brackx (with Claude Code, Claude Opus 5.5) used the joint annealer he built for Espagnol 318 no. 93: every sign gets a letter or a common letter pair, every code group a word, syllable or letter from a fixed list, or “unknown”. The score is a Spanish character five-gram (Celestina, Quijote) plus, for each sign and group, the cost of the encipherment given the key, which stops the search collapsing onto a few letters. Values were fixed only when the best runs agreed and the decoded contexts confirmed them; with every sign fixed the score rose from −3.73 to −3.37 per token.

The code groups were then read by context in three passes: 40 kinds firm (1,005 tokens, 76%), 29 tentative (177), 90 unread (144). Two labels hide more than one sign. sin is ſin (two minims) for es and ſim (three) for en, side by side on page 3; the 151 tokens were split by the language model in context and six close calls checked on enlarged crops. X stands for r, s or z, again chosen by the model.

04 Checks

  • Matched control. Lazarillo de Tormes, held out of the model, enciphered the same way at the same size (3,282 tokens, 38 sign kinds, 158 group kinds). The same first step gets every sign token right and 35–44% of the group tokens: the alphabet comes out reliably, the groups only in part, as here.
  • Statistical validation (docs/_validate.py, firm values only, tokens as transcribed): the reading scores −2.07 per letter, random keys of the same shape −4.04, and a key annealed onto the shuffled ciphertext −2.76 (z 42.5). Verdict: pass.
  • How much reads. Measured on Brackx’s reading of all 124 lines, with every tentative value counted as a hole: 0.636 of words read as sense. Look-alike readings are 3.9% of the letters. That is under the project’s 95% bar, so the letter is decoded, not read; Brackx grades it the same way.

05 What it says, as far as it reads

Ferdinand writes that he cannot help being muy malcontento that, through pride and delay, las cosas de Italia are in danger, and still less content at la flaquesa del papa, who seems set on terms that break what the league capitulated. Yo no creo ni creere it, he says; Vich is to strengthen the Pope de mi parte and not let him lose heart. The reading and Brackx’s English, with every hole marked, are in brackx2026/n73_reading.md and n73_en.md.

06 What remains open

  • 144 unread code-group tokens (90 kinds, most once or twice, many probably names) and 13 signs with no value.
  • 192 tentative group tokens, among them bay visorrey, suh Emperador, rie confederado: each needs a second context or a crib.
  • The sin/sim and X choices: PARES serves the leaves at about 1,147 pixels, too small to tell the minims and sibilants apart. A high-resolution copy from the AHN would settle them.
  • The Barón de Terrateig, Política en Italia del Rey Católico 1507–1516. Correspondencia inédita con el embajador Vich (CSIC, Madrid 1963), prints Ferdinand’s letters to Vich from this archive. Volume I is on the Internet Archive for lending only; volume II was not found online. If it prints this letter, it is the crib.
