The Duke of Sessa to Charles V, Rome, 18 April 1524

Real Academia de la Historia, Colección Salazar y Castro A-31 (9/31) ff. 128–131 · DECODE R9877 and R9878, catalogued as Luis Fernández, Rome, April 1424, Non-decrypted · the imperial ambassador reports to the Emperor on the Swiss, the Bishop of Veroli’s letters and a request to the Pope for money

Chosen because the catalogue date, 1424, would have made it the longest ciphertext before 1450; the letter is dated 1524 on its own last page. The key was recovered from adjacent plaintext: Sessa’s sibling letters in the same volume carry the court’s decipherments. The nine cipher lines are deciphered word by word except two code groups and one spelled clause; two longer despatches, of 14 April 1524 and 24 February 1525, were then deciphered in part with the same key. No earlier decipherment of the 18 April letter was found; Bergenroth did not calendar it.

The letters
The Duke of Sessa, imperial ambassador at Rome, to Charles V: the despatch of 18 April 1524 and its duplicate, and since 2 October 2026 two more of his despatches, 14 April 1524 and 24 February 1525, the day of Pavia.
Where
Real Academia de la Historia, Colección Salazar y Castro A-31 (9/31) ff. 128–131 (DECODE R9877, R9878, catalogued as “Luis Fernández, April 1424”), A-31 ff. 79–86 (R9873) and A-34 ff. 150–156 (R9898).

One nomenclator for all of Sessa’s letters of 1523–25: three-letter code groups for words and syllables, single signs to spell, inline with clear Spanish.
How it was deciphered
The key was rebuilt from sibling letters that carry the court’s decipherment, over seventeen passes. Pass 16 used six marginal decipherments found on R9898, which had been thought to have none, and Bergenroth’s abstracts of R9873 as a check on the order of topics; pass 17 pooled the open groups of three letters and took tentative values that read in every occurrence.

On 18 April, the Swiss and a request to the Pope for money. On 14 April, the disbanding of the army, the Archbishop of Capua’s truce articles, a Pope en grandissimo conflicto. On 24 February 1525, the Datary’s French partisanship and Albany’s march on Naples: dueleme grandemente hallarme a lo que por razon y no por caso temo que suceda.

01The documents and their date

DECODE uploaded the Salazar volumes 9/26 to 9/39 in January 2026 as about a hundred records of Sessa and Lope Hurtado de Mendoza, in Rome from 1522 to 1526. Eight records in 9/31 carry the year 1424 (R9877 to R9884); the next ones, from the same volume and the same hands, carry 1524. The target leaf says so itself, on f. 128v: De Roma xviij de Abril 1524. The volume is the one Bergenroth cites as “M. Re. Ac. d. Hist. Salazar. A. 31” in the Calendar of State Papers, Spain, vol. 2.

02The cipher

A nomenclator in a rounded chancery hand. Three-letter groups stand for words and syllables: gap que, sof de, mus la, dim se, coh Su Santidad, fad rey, Jep paz, bob tregua, cum suyços, po3 Francia, mef Inglaterra. Single signs spell: α a, 4 o, x e or i, ꝺ r, nf p, ott t, # g, tto z, and a p with a loop is the plural -s. A flourished f marks each change between clear and cipher, and v db opens each ciphered paragraph as a null.

03The letter of 18 April 1524

The letter opens in clear. Sessa had seen a letter of the Bishop of Veroli from Constance and sent to the Pope for particulars. Felice, the papal secretary, told him the bishop’s letters of the 6th and 7th reported that the Swiss diet was over and that Lucerne had not agreed to help the French. Bern, Fribourg and Solothurn, the letter goes on, were spreading word that the Swiss in Lombardy were surrounded, so as to raise eight thousand men. The bishop’s friends in Switzerland wanted him to urge the Pope towards some form of peace. Esto es lo que me ha referido.

Then nine lines of cipher. Code groups are resolved; a hyphen joins letters spelled sign by sign; brackets mark what is not deciphered; bold is clear text written between the cipher runs.

las dichas [—]s no le las mostraron, que le ponen sospecha que desea aver algo que refiera de lo susodicho, aunque no / me parece que concuerda mucho con lo primero y postrero que el dicho obispo dize al maestro de postas / hablé en la ora a Su Santidad a pedirle que haya provisión de [—]-r-a los casos de suyços, persuadiéndole los m-re que r-ido los que no m-r-e-van / pues tiene color para ello, aviendo començado la plática de la paz y tregua … entender lo que mas podré. Al visorrey escrivo en el mismo punto.

In English: The said [letters] they did not show him, for they make him suspect a wish to get something out of them that he might report of the aforesaid, although not; it seems to me that it agrees closely with the first and last of what the said bishop writes to the postmaster. I spoke at once to His Holiness to ask him to make provision [of money] for the Swiss business, persuading him … since he has a pretext for it, having begun the negotiation of the peace and truce … to learn what more I can. I write to the Viceroy at the same moment.

Bergenroth’s abstract of the 22 April letter (CSP Spain 2, no. 643) continues the story: the Pope and the Swiss disapprove of the disbanding of the army, and there is “no provision of money from His Holiness or the Florentines beyond what I wrote”.

04The letter of 14 April 1524 (R9873)

A holograph of eight leaves (A-31 ff. 79–86), clear with cipher runs on ff. 80v–84r and a postscript wholly in cipher on f. 86r, dated in clear A xiiij de Abril 1524. No decipherment is bound with it. Bergenroth calendars it as no. 637, “deciphered by Don Manuel de Goicoechea” for him in the nineteenth century; no. 636 is the triplicate of the same despatch with a contemporary deciphering, and no. 638 a court abstract. Those English abstracts were used for the order of topics only: no group value was taken from them without a second Spanish context.

The whole letter was transcribed on 2 October 2026 and read with the pass-16 key. 2,165 of 2,473 cipher tokens read as sense (87.5%), each assembled word scored on a Golden Age Spanish model; 256 tokens are in open code groups and 52 spelled letters make no word.

Pass 17 (3 October 2026) pooled the open groups of R9873, R9898 and R9660 (Sessa’s letter of 1523): 84 groups occur in two or more letters, and a value was taken only where it reads in every occurrence, for example cas = tiene, var = agora, rac = dize, pam = paga, xoc = socorr-, sur = parte, yer = Florencia. All are tentative: none has a deciphered occurrence. With them, 2,295 of 2,474 tokens read as sense (92.8%); counting them as open, 87.8%. About 100 groups (145 tokens) and 34 spelled letters remain without sense; the non-words were re-checked at full zoom and stand as written, apart from one sign (f. 83r, pudiesse).

  • ff. 80v–81r. The disbanding of the army in Navarre was known in Rome; the Emperor is said to be holding cortes in Burgos, then in Aragon and Catalonia, and coming to Italy with the grant, which does not please this court. The Archbishop of Capua left Blois on the 7th with the truce articles. Sessa proposed two things: that the archbishop go from France to England first, para satisfazer a la autoridad del rey, and that the King of France pay the King of England his old pension for the length of the truce.
  • ff. 81v–82r. The 4,000 ducats the Lodi men took at the Stradella, sent by the Pope under another name; the Pope at first muy bravo, then cooled. He will write to the Cardinal of Cortona that his nephew Ippolito is to assist at the government of Florence, has made good the 4,000 ducats and sends 4,000 more; Su Sd. ha estado en grandissimo conflito, not willing to disdain the enemies; those around him keep him irresolute, si no Micer Agostino Foyeta (Foglietta).
  • ff. 82v–83v. Money found on exchange for the viceroy’s army; the nuncio’s letters of 26 March from England: the King has no heart for the war; Wolsey would hear of a truce if the Pope got the King of France to send someone debaxo de alguna onesta color; the English will not contribute in Italy por ninguna via. Advice on the Emperor’s standing with friends and enemies, his nephews’ marriages, Alberto Pio, Count of Carpi.
  • f. 86r, the postscript. The Pope and his ministers are grieved beyond measure at the disbanding; he will not declare himself en liga defensiva ni ofensiva, though there was never a time so fit para acabar de ronper las alas a franceses; what holds him back is fear.

The order and substance match Bergenroth’s no. 636 point for point (the cortes, Capua from Blois, the two proposals, the pension, the 4,000 ducats, the nuncio’s letters of 26 March, the English refusal, Madame Margaret). That agreement is the check on the reading.

05The letter of 24 February 1525 (R9898)

A long despatch in a secretary’s hand (A-34 ff. 150–156), dated in clear De Roma a xxiiij de hebrero 1525, the day of the battle of Pavia and so written before the news. It is not calendared in CSP Spain iii.1. An earlier pass here said it carried no decipherment despite DECODE’s status; that was wrong. Six cipher runs have a contemporary marginal decipherment in a second hand (ff. 150r, 152r, 153v, 154r, 154v three times, 156r), and they were the cribs of pass 16: v em cierto, li mi, le me, net halla, sud datario, yol aqui, kun duque, and the name Micer Agostino Foyeta.

2,361 of 3,059 cipher tokens read as sense (77.2%) after pass 16; 300 tokens are in open groups, and 398 spelled letters make no word, most of them on ff. 150v–151r and 155v, where bleed-through on the images hides the difference between the cursive r, u and n. With the pass-17 tentative values, 2,441 (79.8%), or 77.9% counting them as open; 237 tokens in about 140 groups and 381 letters on the bleed-through pages remain. Re-cut at 1.5 times, those pages do not resolve further, and no other image of A-34 could be found.

  • ff. 150r–152r. The Pope did not answer the Emperor’s letters of 15 November and 15 December; the Datary’s French partisanship que abiertamente se conoce; the French have tried the English ambassador; the Pope offered to secure his master’s pension during the truce. In the margin: aqui ninguna cosa nos comunican, sabiendo que las entendemos y conocemos; por conjeturas sabemos lo que ocurre.
  • ff. 152v–154r. Albany’s march on the Kingdom of Naples; lo de Inglaterra se va calentando; the Archbishop of Capua kept at Piacenza con color de la platica de la paz; in the margin, se de buena parte que el datario ha tentado de reduzir al duque a franceses.
  • f. 154v. Sessa sent to San Germano by the viceroy; in the margin, y cierto si mi obligacion no me forçara … y viendo la malissima providencia que ha avido, dueleme grandemente hallarme a lo que por razon y no por caso temo que suceda.
  • ff. 155v–156r. Pavia expects relief; attacking the French in their strong position would be a manifest disadvantage. Postscript: the Duke of Ferrara has lent the King of France another 50,000 ducats, and (margin) no le plaze mucho al papa, aunque no quiere confessar, es en su menosprecio y que franceses le ayan tomado en su protecion.

06How the key was rebuilt

No key for this cipher survives, and none is in print. What survives is Sessa’s correspondence, and in a number of his letters the court wrote the decipherment on a separate leaf marked A Claro, B Claro. Each of those gives cipher and clear side by side. The work went through eight of them.

  • Three letters of April and May 1524 gave the first 60 groups: f. 138 against f. 140 (17 April), ff. 168v–171 against ff. 172–173 (22 April), f. 322 against ff. 324–325 (19 May).
  • Every cipher line was cut into images of single tokens by a script that splits the line at gaps in the ink, and each token was transcribed and aligned from its image. That separated look-alike signs the eye had merged: two p-loops (plural -s and b), two three-shapes (r and i), two hooked signs (d and u).
  • Six more letters were fetched from DECODE: January and February 1525, 24 and 29 July 1524, and seven of 1523. The January 1525 letter gave dicha; 24 July gave mostrar, quiere and hay. The February 1525 letter was at first taken to carry no decipherment; on 2 October 2026 six marginal decipherments were found on it (section 05).
  • Template matching then searched every downloaded page, about 110, for the target’s groups still without a value, at five scales. The September 1523 letter gave -on, twice, in dilataron and concedieron.

Open:

  • vo, a noun after las dichas, presumably the bishop’s letters or their copies. No match in any page, and absent from R9873 and R9898.
  • per, before -ra los casos. No match in any page, and absent from R9873 and R9898.
  • ruc and hay, after de lo suso, were open until 2 October 2026; the two new letters give them five and four contexts, dicho and, tentatively, aunque (above). Goicoechea’s nineteenth-century decipherment of R9873, at the Academy and not imaged, would confirm hay.
  • In R9873, after pass 17, about 100 code groups (145 tokens) seen once, and 34 spelled letters that do not make a word; in R9898, about 140 code groups (237 tokens), absent from every deciphered sibling and from the six margins, and 381 letters on the bleed-through pages. The pass-17 values (cas tiene, var agora, rac dize, pam/Jir paga, xoc socorr-, leg mucho, sur/luc parte, len/lon buen-, 3ar aca, yer Florencia, gig quisie-, nuf gente and about fifteen from one context) are all tentative (grade I or M). The code is not alphabetical (cad sobre, cas tiene, cap tem-), so no bracketing applies.
  • The clause los m-re que r-ido los que no m-r-e-van: every sign has a value, but the sequence is not yet Spanish, so one value in it is wrong.

Copied 22 Oct 2026 verbatim from NOTES.md, section 3. The cipher transcription is `transcription_f128.txt`; the key `key_working.md`.

## 3. Reading of ff. 128–131 (2026-09-18, in part; eight passes, see key_working.md)

Clear opening (f. 128r): *Teniendo escripto y cerrado el pliego del duplicado de xiiij deste que sera con esta, vi
una letra del obispo Verulano que esta en Constancia, de que aqui embio copia. Luego embie a Su Santidad a
suplicalle me mandase avisar de alguna particularidad para dar noticia della do conviniese. Mandome a mi Felice su
secretario, el qual me ha dicho que las letras que tiene son del dicho Verulano de vj y vij del presente, do dize
que hera acabada la dieta, y que Lucerna no concluyo de dar ayuda a franceses; que los cantones de Berna y Friburk
y Solodor, y para mas facilitarla y traer los animos del pueblo, publican que los suyços en Lombardia estavan
cercados y en grand peligro, con que juntava hasta el numero de ocho mill, que davan nombre de partir muy presto,
pero que el no podia saber el quando, por estar en Costancia donde algunos de sus amigos de Suyça le escrivian para
que exortase a Sus. que buscase alguna buena forma de paz. Esto es lo que me ha referido.*

Cipher (eight lines on f. 128r, one on f. 128v; the duplicate ff. 130–131 is token-for-token the same). Method:
every line cut into tokens by `tokens.py` (sheets in `tok/`, one PNG per token in `tok/*_tiles/`), the same done
for the deciphered siblings f. 138, ff. 168v, 170–171 and 322, and values assigned by aligning those against
their clears (f. 140, f. 172, f. 324). Transcription: [transcription_f128.txt](transcription_f128.txt).

> ¶ las dichas [vo]s no le las mostraron, que le ponen sospecha que desea aver algo que r-e-f-i-e-r-a de lo suso
> [ruc] [hay] no / **me parece que concuerda mucho con lo primero y postrero que el dicho obispo dize al maestro de

Sense: Felice will not show Sessa the bishop's own letters, since they make him suspect that Sessa wants to get
something out of them for himself; Sessa nonetheless finds the report consistent with what the bishop writes to
the postmaster (Gabriel de Tassis: his Italian letter of 6 April, *tutti Elvetij son in arme … expedisca volando a
Milano et Roma*, is bound as f. 131); he went at once to the Pope to ask for a provision of money for the Swiss
business, persuading him that he has the colour (pretext) for it, having opened the peace-and-truce negotiation.
This is the news Bergenroth abstracts from the 22 April letter (CSP 643).

Pass 10 (the January 1525 letter R9897, same cipher, verbose clear on ff. 14v–15; fetched with the saved DECODE
cookie) attested `rad` = *dicha*, `boy`/`bez` = *-ido*, `Є` = *h*, `kel` = `kef` (*los*), and `lif 3` = *quiere*
(key_working.md, pass 10). Unread now: `vo` (the noun after *dichas*: the bishop's letters or their copies),
`z y` (the verb after *quiere*, *mostrar* from the sense), `per`, `rus`, `hay`, and the clause *los m-re que r-ido
los que no m-r-e-van*, where one sign value (probably `v`) is still wrong. The other 1525 letter, R9898 (14 pp.),
was then read through as well (pass 12): it seemed to carry no decipherment (wrong: see section 5, it has six marginal ones), so it gave only
contexts (`z y` also after *si*; `ruc`/`rus` is a person who *ha respondido*; `hud` again in *la plática de la paz*).
Pass 13 (the 24 July 1524 letter R9893, cipher f. 483 with its clear on f. 485) corrected `lif` to *mostrar*
(*ni se quiere mostrar* = `lep dim fa lif 3 α`, `fa` = *quiere*) and gave `zum` = *hay*, `tas` *cosa*, `luh`
*mundo*, `qit` *estado*, `lic` *monsieur*, `gel` *qual*. Open now: `vo`, `z y` (after *mostrar*; also after
*si* in 1525; probably a conjunction, *porque* or *diziendo*), `per`, `rus`/`ruc` (a person), and the clause
*los m-re que r-ido*.

Pass 14 matched templates of the unread tokens against every downloaded page (`tmatch.py`). The September 1523

## Content (cipher passages, in order)

- **f. 80v-81r.** The disbanding of the army in Navarre was already known in Rome; it is said there that the Emperor
  will hold cortes in Burgos and then in Aragon and Catalonia and, with the service granted, come to Italy, which
  does not please this court. The coming of the Archbishop of Capua (he left Blois on the 7th with the truce
  articles; the Pope had a copy given to Sessa) would settle *estas alteraciones*. Sessa proposed two things: that
  the archbishop go from France to England first, *para satisfazer a la autoridad del rey*, and that the King of
  France pay the King of England the pension he used to pay before the war, for the length of the truce. Starting
  from the Pope it would have been easier; it was not done, *no se si del poco aparejo que saben* there is for
  carrying the war on; they try to settle things with less difficult conditions. Couriers by sea and by land
  (Rodrigo de Mayorga); the coming, quality and nature of *el dicho duque*.
- **f. 81v.** The Emperor's estimation; the 4,000 ducats the Lodi men took at the Stradella, sent by the Pope *con
  nombre que yva al [kam] de su casa*.
- **f. 82r.** The Pope at first *muy bravo*, then cooled; his difficulty in contributing to the army; the Florentines
  have given nothing; he has told Sessa he will write this week to the Cardinal of Cortona that Ippolito, his nephew,
  is to assist at the government (of Florence); he has made good the said 4,000 ducats lost and is sending another
  4,000 this week; he is *en grandissimo conflicto*, temporising, not willing to disdain the enemies; nothing hoped
  for has come from England; those near him conform him in this irresolution and draw him away, *si no Micer
  Agostino Foyeta* (Foglietta); Sessa declares himself *mas aficionado* (to the Pope's service).
- **f. 82v.** Sessa has found a sum of ducats on exchange for the viceroy's need to keep the army (with the bishops
  of Salamanca and Ávila). England: the nuncio's letters of 26 March say the King takes no great heart to undertake
  the war and waits for the Emperor's resolution; the Cardinal (Wolsey) said lately that if the Pope got the King of
  France to send a person *debaxo de alguna onesta color*, the truce or peace would be heard, so as not to seem to
  incline to it himself, *que seria perder de reputacion*; the English will not contribute to the war in Italy *por
  ninguna via*; Madama (Margaret) writes the same.
- **f. 83r.** Advice: the Emperor's estimation, in peace or in war, must grow and be kept, more with friends than
  with enemies; the confederates; to treat with the Pope; marriages and the advancement of his nephews; the Emperor's
  coming to Italy, *sin hablarlo con Su Sd.*; *no fiandose V.Md. en la que hizo con la buena memoria del papa Leon*.
- **f. 83v.** If the Emperor came to Italy with great power ...; the pontificate; Alberto Pio, Count of Carpi *(que
  da al B-e-r-t-o del C-a-r-p-i-o)* and his credit with the Datary.
- **f. 86r (postscript).** The Pope and his ministers are grieved beyond measure at the disbanding; those who advised
  his irresolution think they have won; he will not declare himself in a defensive or offensive league, although they
  judge there was never a time so fit to break the wings of the French; if on that frontier and in Picardy something
  were done, the Pope would decide, though Sessa has always known him inclined to abase the French; what holds him
  back is fear; they wait for what the Archbishop of Capua brings.

The order and substance match CSP 636 point for point (cortes; Capua from Blois; the two proposals; the pension; ill
prepared for war; 4,000 ducats at Lodi; the nuncio's letters of 26 March; the English will not contribute; Madame
Margaret; friends and enemies). That agreement is the check on the reading.

## Key additions from this letter (pass 16)

`ruc`/`rus` = dicho (with `rad` = dicha), `hay` = aunque (tentative), `fed` = rey de Francia, `xir` = cardenal,
`lot` = nuncio, `pof` = exercito, `kuc` = liga, `tig` = contra, `cuq` = tengo, `yod`/`quf` = amigo/enemigo,
`gat` = esto (*estonces*), and the name *Micer Agostino Foyeta*. These close the f. 128 residue *de lo suso[rus]
[hay] no* = *de lo susodicho, aunque no*.

## Content (cipher passages, in order)

- **f. 150r.** Reply to the Emperor's letters of 15 Nov and 15 Dec: the Pope did not answer; he wants what was sent
  not seen there; it must not be let known to the Swiss what has been sent from the Emperor; from past works one can
  judge the government from here on (margin).
- **f. 150v-151r.** The Pope's arrangement with the King of France and the French, a copy of which he says he sent
  the Emperor; his accustomed neutrality; the Datary's French partisanship, *que abiertamente se conoce*; the
  French *han yntentado con el embaxador de Inglaterra* ...; the Pope told him he would assure him of the pension
  due during the truce if the Emperor or the King of France did not agree; *el dicho [embaxador] ha respondido muy
  bien*; the invasion of the Kingdom of Naples; *su fin principal ha consistido e consiste* (clear) in a run that is
  open (`hem`, `lin`); the dicha tregua o paz; the Datary and the French; *la experiencia le ha mostrado*; the
  Emperor's agents.
- **f. 151v-152r.** The French *han efetado y efetan*; the Emperor may be sure of the dissimulation used; they
  promise *los mismos partidos*; the viceroy; the ministers outside the Pope's circle; *aqui ninguna cosa nos
  comunican, sabiendo que las entendemos y conocemos; por conjeturas sabemos lo que ocurre* (margin).
- **f. 152v-153r.** The Pope's attitude to Albany's coming and the invasion of Naples; *lo de Inglaterra se va
  calentando*; the Datary and the money that did not reach the camp, *la grandissima necesidad que en el canpo se
  pudiese*; the Venetians and the Pope blame each other; the Archbishop of Capua at Piacenza, *con color de la
  platica de la paz*, to keep him away; *Micer Agostino Foyeta*; the Duke of Albany's reception (clear) and *su
  venida a la ynvasion del Reyno*; the King of France.
- **f. 153v-154r.** No provision made in the Kingdom (of Naples); the Duke of Albany's men are badly paid, horse
  short; *se de buena parte que el datario ha tentado de reduzir al duque a franceses* (margin) with money and
  promises from the King of France; the Datary approves; *Micer Agostino Foyeta*.
- **f. 154v.** Sessa ordered to San Germano by the viceroy *para que ... se pierda, que no para defensar lo; y
  cierto si mi obligacion no me forçara* ...; *y viendo la malissima providencia que ha avido, dueleme grandemente
  hallarme a lo que por razon y no por caso temo que suceda*; *que tenemos toda Italia ... y no partira* (margins).
- **f. 155v-156r.** Pavia expects relief; forcing the French in their strong position would be a manifest
  disadvantage; what results in Lombardy; *el datario*; the Emperor must either content himself with *la perdida*

  and (cipher, margin) *no le plaze mucho al papa, aunque no quiere confessar, es en su menosprecio y que franceses
