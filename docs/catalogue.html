The Unsolved Catalogue

What is still in cipher in the digitised diplomatic volumes, and nobody has read. The first entries came from the French volumes on Gallica; the rest (46) from the DECODE database, every ciphertext record it marks non-decrypted or partly decrypted, grouped into letters and series and checked against this repository's own targets and Tomokiyo's list. A sweep of 22 Sept 2026 added candidates for new solves and outside reads (tagged new 22 Sept): DECODE records never classified, letters Tomokiyo calls undeciphered while naming a key, Gallica notices and the printed calendars. On 14 Oct 2026 open items were pulled from Satoru’s Cryptoole index (DECODE, Tomokiyo’s unsolved list) and checked against the repository; the ones not already here or solved elsewhere were added with source Cryptoole, so a few now come from Tomokiyo’s list. Each entry is scored 1–5 for historical importance (what the text could add), solvability (odds of a full reading with the material online) and difficulty (the technical work), and the priority is a weighted blend you can reweight. Class A means siblings with decipherment in the same volume, B a partial key or known family in print, C no key and no sibling. Entries read, partly read, resolved or found already in print here are taken out of the catalogue and written up (write-ups; look up an old number); those attempted here without a reading stay, tagged attempted, open.

Scores are judgements from catalogue descriptions and the literature, not from the leaves: 18 of 76 entries have been viewed on the image, and each carries the check that would confirm it is open. Data: catalogue.json · text: CATALOGUE.md.

How the scores were set

Importance asks what a full reading would add: 5 for a first-hand report of a major event by a principal witness (du Bellay in London 1529, Lanssac at the Polish election), 2 for routine business. Solvability asks whether the material online is enough: 5 when deciphered siblings of the same hand and year sit in the same volume, 1 for one short letter in an unknown language. Difficulty is the technical work at the keyboard: 1 for aligning a known sibling, 5 for a statistics-only attack on a large homophonic nomenclator.
