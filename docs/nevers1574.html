F = E · 9 = Q · X Y Z IN CLEAR · LYON 1574

Lyon → the duke of Nevers · substitution cipher of graphic signs · September 1574

Henriette de Clèves and Renato Birago to the Duke of Nevers, Lyon, September 1574

→

BnF français 3315 ff. 15–16 and 21, two letters from Lyon in September 1574. One has a minute word-by-word decipherment between its lines and a clear ending; the other keeps Renato Birago’s cipher flap over a complete clear Italian fair copy. The text was taken from these contemporary decipherments: f. 21 from its clear copy, and f. 15 from its interlinear decipherment, checked word by word against the cipher decoded with an alphabet rebuilt here from the gloss (22 October 2026). Only four overwritten lines of f. 15v and a dozen single groups stay open. Tomokiyo had described the system but published no decipherment.

Summary. Both leaves carry their own decipherment. On f. 21 a folded cipher sheet covers a clean, signed copy of Birago’s whole letter, dated Lyon, 28 September 1574. It says Nevers’s visit is late, the bearer’s business has arrived at the worst possible moment, Piedmont is better discussed orally, and Their Majesties wish to provide Nevers with a government. On f. 15 an early hand wrote French plaintext between the cipher lines. On 22 October 2026 that gloss was transcribed line by line, the cipher signs were transcribed and decoded with an alphabet rebuilt from their alignment, and the two agree word for word on f. 15r and f. 16r. The cipher does not stop at the foot of f. 15v: the first 19 lines of f. 16r continue it, and only the last eight are a clear closing. The duchess reports that Pignerol and Savillan have been handed to the Duke of Savoy “au grant contentement du Roy et de la Royne mere”, her exchange with the Queen Mother, and a rumour at court of “une ligue aupres du Roy de quatre ou cinq”. The extent is partial because f. 15v ll. 2–5, overwritten by the decipherer and with their gloss faded, stay illegible (about 70 words); 94% of the cipher is deciphered.

02 Birago’s letter, 28 September

Birago had expected Nevers at Lyon throughout the month for the feast of St Michael. Since he has not come, Birago hopes to see him shortly, though not as soon as he would like. The bearer’s purpose has come at a bad time: affairs of the kingdom are such that virtually anything else would be considered before it. He therefore returns with the dispatch that will make the answer clear.

Et quanto alle cose dil Piemonte, ella intenderà similmente com’elle siano passate … rimettendomi a parlargliene poi più minutamente di bocca, quando Iddio mi farà la gratia di vederla.

Birago will not commit the Piedmont business to a fuller written discussion. He adds that Their Majesties are very willing to provide Nevers with a governorship and every other thing he deserves, and that he has done and will continue to do every good office of a true servant and friend. A semi-diplomatic transcription of the whole leaf is in targets/nevers1574/f21_transcription.md; only one short expression in the first sentence remains uncertain.

03 Henriette de Clèves, 16 September [1574]

The cipher fills two dense pages and the first 19 lines of f. 16r. Above almost every run a smaller contemporary hand has supplied its plaintext. The letter opens:

Monseigneur, je ne veulx faillir a vous advertir que ce jourd’huy a esté rendu a monsieur de Savoye Pignerol et Savillan, au grant contentement du Roy et de la Royne mere, qui ont faict ce beau chef d’euvre.

The duchess then went to the Queen Mother’s council. Catherine asked why Nevers should not pass through, since he did not lose his government for that; the duchess answered that, cut down as it was, she held it for lost. At court it had been told to the King that Nevers had written of “une ligue aupres du Roy de quatre ou cinq”. Two governments “qui ne pouvoient faillir, comme Picardie et Languedoc” are in prospect, and his friends say that if he keeps his government as it now stands “vous vous feriez beaucoup de tort”. The reading (f15_reading.md, English f15_en.md) has 4 holes in 402 tokens on f. 15r and f. 16r; on f. 15v lines 2–5 are overwritten and their gloss is faded.

The outer address is clear: A Monsieur, Monsieur le duc de Nevers, pair de France. The cipher runs on from the foot of f. 15v into the first 19 lines of f. 16r (“Je luy ay” continues there), and then gives way to a clear ending in the duchess’s hand. The writer says it is midnight, her eyes hurt from weeping, she wishes herself in paradise rather than suffer the way they are being made to suffer, and asks God to grant her “bon frère” patience. The closing reads “De Lyon ce 16 de septembre”; the year 1574 is inferred from the archival setting. A superficially similar appeal of Henri III to the duchess printed by Lavisse belongs to an earlier occasion, before his departure for Poland, and cannot serve as a crib for this letter.

04 A related key of 1584

Tomokiyo observed that ff. 15 and 21 do not use the Nevers key copied at ff. 12–13. They closely resemble Nevers collection no. 2, issued a decade later in 1584 and preserved in BnF fr. 3995 f. 16, but differ in decisive assignments: on the 1574 leaves F is e, 9 is q, and x, y and z are left in clear. The 1584 table therefore shows the design of the system (an alphabet of graphic signs, special signs for doubled consonants, and nulls), but it cannot be applied to this text as it stands.

The 1574 alphabet was rebuilt on 22 October 2026 by aligning the f. 15 signs with the French gloss above them (f15_key.md): a=1, c=C, d=a b-shape, e=F or E, g=0, i/j=7, l=3, m=+, n=2, o=8, q and s=9, r=T, t=4, u/v=6, with a doubling sign, name signs for the King, the Queen Mother and the Queen, and XX with digits for a number. Every value is attested in several words. It is close to the 1584 no. 2 alphabet but not the same.

05 What remains

  • F. 15v ll. 2–5 and the gloss-only line at its head (about 70 words). The decipherer wrote over the cipher signs and the gloss above is faded or struck; the original or a multispectral image might recover them.
  • About a dozen single groups on f. 15v (ll. 7, 10, 12–16, 20–23) and f. 16r (ll. 1, 14), which neither the signs nor the faded gloss settle; the group XX before “une ligue” is not spelled.
  • One phrase on f. 21 (grade I). The fair copy appears to write per saluato suo; it may contain a personal name or abbreviation. The rest of the clear copy is transcribed at grade H.

Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 Sept [1574], ff. 15-16 decoded, not read

The duchess of Nevers writes to her husband; a contemporary reader wrote a word-by-word French decipherment between the cipher lines. Only the opening, the address and the clear ending on f. 16r are edited: it is midnight, her eyes hurt from weeping, and seeing how they are treated she would rather be in paradise. No transcription of the cipher signs or continuous transcription of the interlinear gloss exists (the gloss is too crowded to transcribe defensibly).

A mechanical decode with holes in most sentences: shown so that the transcription and the key can be checked and improved, not as a reading.

Decryption

f15_reading.md

# Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 September [1574]

BnF français 3315, f. 15r-v and the clear ending on f. 16r. The two pages of f. 15 are enciphered, but an early reader wrote a word-by-word French decipherment between the cipher lines. The gloss is cramped, crossed by the larger cipher signs, and locally faded. The adjacent clear ending supplies the day and month; the year is inferred from the archival context and the neighbouring Birago letter.

Securely readable anchors include:

> Monseigneur, je ne veulx faillir à vous donner …

The address on the folded outer leaf is:

> A Monsieur, Monsieur le duc de Nevers, pair de France.

The cipher continues to the foot of f. 15v and is followed immediately by a clear French closing on f. 16r. A preliminary reading of that ending is:

> Je ne vous escris davantage car il est minuit et les yeulx me font si grand mal de pleurer que je ne vous sçaurois ... Je n'ay autre chose à vous dire pour ... seulement sinon que voyant de la façon que l'on nous met je voudrois estre en paradis, et surtout Dieu, mon bon frère, qu'il vous donne patience. De Lyon ce 16 de septembre.

The transcription above is provisional and preserves ellipses where the pen is hard to distinguish. A passage quoted by Ernest Lavisse from Henri III's earlier appeal to the duchess, before his departure for Poland, is **not this 1574 letter**. Its similar emotional language is not evidence for deciphering f. 15 and is excluded from the reading.

## Limit of the present reading

The opening, address, and clear ending are recoverable, but a continuous line-for-line transcription of both cipher pages would require a specialist palaeographic pass on the minute interlinear hand or reconstruction of the exact cipher alphabet. The similar 1584 alphabet in BnF fr. 3995 f. 16 is not an exact key: on these leaves `F` is *e* and `9` is *q*, among other differences. Applying it mechanically creates false text.

Renato Birago to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 28 Sept 1574, f. 21

Birago had expected Nevers in Lyon for St Michael's day; the bearer's business comes at the worst moment, as the affairs of the kingdom make anything else more urgent; Birago will speak of Piedmont by mouth; Their Majesties are well disposed to give Nevers a government and whatever else he deserves. The decryption is the clear fair copy on the same recto; the cipher flap itself was not transcribed sign by sign.

# Renato Birago to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 28 September 1574

BnF français 3315, f. 21r. Semi-diplomatic transcription of the clear fair copy revealed when the cipher flap is folded away. Abbreviations are expanded silently except where uncertainty matters; punctuation and word division are lightly editorial.

> Illustrissimo et eccellentissimo signore,
>
> Scrissi questi giorni a Vostra Eccellenza quanto occorreva per [Salvato?] suo, et mi credevo ch'ella si trovarebbe qui per tutto questo mese a far la festa della San Michele; ma poi che non è venuta sin'hora, spero che verrà in breve, che non sarà però sì presto com'io desidero. Nel resto l'occasion per la quale il portator presente è venuto qui è stata di mal a proposito, et fuor di tempo, che non saprebbe esser d'avantaggio, perché le cose di questo Regno si trovano hoggi di modo tal essere, che si pensarebbe più presto a ogni altra cosa del mondo ch'à quella. E però se ne ritorna con l'espeditione tale che Vostra Eccellenza intenderà, sopra la quale non m'occorre dirgli altro.
>
> Et quanto alle cose dil Piemonte, ella intenderà similmente com'elle siano passate, sopra dil che non mi par a proposito d'intrarne per hora in altro discorso con l'Eccellenza Vostra, rimettendomi a parlargliene poi più minutamente di bocca, quando Iddio mi farà la gratia di vederla. Solamente dirogli che lor Maestà sono di buonissima volontà di proveder l'Eccellenza Vostra di governo, et d'ogni altra cosa, come ella merita. Nel che (ancora ch'io conosca non esser necessario) non ho mancato, né mancarò di far per lei tutto quel buon officio che deve far un vero servitor et amico suo.
>
> E co' questo, doppo essermi humilmente racomandato nella buona gratia di Vostra Eccellenza, prego Nostro Signor Dio che gli conceda longa et felice vita. Da Lione alli xxviii di settembre 1574.
>
> Di Vostra Eccellenza affectionatissimo servitore,
>
> Renato Birago

The uncertain name or phrase in the first sentence is written `saluato suo`; it may be a person's name rather than the ordinary participle. Everything else is continuously readable.
