The Milanese embassy at Buda, 1489–1490

Archivio di Stato di Milano, Sforzesco 650 (1489 no. 8) and 642/1, 4 · two letters of Maffeo da Treviglio to Ludovico Sforza from the court of Matthias Corvinus · one of four pages, all cipher, with no published decipherment

Summary. Maffeo da Treviglio (Maffeo de Bullis, 1430–1498) was Ludovico Sforza's man at Buda from the summer of 1489. His ciphered despatches are in the Milan state archive, some with a contemporary decipherment beside them and some without. Judit W. Somogyi reconstructed his cipher in 2016 from five cipher-and-clear pairs, but her article gives the key only in a numeric transcription: every sign appears as a two-digit number, and no image of any sign is printed, so the key could not be applied as printed. The numbers run in order of first appearance in the letter of 2 April 1490, which survives together with its contemporary clear copy in the Vestigia database. Matching the two restores the shapes behind the numbers, and about twenty further signs came from the decipherment itself. With that key the two despatches that have no decipherment were deciphered. Buda, 12 January 1490: the king has left for Vienna and the ambassadors will follow on the 15th. Buda, 22 November 1489: four pages on the peace with the Emperor, the Diet, the marriage of John Corvinus, the rival Neapolitan match, the queen's anger, and a warning that intercepted cipher letters have been deciphered. The 1489 letter is the oldest cipher text this project has deciphered; the previous oldest was of 1497.

02 Turning Somogyi's numbers back into signs

Somogyi's appendix prints the key as a table of numbers: a = 11, 29, 39; m = 12; b = 13; and so on through five nulls, three word signs (et, in, che), five doubled-letter signs, thirty-two syllable signs and six nomenclator entries (“il vescovo varadino”, “S. V.”, “ser.mo Re de Hungaria”, “madonna Bianca”, “zo. Corvino”, “la sua Maestà”). She also prints the first two lines of the 1489 letter twice, once as numbers and once deciphered. She prints no image of a sign, and her numbers are her own labels for the fifteenth-century signs.

The order of her numbers gives the link. Her numbers 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 stand for a, m, b, sa, to, re, which is “ambasatore”, the first word of the letter of 2 April 1490. 17, 18, 19 are d, e, l (“del”), 20 to 23 t, u, r, co (“turco”). The numbering simply follows that letter word by word, so the ciphered original supplies the shapes in the same order. From there the clear copy carries the alignment to the end of the letter and fixes about forty signs.

The rest came from the two letters without a decipherment, each fixed by a word that could be nothing else: the long 7-shaped stroke is f (“Fedrico”, “fosse”, “satisfare”); an n with a crossed 4 is ss (“essere”, “potesse”); x with a tick is do (“quando”, “havendo”); a with a colon is gli (“meglio”); a looped b joined to o is st (“stato”, “questo”); d with a colon is fi (“figliolo”, “iustificare”); an n with a long crossbar is tt (“littere”); and a capital with a cross stands for “Ex.tia Vostra”. Numerals are written as figures, not enciphered: “ali 8 del presente”, “ali 15”, “in 20 o 25 di”. Latin words sometimes stand in the clear inside the cipher (“etiam apud”, “Sed hoc non fallet divinam sapientiam”).

03 Buda, 12 January 1490: the king rides to Vienna

The letter's clear first page is about church appointments; the cipher begins where the news turns delicate.

Questo Ser.mo Re partite de qua ali 8 del presente per andare ad Vienna, non ta[nto] per che la speranza de la dieta lo tiri, quanto per proprio desiderio de andarli; et che sia el vero, questi prelati mi hano dicto che in questo camino divertira ad [Tu…] … che sono molti et gr[an]di, et non si conduc[ono] ad Vienna a piu … che in 20 o 25 di. Lo legato et li altri oratori hano, et io con loro havemo, deliberato partire ali 15 de questo … rimasti dreto, perche … and[aremo per la] recta via ad Vienna.

“This Most Serene King left here on the 8th of this month to go to Vienna, not so much because the hope of the Diet draws him as from his own wish to go; and in proof of it, these prelates have told me that on this journey he will turn aside to [Tulln?] … which are many and great, and they will not reach Vienna in under 20 or 25 days. The legate and the other ambassadors have decided, and I with them, to leave on the 15th of this month … having stayed behind, because … we shall go by the direct road to Vienna.”

The letter confirms the decipherment: its own second page says in clear words that “lo legato et li altri oratori et io siamo insieme deliberato partire alli 15 de questo … andare per la via ad Vienna”. The cipher says the same thing in the same order, and the word “Vienna” is spelled with the same signs in lines 2, 6 and 10.

04 Buda, 22 November 1489: peace, the Diet, and two marriages

Four pages. Somogyi printed the first two lines as a specimen; no decipherment of the rest was found in print. Matthias Corvinus had six months to live, his war with Frederick III was in suspense, and two marriages were in play for his illegitimate son John Corvinus: Bianca Maria Sforza, Ludovico's niece, and a daughter of don Federico of Aragon, Naples's candidate. Queen Beatrice of Aragon opposed the succession of her stepson.

The decipherment below is close but not word-perfect: bracketed words are uncertain, ellipses mark groups not yet deciphered. Pages 1 and 2 are deciphered nearly throughout, page 3 in its first half and in patches thereafter.

05 The clear postscript: an ambassador's expenses

Nothing on this page is secret, and it was never enciphered. It is also the only part of the letter that is about Maffeo himself.

Credo che la Ex.tia V.ra havera facto dar quelli ducento ad m. Fran.co; che, se cosi e, la ringratio infinite volte de la clementia et bonta sua infinita. Et perche quelli denari non mi portavano fin al fine de decembre, et partendosi lui non saperia da chi imprum[ut]are uno quatrino se non dal Thexaurero Regio, che seria con graveza de la persona vostra propria … Se de questa spesa non gli ne faria honor, sonno contento che non me ne proveda; io gli posso affirmar etiam cum iuramento che, doppoi che sonno qua, ultra quello ho havuto et tolto in nome del Ill.mo S. V.ro, ho speso di mei piu de centocinquanta ducati tra mi, veste et cavalli … che la provisione che me e ordinata non mi basta, dovendo tener questo loco secundo la dignita sua. Et perche in questa novita facta de Cancelleri forse poteria esser dome[n]ticato, La supplico ordini ad chi li par che provedi sia compreso in el numero de li altri Can[cellieri] et in lassignatione facta ad la Cancellaria. A V.ra Ex. de continuo me recomando. Bude xxij Novembris 1489. E[iusdem] Ill.me D[ominationis] V[ostre] servus, Magister Trivilien[sis].

“Since I have been here, beyond what I have had in Your Lordship's name, I have spent more than a hundred and fifty ducats of my own on myself, clothes and horses … the allowance assigned me is not enough to keep this post according to its dignity. And because in this recent reshuffle of the chancery clerks I might be forgotten, I beg you to order whoever you think fit to see that I am counted among the other chancellors.” The phrase “et in lassignatione facta ad la Cancellaria” is the explicit by which the archive catalogues the letter.

06 Method

The images are the Vestigia plates (3240×4320, no login needed: the record page at vestigia.hu/documents/<id> carries the whole record as JSON, and the originals are served from /media/<file id>/<file name>) and, for R1098, the DECODE scans of the same negatives.

The working loop was: connected-component segmentation of each page into glyph boxes, line assignment by the peaks of the horizontal ink projection, merging of the two-stroke signs and of superscripts with the letter below them; then numbered strips, one line at a time, transcribed into values by hand against a sheet of sign exemplars cut from the letters themselves; then the values segmented into Italian, and any place where the Italian failed sent back to the image. The 2 April pair was aligned by dynamic programming against its clear copy to seed the key. Several rounds were needed: the first pass on the 1489 letter produced letter strings, the second, working from contrast-stretched crops of whole lines rather than from boxes, produced sentences.

Two practical notes for anyone continuing. The automatic line segmentation misassigns glyphs in the lower half of pages 2 and 3 of the 1489 letter, where the lines converge; transcribe those from the plate with per-row crops. And the stacked line crops drift from line 18 onward on page 3, which cost one pass its accuracy.

07 What remains uncertain

  • A recurring pair (ro + et by the table) occurs three times on page 3, twice directly after “ad”, in a slot that wants a place name. It is probably a nomenclator entry Somogyi's key does not list. I
  • Four signs are still unvalued: a “♀”-like sign with a cross below, a capital E with three bars, a wavy horizontal null, and a stroke that may be a second null. I
  • Page 3 after line 19 is deciphered only in patches: the ink is lighter there and the plate's resolution runs out. About a third of that page's words are not deciphered. M
  • Page 1 lines 21–26 have stain damage at the right edge; the sense carries, the last words of each line do not. M
  • The letter of 12 January 1490 is deciphered in gist throughout, but lines 5, 6 and 9 have short groups that do not yet resolve, including the word after “divertira ad”, most likely Tulln. C
  • The nomenclator signs for “madonna Bianca”, “zo. Corvino” and “il vescovo varadino” are in Somogyi's list but were met only once here, as three capitals in a row on page 2 of the 1489 letter. Their shapes are not yet confirmed. C

# DECODE R1098 — Maffeo da Treviglio to Ludovico Sforza, Buda 12 January 1490 (ASMi Sforzesco 642/1, 4)

Two pages. Page 1: clear text (the bishop Stephano Crispo, the sees of Bosnia, Sirmio and Vesprimio, Cardinal
Ascanio's recommendation to Rome) ending "…questo faro bon principio de indrizare tutte le cose de qua in mane
sue", then ten lines of cipher (≈ 470 signs). Page 2: seven clear lines ("Lo legato et li altri oratori et io siamo
insieme deliberato partire alli 15 de questo et siamo rimasti d'acordo … andare per la via ad Vienna …").
Read 18 September 2026 with Somogyi's key pinned to glyphs (`key.md`); transcription `trans/r1098p1_v.csv`.
Bracketed = uncertain; […] = unread.

**Cipher, page 1, lines 1–10** (second pass by eye on contrast-stretched raw crops `img/crops/rawR1098_L*.png`,
18 Sep 2026, evening):

1. Questo Ser.mo Re
2. partite de qua ali 8 del presente per andare ad Vienna, non ta[nto]
3. per che la speranza de la dieta lo tiri, quanto per proprio desiderio
4. de andarli; et che sia el vero, questi prelati mi hano dicto
5. che in questo camino divertira ad Tu[ln?]a […] che io ve ha s[crip]to […]
6. e di […]care, che sono molti, et non si conduc[ono] ad Vienna a piu […]
7. […] che in 20 o 25 di. Lo legato et li altri oratori hano,
8. et io con loro havemo, deliberato partire ali 15 de questo, et co[si …]
9. rimasti dreto, perche […] dire che and[aremo per la] recta via
10. ad Vienna […] secondo […] non sono piu […]

**English sense:** This Most Serene King left here on the 8th of this month to go to Vienna, not so much because
the hope of the Diet draws him as from his own wish to go there; and in proof, these prelates have told me that
on this journey he will turn aside to Tu[lln] … which are many, and they will not get to Vienna for … 20 or 25
days. The legate and the other ambassadors, and I with them, have decided to leave on the 15th of this month,
having stayed behind because … by the direct road to Vienna …

**How the reading is checked:** the clear second page of the same letter says, independently, "Lo legato et li
altri oratori et io siamo insieme deliberato partire alli 15 de questo … andare per la via ad Vienna"; the cipher
repeats it (ll. 7–10). "Vienna" is spelled with the same signs in ll. 2, 6 and 10. Numerals are written as
figures (8, 15, 20, 25).

**Signs still open:** the "7"-shaped stroke (ll. 2, 6, 9), the barred 6 + p group (ll. 5, 6; probably a
nomenclator word), q with superscript o, the dotted x (l. 10), "n+" = nn (Vienna).
