## Translation

I will tell you that I have been pressed, and the sole goodwill of a man you know has set things in motion, by means of
his reputation: his name is Fumée, vice-admiral of Guyenne, a close confidant of the King of Navarre, although he is
a Catholic and loyal to me. He has made the return journey here for some negotiation, for I know how [things went] on
this journey to Nérac and to the court, and he is well informed. He has several times proposed this reconciliation to
me, which he says is as easy and simple for him as I make it hard and awkward for him; and he has gone so far these
ten days that he has all but begged me, on behalf of the King of Navarre, to [?propose] it in good part, and that if
you would agree to it, he promised himself to bring this business to a good end with me; and by this one means an
opening would be made for the war [?if] in England, with a promise and assurance that the Queen of England could not
obtain [help] from the Huguenots at court; and that he, Fumée, if you would entrust him with this charge under cover
of another journey, would supply you with as many ships as would be needed for the embarkation.

I have not [?early] [?pressed on] with these matters, finding them as thin as they are of little foundation, and it
seems [to me] like when the Queen Mother sent the late Admiral to Florida; but it is for you […] to judge whether,
relying on one side, the same is done from elsewhere, [?to your servants].

I have sent to Randan the letters for the Queen of Navarre, and I believe she will have them soon. [I] received
yesterday a letter from Villeroy, who wrote to me that, to satisfy Monsieur de Mayenne at his urging, prayers and
requests, [the King] has made his [?edict] at the first [?return], as the reiters and Swiss and the rest are also
withdrawing. For I [?myself] believe that, for want of everything, and even of men, he could not do […]; nevertheless
this occasion will be used to conclude the peace, and if some advantage is given to the Huguenots, they will rely
[on the] breaking up of this army, which will come and pass over our bellies and finish ruining this poor country.
[He] is well, [?I am your servant]. I send [you] [?letters] from M. de la Forest.

28 October 1586.
