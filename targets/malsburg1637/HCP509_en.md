## Translation

My lord, although the retreat of Gallas from France, as also the agreement brought by the Most Christian King to the marriage of Monsieur and the return of Madame his wife, which it is believed will follow soon, and the passage newly opened for letters and correspondence through the [?Brabant], give some people some suspicion that there might be good understanding between the Crowns of France and Spain, yet I am now of a contrary opinion, and hope that all will go better than was proposed not long ago, being greatly confirmed in my opinion by the letter *of our ambassador extraordinary who is in London, of the […] [?seen] […] the copy*. He does me the honour of keeping close correspondence with me; I have also addressed all my letters *to Monsieur Sengel in London*, always leaving my [letters] that I [?send] *to the said ambassador* under flying seal, so that *the said Sengel* may be fully informed of what passes here. He is very content that I have [?…] addressed it *to the said [?ambassador]*, and honours his person as his worthy qualities also deserve. One thing displeases me, however, namely that [?Anger] is so long delayed in France. For since it is certain that he brings more satisfaction from England to the Most Christian King than the King himself had asked, I find it strange that he is kept dragging on so long in Paris. Perhaps it is for some good end. My letters of 27 March from Paris say that six armies of the King will march in a few days, four of them of ten thousand men each and the other two of twelve thousand men each, so that the war there is beginning in earnest. I also hope that Your Highness will be much relieved by the march of the said armies, and I also hope that a good diversion will be made on this side. And having nothing else for the present, I shall leave the news we expect from day to day of the treaty concluded between the two crowns to the next messenger, signing myself always, High and Mighty Lord, my lord, Your Highness's most humble, most obedient and most faithful servant, 232.
