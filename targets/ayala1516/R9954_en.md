# Diego López de Ayala (Brussels) to Cardinal Cisneros, 30 August 1516 (AGS Estado leg. 496 fol. 22, DECODE R9954): English translation of the ciphered part

AGS 0002

As to the offices that are vacant in these kingdoms, and the drawbacks of their being filled from here with persons
who sell them to base men, His Highness says that this will be looked to closely from now on. People say that Your
Lordship fills all of them, and His Highness hardly any; so let Your Lordship do there as well what you advise here;
certainly they would see that none was filled there, and so His Highness writes.

The offices that His Highness has given and promised, as far as I know, are these: to the Marquis of Villena a
regidorship of Toledo that was Baeza's; to Rodrigo Niño another of Plasencia; and this one ⟨is known? — ciphertext
"rabese"⟩ here that Your Lordship gave to a nephew of Carvajal; that His Highness, they say, holds the regidorship of
Cuenca for Vasco de Acuña, brother of Pedro de Acuña of Toledo, who is colonel of certain troops of the ⟨a nomenclator
word, value unknown⟩. Your Lordship may believe that whatever is vacant has been asked for and either promised for
there or the appointment made.

And on this, too, Your Lordship should consider that the King has many here who have served him, and that he should do
them favour, and that Your Lordship should obtain offices for them when they are capable of them; for here it is said
that you give most offices to your own servants; remember those of the King.

The Genoese refer to Your Lordship the matter of Cartagena and the galleys; the provision carries some favour; let Your
Lordship provide there as you see fit for the King's service; do not be moved by any letter or warrant that you

may see there, for here many things are granted out of importunity and not from any wish that they be carried out.

His Highness is very glad of the pacification of these kingdoms and of the peace there is in them, and to know that
these lords, the Constable [and] the Infantado, ⟨unread word⟩ are in agreement with Your Lordship for his service. I say
again that it should be greatly valued.

I also informed His Highness that Queen Germaine is having dealings with the French, etc. Your Lordship is much to blame
for what is done with her, because of what was asked at the beginning. They are informed; likewise of how she is guided
by Alba in this business of Arévalo, she has won; for it is written from here that she is negotiating a marriage with
the Duke of Alba. These people are still for giving her those places, and I always advise them to put it off; and
everything I said about her, I said with the protest that it should be secret.

I also told His Highness who the ambassador was, and how ⟨pledged? deceived?⟩ to write a thousand lies; they are quite
sure that it is so, or else no great credit is given to his reports; and I do not know why Your Lordship chose to give
that man so much authority, having been warned how badly everyone stood with him in the matter of the orders of
Calatrava, Santiago and Alcántara. That is why the power was sent to Your Lordship to remedy it, and nothing more is
needed for it

AGS 0004

... the forces of them, which Your Lordship has, to remove and appoint whom you see fit. Don García de Padilla, as Your
Lordship will already have learned, cannot go, for he is so busy now. I send the provision or warrant for the matter of
the orders as Your Lordship asks for it. The Comendador Mayor of Calatrava sent a courier about Your Lordship's power;
here they have laughed a great deal at his coarse conscience, and the one of the nu[n]cio would not properly involve
himself in it, although the courier came to him, only through another letter to Your Lordship. In this step there has
been good, and that is why I write it, and I would wish it to continue.

Málaga has put us in great confusion with a courier they sent with five thousand and some signatures, saying how
much[?] everything is lost; and from the letters Your Lordship had written we held it as settled. There have been many
opinions on it: whether to send someone to deal with it, or to revoke the first provision. I have always insisted that
they should obey first and their disobedience be punished, because if it is not punished it will be to give an example
of disobedience to every town in the kingdom; it is being put back to Your Lordship. As you will see, let Your Lordship
deal with them as His Highness wishes.

Those of Arévalo protest ... the Queen has won in this tenure[?] ... that from there they have written here that she is
negotiating a marriage with the Duke of Alba[?], very wickedly. Those of that league write from there; now they have
written that Your Lordship speaks ill of Chièvres and is arming against him, and that in Your Lordship's house there it
is said that the King is incapable, and that I have written a thousand evils of the kingdom and of them; by God, let
Your Lordship take ...

AGS 0005

Peace has been concluded between the King our lord and the King of France, as Your Lordship will see from its articles,
which I send you herewith. His Highness orders Your Lordship to have it proclaimed at once. I have never been able to
get them to let this courier go sooner. What kind of peace this is, there is no need for me to say, since they show it.

Of the journey I inform Your Lordship that the King held a full council on Saturday the thirtieth of August, and in it it
was agreed to leave without fail, for on the fifteenth of September he will be at Antwerp; he will take leave of the
estates and hold the chapter of the Golden Fleece, and before that he will leave for Zeeland, where without fail
everything will be ready, and he will wait for the weather, and with it, God granting, he will embark; and for this he
has no hindrance but the weather.

What I suspect is that, once His Highness has arrived there and done this ceremony, some hindrance will not be lacking,
although, as I say, everyone desires the journey, and the King much ...

Secretary Barroso arrived here six days ago, and I[?] spoke to the King before he did, and gave him my credence, and
urged him not to give up his departure for anything, with the protests that Your Lordship writes; and without doubt it
has done much good for everyone to answer His Highness, who will soon [write] by another post; and from now on let Your
Lordship not allow any to be despatched without Your Lordship's knowing of it.

AGS 0006

I think this letter will carry the matter of the orders, and it is not possible; I am asking for a warrant so that Your
Lordship may appoint presidents as you think fitting, and they answer me on this that since the King is going, it will
be done there; and if the journey were called off, they will give it. It seems to me that Your Lordship should write
about this[?] and [?] name the persons who seem good to you for it; and here I have named Don Alonso Téllez and Don
Pedro his nephew; they are satisfied with the two.

In the matter of the corregidor of Toledo, it seems to me that Your Lordship should at once appoint a corregidor there,
which will please the King, and once it is done it will put an end to importunities; and I say: pay no heed to any
letter they write to you about him, because they order me to write this, so that if I make no mention of it in my own
letter, Your Lordship should overlook it.

Monsieur de ⟨6·⟩ [?] I [gave him] Your Lordship's credence, which I gave him alone, and [?] what means of keeping what I
told him; and after my going he told me that since he and the Chancellor are one person, ... we three should communicate
in everything, to make a good friendship between Your Lordship and him, which he desires; and as to providing for the
rest I have no answer yet; I expect a good one, and with this friendship much service will be done to God and to the
King.
