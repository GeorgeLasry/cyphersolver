# R1875, Sebastiano Foscarini to the Doge and Senate, Escorial, 13 October 1761, no. 178 "sola": English translation

Most Serene Prince.

No. 178. Sole copy.

As I had the honour of reporting to Your Excellencies, on Wednesday His Majesty and the whole Royal family moved to
this residence. The short distance to where I too have come, prompted by my most respectful desire to show Your
Serenity in every best way the true commitment of my good will, and moved by the troubled configuration of the
present times, which demand the greatest vigilance, as they would need an enlightened and active mind to ease the
way to knowledge of those dealings which, in every respect, may concern the wisdom of the Most Excellent Senate.
The broken-off negotiations between the Courts of London and Versailles are the source from which spring endless
conjectures and political systems. The most sensible persons, and those who best know the maxims of Princes and the
true interest of this Crown, are firmly persuaded that it can no longer look on with indifference at the entire
defeat of the Most Christian King in America, at the immense acquisitions that they had made, and that may be added
ever more to England in her powerful forces at sea. These arguments find many followers, but they are not able to
outweigh those who, carefully examining the military and maritime state and the strength of the treasury of this
Monarch, the peaceable dispositions of his mind and the bent of his Ministers, think it unlikely that he could depart
from the maxim professed until now. Then these last two objections are overturned by the resolution that Princes and
their Ministers cannot always serve their own pleasure, but that necessity very often lays down the law and
overthrows all the most contrary designs; and going on from this to show the urgency and abundance of the evils,
they come even to reflect on the constitution of some other Court, and think easy the case of a union fit to
sustain their mutual interests.

The authors of these thoughts are respectable on every account, but I cannot put them before the Most Excellent
Senate except stripped of any authority, though they seem to me worthy of all the attention that I promise Your
Excellencies in this grave matter too. Thanks.

From the Escorial, 13 October 1761.

Sebastian Foscarini, Ambassador.

Address: To the Most Serene Prince of Venice etc. etc. No. 178, sole copy. Docket: 1761, 13 October, received 2
November, Escorial, Ambassador, no. 178, sole copy.
