# Letter 1 (BnF fr. 2988 ff. 2r-v): Hieronimo Ranzo, Madrid, 26 March 1528 (postscript 31 March) — English

Translation of `letter1_reading.md` (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026), with the same holes: `[?word]` = a tentative
value (Lasry's seen once or twice, or Rosson's alone), `[g171]` = a code with no value. "His Majesty" (*soa Maestà*) is
Charles V; "cte" is the liquid used for the secret lines, left as written.

## Translation

To the other letters [?today] I have replied, to the three mentioned in them. I have also received another by way of
[?Lyon], [?dated] [L78] [?twelve] February, with some lines written with the cte on the matter of [?Milan]; but most of
them could not be read, because you did not take care to write them with the pen so full of the cte, and that it should
leave the rest, though incorporated in the [?paper]. So from now on you will take better care, and you will make the
lines of the writing in ink wider apart from one another, so that those written with the cte can then be read better.

And as for the said matter of [?Milan], I can hardly believe it, and I [?wonder] that in the three most recent he has
said nothing more of it; you will try in good faith to learn more of it and send word, because it is a thing
[?uncertain] in fact.

Of the office I affirm what I have written in the others, and not so that the work done or to be done by Stefano de
[g171] may [p196] and His Caesarean Majesty and [L39] in [?vain]. I hope that the offices assigned in Naples will not
fail me, provided that the affairs of that kingdom are [?settled] for His Majesty, as I hope, considering what you and
Iacomo have written to me about it. And I do not yet despair that, if the death of [?Sor] Pere[?a] in Rome, assigned by
[n83], [?shall] have been [?true], His Majesty may perhaps make use of me in that place, and will at once send me there
to stay in his [?affairs] with good [?treatment] and also [?enjoying] the profit of the said offices in their absence
from them; for these lords have spoken of it, and the Grand Chancellor wishes it and is working for it. Which, with
having the said offices as well, would be a thing that could [?yield] more than I could have at present, especially for
the advancement of our [?Agostino]. And on all of it I hope to have a decision before [?Easter], which I shall notify
you at once.

And because I think perhaps of making use of a [?grudge] so as to get across more surely in any event — that
is, to make a show of leaving here discontented to go home — I would be very glad if you had ready the [?requested]
safe-conduct, of which I await news with longing. But take care above all, as you hold my life dear, not to
communicate this to anyone in the world other than your mother, for you see already how much the secret [p141] more
[r194]. May God [?choose] the best of all and [?guard] me and [?bring] me to [?safety].

I affirm that I have given our Cataneo another large packet of letters, which you will receive [?safely]; and keep on
writing continually all the news under the assigned names, until you know of my actual [?departure] from this side,
because it will serve several purposes. And the letters that I shall write to you by land and address to Adano
[?Centurione] will be short, as he has advised.

I have nothing else to say at present, referring myself to what I have written in the others. From Madrid, the
twenty-sixth of March, one thousand five hundred and twenty-eight.

It is the thirty-first. Since then I have received another of yours in cipher, dated the fourth of this month, in
answer to which it is enough to tell you that I have seen what you have gone through with messer [?Simon] about the
matter of the coming, which I was pleased to hear; and on it I shall make every effort to have obtained here what is
needed for that purpose, as I shall write more fully later, not having time now to go on further. Again, farewell.
Your father greets you.
