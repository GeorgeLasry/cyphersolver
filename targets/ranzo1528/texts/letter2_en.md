# Letter 2 (BnF fr. 2988 ff. 9r-10v; parallel copy fr. 20506 ff. 136-138): Hieronimo Ranzo, Madrid, March 1528 — English

Translation of `letter2_reading.md` (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026), with the same holes: `[?word]` = a tentative value
(Lasry's seen once or twice, or Rosson's alone), `[h51]` = a code with no value. "His Majesty" (*soa Maestà*) and "the
Emperor" are Charles V; "His Lordship" (*soa signoria*) is the Grand Chancellor Gattinara. `cte` (the liquid for
secret lines) and `tlgar` are left as written.

## Translation

As I have written to you many times already, you are very sparing in your reports of events, so that if I had not been
more fully informed by Iacomo through a letter of his of [?ten] February, received with the three of mine of the
eighth, I could have given His Majesty little [?information] about them, and above all about the things that happened
in [?Savoy], of which no other report was [?enclosed], nor did other letters arrive with them. Wherever you have the
chance to learn everything day by day, make a note of it at that very moment, so that when you then write to me you can
give particular and [?distinct] news of everything, knowing how much such [?accounts] can help me and you; so do it in
future with great care.

Whenever you have anything new to write to me that is to be shown to His Majesty, write it in separate letters in a
[?chancery] hand and in good order with our large cipher sent herewith, in the way I do, signing them in the name of
[h51] or of messer Carlo Gazino, and addressing them here to Hieronimo Ranzo with a counterfeited letter, so that it is
not taken for yours; make no mention of such letters in the other three letters, so that in any event you can deny
knowing anything about it. From now on send all the letters you write to me addressed, in my absence, to messer
Bartolomeo Manno, [?merchant]; I shall leave him orders in mine that he give to the secretary those that are addressed
to the said Hieronimo Ranzo of [?zoe], since I shall have to leave nobody a copy of the cipher.

And consider that such letters will have to be seen by the Emperor and by all these lords of the council, with whom I
have put you in good opinion; and therefore strive all the more to make them well composed and with [?prudence], so
that the good opinion impressed of you may rather [?increase] than diminish. And it does not [?seem] to me
inconvenient for you to write in cipher in the aforesaid way, because you will always have the means of giving, or
having given, the letters you send by [?sea] to some [?safe] friend who will carry them surely, so that they need not
be taken from you as [?booty]; and to secure them further you will make some other [?leaf] that looks [?public] under
letters of [?someone], and you will address the [?secret] ones in [?Barcelona] to [b25] [t66]o, or in Valencia to
Bernabè Grimaldo, or in Cartagena to Nicolò de Fornari and company, with orders that they send them at once to me, and
in my absence to the said Bartolomeo Manno.

And sometimes, if you [?fear] that the letters in cipher [?may] be looked at and not let through, especially those you
send by land, you can make use of another new cipher that I am sending you with the said Cataneo, with which you can
write briefly in [?small]; and to arrange everything better you can share with Iacomo both the practice of the cipher
and the news you will have to give day by day, because in this way you will train yourself sooner and better in
everything.

I meet a greater difficulty in what I write to you in cipher, because the letters written to you can more easily
[?fall] as [?booty] into the hands of the governors than those you will write in the aforesaid way; and therefore I will
not write to you in cipher without great necessity and a sure means. And sometimes I shall use the cte, and when I do,
you will notice it at the beginning of the letters, which will [?begin] "Fili dilectissime" (Dearest son), whereas I am
used to writing "Amantissime fili" (Most loving son); and I shall use the Lyon route, through Polo [?Sper]ato,
partner.

Take care also always to keep the ciphers and my letters written in cipher, and the copies of those that you too write
in cipher, so surely hidden that if the three written ones were searched for they would never be found; and with the
said means you can deny everything with good boldness and [?firmness].

I have understood what you write that [n84] has gone through with you about the matter of the coming. And because it
does not suit me at present to be involved in this, nor in anything else away from the court, since I must occupy
myself in the Emperor's service, and since it satisfies me more to exercise myself in perpetual and profitable offices,
with which I can [?sustain] my life honourably and raise up our house, than in momentary things and [?consequently]
thankless ones, of which, besides the [?tlgar] proverb, I have already, as you know, been well taught by experience;
therefore, if you heard that they wanted to give me some charge in it, you will say that you know for certain that they
will not find me here any longer, and that I [?must] without doubt have left to go home, so that I may [?honestly] be
excused from it; and you will inform me at once.

Of the office of [?general] of the State of [?Milan] there is no more [?account] to be made, because there is no doubt
that since the death of Bourbon His Majesty has recognised that it is fitting in any event to restore the [?duke] to
that state, and in no way to [?keep] it for himself, since he wishes to pacify Italy, as he desires and strives to do;
so that he is resolved on this and can do nothing else. For which reason, and because during the wars that office
cannot be exercised, and it cannot be [?reckoned] stable, I am advised and persuaded by [c146] and by other intimate
friends that it does not suit me to lose myself [?chasing] vain hopes, and that I should rather seek some other
honourable and profitable office for my whole life; because, having already something stable for the maintenance of
myself and my house, His Lordship will always procure for me, day by day, other [?employments] and extraordinary
charges, such that with them I may [?advance myself]. Therefore, having [?resolved] on this, His Lordship is working to
have me provided with a presidency in the Camera della Sommaria of Naples, which has fallen vacant, and with the office
held by the [?late] [?Sor], and I resign in the said place, both offices being very important, honourable and
profitable for life; and if it is not prejudiced by a [?difficulty] — that he for whom the said presidency fell vacant
was a [?doctor] — I hope to have it, because I trust that the favour the said Grand Chancellor shows me will overcome
the many [?competitors] there are for the said office of the presidency, although there is one who offers four thousand
ducats for it alone, considering the good will that His Majesty shows me and the [?decision] to make use of me in
[?important] matters; of which the decision will soon be seen.

[v27] [g73] [?informed]: Tomaso de Fornari, who has left for Italy [?together with] [?Giuliano] to [?carry] a sum of
money for the payment of the bills of exchange which he and [s327] have made payable to the [?army], has worked hard,
through the said [?Giuliano], to be provided with the office of receiver in the said [?army], and would not otherwise
make the exchange; and [?cort], because [s213] in that necessity, as he has done at great loss and risk to himself, he
has [?referred] him to the [?ministers] with [?tlgare] letters of recommendation from His Majesty, which is a [?little]
of [a89]; and he has fed himself on this [?smoke], and I judge his affairs badly. But let everything stay buried in you
and your mother, and of me always say that I am to go home. Do not cease to seek the safe-conduct [r61] for every good
[?reason], and send me word.

I send you another addition to the cipher herewith, with which you will [?go over] it and [?add] to it in order what it
lacks, and then you will send it to [h51] and let me know.

For the rest I affirm what I have written in the other. Farewell. From Madrid, the … of March, one thousand five
hundred and twenty-eight. [m66]
