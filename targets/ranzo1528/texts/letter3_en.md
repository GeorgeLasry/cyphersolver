## Translation

⟨Reverend sir, as a brother. Since having written to you in several letters addressed⟩ to [?Genoa] by the hand of
Hieronimo, ⟨I have received yours of 23 November, 3 and 28 December and 4 February, most welcome, to which I have not
made a particular answer⟩, and I have [?made up for it] with what I have written continually to Hieronimo, whom I have
ordered always to send you a sure copy of [?testimonio], since, finding myself without any help, and notwithstanding the
[?occupations] that are continually given me here, I have [?served] both with one writing. And now I have not wanted to
fail to make you the present, more particular answer to all your said letters, without however repeating the [a64]
[d27] of the events here, since through the said Hiero you will have understood everything; who also tells me he has
sent you a copy of our cipher, which you will be able to use.

⟨I have continually urged on the lord Grand Chancellor your coming here, letting him understand your wish and the
account that he should hold of it and of all your other qualities; but although he knows it all and responds to it no
less, his means are still so tight so far that not only can he not extend himself to what he would wish and what would
be needed, but he has even had to cut down his expenses, inasmuch as His Majesty does not provide for him according to
what his authority, quality and the merits of such great service, and His Majesty's promises, require; which he still
hopes will not be much longer put off. And so he has concluded to me, after many conversations, that it much displeases
him not to be able at present to take you in and keep you, and still less to place you in some other way as would be
right; but that, having any opportunity of doing so, you will at once be called by him, as he holds you in special
memory and esteem. And therefore I would not know how to advise you to come here, fearing that, things standing thus,
you would not find what would persuade you and what you need; nor has there been any way to bring about your going to
the camp in the manner and with the help suggested, which I promise you I have worked for not a little; but things here
proceed so differently from what is being contrived there, though in fact, as I shall let you understand more fully some
day, that no foundation can be laid on one's own ideas. With all this I shall never fail to work for you as I would for
myself;⟩ and if I am to come to Italy with [q24] good [c98], as I hope [?must] soon follow, you will [?share], to your
heart's content, in all my fortune, as I have always told you. On which, God willing, I hope to be resolved in any
case, having [?gone] to Valencia, where His Majesty [?leaves] for on the twentieth of this month; ⟨and you will be
informed of what follows.⟩

⟨I have always given your letters to the aforesaid lord Grand Chancellor, and continually communicated to him what you
have also written to me, and all of it has been welcome to him,⟩ and he [?asks] you to continue, [?since] you have a
[?copy] of the said ⟨z15; he is at present struck by his usual gout; God grant that he be cured before His Majesty's
departure.⟩

[q118]a [L57] the [?particulars] that you have assigned concerning the lord Duke, he has also been very glad to learn
your news after [d227]; and His Lordship greatly desires that His Excellency [?securely] [?keep] himself in the good
graces of His Majesty, as he is at present, so that His Majesty may be assured of [?carlo] his [?mind] being [?bent]
on his usual [?devotion] and service to His Majesty, and that his contrary [?deeds] [?done] were not voluntary but
[?forced]; because he does not doubt that this is the [?true] and [?only] way to [?bring] him to [?port]. Of which His
Excellency will have been able to see a good [?sign] when His Majesty [d135] to the ambassadors of the League at the
[?total] then [?sum] restoration of His Excellency; I also make him understand that this is not new in his mind, as I
have also [?informed] His Excellency through Hiero, by order of Cavalier Bilia, his friend, who [L87] seems [d161] in the
company of the other ambassadors […] and [g7] malicio[…]. The lord Grand Chancellor is truly a good friend to him, and
I [?strive] to be one, as [?zealous] in what I know and can do as if I had eaten His Excellency's bread all my life, as
his own ambassador can be good [?witness] of it. Let all this stay with you, if you have no way of making it understood
most surely to His Excellency alone.

⟨For the lord Prior of Barletta I have worked and am working as much as I can, as he can be assured by his agents here,
being servants of Cor:; you have, I think, received the letters of His Majesty to his ministers that I sent you about
the said lord Prior's need; and if he wishes to employ me in anything else, he will find me always most ready for any
service of his; and so you can let him understand that, being in doubt about the delivery of the letters, I do not
write to him in answer to one of his; and you will commend me to his good graces.⟩

His Majesty has provided in Italy [?nearly] [?six hundred] thousand ducats, and will pay another [?seven] [?hundred]
that [?Ansaldo] has [?newly] taken up in [?Milan] from [?mess]; he waits to [?know] what has happened to the armies in
the Kingdom [of Naples], and [?thinks] that they have given battle, and [?hopes] well of it, considering the news […]
from [?Genoa]. ⟨In the building and arming of the 50 galleys they proceed with diligence, and His Majesty has already
collected a million ducats in this kingdom of Castile, and he will draw another 500 thousand from the Cortes of the
three kingdoms of Aragon which he is going to hold, and from the Indies he also expects a good sum.⟩ It has been
proposed and suggested to him to make use of the superfluous gold and silver of the churches as a loan for [?some]
[?years], saying it is a thing prejudicial to no one, and that it would at once yield an infinity of money, as they are
very rich; but he has not [?yet] wanted to do it, having [?reserved] this [?supplement] for greater need. Still, if the
war goes on, I believe he will lay hands on everything, because he is [?determined] to [?play] his last card. But it is
[?vain] to [?think] of putting into their [?heads] the suggestion of a council [c164] in Italy, notwithstanding however
many reasons may be [?alleged] to them and however many can be [?imagined], for they [o208] not hear it spoken of; and
so that consideration must be left aside.

The most [?dangerous] thing that [?reigns] at [?court] is irresolution, for they never, or so [?late], resolve in
everything, that their every effect is most times [?out] of season; so that if God had not helped them, [?these]
[?ministers] would be in a fine state. Nor [f291] have they here [?yet] resolved on the [?provisions] to be sent to
Italy, both for the [?restitution] of Rome and for the government of the Kingdom of Naples: at times there has been talk
of the Grand Chancellor, at times of [d306], and at times of the [?Archbishop] of Toledo, then of other, secondary men,

things are governed here, and whether they so easily resolve [e68] in a matter like that of the said council above. And
not seeing that [?anything] is enough to remedy such troubles, I remain so [?astonished] that I [?lose] all [?reckoning].
I wanted to make you this [b215] because, knowing how much these [?judgements] [?differ] from your considerations, you
will no longer have to [?ground] yourself on the usual designs, except to [?guide] them from now on according to the

Here it is very well known how much friendship with the Venetians suits His Majesty, and his ambassador here has sounded
it out a good deal in secret; but he would not lend an ear, saying he could not do it without orders from the Signoria;
and so he has given [?clear] [?commission] to his ministers in [?Italy] to seek it by every way they can, [?even] by
[?sending] a person [?agreed] upon, reminding them of the [c92]. But I fear that the defiances that have since followed
[?will] prejudice that [?inclination]. May God grant that some [?fruit] come of it; and [g138] the said Venetians were

[?discord]. To the friend [?Sanchez], who must already have [?come] from Venice, so that [?Navagero] may also [?leave]

⟨The Empress was staying here with the Council of Castile, because she is already in the seventh month of her
pregnancy; the Prince, the first-born, will be sworn here in the first week by all these kingdoms as heir and legitimate
successor to them. I have nothing else to say at present; to you I offer myself with all my heart. (?) From Madrid, 11
April 1528. Yours.⟩
