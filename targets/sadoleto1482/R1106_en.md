# R1106, Nicolò Sadoleto to Ercole I d'Este, Pozsony, 27 November 1482: the cipher passages (partial)

## Translation

However [...] the gracious letters may be, for this Queen Your [...] would not give him any design [...] he should hope
for, unless perhaps things were settled in common for everyone. I hold to what I say on this side, and [?always] I would
wish I had not come here; yet, having come, I have this consolation, that I have well learned and tasted the country and
the people and their ways and minds, which in time are to be made known, and the more to abhor it [...].

I do not say to this Queen more than has been said other times about the move His Majesty the King of Hungary could
make, even only on his own borders, because she holds it as said that the League would not want to spend money for him
to come into Italy, but that if he wished to come, it would oppose him. And this King has firmly resolved not to show his
hand, and in no way, but rather to show himself neutral [...], than to involve himself in war with [the Signoria of
Venice], unless perhaps the thing had to be done [...].

And let Your Excellency be certain that this lord King has very great [...] that I could speak with him often, as I
cannot, not because he is not gracious and pleasant, and he makes much of me and speaks to me willingly, [...] of the
Queen, and that she too had not first consulted everything [...] that you never saw such a thing, that if things were
still [...] a fine thing. Now I have already told you that this lord King is most subtle [...] and feigning at the right
moment and [...], nothing beyond it. And for this [...] I shall be able to; it is enough that I know where I am, and
perhaps [...] if I can always keep within the terms, that [...] I shall not be able to keep perhaps [...] my office in
that [...].
