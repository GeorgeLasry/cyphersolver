# Hindu–German conspiracy cipher material: Gupta's cipher letter, the Chakravarty cipher message, an undeciphered telegram (Unsolved Catalogue no. 694)

Status: in progress

No ciphertext from the three items has been seen yet. The Gupta letter (MID 10020-87) is known only from an index card.
The telegram is printed in substance only. The Chakravarty case file (BI OG 1396) is scanned, but only the OCR'd part has
been searched, and it holds no ciphertext. Nothing below is a reading.

## The documents

1. **Gupta's cipher letter.** M1194 roll 91, frame 0195 (naId 118118268), the MID cross-reference card "GUPTA, HARAMBA LAL",
   Dorchester, Mass. / 6a Balderos 88, Mexico City, alias J. Parkhall:
   `10020-87 | C, MIS. Nov. 7-17 | Cipher letter of Prince --- (Ma`. Here "---" stands for the card's subject, Gupta; the
   right edge is cut ("(Ma", likely Major ...). No solution is recorded. The same card cites 9771-23 (DJ, 23 Aug 1917:
   sailed from San Francisco on the s.s. Mongolia, Aug 1915) and 10345-4 (24 Dec 1917, a close friend of
   Dr Chandra Chakraberty). The duplicate card is M1194 roll 3, frame 0487 (naId 117901432). The British summary in OG 1396
   gives the alias as "J. Porknall" (from a letter that says the name is no longer safe to use).
   MID correspondence files of this period are not filmed, so the letter itself is not online.
2. **Chakravarty cipher message**, BI OG 1396 (M1085 index card, roll 16, naId 367925077). OG 1396 is the BI case file of the
   Hindu conspiracy, filmed whole on **M1085 rolls 284-285** (naIds 369436369, 369437370 = roll 284; 369437845,
   369438846, 369439847 = roll 285; also 369434235 "1287 THRU 1396"). Only three of the roll PDFs carry catalogue OCR
   (M1085-284A, 284B, 285C); the rest, about 3,000 frames, have none and have not been viewed.
3. **The telegram 'not as yet satisfactorily deciphered'.** M1085 roll 760 frame 0286 (naId 370916833) is p. 52 of the
   Committee on Public Information pamphlet *German Plots and Intrigues in the United States during the Period of our
   Neutrality* (Red, White and Blue Series no. 10, 1918), ch. IV "Germany's effort to incite revolution in India"
   (frames 0285-0286, pp. 51-52; filed upside down). Page 51 introduces "telegrams from Chakraberty to Zimmermann, the
   German Secretary for Foreign Affairs" in 1916: May 1916 and 5 Sept 1916 (both given in substance), then the telegram the pamphlet
   says "has not been as yet satisfactorily deciphered", given in substance (money for arms through Batavia, Shanghai
   and Siam; the Maverick; Lahiri, Mukherjee, Sanyal, Roy alias Martin). Then an extract from a cipher message of Gupta's
   "from New York, to an acquaintance in Switzerland", 16 Nov 1916, given as already deciphered.
   The pamphlet prints no ciphertext.

## What the OG 1396 OCR shows

The OCR'd parts (roll 284 A/B, roll 285 C) hold a long British/US evidence digest ("B.B." and "C." exhibit numbers):

- "C. 11 Extracts from Chakravarty's messages to and from Germany" with dated extracts (May 1916; 13 July 1916 from
  Berlin; 16 Aug, 5 Sept, 27 Sept 1916 to Berlin). The extracts agree in substance with the pamphlet.
- "C. 11. 34. Dr. C's draft cipher message of May 1916." Chakravarty's drafts were among his papers, seized at his arrest
  in New York on 7 Mar 1917, so at least some of the messages were read from the drafts or decrypted by the time of
  trial.
- "The interesting Chakravarty cipher messages are two and from this committee" (the Berlin India Committee;
  the reports went to Chattopadhyaya).
- Gupta's letters: references to Gupta "occur in the cypher messages", and a letter in cipher from D. N. Sen to Martin.
  A Manila code letter was handed to the German Consul, and a message "in figure code" came from the consul.
- In roll 285 C a BI agent at San Francisco (Mar 1918) reports that the German code book from the Washington embassy
  would not decipher Hintze's (Peking) telegrams. Another report says that "it has been impossible to decipher the
  remaining code messages" (a variant of code "9972"). These are German diplomatic telegrams in the same case file.
  They are not this target, but they are a lead for the Chakravarty → Zimmermann telegrams, which went "through Embassy".

No frame in the OCR'd parts shows ciphertext of the Gupta letter or of the Chakravarty message.

## The system family (from the literature)

The Riverbank group (William and Elizebeth Friedman) broke the Hindu conspirators' letters in Sept-Oct 1917 for the
Chicago trial. The letters were a book cipher in page-line-word triples (e.g. 7-11-3), keyed to Price Collier,
*Germany and the Germans from an American Point of View* (1914 ed.). The book was identified only after the break.
William Friedman testified on 19 Oct 1917. Jacobsen, Wehde, Boehm and Gupta were convicted on 20 Oct 1917 (pamphlet p. 51).
The San Francisco trial (from Nov 1917) used other messages, which Riverbank traced to an 1880 German-English dictionary.
The British had decoded the messages first (DVIDS, "Friedman testifies at Hindu Conspiracy trial", 2017; Wikipedia,
William F. Friedman). MI-8 (Manly) read the Ghose letter (MID 10531-409, Oct 1918). So the family is known and readings
exist for siblings. If the 7 Nov 1917 "cipher letter" in 10020-87 is in the Collier or dictionary system, the key is known.
Its card date falls between the Chicago verdict and the San Francisco trial.

## Chicago trial exhibit 'Code' (found 28 Oct 2026, NARA Chicago sweep)

The Chicago trial of the Hindu-German conspiracy (US v. Gupta et al., N.D. Ill., Crim. 6082) is digitised: Bill of Exceptions naId 540798683 (258 pages), Case Documents 540798684 and 540798685. At pp. 157-158 of the Bill the government offers a page from Gupta's address book (M. Albourge, Hotel des Alpes, Territet, Montreux) and William F. Friedman of the Riverbank Laboratories testifies that he has decoded 'the letter handed me', marked Government's Exhibit 'Code', and that 'the decipherment is a true and correct decipherment'; the decipherment is then read into the record (the NARA Chicago sweep reports it as Gupta's letter of 16 Nov 1916 with a key from Price Collier, *Germany and the Germans*; the CPI pamphlet cited above quotes the same letter 'already deciphered'). The numeral sheets themselves are not reproduced in the Bill. So the **Gupta letter of 16 Nov 1916 was read at the time**. Not checked: whether the MID card 10020-87 ('Cipher letter of Prince ---', 7-17 Nov 1917) is this letter or a different one; the dates differ by a year.
