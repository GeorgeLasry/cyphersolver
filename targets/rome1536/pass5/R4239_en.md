# R4239, Mâcon to Montmorency, Rome, 6 April 1537, BnF fr. 3053 ff. 35r-38v: English translation

**Letter 1 (f35r-v).** *[clear: I was writing to the King ... our Holy Father told me]* about the marriage of the lord
Ottavio, his nephew, with the Emperor's daughter ... of the marriage of his niece with Monsieur the Count of Merle(?) ...
that he would do nothing against his neutrality, and that peace ... and the other, he would think nothing ... to corner
his neutrality; [to] take advantage of the said talks of the said lord Count. And as for Novara, the lord Pierre Luis
proceeds entirely from His Holiness, and he thinks that ... either the grant or the refusal: if he grants it, to use it
to keep up the gift to the King ... right to recover the Duchy of Milan, as, God willing, he will; if he recovers it,
to take occasion, with the coming of the Turk, to leave his neutrality. And therefore the said [?] would advise that
the King give Monsieur the Cardinal of Carpi and the nuncio to understand that he is not displeased at it. As for me, I
would be of the opinion that the King, so as not to [?] in anything, ... show the said cardinal and nuncio no sign
[of] anything; that if it were spoken of to them, he should tell them that, because I write to him of it, it seems
that this gift proceeds from the will of the said Pope, and that he had told me it was a thing that had not been
communicated to him by the Imperialists.

Cardinal Ridolfi told me that, coming from [?] and passing through Urbino, he sent Antonio Francesco degli Albizzi to
visit the Duke of Urbino to speak to him of the affairs of Florence, and that the said Duke told the said Albizzi
*[clear]* [that there was] close dealing between the said Pope and the said Emperor's ministers, and that it was
certain that the Emperor would spare nothing to win the said Pope, not that he bears him friendship, but to have from
His Holiness tenths and crusade on the clergy of his countries ... which he knows he has together in order, after
making him declare himself partial, to make a league with the princes and potentates of Italy, which he knows very well
he cannot do otherwise. Of which the said Duke made a show to the said Albizzi, who is a close friend of his, of being
in great suspicion, judging that if the said Pope is won over to the said Emperor's side, it will be without
[p]articular [regard] against him, because of the enmity he knows the said Pope bears him. *[clear: ...]* to have
written to me by this(?) an article in the first letters he writes to me, such as you will find in cipher in the
present letters, to show it to the said Pope, so that if reason and honour do not turn him from the said dealings, fear
may divert him from them. The said Ridolfi also told me that the lord Valerio Orsini *[clear: the condottiere passage]*
... the lord Girolamo Orsini.

**Letter 2 (f36r-v).** *[clear opening]* ... a knight of Rhodes and a Greek by nation, servant of Cardinal Salviati, who,
as Cardinal Ridolfi has told me, wanted to persuade him to open dealings with the Imperialists on behalf of the exiles
(fuorusciti), and did the same with Monsieur de Saintes, from whom he received a good and stiff answer, they saying that
they would want nothing, knowing very well that the Emperor wants to make himself master of the state. I have put my
feet on the knight's track, and I find that there is no day when he does not go to the houses of the Imperial
ambassadors and cardinals ... without his master's command.

The lord Pierre Luis has had me told underhand by the Bishop of Cesena, after he had spoken to me, that a new [offer]
had been made to him, that the Imperialists were pressing him to accept it [Novara], and that I was doing nothing for
him on the King's behalf. Wherefore, considering his father's old age and his own poverty, if I did not have a
reasonable and secure estate in France spoken of to him on the King's behalf, he would serve to listen to that of the
said Emperor; the said Bishop persuading me to make him some offer to divert him from it. I answered him that when the
King was assured that he would be willing to accept it, even that, to have the said Pope consent, it would be of no use
at this hour to make it to him; but that I had always found the said Pope, using sanctimony, ill disposed in this
affair, and that I suspected that the said Pierre Luis was so far advanced in dealings with the said Imperialists that
he would want to take advantage of what the King might offer him at this hour; but that if he wished to make some
request of the said lord, I would not fail to inform him of it and to do such office in it as I ought, as a creature of
the said Pope.

Whereupon ... the said Cesena sent me word that he had let the said Pierre Luis know the language I had held to him,
and that he had told him that the last time he was with me and spoke to me of the said Novara, it was not only with the
said Pope's permission but by his command, as he had since [told] him to accept it; and that therefore [?] believe that
the said Pierre Luis did nothing without His Holiness's knowledge and consent. Considering which, and finding the said
Pierre Luis the next day in the said Pope's antechamber, I entered on this subject with him, about which he begged him
to be willing to speak to the said Pope; who, after many excuses [of] wanting to remain in his neutrality, told me,
without accepting or refusing, that I should not write of it to the King; which would not seem so to me. Therefore,
Monseigneur, consider whether it will be good to communicate it to the King, and send me word of his will on it. [?] it
would seem to me, considering the desire His Holiness has for the advancement of his house and posterity, that there
would be no great harm [f38r] in opening to him some honourable estate in France, for after his death ... could by this
means divert His Holiness ... [the rest of f38r L05-L14 is read only in part].
