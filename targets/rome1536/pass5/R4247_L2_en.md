# R4247 letter 2, Mâcon to Montmorency, Orvieto, 1 September 1536, BnF fr. 3053 f. 78v: English translation

The Count of Pitigliano, who through your means is now the King's servant, has come to find me in this place; with him
I have discussed the Emperor's retreat. And his opinion would be that, if the said Emperor wishes to winter in the
Duchy of Milan, the greatest pleasure one could do him would be to make an enterprise against the Kingdom of Naples,
since he knows very well the discontent of its subjects, and that there are some towns and no strongholds, except in
the places on the sea, which could easily be starved; for Sicily would not fail, with little help, to revolt, since it
is even more discontented than the said kingdom. For which reason the said Emperor could not be brought to reason by
any better means. It is to be considered, however, by whoever would make the said enterprise, **that it would be very
difficult here to obtain horsemen or artillery; but one would find as many foot soldiers, and good ones, as one
wanted, and many exiled gentlemen, who would serve for little.**
