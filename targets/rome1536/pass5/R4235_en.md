# R4235, Mâcon to Montmorency, Rome, 15 February 1536 o.s. (= 1537), BnF fr. 3053 f. 21 ff.: English translation

[...] the Pope is aiming to have the strongholds of the Duchy of Milan in his hands, giving it to be understood that he
had been told so on the King's behalf, a thing that did not come out of my mouth. I do not know, however, whether
[you] might have said it to Monsieur the Cardinal of Carpi. *[But I assure you I have learned from a good source]*
that, although he makes every demonstration possible to please the Emperor, the said Emperor nevertheless does not
want him at all, and believes His Holiness to be in good faith; I do believe that, as far as he can make use of her,
even [?], knowing that she is well provided with it, and [?] sleep, he will not fail to seek the subtlest means he can
think of to draw it out from under her wing. To prevent which, it seems to me, under the correction of the King and
yours, that he should be made to despair; the said Pope [should] not show any sign of being discontented with him,
and it is well not to tell Monsieur the Cardinal of Carpi. As the King is informed by the spies he has at the
Emperor's court, the going of [Mon]sieur Pierre Lois to Parma and Piacenza follows the request that the Emperor had
made to His Holiness about it through messire Ambrogio on his return from Provence, so that the said Emperor makes a
show of wanting to give his natural daughter to the son of the said Pierre Lois, of which he [?], and still less the
Duchy of Milan as dowry, which His Holiness or the said Pierre Lois are asking. *[Monseigneur, what I have learned
from a good source]* [?] His Holiness will not yet be able [to come] to the said Duchy of Milan; she is aiming to have
the lordship of Siena by making the said marriage, a thing which His said Holiness will nevertheless do. But all the
things that I write to you by this [...] one cannot greatly rely on it.
