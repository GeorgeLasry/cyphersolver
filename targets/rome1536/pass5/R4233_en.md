# R4233, Mâcon to Montmorency, Rome, 26 January 1536 o.s. (= 1537), BnF fr. 3053 f. 6 ff.: English translation

*[P3 clear: Jehan de la Plume ... the lord Jehan Paule ...]* I have not delivered to the lord Jehan Paule the five
hundred écus *[that you wrote to me of]*. *[You have seen ... the talks that there were]* about the marriages of the
children of the lord Pierre Lois. *[It is a thing which the Imperial ... in words]* The said Pope, who is certainly
devoted *[... Nevertheless the opinion of people of good judgement is]* that the Emperor, considering the said Pope's
old age, will never consent to it, but will rather listen to the marriage of his natural daughter with Cosimo de'
Medici.

*[... And if the King wished]* to prevent the marriages of the said children of the said Pierre Lois, [the Cardinal of
Trivulzio] is of the opinion that it would be good to keep the said Pope in talk about that of Monsieur the Count of
Mer[?]le, even if one did not mean to do anything, judging that the said Pope, knowing by reason [...] more surely
placed in France than in the said Florence, would perhaps find it more agreeable.

*[And they say further, what I think a true imposture]* that Messieurs of Rodez and Lavaur had gone to meet the said
ambassador on the day he arrived in the said Venice; and Monsieur the Cardinal Pisani told me [...]; *[the Cardinals]*
Trani, Cesi and Pisani maintain(?) what they can.

*[The King is not one to do anything unworthy of his name]* Nevertheless the [...] of the passions [...] to the
contrary, or of those who fear the coming of the Turk, which is far greater. And if a part of the Most Reverend lords
who are in France were here, they would serve to give heart to the others; and moreover, if any mishap befell the
Pope, which God forbid, they would help greatly to make the others wait, which otherwise I do not see as possible. The
good man is well, but he is seventy and more, and one can hope for no more than from an old and decrepit man.

*[... gives him a further pension]* The King has not yet done any favour to any of the said Pope's servants; so I leave
you to think what favour can be drawn from them for his affairs.

*[It is a certain thing that]* the Pope is sending to Switzerland the secretary of Cardinal Verulano, who is a very
bad fellow. *[...]* The said lord told me nothing [...]; [...] the said Pope wants to take the legation of Parma and
Piacenza from Cardinal Salviati and give it to the said Cardinal Verulano, which makes me suspect that he has some
design on the Duchy of Milan.

*[... captains had sent to Genoa and other places on the sea]* a marvellous quantity of sacks of wheat, *[so that there
was]* hardly any more wheat or victuals in the said country of Piedmont. *[And they say further that our captains
were]* banded one against another, and even that there was mortal dissension between Count Guido and the sieur Ragni,
[...] that to ruin each other, one of them [...] call in the enemies [...]. *[And they say theirs are well informed]*
of the said disorders, and I believe it. *[... The said Marquis knowing]* the disorders there were among our people
[...]. *[P22: ... favour]* of Monsieur the Cardinal Cesi ... the said Cardinal Cesi ... Monsieur the Cardinal Trani
[...] ... in consistory and elsewhere.
