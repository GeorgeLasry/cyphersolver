# R4247 letter 1, Mâcon to Montmorency, Orvieto, [early September] 1536 (BnF fr. 3053 f. 77r-v): English translation

… and further I inform you that, having heard that Andrea Doria had sent word to the Count of Anguillara to hold his
galleys ready to join his own when he should send for him, and finding this very strange, and knowing (because the
said Count and his people had told me so, as I wrote to you before) the enmity he had with the said Doria, since
Antonio Doria, his cousin, had taken one of his fustas last summer, judging that if there was a reconciliation it was
by the Pope's command, considering even that half of the galleys that the said Count has belong to His Holiness, and
that, if this were so, it could not be without some treaty with the Emperor: I said so to His said Holiness, who
answered me that it was true that the said Count of Anguillara had let her know how the said Andrea Doria had written
him the above, at which she told me she wondered, given the capture of the said fusta; and also that she did not think
the said Doria was content with her, since last year she refused to pay him, for three months, his three galleys of
the Genoese, and also had made him pay for the matrimonial dispensation of his wife's son to marry the second daughter
of Antonio de Leyva; and that she could not think what was the cause of the said Doria's having written the said
letters to the said Count of Anguillara, telling me that she was entirely ignorant of it.

And I replied to her that this could not be done without His said Holiness having some treaty with the Emperor, which
I could not believe, or else that the said Anguillara had taken his service. To which she answered me [?] only that she
knew nothing of it.

⟨Nevertheless, for the reason I write to you … informing you, Monseigneur, that⟩ the Bishop of Lodi came to me two days
ago, sent, as he told me, by Cardinal Verulano, who begged him to sound me, as of himself, whether there would be any
means of making the King consent that the Duchy of Milan should go, through the Emperor, to the son of the lord Pier
Luigi, or else to the little Sforza, son of the late lord [?]. To which I answered him that he knew very well, from the
talks we had had together before, [that] I held it for an assured and certain thing that not only would the King and
Messeigneurs his children not consent to it, but that as long as the crown of France reigned it would not allow the
said Duchy of Milan to pass into other hands than its own, being their true inheritance; and that for this reason it
was time lost for the said Pope [?] to aspire to the said state; and that he would much rather that he help[ed] [?to]
recover it, and [?asked] him [?to set himself] to make his said nephews kings of Naples and Sicily; [?even] that he
could let the said Verulano understand it; informing me that he had learned from a good source that the said Pope is
working on the Imperialists so that they persuade the Emperor that the said Duchy of Milan be deposited in His
Holiness's hands until the conditions of peace that would be negotiated between the Emperor and this [?] are fulfilled;
and that the said Pope does not do it in order to mediate the peace, but so that, once the said [duchy] were in his
hands, he might give it to one of his said nephews, then …
