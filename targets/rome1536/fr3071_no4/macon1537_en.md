# BnF fr. 3071 no. 4: the cardinal de Mâcon to Montmorency, Rome, 11 April 1537 — English

Translation of `macon1537_reading.md`. Same holes.

My lord, you will see from the long letter I am writing to the King what has happened and the talks I have had with our
Holy Father since the despatch I made to him and to you on the [?6th] of this month by a courier I sent in [?part], the
said Pope […]. Further, I inform you that lord Pier Luigi [Farnese] has sent to take possession of Novara, and to the
captain of the [?citadel] of [?Pisa] to see whether he can corrupt him.

The said Pope was spoken to about the marriage [?of Cosimo] with his niece, daughter of the said Pier Luigi. It also
seems to me that if […] his will over the said marriages, the said Pope is set on throwing himself entirely into the
Emperor's devotion; but, pretending not to find them bad, His Holiness can remain more reserved, hoping to have the
support and favour of the said lord. Following [?my letter], messire Latino [Giovenale] has said so […].

Moreover […] of Lyon when lately […] at [?Castillon] […] found all […] the lord Cardinal of Tournon. The King's
confederation with the Turk is clearly confirmed. Which I do not believe; but it is a thing that I think the said Pope
had told me through the said Latino, to give me to understand that, if the said marriages are [?refused], he will have
occasion to declare himself partisan of the Emperor: which you will be pleased to consider, [and be] advised by your
good and prudent counsel to induce the King to this […].

Also, the said [?Pope] declaring himself, it [?will be] […] to be feared that the Venetians [?will follow] him entirely,
seeing that I have [?learnt] from a good place […] only the Turk and [?promising] […] always with the Emperor, which they
[?hoped] to obtain, seeing that it is said that the King had promised the Turk to send a [?great] force into Italy, and
that he amuses himself in Picardy. Therefore he does not keep his promise to him. It is a certain thing.

And Alessandro Vitelli is not content with the Emperor, because he would not answer him by the man he had sent him,
who only reported to him that the Count of [?Cifuentes] had charge of the [?war]. Of which I have informed Messieurs de
Rodez and Lavaur [the French ambassadors at Venice], who have written to me that they have a good means of working on
him, which otherwise I would have taken pains to do on my part; but I was afraid of spoiling the said lords' dealings.
However, if they fail on their side, I will afterwards do what I can there, or better; although I fear that, if the
marriage of Cosimo with the Holy Father's niece is made, the said Vitelli will follow that way, for the said Pope has
the means to content him in the lands of the Church, even with the town of [Città di] Castello, of which he is beginning
to make himself tyrant.

I wrote lately to the King that the Duke of Mantua would not [?give the town] for the assembly of the Council, which
since then the Cardinal his brother has made known to our Holy Father and to all the lords Cardinals in full
consistory, the said Duke [?weighing] the [?demand] of the nations that might attend the Council, and the time of
[?keeping] or […], offering nevertheless his said town provided he were [?given] a good number of foot and horse in his
pay. The said Holy Father has sent to him in haste to learn his intention more clearly. But the said Cardinal told me
that it is not time [?lost], and that he was not for any persuasion whatever to be made to give up his said town, which
I judge to proceed from the Emperor's command, seeing especially that the said Duke has difficulty in […]. Yet it is
thought that the said Holy Father, wishing to show that it is not his fault that the Council is not held, after the
Duke's answer will be for transferring the Council to [?Pavia], Piacenza or Bologna and going there in person: which
seems to me ill-timed. For, as I wrote to you lately, I am so poor that I have no means to [?manage], being in debt to
everyone. And had I had money I would have sent a courier on purpose to inform the King of what I write to him. But I
have also decided, to avoid expense, to send this despatch to Venice, so that it may reach you by the first courier
Messieurs de Rodez and Lavaur send.

Begging you, my lord, for the love of God, taking pity on me, to have me paid what is owed me, of which my man has the
receipts; but I [?tell] you it is impossible to find money. Also to have some benefice given me by the King, otherwise
it is impossible for me to stay here. Whereupon I commend myself most humbly to your good grace, praying the Creator,
my lord, to give you long life in health. At Rome, 11 April 1537.

Your most humble servant and perpetually obliged, Cardinal of Mâcon.
