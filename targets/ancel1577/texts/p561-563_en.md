# Ancel, pp. 561-563: English translation

I have not been able to learn what he … negotiated, but this [manue] said to a person he visited, speaking to him of
Archduke Matthias, that from now on it is not right …that the Emperor should proceed as coldly as he has begun; that
it is necessary that he decide which party he will be of, either of the said King of Spain or of the said lord
Archduke; if of the first, that he show it by deeds, giving permission to raise soldiers throughout Germany, as the
Marquis of Varambon has begged him in the name of Don John of Austria, even offering to give security to comply fully
with everything that was ordained concerning men-at-arms in the last two imperial diets held at Speyer [and] Regensburg;
which being observed, the said lord the Emperor cannot refuse it directly, since he [bel] specially contained […] in
the aforesaid diets. Notwithstanding this, His Imperial Majesty has kept the said Marquis here more than three weeks,
putting off giving him an answer until the arrival of the said Sieur Borgia … he keeps them waiting as long as he can.
[On the] other hand, this Borgia has said that if the Emperor means to support his brother, he will have to do it in
earnest, since the Catholic King will make every possible provision to [pre]serve/maintain his right. From this,
Monseigneur, you can judge in what position the said lord the Emperor finds himself, who, as some claim, had perhaps
not thought that the King of Spain would take this affair by the end that he now seems to wish to, but rather hoped
that, seeing his country almost a prey, he would be content to have Archduke Matthias as his governor there. [One]
cannot yet know which way [he] will turn, for he entertains the Spaniards with fine words, yet secretly does several
little things that reveal the good will he has to favour his brother… if some officer presents himself who wishes to
go and join him, he is at once rewarded for his past services, he is furnished besides with [money]… his journey,
which has lately happened to several others, to a very experienced Neapolitan engineer; these last days likewise he
sang in the said lord the Emperor's chamber … [a so]ng to the disadvantage of the Spaniards … the said lord the Emperor
showed that he took singular pleasure in it, and soon after sent him to Archduke Ernest to let him share the
entertainment. I tell you these small particulars, Monseigneur, while waiting to know for certain what will be the
final resolution of the said lord the Emperor, who in truth, as several wise persons judge, has no need at all of ill
understanding, still less of war, with the said lord the King of Spain, not being too well provided with finances, and
it being difficult to draw the estates of the Empire into any extraordinary aid, seeing that one cannot even obtain
their ordinary taxes from two of them, except perhaps the Electors of Saxony and Brandenburg, who however will not want
to weaken themselves much.
