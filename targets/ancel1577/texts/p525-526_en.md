# Ancel to [the French court], Prague, 22 October 1577, pp. 524 end-526: English translation

By these [?words], Monseigneur, it [?is] easy to recognise in what way the said lord, the Emperor, wishes his
intention to be interpreted … likely [pur] that he will not wish to admit this deed directly before the King of Spain,
but rather to excuse himself, and to persuade him, if he can, that he never thought of it. But considering the gentle
nature of the young prince, and his upbringing, who has always been in fear and [re]verence, so much so that he could
hardly take a step out of his room without the leave of that lord the Emperor his brother — so far was he from being
able to leave the castle, which is shut all night, at such an hour — and likewise never having had anyone about him in
whom he had [mim] confidence, so as to take such a resolution without the knowledge of His Imperial Majesty, with whom
on the contrary it is certain that he has communicated more in the last few days than he used to; considering also
that the Sieur de Malstet, as has since been verified, had asked the Emperor himself for him, not as governor, as
some said, but outright as lord of the said Low Countries, and was negotiating with His Imperial Majesty, not with the
said lord Archduke [pur]. I see no one here who shows any doubt in his conscience that such a thing was not thought
out by that lord, deliberated and concluded in his council, notwithstanding all the other conjectures that one may
wish to bring against it, except that several claim that he had no occasion in such a case to advance one brother in
order to strip his uncle, his nephew[s], of what belongs to them more. I understand [instead] that it took a good
deal of trouble to incite the said lord Archduke to this, the that otherwise … too great …; it is also added that if
the said lord the Emperor had wished to have him stop[ped], he could have despatched the said gentle[men] earlier in
the morning, which was not done; the time, they left only about noon … giving suspicion; whereupon he wrote all the
letters that he sent to the princes.
