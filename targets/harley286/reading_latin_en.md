# Harley MS 286 f. 61: D'Ewes's log of his son's fits, 1635-36: English translation

Heading, clear: 1635.

## Translation

April 3. In the morning about the ninth hour he had a convulsion. Friday.
April 23. In the afternoon he had convulsions twice. Thursday. With the full moon.
May 14. At the end of three weeks [?nine] convulsions again. The moon [?then] changed.
May 23. Saturday, an open flux.
May 25. Between the hours of 12 and 1 he had a convulsion, the full moon being on that day.
June 12. With the moon waning [?] he felt the first convulsion.
June 13. Another mild [?] convulsion followed. And on June 14 another.
July 17. Before the full moon, which fell on the 19th following, two mild convulsions.
July 23. Another severe convulsion.
August 2. The moon [?then] new, a severe [struck] convulsion.
August 3. A mild convulsion in the morning. About the first hour, when he was in the arms of my people, another
terrible convulsion, which after dinner [?shook] him with no slight, great trembling. In the afternoon again
[?afterwards] another severe convulsion.
Aug. 24. In the morning another severe convulsion.
Aug. 20. A little after the full moon (which fell on the 18th of this month) three very severe convulsions, in the
third of which his mouth was drawn almost to the right ear, which I had never observed before. The same night before
the first hour two other convulsions, but milder.
Aug. 21. After the second hour in the morning and before the fifth, two other mild convulsions. Before the twelfth
hour another severe one. [margin: up to here 32.]
Aug. 31. [?released] [lighter ink, above rule]
Sept. 21. In the day, and before the first hour of the night, a [?seventh] agony; but whether {?} [?or] other preceding
[?at the foot] a convulsion, but also [?inborn] epilepsy.
Sept. 27. After the second hour in the morning and before 7, three other agonies.
Jan. 24. After seventeen weeks' freedom, at night between the hours [ninth struck] 8 and 9, a severe convulsion. I
hoped, however, that it had come from too large a supper, which he vomited up after the convulsion.
