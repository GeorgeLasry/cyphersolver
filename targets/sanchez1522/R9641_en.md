# R9641 (R9642): Alonso Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 27 October 1522 — English

Translation of `R9641_reading.md` (the contemporary decipherment, ff. 187r-188r). Same holes.

I have written to the Duke of Sessa that the lords of this Republic say they are content with what the Pope answered
their ambassador on the complaint they made about the quartering of Your Majesty's army in the Ghiara d'Adda; I marvel
that, in a matter touching Your Majesty, the Pope should answer these men favourably, and this manner of answer that
the Pope gives them is harmful to this negotiation. If it could be obtained from His Holiness, it would be well that
this were provided for once and for all: that when their ambassadors speak to him about matters touching Your Majesty,
he should answer so that they know His Holiness has no less care for Your Majesty's affairs than for his own, and that
if they draw close to Your Majesty His Holiness will look after them, and if they do the contrary he will look on it
with disfavour, not as suits them. And Your Majesty should know: this nuncio who is here, whom these men trust because
he is very much theirs, as I have written, says that if the Pope wished, these men would come round to Your Majesty,
by his saying to their ambassadors what I say above and their seeing this will in His Holiness; and that the good
answers the Pope gives them, and the hope of bringing Your Majesty and the King of England to an agreement with the King
of France, keep them irresolute with Your Majesty, because if that agreement is made, as they hold for certain, they
think they will enter it through France without having to come to terms with Your Majesty or give you anything. The
nuncio has not said this to me himself, but he said it to a reliable person who reported it to me; and he has also said
that he believes these men will delay as long as they can with fair words of [?conversation] with Your Majesty. Of all
this I have informed the Duke of Sessa.

Having written this, I received yesterday a letter from Girolamo Adorno, and [?Your Majesty will see] the copy with this;
together with it he sent me a letter of Your Majesty's of the 26th of last month, in which you command me to be informed
of the coming of the said Girolamo Adorno and that he brings powers for himself and for me; and I am writing to him by
this messenger and informing him of the state of the negotiation, as he asks me; and when he has arrived, with God's
help, I shall do what lies in me for Your Majesty's service, as I have done until now.
