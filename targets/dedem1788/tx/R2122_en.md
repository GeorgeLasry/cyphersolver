# R2122, Van Dedem to Van de Spiegel, "Duplicaat", Pera 15 Jan 1789: English

Translation of `R2122_reading.md`, with the same holes.

## Translation

Duplicate. High Noble Honourable Sir!

Being of the view that the interest and political system of Their High Mightinesses and that of [1230] their two new
allies and [1330] the present state of affairs, through the resolution of the second of October, must more than ever
before be held to be one and the same, all my démarches, both at the Porte and elsewhere, which until the receipt of
that resolution, for want of the [?troops'] instruction, had to remain largely idle, have since then been regulated in
every respect; and having the advantage of perceiving most clearly on every occasion [that] the whole Ministry places
a particular trust in me, I can assure Your Honour (in truth) [that] my insinuations have been of no good effect, and I
do not doubt whether, if continued, they will be reckoned of enough importance to find acceptance.

For the first-mentioned reason I have also thought it useful not to let the Prussian envoy wholly ignore the Ottoman
Porte's wish concerning the making of a negotiation in the Republic, so that the King his master, whose interest it
certainly also is [that] the Porte be not now left embarrassed nor exposed to any unseemly humiliation, may be given
notice of it for its further opening.

I [have] not dared to observe the same towards the English ambassador, since no reliance at all can be placed on it and
he is a most dangerous subject and full of intrigues; and as soon as it suits his particular interest he is always
used to making his own words those of another, and, to reach his end, to busy himself with a thousand lies and to
compromise his best friends; for the proof of which I dare confidently appeal to and rely on the testimony that His
Majesty the King of Prussia and all other courts that have a minister here can give of it.

And without [80] least trouble I could wholly oppose [1330] the granting of a new subsidy to Sweden, but, considering
[?the Mediterranean] could be harmful to the execution [760] of the designs of the three allied powers, I have already
made an advantage of it in [?Maison]. And although the Minister of the Sea [the Kapudan Pasha] acts very reservedly
[760] about everything and never [4414] makes known anything of it [?the Emperor of Russia], I shall nevertheless,
continuing, recommend that point. And what would do that matter no little good is the success of the known
negotiation, since a sum could then be used directly in that direction.

Not wishing to detain Your Honour's attention further this time, I refer for the public news to my letters of
yesterday and today to the Griffier, with which, assuring you of my respect, I have the honour to remain with all due
esteem, High Noble Honourable Sir, Your Honour's humble and obedient servant. Pera of Constantinople, 15 Jan. 1789.
