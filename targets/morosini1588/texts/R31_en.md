# R31 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 23 October 1588: Gondi to Rome, Épernon and Provence, the fundamental law, Martinengo)

Translation of `R31_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 173r] […] in the last audience I had with the King I did with His Majesty the office that Your Most Illustrious
Lordship charges me with in your letters of the 29th of last month, on the memorial presented to Our Lord by order of the
Most Serene [Duke] of Savoy about drawing Monsieur de Montmorency away from the union with the Huguenots. I found His
Majesty very well disposed, going on to tell me that for this purpose he wanted to send Signor Girolamo Gondi to Rome, so
that Our Lord might be well informed of his intention and know the generous terms he meant to offer him to draw him
into his service. But he did not think it good advice to send him a gentleman with the confirmation of his government
free and the means to maintain the garrisons unless he first promises to separate himself from the Huguenots; and it
seems to me that His Majesty [has] remained somewhat in suspense on learning that this was a proposal of the Duke of
Savoy, with whom he is greatly irritated, the events of the marquisate of Saluzzo pressing on his very heart, as they
also press on all these princes, lords and peoples; so that there is great reason to fear that, unless a remedy is
found, some mischief [may] easily [f. 173v] follow, to the grave prejudice of the Catholic religion. His Majesty told
me in this connection that he was greatly satisfied with the words Our Lord had spoken to his ambassador, and that he
hoped for every fatherly demonstration from Our Lord.

As to Monsieur d'Épernon and La Valette his brother, [I] have not failed to do every good office; but since they would
not accept the terms offered them by His Majesty [de], as I reported to Your Most Illustrious Lordship in other
letters, I fear that matters are now reduced to a worse state, and that the King could hardly give [him] now what he had
offered them before. Nevertheless Monsieur de Mases, sent by the said Épernon, is still staying here, and is in some
hope of a settlement; but I hope little from it, because affairs in Provence keep getting worse. On this account His
Majesty is sending Monsieur de Sainte-Marie back into that province with orders both to La Valette and to Monsieur de
Vins that they lay down their arms and live in peace, until, once better informed of those matters than he is, he can
[f. 174r] take whatever course is most suitable for the quiet of that government. But I seem to have discovered that
His Majesty's intention would be to put another governor there, being very ill satisfied with La Valette, not only for
the troubles in Provence but also because he thinks him to blame for the loss of the marquisate of Saluzzo, which was
his government.

I have again commended the states of Avignon to the King and to Monsieur de Guise, and I am given very good words, but I
do not see that they have the means to give much help, the Duke of Mayenne not having forces enough to do it. I have
also reminded the Cardinal de Bourbon of the same, considering the […] he has not only as a Christian and a cardinal but
also as legate of this […]; he shows himself most desirous of being able to do every service.

I have not failed and shall not fail to work that no novelty be made in the Estates to the prejudice of the Catholic
faith, for which Your Most Illustrious Lordship will see that much has been done, since by a fundamental and
irrevocable law of the kingdom it has been established [f. 174v] always to maintain the sole Catholic, Apostolic and
Roman religion, without ever being able to make peace with heretics; which, having been embraced with the consent of
the Estates General, who represent the whole kingdom, is a thing for which to praise the Lord God, since it followed
with the applause of all the people and the incredible satisfaction of everyone.

I have done with His Majesty the office about the faculties of the legation that Your Most Illustrious Lordship
committed to me in the previous dispatch; but in fact, unless they are verified by the Parlement, they will not be
obeyed anywhere, even if the King commanded it. And I am told that the Keeper of the Seals, who still has them in his
hands, lets it be understood that the Parlement will never consent to my having authority to visit and reform as the
bull contains, saying that this is directly against the liberty of the Gallican church. To me not a word [has been
said] of this.

By letters of the Cardinal Santa Severina I am advised of Our Lord's wish that a Count Ulisse Martinengo, a heretic,
should be seized, [f. 172r] [who came] to Lyon to deal with the Huguenots, and then sent to Avignon into Our Lord's
power. Having taken this up with His Majesty, he at once, with great readiness and desire to please Our Lord, had
Monsieur de Mandelot, governor of Lyon, written to that he should use every diligence to find this man, and when found
keep him in good custody, sending word here that he has been detained, so that order [may] be given to have him taken
safely to Avignon. The Cardinal of Guise has told me he has heard that Our Lord was recalling the vice-legate of
[191 = Avignon?], and therefore begged me to do an office that Our Lord be pleased to give him that charge, as had been asked on
other occasions by the Cardinal de Bourbon, affirming that he would do such work for the security of that state that
Our Lord would be well content. I answered him that I did not know that Our Lord had any thought of recalling the
present vice-legate, but that I would not fail to advise Your Most Illustrious Lordship of all he told me, as I do [with]
this letter, [which] I believe will be carried as far as Florence by a courier whom Signor Orazio Rucellai is sending
[f. 172v] with the articles of the marriage contract between [the] Grand Duke of Tuscany and the Princess of Lorraine.
[…] some little difference having arisen from the diversity of the customs of [141] and those of France in the case of
restitution and increase of the dowry, the said Rucellai has not been able to conclude without new orders from His
Highness on this point, in which I believe there [will] be no difficulty. […]
