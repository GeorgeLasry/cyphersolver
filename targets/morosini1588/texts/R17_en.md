# R17 — English (nuncio's cipher enclosure, Paris, 1586–87: the Duke of Lorraine's army and the oath)

Translation of `R17_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 233r] […] to try to remove the difficulties and mistrust and to bring in union, which alone can help the present
needs […].

[…] the Maréchal de Retz, who greatly fears these events, concludes, in a long conversation he had with me today, that
unless Our Lord, as father of Christendom, interposes his authority vigorously both with the King and with the others,
he sees no remedy to keep this kingdom from being lost. [So] I, to understand what he meant that His Holiness could do
in this case — after a long circuit of words he said that this meant seeking to understand the claims of
the one party and the other, giving right and wrong to whoever had it, and then working, with his supreme authority, to
make everyone content with what is fair; and that there was no other remedy for this evil.

[f. 234r] […] as […] great as was the joy that all good men had felt at the resolution taken by the King to call the
Duke of Lorraine and the Duke of Guise with their forces to join His Majesty, so much greater has been the displeasure
felt at the change made by His Majesty. He had taken the first decision, so it is said, with the advice of his council,
but at a time when Monsieur d'Épernon was not present, having then gone with a large band of men to try to do the
enemy some [harm]. But when he returned and learned of this order, already sent out, he managed to say so much that he
persuaded His Majesty to revoke it; and His Majesty therefore ordered the Maréchal de Retz by letters in his own hand
that, although by other letters sent to him from the secretariat he had been commanded to go with the troops he has with
him to join the Duke of Guise, he should nonetheless take great care, as he values his life, not to do it. And to hinder
the coming of the Duke of Lorraine as well, he sent a gentleman [f. 234v] to him, renewing the difficulty, already
settled, about the oath, saying [that] if he wishes all his troops to take it, he must stay six months [in] his own
country. To which the said Lord Duke answered that, both because of the first order His Majesty gave him to come and
join him, and because of the protest sent to him afterwards through Monsieur de Schomberg (that if in this need of hers
all his troops did not go to her help she would have cause to complain of him), and also to show his affection and
devotion to His Majesty, he had at great cost found money to pay his troops, as he has already done, and to go and
serve her; that she had no reason to ask an oath of troops paid by him, since what he himself would give to Her Majesty
should suffice; and that he was therefore resolved to come in any case. The King, very ill satisfied with this answer,
has [f. 235r] sent two couriers, one after the other, to repeat to the said Duke that he must not enter France. But
being unable even so to move him, since he stands firm in wishing to [come] in any case and has already advanced so far
that he is […] leagues from here, His Majesty has again sent him a protest that, if he comes any further and does not
withdraw out of his kingdom, he will turn all these armies against him; and it is not yet known whether this will be
enough to make him turn back. [At this] the Queen Mother is in great distress, fearing [that] some irremediable mishap
may follow, and so yesterday she sent Monsieur de Bellièvre to him, begging him most urgently to give the King every
satisfaction, either by having his troops take the oath to His Majesty or by returning to his own country. His answer is
now awaited; and if it is that he wants to come in any case [f. 235v] without the oath, it is held for certain that the
King will turn his forces against him, being sure that his intention, and that of the others of the League, is aimed at
taking the crown for themselves. And people of great standing are already saying that he might come to Paris intending
to have a council of the three Estates of the kingdom assembled to declare the King unfit to govern. Although this does
not seem likely at a time when there are so many heretics in the kingdom, nonetheless the Queen Mother, in the
conversation I had with her today, showed that she greatly fears some unhappy outcome if the Duke of Lorraine goes on
wishing to advance. I have not failed to encourage the Queen Mother, and if I see that things are heading towards
fighting among Catholics I shall not fail to do all that is in me to prevent it, as I do not omit even now to do all
those offices that I can […]
