## Translation

[f. 524r] [For] Your Most Illustrious Lordship […] translation of the letter written to Monsieur de la Guiche.

My lord, while I was looking for an express messenger to send you, I found this bearer, a messenger from Moulins, who
promised me to come and see you and give you the present letter, by which I shall let you know the happy outcome of my
journey so far, thanks be to God, with very good news, which I hope, by the grace of God, to bring to the King, better
than he expected. His army of Swiss [?…] four leagues from Montbéliard […] now that […] left […] will be at Langres […]
this month at the latest; they number thirteen thousand Swiss, two thousand landsknechts and 1,500 reiters; the rest
are French; in all they may be seventeen to eighteen thousand men, with […] twelve pieces of artillery. It is a strange
thing, the credit of His Majesty and the great offers of men and money made to him: the city of Strasbourg alone
[has] lent […] thousand scudi, out of which Monsieur de Sancy has paid [?6,000] reiters and twelve thousand Swiss who
will follow this army for six weeks. I found Signor Cesare [?at] Semur and in the […], to whom I said what he must do
in passing through the county, because […] is holding out for the League, [which] runs a great risk. I gave him a
passport and showed him what [f. 524v] he must do. The said Swiss are coming to Langres, and from there I believe
they will pass by [?the] gates of Dijon, and from there to Autun, [and] Decize. This [?10th] day of June 1589. Your
more than most humble and most obedient servant, Regnault.

[…] translation of the oath taken by those of the town of Moulins on this day, [?16] June 1589, in the presence of
Monsieur de la Guiche, councillor of the King in his privy council of state, grand master of the artillery of France
and lieutenant [?general] for His Majesty in his army of foreigners, of Swiss. The consuls and échevins, residents and
inhabitants of the town and [fau]bourgs of Moulins in the Bourbonnais, assembled in the castle of Moulins, have
unanimously made and taken the oath of fidelity to the King our sire Henri the Third, by the grace of God King of
France and of Poland, and in the hands of Monsieur de Chazeron, lieutenant general for His Majesty in the [government]
of the said country, and have promised on their faith, life and honour, and on their loyalty and conscience, to live
and die in the Catholic, apostolic and Roman religion, under the obedience of His Majesty, and not to depart from it in
any way, nor to undertake or favour, in any way whatever, the enemies of his state and person, and to remain his
faithful [f. 525r] subjects and most humble servants, [and] to [?…] it and defend it towards and against all, he being
their legitimate and sovereign king, and to [obey] the commands that shall be given them, whether by Monsieur de
Chazeron or other lieutenants general of His Majesty, for his service; and to this effect they have renounced and
withdrawn from all promises and associations they may have made to the contrary, if there are any. In witness whereof
those of the said inhabitants who can sign have signed, and as regards those who cannot sign, they have given their
names, which have been transcribed by the notary of the lordship of Bourbonnais at the end of these presents. […]
