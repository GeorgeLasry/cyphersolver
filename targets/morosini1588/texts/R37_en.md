# R37 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, [21 November 1588]: Saluzzo, the King and the Duke of Guise)

Translation of `R37_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 256r] […] yesterday morning, finding myself with Their Majesties, […] I made the representation I had planned about
the affairs of Saluzzo, urging them to proceed in this business with moderation and not let themselves be carried away
by passion; and that it seemed to me a way would easily be found to settle everything, if what I had heard from the
ambassador of the Lord Duke of Savoy was true: that the day before he had clearly told the Queen Mother that his master
would promptly have restored all the places he held of the marquisate of Saluzzo, if His Majesty were content to put
them in the hands of a Catholic knight from whom His Highness need not fear bad neighbourliness; that, if His Majesty
held to the resolution taken to give that government to Monsieur de Nemours, who is so close a relative of the Duke of
Savoy and of whose Catholicism there can be no doubt, it seemed to me he should let this be known, because the said
ambassador had told me he held it for certain that His Highness would be content with him, which would put an end to
this trouble, […] the business without shedding of blood among Catholics. The Queen Mother was the first to answer,
and told the King that in fact the ambassador had said the same to her too, and that my advice seemed good to her; and
turning [f. 256v] towards me she said that the Duke of Guise advised the contrary, saying that it was not for the
King's reputation to negotiate, as if by way of a settlement, to have his own restored to him; that they must attend to
strengthening the forces of the Duke of Mayenne and wait to see the answer the Duke of Savoy will give Monsieur de
Poigny, so as afterwards to do himself justice, showing that with the greatest ease Bresse and Savoy could be taken
from that duke in a few days. The King, after being thoughtful for a while, said that he valued my opinion as a
cardinal's, and that he wanted to follow it, because when the Duke of Savoy then refuses to give back those places, his
ill will will be all the better known. I told him that it was not enough to name the governor, because he could not be
obliged to [?…] in Piedmont, but that His Majesty must also name the lieutenant who was to be kept in the marquisate,
of whom the said duke would perhaps take more account than of the governor; and that therefore His Majesty should choose
a worthy Catholic gentleman whom both he and His Highness could trust, because in this way he would justify his actions
so well that nothing more could be said against them. Their Majesties praised this proposal too, saying that they
[would] [f. 257r] think about it, since until now they had not done so; and I left with the resolution that a courier
would be sent today to Monsieur de Poigny with these particulars on this business.

Besides what I wrote to Your Most Illustrious Lordship in the past days, I have to tell you that it is said here that
the ambassador of the King of Spain resident at this court has complained to the Savoy ambassador because he said that
the King his master would help his duke in this action; and so the Lord Cardinal Gondi has reported to me that the Savoy
ambassador, talking with him, told him that his master would not lack help even if he did not have that of the King of
Spain; whose ambassador publicly blames this action over Saluzzo, assuring everyone that it was not done with the
consent of his King, nor of any of his ministers in Italy. This makes me all the more believe that it is not good to
allow it to be said that this was done by order of His Holiness, because in effect it would make the person of His
Holiness hateful to all France; nor can one hope, by any sort of offices, to move His Majesty and the whole kingdom
together from the firm resolution to make war on the said lord duke if he does not restore the marquisate, as Your Most
Illustrious Lordship will have heard more clearly from Signor Girolamo Gondi, who left here on the 17th of this
month, it not having been possible to send him off earlier. This will [f. 257v] serve for now as an answer also to that
section of your cipher, which came with the letters of the last of last month, where you deal with the affairs of
Saluzzo; to which, for shortness of time, the departure of the ordinary post pressing me, I cannot answer more in
detail; but at the first opportunity I shall not fail to do so in those parts that need it. […]
