# R39 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 8 December 1588, duplicate: the Council on Saluzzo)

Translation of `R39_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 289r] […] Our Lord […] A short while ago a courier arrived here, sent from Turin by Monsieur de Pugni, and I have
not yet been able to learn what he brings; but a very important person tells me that last night there was long
discussion in His Majesty's council about the affairs of Saluzzo, and that Monsieur de Guise had maintained with great
obstinacy that, even if the Duke of Savoy restored the marquisate, […] that by restitution alone he did not give
satisfaction for the offence done to this crown, a prince so inferior having dared to attack so great a king; but that
all the others, while admitting that the said Duke deserved every severe demonstration, advised […] and if His Highness
were content to restore the places, it was good not only to forget the offence but also to renew good friendship with
him, so as to attend with greater forces to the extirpation of heresy; and in conclusion it was resolved to hasten the
despatches, and to send to Lyon without any delay a good quantity of munitions and a number of pieces of artillery, to
which they are attending with great diligence. He also told me that, the council having discussed what was to be done
in case the King of Spain chose to move in favour of the Duke of Savoy, the said Monsieur de Guise had maintained that
that Majesty would not do it, [f. 289v] his own interest not allowing it; and that if he did declare himself, he would
show His Majesty the very easy way to make him lose all the Low Countries within a few months, it being in his power to
cut off the victuals and the supplies of men and money needed to hold those parts. I cannot know whether all these
things are really true [that] are being said to frighten the Duke of Savoy, but I must not fail to report them to Your
Most Illustrious Lordship, being things of such great importance and consequence; you will then weigh them as your
singular prudence sees fit. As for me, I see quite manifest signs of war, and the preparations seem to me to go forward
vigorously; yet it may be that my great wish that things should pass without arms makes me fear more than I should.
[…] Your Most Illustrious Lordship […]
