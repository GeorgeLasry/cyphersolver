# R21 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 10 September 1588: the King dismisses his ministers)

Translation of `R21_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 94r] […] Our Lord: the resolution taken by His Majesty to remove at one and the same time all his secretaries of
state, the Chancellor and Monsieur de Bellièvre, with some other officers, has thrown the whole court into great
suspicion and talk; and as His Majesty has given no reason that moved him to so sudden a change, everyone reasons
according to his own sense, and few are those who praise this deed, with which Monsieur de Guise shows himself little
satisfied, since it seems to him that His Majesty's not having communicated anything to him on this matter is a clear
sign that he has little confidence in him. The same happens with the Queen Mother, with whom, they say, His Majesty no
more wanted to take counsel in this case; and she shows, at least in appearance, that she resents it greatly, because
most of these men had been brought into their offices by her in the time of the minority of the Kings her sons. All
the same I am assured that, on leaving Chartres, the King communicated this plan of his to Her Majesty, a plan that
[had] its origin in his mind from the time he left Paris because of the revolt of this [f. 94v] city, as he then
thought he had found in these men little secrecy, not to say loyalty. All the rest of the court also shows displeasure,
and many have begun to fear that the same may happen to them as has happened to these, because the change happened the
same day. And […] audience of His Majesty I took the occasion to ask him whether what I had heard of this change was
true. He answered that it was most true. And I told him that, although all that His Majesty did ought to be counselled
with great prudence and settled with mature deliberation, I, with the affection I bore to his service and to the
universal good of the whole kingdom, wished to say that this resolution was of the greatest importance, since His
Majesty was depriving himself at one stroke of all those who understand the true state of his affairs, to bring new
men into the government; and even were they the best in the world, they would still have to learn at his cost the way
to serve him well, and only with difficulty and after a long time could they reach the perfection that these others,
by their [f. 95r] own worth, being most excellent men and with long experience, had already acquired; and that it
seemed to me that, these men having full knowledge of his every secret thought and knowing where every weakness of this
kingdom lies, they could do him great harm if, ruled by indignation, they let themselves be persuaded to join His
Majesty's enemies; adding various other reasons that then came to my mind, which it would be too long to relate in
full. His Majesty answered that he did not think these men were of so bad a condition as to turn against him; they were
all rich and would be glad to enjoy in peace the many goods they had acquired in his service; and he wanted to learn
from Our Lord, who made himself obeyed and feared by everyone — as he said he had just been reading a letter from his
ambassador resident with Our Lord, in which, by order of Our Lord, he wrote to him that he too should make himself
obeyed and feared by his ministers and his subjects; that he was now 37 years old, and wanted from now on to attend
unweariedly [f. 95v] to the government of his kingdom, and to see whether, governing in his own way, he could bring
things to a better state than he had done with the counsel of these men; of whom he said that, had he not removed them
now, it would easily have happened that the assembly of the Estates itself would have asked for them to be removed
from the council, because they had been the instruments of putting forward many [courses] that have caused great harm
in the kingdom and the worst dissatisfaction among the people. I praised His Majesty's wish to make use of the […]
fatherly counsels of Our Lord, assuring him that, if he did so, it would put his affairs in a [state] […] better than
they are; but that I did not see Our Lord advising him, in a time of such need as this, to deprive himself at a single
blow of all the ministers who know his secrets, and that this was a dangerous act and would give the whole world much
to talk about; that if he would have it so, he should at least try, since he did not show that he had any fault
against them worthy of punishment, to keep them in his [f. 96r] devotion, so that they do not take another side, as
Monsieur du Ferrier did, who, not being esteemed by him, became chancellor of the present King of Navarre. His Majesty
told me that he was quite disposed to do this, and that in effect he would [hold] them under his protection against
anyone who wanted to offend [them], but that he no longer wanted to use [them], being resolved to have people who serve
him in his way, and not like these, who served him in theirs. After this conversation His Majesty went on to thank me
for the good offices I had done with Monsieur d'Épernon and with the abbé del Bene to keep him in his devotion, and
told me that he had sent his physician back to the said Épernon to see whether he could bring him to give up Boulogne
and Metz, [for] he would then be content to leave him all the rest of the governments he holds; but from what I hear on
the other side, it seems to me that he shows himself most resolved not to do it, and it is much feared that he may
join Montmorency and Navarre. Although His Majesty does not believe it, thinking that he, so as not to lose the 400
[f. 96v] thousand scudi he finds he has on the salt farm, will not abandon his side. I do not fail to do what I can,
both with the said Épernon and with the King and the Duke of Guise, to see that this man is kept on the side of the
Catholics, judging that this is of the greatest importance because of the large forces in his hands; and he quite often
gives me firm assurance of it in his letters, showing the greatest desire that Our Lord should remain well satisfied
of his actions, for which reason he still says he wants to send a man to Our Lord. […]
