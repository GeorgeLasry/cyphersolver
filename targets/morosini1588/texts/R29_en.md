# R29 — English (Morosini to Montalto, [Blois], 10 October 1588: the Tuscan marriage, Saluzzo, Soissons, Denmark)

Translation of `R29_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 152r] […] yesterday evening a courier arrived here on his way back from Florence, sent by the Queen Mother for the
conclusion of the Grand Duke's marriage with this Princess of Lorraine. He reports that Signor Orazio Rucellai will be
[here] within four days to complete this business, the Grand Duke wishing the bride to be at Marseille by the end of
[November], where the Queen Mother herself intends to accompany her. This courier says he was held for four days in
Savoy, that Lord Duke not having been willing to let him pass, so that he should not carry news of the enterprise
carried out by His Highness, who, the said courier says, has made himself master of all the strongholds of the
marquisate of Saluzzo. At this news everyone here is left astonished, and the Duke of Guise has sent to tell me that he
sees very well that this event will be the ruin of the Catholic religion: […] in order to be able to make war on the
Duke of Savoy they will try to make peace with the former King of Navarre and with the heretics, since one cannot at
the same time make war on the one [f. 152v] and on the other. And although he promises me to hold firm, with all his
friends, so that no other religion is permitted in the kingdom, […] and that before this event happened he was sure that
the Estates would all have demanded the total destruction of the others, whereas now he feared that necessity might
lead them to some other resolution. This most important and dangerous event, which could cause very great
mischief, has made me add this too to the long despatch of yesterday, the courier not having left yet, as I wish Your
Most Illustrious Lordship to be pleased to give me orders on what I am to do if it came to asking for this universal
peace, with permission of a religion other than the Catholic, in order to attend to the recovery of the said
marquisate, as is greatly to be feared; which would truly be the total ruin of this kingdom and of religion.

To remedy the discontents of the King and of this Lord of Bourbon at the refusal [f. 153r] of absolution for the
Count of Soissons, and believing it to be in Our Lord's service to soften them, and also to remove the occasions of
greater [disturbances], I have tried, through the Cardinal de Gondi, to persuade them to be content to make the
abjuration in the manner and form Your Most Illustrious Lordship sent me; and this is why the courier is being held, to
see whether they can be brought to this. I have not yet had their resolution on what they intend to do.

Just now I am told that here they greatly praise the good offices done by Our Lord's nuncio resident in Turin on this
occasion of the taking of the said […] strongholds by that Duke.

The Cardinal de Gondi has assured me that the ambassador of Denmark has treated nothing else here than the restitution
of certain merchandise taken from his King's subjects by French ships, and that it is not true that he spoke for the
Queen of England or on any other particular matter. I have [heard] the same by another way: he has not spoken more
[f. 153v] than once with the King, and few […] words; and he is now pressing [with] great diligence for his
despatch, which His Majesty has [ordered] to be made […].
