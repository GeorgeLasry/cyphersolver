# R32 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 24 October 1588: Saluzzo, Provence, the Cambrai canon, the bull of the *confidenze*)

Translation of `R32_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 181r] In the Assembly, to see how his authority [may] now give [to] this business the good end that is desired;
and so […] His Majesty promised me. He has been warned (that the Duke of Savoy, not content [?] with what he has done
in the marquisate of Saluzzo) [that he] also has other secret […] in Provence, and especially [in] the cities of
Marseille and Arles; for […] in that direction, and he holds it certain that as soon as the Estates are over His
Majesty will go to Lyon […] importune […] by the whole world to [resent it] against the one [who has helped]; and the
Duke of Guise has said publicly that if the King will not do it, he will do it in any case by himself alone. What grieves
good men is to see that these events will be the ruin of the Catholic religion in this kingdom, and perhaps in others
too, which the Lord God forbid. Today Monsieur de Poigny is to leave to make representations to the said Duke, that he
restore the marquisate, offering to repay him whatever he has spent on this enterprise, and [f. 181v] to give him
satisfaction and assurance that he need not fear that in future the marquisate might fall into the hands of a governor
not in his confidence, they offer [962] […] to give it to Monsieur de Nemours, who is of his house and a descendant
[…], [of which] he had no cause to be discontented. […] Monsieur de Poigny came to see me and take leave of me, asking
me to write to Monsignor [of Fano], nuncio of Our Lord, that he would help […]. [I have written] without going into any
other particular than to tell him about this gentleman and commend him to his courtesy. […]

[f. 182r] […] the Cardinal de Lenoncourt has told me that he has given Our Lord a detailed account of the obstacles
put in his way so that he cannot use the indult granted him by Our Lord; which spares me the occasion of repeating the
same to Your Most Illustrious Lordship. I will only tell you that I have not failed to show His Majesty the greatest
resentment I could, as was fitting for the dignity of Our Lord and of that Holy See; and nevertheless […] I [have
gathered] that His Majesty was much displeased to hear that the Parlement of Rennes had gone so far, not only […] in
the bull […] to forbid the said cardinal its execution against His Majesty's express order, that I should have [the]
memorial given to her. [He will see] to provide in the best way possible, and tomorrow I shall draw up the said memorial
with the advice of the said cardinal and have it presented to the archbishop; nor shall I fail to do all in my power to
have the Parlement's arrêt revoked and annulled; and with the bishops of that province I shall also do what is fitting
to move them from the opposition they make to Our Lord's orders, although I have gathered from some that regard for
the person [f. 182v] of the said cardinal, who is little liked by many of them, more than the will to oppose the
authority of Our Lord, makes them […] contest the indult; to whom I have said plainly that this increases their fault,
since they oppose out of their own passion and knowing they do wrong.

A canon of Cambrai has arrived here, sent by that archbishop to see that in the truce now being negotiated again
between that Majesty and the Duke of Parma for the said city of Cambrai it be expressly declared that from now on the
archbishop and other clergy […] enjoy freely their revenues and temporal goods. [I] have already spoken of it with the
[…] and with [162], showing them that they ought to make no difficulty, because it has never been heard that the […]
Most Christian and Catholic [kings], on taking a city, made themselves masters of the revenues of the [741]. They
answered me that they would speak of it together and then let me know their intention. I did not fail to reply that
the matter was so clear that it needed no counsel, and that I did not know how their confessors could absolve them of
this sin of wanting church property to be enjoyed [f. 183r] by soldiers. The Queen Mother told me that she did not want
it, but that the affairs of this kingdom are at present in such a state that every governor wants to act in his own
way, and that Monsieur de Balagny, who holds the government of that city, said many things on this subject; so she
feared that even if the King did what I asked, the said Balagny would not obey, […] that Their Majesties should do on
their side what they ought, to show the world their justice and piety […] remedy that Balagny should obey. With all
that I could get no other decision; I shall not fail to press [the dispatch], and of what follows I shall give notice to
[161].

Today I went back to [speak] with His Majesty about Monsieur d'Épernon and La Valette, and His Majesty told me that it
is most [true …], and that they be removed from Provence, because otherwise that province was ruined, and there was no
way to hope that those peoples would ever be content to obey La Valette, having declared themselves too much against
him; and that in exchange he meant to leave him the [f. 183v] government of Metz, and that Monsieur de […] should be put
in Boulogne, to leave free to Monsieur d'Épernon the government of Angoulême, [Saintonge] and Cognac. I told His Majesty
that I feared those lords would not be content with this, and that to preserve themselves they might take some bad
decision; but he replied that for now nothing else could be done, and that he hoped they would be content.

I presented to His Majesty the bull of the *confidenze*, letting him know that Our Lord, by special grace and for […]
souls of this kingdom, had reformed it and brought it to such terms that no one could any longer reasonably oppose it;
and therefore I begged him to have it published and inviolably observed. His Majesty showed that he [valued it] and
said that it had come very much in time, that he would have it examined and then speak to me. I have also again reminded His Majesty of
the publication of the Council of Trent, which he has promised me, and tomorrow he will deal with it in any case,
especially since it is to be discussed in the Estates tomorrow as well. I have pressed the Cardinal of Bourbon that he
too be present […]
