# R47 — English (Morosini to Montalto, n.d. [Blois, January 1589], two leaves of a longer letter: the Council and his own defence)

## Translation

[f. 386r] […] to consider that in these turbulent times one had to proceed with many regards, fearing that, even if he
had chosen to order the Parlement to publish the said council without any reservation, they would not have obeyed him,
because, as I knew, all three orders of the Estates General were asking for some reservation. Therefore, he having said
several times that Our Lord did not mean by this to remove his privileges or the prerogatives of the kingdom, and asking
me whether I thought it good that His Majesty should send a personage to Rome to show Our Lord the heads in which his
authority is touched, begging him to be pleased to reserve them, because once this was done he would then publish the
council without any other condition or reservation — I told him that, to do well and gain favour with Our Lord, he must
now, without further delay, publish the council in the same manner in which it has been published in all the [rest] of
Christendom, and then send the personage to Rome to ask [f. 386v] Our Lord for the graces and privileges he desired,
promising him in Our Lord's name, in accordance with the orders sent me by Your Most Illustrious Lordship, that in all
honest and reasonable things he would be satisfied. They told me they feared this was perhaps impossible to do in that
way, because neither the people nor the parlements would accept it. I answered them that neither the people nor the
parlements are called to the celebration of councils, but only the prelates, to whom [it belonged] to teach the others
doctrine no less than morals; and that when this was confirmed by the Holy Apostolic See the people had no other
consideration to make than to observe the orders and constitutions laid down, because what we see in our faith is good
and true in so far as it is commanded and taught us by the Church; and that therefore His Majesty ought not on this
account to fail to publish the said council, because, [if] the people would not obey him in this, he at least would have
done his part in what he is bound and obliged to do, and to give [f. 385r] to the King […]. And he certainly could not
[do it] with more prudence than I, nor with greater ardour nor greater fidelity, since I have never had any other aim
than the honour of the Lord God and the good service of Our Lord and of that Holy See.

And although I know that someone shows himself dissatisfied with me because I have had more regard for my duty than
for their wishes, I nevertheless count it to my greater honour, since this happened because I, without regard for
others, always chose to serve my master well, whose dignity is more at stake in the [treatment] given to me in his
presence and in my own. Nor has this dissatisfaction of others ever been able to turn me from continuing always in the
same purpose of serving my prince well, because, besides the deadly obligations I have to him, for which I shall always
spend my own life, he is also the Vicar of Christ, which can suffice for every Christian to make him walk in his service
with the same fidelity and truth that he would with the Lord himself, who sees the inmost depths [f. 385v] of our hearts.
Therefore, when Our Lord finds in me — I do not say the enormous and execrable failing of having had dealings with the
ambassador of England, whom I neither know nor ever, to my knowledge, have seen, as I am advised and as is written to
the King himself to have been said to Our Lord — but any other least failing that I may have committed through malice
in his service, I beg him to have no pity or mercy on me, but to punish me with the [harshest] and severest punishments
that can be given to the most wicked man in the world. [But] I, if I am not mistaken, would believe that neither with
Your Most Illustrious Lordship nor with Our Lord ought anyone to have had more trust than I, since there is no one more
obliged to him; besides which I believe that, by the grace of the Lord God, I have been known by all who have had
dealings with me to be utterly removed from the vice of ingratitude; and when Your Most Illustrious Lordship chooses to
test my fidelity and gratitude, you will find that you have no one in this world on whom you can have more […]
