# R35 — English (copy of a memorandum given to the Queen Mother by the Spanish ambassador; Spanish, with an Italian heading)

Translation of `R35_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 202r] […] Copy of a paper given to the Queen Mother by the ambassador […] of the King of Spain resident at the
court of France; the said paper is in the Spanish language.

[…] What I said to Her Most Christian Majesty the Queen Mother on behalf of the King my lord, in answer to what [she?]
ordered to be written to him, from hers, concerning the desire to see these two crowns in a very close friendship and good
understanding, is as [follows]: the King my lord cannot fail to praise and esteem highly the desire of [Her] Most
Christian Majesty, exhorting the two crowns to greater union and friendship, a thing well worthy of one who is mother of
the two Kings; and that, besides the past proofs, in which by deeds were seen the assistance and aid that the King my
lord gave to this crown, and the love and plainness with which he did it, this now […] more disposed to draw closer than
ever, [inasmuch as] he knows better that, these two powers together, no one is enough to provoke them, and that they
can settle public and private affairs to the great service of God and their own entire security. [f. 202v] But, to
speak with the plainness that the troubled state of Christendom's affairs demands, it would not be fitting that these
talks should end in fine words and in [private] ends, which make no lasting union nor sure [correspondence], but that,
seeing that the vigour of human forces depends on the power of God, and that if […] enemies the heretics […] the two
crowns, and have taken the pretext and colour of religion to […], they settle their discords in their erring opinions,
their own malice […] in the outward designs, and that it is not the private business of each of the two provinces in
which it is spreading but common and general to all Christendom, […] to this fire, if it come to […] or be, since one
already touches with the hand that, when for the honour of God, which is the greatest obligation and must be the
principal aim, [they] did not move for […], the interest of each one ought to [set aside] for […] very earnestly
[f. 203r] to this, in which so much is at stake for both; and the King my lord offers to Their Most Christian Majesties
that, if what is said to him to go forward on this footing, dismissing all distrust and removing its cause, and both
taking as their target the service and glory of God and that of the Catholic cause and the obedience of the Roman
church, on which depend the entire obedience and security of their states together, and if the two Kings answer in
deed to the name and [attributes] by which they are styled, and […] inherited, then in that case, the King my lord
forgetting [other] […] that he could remember, […] wishes to set it aside; His Most Christian Majesty will find him so
good a friend and brother, and so ready to draw close and become one and the same thing with him, as […] Our Lord […]
