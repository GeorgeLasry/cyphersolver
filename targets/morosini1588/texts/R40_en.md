# R40 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 8 December 1588, duplicate: Savoy's troops in Bresse, the King's levies)

Translation of `R40_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 293r] […] Our Lord. News came here yesterday from Mâcon that the Duke of Savoy had had a good number of pieces of
artillery brought from Montmélian to Bourg-en-Bresse, and that on the borders of [Savoy] and Bresse there were about
eight thousand foot; whereupon His Majesty assembled his council, and, considering that His Highness must be making these
preparations with some new design, after [long] discussion it was resolved to send at once to raise 15,000 Swiss [and]
8,000 Landsknechts with [Frech], and 1,500 reiter horse, and that all of them, with the greatest speed possible, join the
other troops that are with the Duke of Mayenne; and orders have already been given to find the money for this need,
which will not be much, since there is someone who offers to raise the cavalry without [?or with little] money; as the Duke of Guise
has also offered His Majesty that Colonel Pfyffer will raise the Swiss for ten thousand scudi, and Monsieur de
Bassompierre the Landsknechts [Frech]; and besides, the Estates say that tomorrow they will find 500,000 scudi, and the
[…] and the treasurers offer to give one million three hundred thousand scudi, [f. 293v] if the Estates will assure
them that they will be repaid this and their other credits within four or five years. So it is clearly seen that this
business cannot end without arms, to my very great grief, seeing the great disorder that must […] come of it — which God
forbid. This morning I went to the Capuchin fathers in the hope of finding the King there, to renew with him (although
he already holds me suspect in this business) my efforts to turn him from thoughts of war; but His Majesty did not come.
[…] I did find […] told her what I judged fitting for the general peace; but I have very well perceived that His Majesty
has not been willing to open himself to me as he used to. I shall try to speak again with the King and do all in my
power to bring him to the end Our Lord shows he desires; but I fear my counsels will be of little profit. Now it seems
that with this resolution of the […] His Majesty has drawn very close to the Duke of Guise, and on this account it is
believed that he has dismissed Monsieur de [Cenagli?], head of his finances, and the physician Miron, his [f. 294r]
brother, for being little liked by the said Guise, although before they were among His Majesty's most intimate and
trusted men. It is said too that he will do the same with Monsieur d'O and some others who have always shown themselves
opposed to the said Monsieur de Guise. Of what follows I shall not fail to keep you informed. […]
