# R30 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 3 October 1588: reconciling Nevers and Guise, the Council of Trent, the clergy)

## Translation

[f. 162v] And the other […] from the conversations I have had with Monsieur de Nevers I have discovered quite plainly
an ill feeling that His Excellency (Nevers) has towards Monsieur de Guise; of which, though I had some notice of it before, I
did not believe it had come to the point of being able to bring the harm to the quiet of this kingdom that I have
since recognised. So it seemed right to me to do every effective office with His Excellency to bring him to agreement
with the said Duke of Guise, knowing that their continuing on bad terms together was the reason why both the one and
the other did not [cease] to do ill offices with the King, to the grave prejudice of the quiet and preservation of the
[…]; and after many reasons and persuasions, using also the authority of […] Our Lord, saying that this union would be
most welcome to Our Lord, I brought him to the point that, after a long account of the offences he said he had
received from the said Guise, he at last agreed to promise me to do in this all that I should judge proper. So, in
order to proceed in this business with the circumspection it requires and to give no suspicion to His Majesty, I went
some days ago [f. 162r] to find His Majesty, and put to him the harm he was suffering from this disunion, which is very great
in every respect, but principally because, in harming each other, the one and the other came most often to harm the
affairs of his service as well, besides the confusion that arose in His Majesty's mind from the distrust and
suspicions sown between him and the others; and I offered to interpose myself between them to reconcile them, if His
Majesty thought it good; […] I said that I had not wished to put my hand to it unless I first knew His Majesty's mind,
although I had the Duke of Nevers' word that he would do all I should judge proper. The King showed that he found this
office most welcome, admitting that it would be of great benefit to his affairs if they made peace together, and he
asked me to speak of it to Monsieur de Guise and see whether I could bring him to this settlement, as I did the next
day, His Excellency (Guise) having come to see me; who, though he wished […] to give me an account of all the grievances
Monsieur de Nevers had given him, beginning from the time he married the [f. 161v] sister of his (Guise's) wife,
nevertheless said in the end that, although I [trusted] much in the word that I said had been given me by the said
Duke of Nevers, he too promised me to do all that I should think proper. Now they will set about drawing up the
articles of the agreement with the King's consent and will, to see whether so good and salutary a reconciliation can
be completed, in which there will be no lack of difficulties. May it please the Lord God to favour me with His most
holy grace, for I, as far as lies in me, shall not fail to labour at it with all my spirit; and of what follows I shall
inform Your Most Illustrious Lordship.

I also made a most effective office with Monsieur de Guise for the publication of the Council of Trent, telling him
openly that I knew that the Cardinal his brother and he had so much part and authority in the Estates that, if they did
not jointly ask for the publication of the Council, […] would be theirs: they would not have worked for it in earnest.
The Duke […] promised me to do all in his power, and everything more that I should remind him of. I have done a
similar office with the Cardinal of Guise [f. 161r] his brother and with all who come to speak to me; but I have heard
that, this point having been proposed among the clergy the other morning, a very great division had appeared among
them, since, apart from the bishops, all the other lower deputies, while not refusing the publication of the Council,
want it first seen what prejudices the liberty of the Gallican church, and that this be excepted in any case. I have
also not failed to speak to His Majesty, urging him to publish the said Council of his own free will without waiting
to be asked by the Estates; and if he should claim some favour, he already knew what I had promised him in Our Lord's
name, that in all honest things he would be gratified; and he has promised me to do so. I shall not fail to continue
the offices with the greatest insistence I can, and of all that follows I shall inform Your Most Illustrious Lordship.
I have [heard] now that one of the things proposed among the ecclesiastical estate is […] that Our Lord may not grant
alienations of church property in this kingdom without the consent of the clergy; and I have not failed, nor shall I
fail, [f. 160r] to do every office so that this point is not taken up, saying that it does not belong to them to lay
hands on Our Lord's power, and that it was also superfluous to discuss it, because I was sure that Our Lord, neither
with their consent nor without it, will allow any more sale of church property, since all too much has been sold, to
the great displeasure of Our Lord, who desires nothing more than its preservation and indemnity […] Our Lord […] to
this […]
