# R55 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Moulins, 11 June 1589: Soissons taken by Mercœur, the King's march on Paris)

Translation of `R55_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 516r] […] although I have written to Your Most Illustrious Lordship by the ordinary post all that occurred to me,
[and] also sent the duplicates, I will not fail even now, on the occasion of a gentleman of the Grand Duchess of Tuscany
who is returning to Florence, to inform Your Most Illustrious Lordship that from the account of the said gentleman I
have learnt that the Count of Soissons, who had gone into Brittany, while he had halted at Châteaugiron to give the
troops he was sending towards Rennes time to advance, was taken prisoner by the Duke of Mercœur in his bed and asleep,
together with some other gentlemen of standing; this capture, since this prince is of the blood, is thought of great
importance. On the other side he tells of the death of the Count of [?Santignan], who was of the League: inspecting
some barrels of powder at night with a light in his hand, a spark fell and set the powder alight, and he was burnt.
[…] that the rout at Chartres of some companies of horse of the Duke of Mayenne […]. He says that the King was to be at
Beaugency tomorrow, and that his resolution was to go towards Paris, where the Duke of Mayenne already was, since that
city is in great confusion, having already barricaded [f. 516v] all the narrow streets, and the people being in great
fear and at odds among themselves. He told me that the King showed very great courage, having had very often news of the
happy successes of his troops against those of the League, and that he would no longer hear talk of peace, saying that
His Majesty has on foot four thousand horse and seventeen thousand foot, besides the Swiss who are newly coming to his
help and were near Saint-Jean-de-Losne, as also Casimir, who with a good number of cavalry was ready to enter France;
and the Duke of Épernon having taken this very day Montereau-Fault-Yonne, a very strong place. It is also said that one
Georges de Voi[?], a Norman, had been taken, who had resolved to kill the King. I wanted to tell Your Most Illustrious
Lordship this much, although I do not hold it all for true, since the news comes from the court, where news is always
shaped to the advantage of [its] side. Our Lord […]
