# R46 — English (Morosini to Montalto, [Blois, 24 January 1589]: the Council of Trent; the Pope's displeasure)

Translation of `R46_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 381r] […] with my last letters I had no time to answer Your Most Illustrious Lordship's ciphers of the 24th of last
month. Now I tell you that, having understood what you write me about the Council of Trent, I at once let the King know
that Our Lord not only does not accept the clause "saving the rights of the King and of the kingdom", but curses it;
and so I urged His Majesty, who had resolved that day to publish this in the Estates, not to do it except freely and
without any condition, because doing otherwise he would offend God, with whom one does not make bargains, and would
give dissatisfaction to Our Lord and to all Christendom; reminding him that the words of general councils are words of
God, for the Fathers say "it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us", [and] repeating to him in substance what I had
told him and written many other times, to Your Most Illustrious Lordship with my last letters. On this His Majesty,
having considered again, left off publishing this point in the Estates, but said that, as for the publication of the
said council, he wanted it in any case to be published and accepted, and was letting them know so, that they should
hold it as such; but that, since certain difficulties about reservations [f. 381v] put forward by those Estates were
not yet well resolved, he wanted to give them still riper consideration, and would resolve them together with the
other articles, which for shortness of time had not yet been settled. I then, to discharge my conscience — although
Your Most Illustrious Lordship writes me not to trouble myself further about it, believing nonetheless that your wish
was to see this business well concluded for the glory of God and the good of this kingdom — have not failed to do every
effective office with the Abbé of Senlis, His Majesty's confessor, with the Keeper of the Seals and with all those others
who have authority with him, urging them to persuade him to publish it without any condition; and it seems to me that
I have in any case had some result, for yesterday His Majesty sent the Keeper of the Seals to my house together with his
confessor, who told me in his name that His Majesty, having considered the memorials I had sent him on this matter,
would much have wished to find a way of satisfying me without displeasing his whole kingdom, [and] that he begged […]
[f. 382r] occasion for Our Lord to work, with his authority, to make him obeyed by his vassals in so just and reasonable
a matter. They showed themselves well persuaded of what I told them, and left promising to tell the King that my
reasons were excellent and that His Majesty ought to do so. They are now awaiting his resolution, and I shall send word
of it to Your Most Illustrious Lordship.

As for the affairs of Saluzzo, I have nothing more for now, since these latest events keep His Majesty busy with other
thoughts that press him more at present.

And as to my never having acknowledged receipt of the copy of the letter of His Most Serene Highness of Savoy [written]
to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, which you sent me with yours of the third of October, I think your many occupations
have not let you notice that in my very ciphers of the 1[8th?] of November, to which you answer with this same cipher,
it is mentioned, besides what I acknowledged in mine of the 23rd of October in answer to yours of the third of the same
month. What grieves me extremely is to see in this cipher of yours, as also in some of the [f. 382v] preceding ones,
that Our Lord shows himself ill satisfied with me, without my being able to imagine the cause; and it presses me much
more that this rumour has already reached the ears of the King and of this court, certain vexatious particulars having
been written to His Majesty […] so far not only from the truth but even from the possible that I remain more than
certain that neither Your Most Illustrious Lordship nor still less Our Lord, who is full of such prudence and of such
rare and exquisite judgement, could have given [them] any kind of credence. And although my actions of many years can
clear me before the whole world of such impostures, nevertheless I shall receive it as a singular grace from Our Lord
if it please him to grant me to come as soon as possible to his most holy feet to justify all my actions; for, with the
favour of the Lord God, I hope he will see quite clearly that I have served him with as much fidelity, sincerity and
devotion as could be wished in any other minister, who, although he could have served with greater […] [f. 383r] […]
greater authority and dominion than over me. If then the fruit Our Lord expected from my legation does not […] come,
I beg you to consider whether the fault comes from me or from […], and not, for the love of God, to lay on me faults
that are not mine and give me the penalty of others' sins. This grieves me no less than Our Lord; but let you consider
whether he has ever commanded me anything that I did not promptly carry out, and whether for regard of anyone at all I
have ever failed to serve Our Lord faithfully, always working with all my spirit and all my vigour for the preservation
of his reputation and dignity, hoping by these means to make myself worthy of his good grace and to keep the little
reputation that I have tried to win with the labours of many years, which I confess I value more than my own life.
Therefore, seeing myself, through no fault of mine and while doing the best I knew, in this bad state with the one and
with the other, I must live most disconsolate [f. 383v] until it please Our Lord that I come into his presence to
justify my actions; […] I beg Your Most Illustrious Lordship with all my heart to work to obtain this leave for me,
since you will do not only a favour to me but a service to that Holy See: for if until now I have done little good
while the King [and] the kingdom thought I had credit and some authority with Our Lord, now that they are persuaded of
the contrary it will have to be much less, and they will take no account of me, since all the esteem there was came
wholly from the reflection of Our Lord's good grace […] And to tell the truth I would not know how to serve any better,
seeing that, doing the best I knew, I have nevertheless so displeased Our Lord that he even shows himself ill satisfied
with what I write in all fidelity of the things that come to my knowledge; so that in future, whenever I have occasion
to write anything, about Saluzzo or anything else, I shall have, both in writing and in keeping [f. 384r] silent, always
to fear displeasing Our Lord. I beg Your Most Illustrious Lordship to excuse me if I have wearied you with this long
discourse, considering that I was driven to it by the extreme distress I feel at seeing in such danger what I value
above everything in the world, which is the good grace of Our Lord, together with my honour. I commend, then, to Your
Most Illustrious Lordship the one and the other with all my heart, in order to sustain a creature of yours who will
never fail to work in your service, begging you to believe that I have not been moved to write to you in order to be
in the right against Our Lord — for from him I desire nothing but what pleases him — but so that you may be informed of
the truth and may set His Holiness's mind at rest, should he be informed otherwise by persons of little conscience […]
