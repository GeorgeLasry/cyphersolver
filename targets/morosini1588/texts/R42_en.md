# R42 — English (Morosini to Montalto, Blois, 19 November 1588: the King, the Council of Trent, Guise and the sick Queen Mother)

Translation of `R42_reading.md`; holes as there.

## Translation

[f. 303r] […] in answer to the cipher […] in Your Most Illustrious Lordship's letters of the 7th [of last month] […]
that, being with His Majesty this morning at the Capuchins, I told him of the displeasure Our Lord [feels] that in the
publication of the Council of Trent His Majesty has not yet taken the resolution that befitted a most Christian king,
and one who in his actions shows himself so zealous for the honour of the Lord God and of the Catholic religion, as […]
His Majesty; begging him with much affection not to wish any longer to […] this due and holy resolution, without
lending his ears any more to those who, under […] pretexts of worldly interests, want to deprive him of what belongs to
heaven; assuring him that Our Lord, as a pious and most kind father, will not fail to grant him most abundant graces in
all that he can [grant with] his authority and the [good] of this kingdom; and I made use of the imprecations
contained in your cipher, in case His Majesty, forgetting his obligation to the Lord God, did not come to the
publication of the Council. The King answered that it grieved him that his good will was not recognised, for [Our
Lord] could be assured that, if His Holiness could see his heart as blessed God sees it, he would have no cause to
remain dissatisfied with him; that he would await [f. 303v] the answer of [266], and on this matter he had ordered
Signor Girolamo Gondi to treat with Our Lord, and then he would give me his decision. And because Monsieur de Guise
had complained to me in the days before that His Majesty did not confide in him, and was very slow in carrying out
what was resolved in council, showing that he was thinking of withdrawing, I began to speak with His Majesty about the
affairs of the kingdom, [and] urged him to find a way to satisfy the said Monsieur de Guise by making use of him and
giving him occasion to exercise the power he has given him, otherwise I feared he could hardly be calmed and
contented. His Majesty showed that he valued my advice, and asked me what way I thought he should take to content him.
I told him he was too wise not to find for himself the way to satisfy him, but that for my part I believed that what I
had told him many other times was the only means of doing well: to make his heart sincere, not to lend his ears to
those who bring him evil reports of him, and, knowing that he is poor and laden with debts, to give him some means to
maintain himself, keeping him […] always [f. 304r] employed in some business suited to his inclination and profession.
The King said he found this good, and that I should speak of it with the Queen Mother, who […] knew that, although she
was not [yet] wholly free of her illness, she would nonetheless see me willingly. I also did an office with His Majesty
for the Archbishop of Arles, as Your Most Illustrious Lordship instructs me, and for the publication of Our Lord's
bull against […] by the Archbishop […] first. His Majesty answered that I should speak of it with the Archbishop Gondi,
[…] other, that the bull was most holy and that he will have it published. Then, having left His Majesty, I went to the
Queen Mother, who, although she had the chill of the fever, wanted by all means to see me and to discuss with me the
particulars I had treated with the King, and showed herself very pleased with the office I had done with His Majesty.
She said that I should come to see her as soon as possible, and that she […] would speak with me. I, to tell the truth
about the state of the said Queen, am not […] content, because her chest is still very, very full of catarrh, with a
most troublesome cough, and the fever, which comes to her every [f. 304v] day, besides her being much weakened by the
illness, [and] with seventy years on her back, which makes everyone fear […] the others, these lords of Guise, who let
it be understood that, if she came to die, they would no longer trust themselves to stay at court, fearing that the
King, persuaded by their enemies, might attempt something against the person of Monsieur de Guise. May the Lord God in
his mercy […] to what is for his greater glory and the service of Christendom. […]
