# R956, Gottfried van Swieten to Count Cobenzl, Bonn, 4 February 1759 (ff. 344-345): English translation

It is certain that the Prince de Soubise's army is in the best state imaginable … before the countryside is stocked,
«no one is thinking of making magazines» except «at the expense of the Estates of the Empire, which» are entirely
exhausted; the great principle seems to be to make «the King's army subsist there without its costing a sou». The past
campaign is proof of it; and so «the prince [147 100 126 148]» admitted that the «contributions of Guelders» had «sufficed
for the upkeep of his army» and that enough money had even been «sent to Contades's army» to meet the costs «of the
troops' pay during the winter», and that all the operations of the past year had «had no other aim. The prince thinks»
well, but the rest are «ill-intentioned»; besides that, «there is not one capable general officer in this army except
the Duc de Broglie; the m[arshal] (I) general of the quarters (I), de Vaut, is self-interested and ignorant, and not the
intendant needed to put into practice the system» that I have detailed above, without forgetting himself as to his own
interests, to which he is much attached. Moreover, «a man not violent», I have no need to «speak of Dumenil»; he is
universally known to be «Prussian; Crillon, the Prince Camille (?)» several good, but that is the limit of all the good
they can do for the common cause. Among «the marshals of th[…]» there is only «[290 313 316 313 373] who is esteemed».
As on our side we cannot resist «the well-founded complaints of the Estates», and «the orders of the court» oblige us to
make representations … occasion has been taken to make complaints against «the Count of Pergen», as if he were not
acting in accordance with the interests of the common cause …
