## Summary
From the camp before Monségur to the King (May 1586).
- A battery from dawn to sunset, of about 1,200 shots, did less than hoped: the wall proved stronger than expected. Three breaches were made, the plan being to lodge on one of them.
- In the evening the sieur Sacramore de Birague, on guard on the side of the breach, went for the lodgement, was badly wounded and had to withdraw. The breach was defended from behind by a piece of wall that the guns had not brought down, and the lodgement was not taken. The defenders lost many of their best men; on this side some soldiers were lost and about twenty "b…" (with "…essés" at the head of f. 218v, read as blessés, the wounded: an inference).
- The young nobles were held back. From now on, less firing and a step-by-step approach by lodgements.
- The army is worn out and needs relief and provision for the future. The writer awaits the King's decision on how to use the army after the siege, on the Duke of Mayenne's proposals.
- Something (sign 36, value unknown; guessed to be victuals, since it must last until the harvest) will be hard to find in sufficient quantity in the province and must be brought from Brittany. The forces shrink every day, and the troops not from the region want to leave. Most important of all is the payment of the troops.
- The Duke of Mayenne has a fever, but the physicians are hopeful and there is no sign of danger.
