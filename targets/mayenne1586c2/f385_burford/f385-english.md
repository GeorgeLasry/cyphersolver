# BnF fr. 15573 f. 385r–v: English gist of the readable stretches (a translation, not the plaintext)

**This is a translation by Claude of the stretches of `f385-french.md` that read as French.** It is not the literal plaintext, and it is not a full text. "…" marks unresolved stretches. Words in [brackets] are supplied or doubtful readings from `unresolved-v02.md`; *(?)* marks doubtful sense. Nothing is solved; the reading is partial (v0.2).

Version 0.2 reads four code signs that v0.1 left open, learnt from other letters in this cipher deciphered at the time (stage F): *armée* (army), *tout* (all), *tant* (so much, so many) and the ending *-ée*; a fifth, *droit*, and a sixth, *tre*, are doubtful. The places they unlock are marked ⁺ below.

The writer is probably the Duke of Mayenne, or someone in his camp, writing to the King or to Villeroy. The cipher is the one Mayenne and Forget used. The letter is about the royal army in Guyenne at the start of winter 1586: it is breaking up, and someone must decide what to do with the foreign troops over the winter.
