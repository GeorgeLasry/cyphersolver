## Translation

Berlin, 12 March 1796.

The conference which I announced as due to take place on the eighth between Haugwitz and Elgin was indeed held, and
Lord Elgin has already told me its result, of which I [?shall] give an account; while Count Haugwitz told me the day
before yesterday at the King's ball that he would come to me one of these days to speak to me on this subject.

And he made to Elgin the communication of the idea we had agreed on together, of making an approach to France to
induce that power to consent to restore affairs in the Republic on the old footing, adding that, given the degree of
influence he thought he had in Paris, he hoped that such a proposal would not be fruitless; but that, as the first
question he expected on the part of France would be whether in that case England would give back the colonies to the
Republic, he wished to have assurances beforehand so as to be able to handle this affair all the better in France.
Lord Elgin told him he was not in a position to answer him [direct]ly on that, and that, before seeking new
instructions in this respect, he wished to know whether, in case an overture made by the King in Paris were
fruitless, the King would support it by arms; that he believed he ought to ask this, because it is in the spirit of
his instructions, since in England it is not supposed possible to obtain anything unless one supports one's demand by
arms, and that in this respect at least a demonstration [would be] necessary, for which, according to his latest
orders, he could give assurance that the expenses would be amply repaid to Prussia; that, for the rest, if the King
did indeed have an influence preponderant enough in Paris to be able to bring about there, by simple negotiation, the
restoration of the affairs of Holland, the prior assurance of the restitution of the colonies was superfluous, and
that it would be time to ask for it in England after France had declared itself. Haugwitz replied that the aim was
solely to open a negotiation without having recourse to the way of arms, since in present circumstances the King
could in no case take up arms for the restoration of affairs [in Holland]; whereupon Elgin declared he could not ask
for orders contrary to the spirit of his latest instructions, but he nevertheless recommended Haugwitz to make
approaches in France in our favour, England's principal aim being the restoration of the Republic; and if that power
differed in opinion from Prussia on the manner in which this could be brought about, it would be no less satisfied
in case the King achieved it by the way he proposed to follow. I agreed with My Lord Elgin that, when Haugwitz speaks
to me on this matter and gives me an account of his conversation with him, I shall try to engage him to make an
approach in France without having the assurance he wishes to have concerning our colonies, since it could never do
harm, and if it bore any fruit the benefit would be considerable; in which I flatter myself that I meet my father's
intentions, it being further to be remarked that, if the first step is taken, it is perhaps possible to bring about
the second.
