# Mémoire en chiffre to the connétable de Montmorency, 12 December 1560 (BnF fr. 3157, ff. 152r-156v): English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of the reading in `reading_152r-154r.txt` and `reading_154v-156v.txt` (the
whole mémoire is in cipher; key broken here from the ciphertext), as running text by folio. Same holes: `[word]` a
word emended in the reading where the mechanical decrypt differs (translated as an ordinary word, in brackets),
`[..]` not read. "The chief" (*le chief*) is the King of Navarre, as in the reading. Where the French is broken the
English follows it without supplying the syntax.

## Translation

**f. 152r.** Since the last advices that [I] sent to Your Greatness, affairs over here [have] [gone] from bad to worse,
the sermons and assemblies continuing more than ever; and [in the] towns where there used to be one preacher there are
now two; at which sermons there [are] two notable women who ordinarily attend with a great following, who are Madame
de Castelpers and Mademoiselle de Boesse, wife of Captain Montseignac. It is true that the rebels, having heard that
the King was sending the Sieur de Termes over here with some companies, [have] worked upon some lords [..] to write to
His Majesty and [make] him understand that, [since] everyone over here lives under the King's obedience, [that]
[which] might have done what [was] reported, as much for [some] private profit which they might have felt as for
being of the league of the said rebels. On which the King and Your [G]reatness must not dwell at all, for they do this
only to [..] [..] increase their troops and gain time to have help from some foreigners of whom they say they are sure.

**f. 152v.** [It] is now easy to punish them, and for the future I do not know what it may be, because of the great
factions and intrigues [which] [are found] there, with many persons of quality. [..] that it is true: a few days
ago an assembly was held in the town of Clairac, in the Agenais, of about two thousand rebels, [where] it was resolved
and concluded that, if the King sent forces over here, they would put themselves on the defensive, saying that they
would rather die arms in hand than have the King execute them by a hangman. Their troops and sects increase every day.
Your Greatness could not believe the number of great lords and nobility [who] are in understanding with this rabble;
it is true that they do not declare themselves, but [that they] give them all the favour and counsel they can. The
brother of the Seigneur de Caumont is the Sieur Abbot of the said Clairac. The said de Caumont [have] lordship, place
and castle [..] [..] have no ministers, and that no assembly of rebels be held there, of which I have heretofore
advised Your Greatness. The judge of the said Seigneur de Caumont's lands, named Reclus, has left to

**f. 153r.** go to court, who by the means of the said de Caumont and by the favour of the chief [has] been elected,
for the reasons [which] [I have] written heretofore [to] Your Greatness, to go [and make] the remonstrances of the
grievances for the Third Estate of the country of Agenais, with a syndic of the country of Agenais named Boissonade;
who are now over there, [who] will palliate and dissemble the truth of all the things [that] have happened over here;
for it was resolved by the juge mage and other magistrates of the presidial seat of Agen that there was no need for
the said dele[gate] and syndic to set out the whole truth of things, so as [not] to irritate the King and to prevent
His Majesty from executing his rigour on the said country of Agenais. If Your Greatness thought it good to hear the
said syndic and delegate privately, [you would hear] from their mouths great things that have [been discussed] in the
said country of Agenais. The said juge [mage] has left to go over there expressly to solicit the said syndic and
delegate [to] keep silent the truth of the things [discussed] in the said country of Agenais. Your Greatness will
consider whether it would be good to catch him in the act while he is over there, [and] [..] make him give account of
the duty he has done in his province. Your

**f. 153v.** Greatness has many memoranda against him containing the abuses and malversations committed in his office
of chief of the province. The said Reclus, delegate to the judicature for the said Seigneurs de Caumont of the town of
Tonneins, Caumont and other places and strongholds that they [hold], all of which ordinarily have ministers and
preachers, at whose sermons he has attended, and, in his presence, an assembly of rebels was held there. If he is
heard on the said facts, I do not know what reason he will be able to give for the duty of justice he has done there.
Monsieur de Biron, a mo[n]th ago, [there] was an assembly of about five hundred soldiers, most of them arquebusiers,
whom he said he wished to lead, following the King's command, into the town of Bergerac to win its castle and to
carry out on the inhabitants some command with which His Majesty had charged him. However, having made the said
assembly, after conferring with the principal inhabitants of the said [Bergerac], he sent the said soldiers away and
went back to his house without [other] execution, a thing [which] gives cause to presume

**f. 154r.** nothing good, because of the abuses that may be committed there, inasmuch as he does the like at
Villeneuve, Monflanquin [and] other suspect towns over here. I do not know whether he has done these things by the
King's command or yours; I did not want to fail [to] give warning of it, because since he parleyed with them [they]
have not mended at all, but are worse than [ever]. Also, I do not know whether the King and Your Greatness are
informed that the said [Sieur] de Biron is allied to Mesmi, who is one of the principal captains of the rebels over
here. The said Mesmi, Captain La Caze, Captain Jehan de Mesmes, Captain La Borde of Villeneuve, Captain Chaulmont and
other chiefs of the rebels, whose [names] [I] sent heretofore, are over here, carrying on their usual intrigues,
residing most of the time in the town of Nérac; indeed the said Mesmi is ill there at present, and had himself carried
[in] a litter a week ago. If Your Greatness thought it good, while the chief is over there and wants to call himself

*[Dorse, f. 157v, one cipher word:]* Advices.

**f. 154v.** innocent of the conspiracy, that the King should command them to produce the aforesaid [c]aptains, to
[..] answer [by] their own mouth, considering even [that] they have resid[ed] heretofore near the person of the said
chief and in the train of his court in his government of Guyenne, his residence being at present in his town of Nérac
most of the time. If His Majesty gives the said command, it will be the very means of being able to recover them,
otherwise it will be very difficult; the aforesaid capta[ins] having been recovered, given [by] the means of their
troop, La Caze's; and even the King [w]ill [come] to [k]now thereby entirely the conspiracies and enterprises made
against His Majesty [and] his kingdom. Monsieur de Lioux, brother of the Sieur de Montluc, is going to court, who is
made of the same humour as many others who want to serve the King masked; [which] he has given warning of heretofore,
so that the King and [Yo]ur Greatness may not [trust] it, [..] [..] heard their intrigues and factions. The said de
Lioux, while he was over here, made great threats to

**f. 155r.** those who do the King service, saying that there is no one who has spoken against the chief whom it will
not cost his life within a short time; [and] all those of the said chief's company use like threats and words, even
those [who] had accompanied him [on] his journey to court, who have come back over here, [and], holding their last
muster in the town of Agen, spoke that language publicly. Assemblies of rebels are held every day, of most of which
Captain La Caze is the leader and chief. If the King does not send someone over here to punish them, it is to be
feared that there will be some great trouble or mischief [in] the future. The said rebels have reduced me to such a
state that I [do not] dare leave my house except by night, and they are spreading the rumour that Monsieur de Termes
and his companies, who were said to be coming over here, have been countermanded to go back, a thing which has given
great pleasure [and] contentment to the said rebels [..] [..] to continue their factions.

**f. 155v.** If [so] it were, and thinking that Your Greatness would not find it [bad], for the preservation of my
life, I would withdraw to the train of the court, there to employ myself in very humble obedience in all that it
would please the King and Your Greatness to command [me]; for in any case, the said rebels [having] such authority
over here as they have, they have taken from me all means of being able to see to my affairs at all, except at the
hazard of my life. The said rebels continue, and daily make new compositions which they [..] and send throughout this
country to [..] the common people from their good way and make them s[ch]ismatic, as Your Greatness will be able to
see [by] a copy that I send of it, which they entitle the edict made by God the Father, although I believe it has
been [..] sent from elsewhere. This bearer being ready to mount his horse, I have received a letter written and signed
by a burgess of the town of Agen, a man worthy of credit, on which Your Greatness may place

**f. 156r.** reliance; and if it please [you] to take the trouble to see it, you will find that it contains advices of
great consequence, even of the disorders committed and continued daily by these rebels [over] here. It is needful
that the King and Your Greatness provide for it promptly. The language the rebels hold among themselves, [as] far as I
have been able to discover, is [that they] are only trying to gain a little time to have the help [which they] are
sure of from a prince and from some foreigners on the side of Geneva and the cantons of the Swiss, into whose prince's
hands they are sure of putting two strong frontier towns of this Guyenne. It is very necessary that the King [and]
Your Greatness provide for it diligently, assuring [you] that great intrigues are being carried on there, with such
understandings in so many places that I do not know what may come of it. I would not dare write further of it until I
am better informed of things. Monsieur de Fumel, who is my nearest neighbour, arrived from the court a few days ago,
and since his said coming he has been making

**f. 156v.** great threats against me; I have not been able to learn the reasons. I had given [to] the said Sieur de
Fumel, as he was going to court, a packet to have delivered to Your Greatness, in which there were advices of great
consequence, together with a letter which the chief had written to me. I beg Your Greatness to have me informed
whether you have received the said packet, advices and letter of the said chief. I have also just now received a
letter from the keeper of the seals of the presidial seat of Cahors, which I send [you], and [by] it Your Greatness
will be able to learn of the disorders committed daily by the rebels in the provinces over here.
