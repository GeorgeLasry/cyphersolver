# R9769 (and copy R9770): Caracciolo and Alonso Sánchez to Charles V, Venice, 16 August 1523 — English

Translation of `R9769_reading.md` (clear text of the original; the cipher paragraph from the contemporary decipherment on
f. 574r, checked against the figures). Same holes.

By our letters of the [?29th] and 30th of last month, which we sent to Your Majesty by our own messenger, Your Majesty
will have seen the conclusion of peace and league that we reached here with the lords of this Republic. And although by
letters of the 4th of this month from Genoa we are informed that on that day our messenger had left there with good
weather, and although we had concluded the peace and league here before the Rome one was concluded, our messenger
nevertheless made poor speed, for we are informed that those who carried the news of the Rome league crossed in the
same brigantine.

After what was written to Your Majesty, the English ambassador left about fifteen days ago; he stayed more than eight
days in Padua; he has now left there on the way to Ferrara, to go to Milan and then to the Swiss. Also, five days ago
the lord Infante's ambassador left here; he is the brother of the bishop of Trent, and he has truly conducted himself so
well in Your Majesty's service that more could not be wished of him.

The lords of this Republic have freed from prison two men who were sentenced there for life: one is called Geronimo de
Zotti of Treviso, who had been there two years for some plot or other he was carrying on at Treviso; the other, called
Vital Muto, had been in prison ten years. Zotti lost all he had, for it was confiscated; Your Majesty will see whether
it please him to order him put among the others who are to have their share of the [?…] gold ducats. The lords of this
Republic have told us that they have given orders throughout their lands that the exiles be freed so that they may
return to their homes.

The lords of this Republic have wished to wait for news from Rome before publishing the peace and league; they have
already told us that on Our Lady's day the publication will be made with a solemn procession, with all solemnity and
good display. They asked us to write to the lord Infante that His Highness should order a display on the same day at
Innsbruck too, and the duke of Milan likewise; and so we shall write and beg His Highness. And I the Protonotary would
like to set off on my way, and to be present at this solemnity I shall stay until the 17th of this month, when I shall
leave for Ferrara and from there for Rome.

In Friuli the restitution of the [?possessions] has begun, according to the capitulation of Worms, and it will go on
until it is completed. The money, that is the 38,000 ducats that the lords of this Republic are to pay once the said
restitution is made: the promise has been made to us as Your Majesty's ambassadors, because Filinguer's quittances are
old and he did not have sufficient authority. We, or I Alonso Sánchez who stay here, shall take the money and give it
to Filinguer, as is Your Majesty's will and command; but I shall keep back from the said money a thousand and some
ducats that I have spent on couriers, as Your Majesty can have seen from the account I send, and I shall also take what
is owed me of my salary; all the rest will be given to the said Filinguer.

We do not send Your Majesty with this an authenticated copy of the capitulation, because it could not be made in time;
and so we beg Your Majesty to be pleased to have the ratification sent to us as soon as possible.

We send Your Majesty with this the bonds of the duke of Milan, both for the troops that Your Majesty is to keep for the
defence of the state of Milan and for the 18,000 ducats of the exiles; may Your Majesty be pleased to order how the said
18,000 ducats are to be distributed among the exiles. And let Your Majesty not be surprised that we promised there would
be five hundred men-at-arms permanently in the state of Milan, for these lords showed they wanted it very much and
would not do otherwise; and it is certain that the duke of Milan will keep them, either Your Majesty's or his own, in
time of peace. Nor let Your Majesty be surprised that the lords of this Republic are bound to keep no more than five
hundred men-at-arms in time of peace: they wished to set it so in order not to show a greater obligation than ours to
them; but they have sworn to us that they have more than nine hundred and will never have fewer, nor can they, by their
ordinances.

To a man whom the marquis of Mantua keeps here we have said that we will do everything possible, and he presses us on
behalf of the said marquis; but with the Rome league we believe the Pope will keep the said marquis, and there will be
no room for this here. We understand that the duke of Ferrara and the duke of Urbino are working as hard as they can to
obtain the captaincy of these lords; we believe they will remove Teodoro Trivulzio, who is expected here soon.

It has been confirmed to us that the lords of this Republic have written to their ambassador in France to come home.
Renzo da Ceri is still here, and the French ambassador.

Yesterday we received a letter from Your Majesty of the 15th of last month; and as for the difficulty about making the
peace perpetual, Your Majesty will have seen that we did not stop at it, knowing from good lawyers that it did not
matter.

May Our Lord prosper and guard the imperial life and estates of Your Majesty with the fulfilment of the monarchy. From
Venice, 16 August 1523. Of Your Caesarean Majesty.

These Venetian lords have made the duke of Sessa a gentleman of Venice, as the Great Captain was.

We have already told Your Majesty that the lords of this Republic wanted us to promise them specifically to work with
Your Majesty for the mutual restitution; on this they wanted an instrument to be made, of which we send Your Majesty a
copy with this, by which you will see that there is no obligation beyond our working for it with Your Majesty, who may
order the answer to be as you please; but since it is not promised them, it seems to us it will be no bad thing to give
them good words.

By letters from Milan from the duke and Prospero [Colonna] we are informed how the French and Swiss boast of coming now
to make the enterprise of Italy; but after they have learned of the conclusion of this peace and league we believe they
will think again; but for all that, provision will not fail to be made everywhere as is fitting, as Your Majesty will be
informed more fully by letters from Milan.

Your most humble servants and vassals, who kiss your imperial feet and hands. Protonotary Caracciolo. Alonso Sánchez.
