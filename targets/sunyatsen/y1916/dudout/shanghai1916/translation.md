For Yingshi's (Chen Qimei's) illness, the best is to have him treated by Dr Takano's method. I myself have long suffered from my stomach and was treated many times without effect; recently I tried Dr Takano's method and his diet, and was at once completely [cured?]. But whenever I take a little tea, coffee, […] or meat, the illness comes back. I therefore propose to engage Dr Takano or his […] to come to Shanghai. Have Yingshi told at once to give up meat entirely and eat only vegetables and fruit; during […], to avoid hard food, fruit being best cooked; and to go to bed early. This is essential.

## 0402 | 17 March 1916 | Shanghai → “Rosamonda”, Tokyo (34 words, two forms) | JACAR B03050088200, PDF page 20

Received the 110,000 in gold. Guangxi did, on the 15th, cable its break with the Central [government]. Tian [Tong] is taking on Hubei: yesterday 3,000 yuan were remitted; 15,000 are needed in all, which I shall give. Hunan, beyond its original allotment, needs another 20,000; I give it to Lin tomorrow. The Jiangxi troops need funds to rise: they are ready. In Shanghai the rival faction is very active; we intend to speed up preparations. Wang Tong[yi]'s plan: when can it be launched? Can he come to Shanghai to confer?

## 0392b | 18 March 1916 | Shanghai → “Rosamonda”, Tokyo (40 words, two forms) | JACAR B03050088200, PDF page 10

The secretary-general of the Second Squadron came from Hankou to Shanghai; I saw him yesterday. He says things are under way: all the ships have made up their strength, but cannot move first; they must wait for Shanghai or Nanjing to rise before gathering to take orders. He returns to Hankou in a few days. I have already advanced him 20,000 yuan; more in my next report. Xia Ruifu came to say that Feng Linge has agreed to rise; if one Yangtze province moves, the response is fairly certain. Please appoint Xia by telegram commander-in-chief for Fengtian. […] may need 10,000 yuan, which I intend to give. — Mei (Chen Qimei).

## 0395 | 19 March 1916 | Tokyo → “Junzaburo”, Shanghai (“Ting”, two forms) | JACAR B03050088200, PDF page 13

## 0392a | 20 March 1916 | Shanghai → “Rosamonda”, Tokyo (24 words) | JACAR B03050088200, PDF page 10

Saw Aoki yesterday: he says that unless our party quickly seizes Canton and Shanghai, [Japan's] diplomacy may turn to others. Let Wang Tong[yi] come quickly […], so that we can push ahead urgently. Ming Shaozhen cables asking for 10,000 yuan: his troops are very angry that Zhu refused their transfer, and he fears they will be taken over by the rival faction; he dares not decide first and asks for instructions.

## 0430b | 22 March 1916 | Shanghai → “Rosamonda”, Tokyo (32 words, two forms) | JACAR B03050088200, PDF page 48

Yesterday's telegram was forwarded to Zhanyun (Huang Zhanyun) as asked. Today I received an urgent telegram from Zhongyuan (Deng Keng) asking for 20,000: it has been wired. Long [Jiguang] intends to declare independence; if he does, Feng [Guozhang] and Zhu [Rui] will certainly follow, and nothing more can be done in Shanghai. Please wire Guangdong to get rid of Long; do not be deceived by his pretence. […] if we wait too long, I fear others will strike first and we shall be forestalled. For Wang Tong, please raise [the funds] quickly. The funds needed for Guangdong, Fengtian, Hunan, Anhui and Jiangxi are all in the earlier budget; please allocate the rest. — Mei (no doubt Chen Qimei).

## 0468 | 25 March 1916 | Shanghai → Tokyo (Rosamonde) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 12.42 pm

With the monarchy abolished, Feng and Zhu (presumably Feng Guozhang and Zhu Rui) will gradually wait and see. In Shanghai the opposing faction is weak: we can make our arrangements without haste. Having the navy […] move first is the safest plan, but [the other side] remains on its guard. We fear that action in Shanghai would hinder Wang's plans: please order Wang to come quickly for consultation, so as to [seize] the right moment. […] then act.

## 0465a | 26 March 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai (Junzaburo) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 2.40 pm

Letter from Beijing: Morrison's plan (George E. Funds for Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shanghai and the 2nd Squadron can more or less be covered, but with no margin. ## 0473a | 30 March 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai (Junzaburo) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 12.45 pm

To be passed on to Wu Shifu, Huang Jinqing, Xu Ruilin, Guanwen and the other brothers: do your utmost to raise funds from the Fujian community; if you obtain a great deal, it will greatly help the cause. I eagerly await good news. — Sun Wen (Sun Yat-sen).

## 0475 | 30 March 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai (Junzaburo) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 12.45 pm

Telegram received. The funds are being arranged at once, without knowing whether we will succeed. That the troops, now that Yuan is retreating, should on the contrary wait and see is unexpected; we fear it can never be relied upon. Failing anything better, then […] Wang Tongyi will gain a foothold. For the action in Shanghai, 10,000 have already arrived; there are no funds to allocate here; Iloilo (怡朗) has promised to raise several tens of thousands. When the […] affair is settled, we will remit everything. Chen Minzhong is coming to Shanghai: you should confer with him. Please pass this on to Ruwei (Xu Chongzhi). Further: are the Chaozhou troops moving? Zhong[yuan] is handling it: can you ask him by telegram? — Wen.

## 0485 | 31 March 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai (Junzaburo) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 9.15 pm

Telegram from the North: the monarchy is abolished and the troops' morale is rising. Shanghai, on the contrary, waits and sees. I fear the people contacted earlier are mostly unreliable and use this as a pretext […] to get more money. Look into it closely, brother, and do not let yourself be deceived. — Sun Wen.

## 0486a | 31 March 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai (Junzaburo) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 7.40 pm

Zhongyuan (Deng Keng) telegraphs to urge Hong Zhaolin to come back, but Hong can hardly land [in] Hong Kong. If he has to go to Chaozhou, it would be better for him to go via Taiwan to reach Shantou. Please discuss it by telegram with Zhongyuan. Reply.

## 0489 | 3 April 1916 | Shanghai → Tokyo (Rosamonda) | JACAR B03050088300 | sent 6.37 pm

The funds for Ruwei (Xu Chongzhi) have been received. About Chaozhou and Shantou, we telegraphed Zhongyuan twice, without reply. What is being reported here does not seem to come from our party. In the North, the troops' mood diverges from Shanghai's, according to their stance for or against the monarchy. As the Shanghai troops were mostly opposed to it, they will easily soften after its abolition; on top of that, the speeches of Yang Shande (military commander of Shanghai) lead them astray, and they lean more and more towards appeasement (?). Having investigated, it is not a false report; but we learn from a reliable source of troop transfers: this is most worrying. We propose to move quickly.

## 0495 | 4 April 1916 | Shanghai → Tokyo (Rosamonde) | JACAR B03050088300
