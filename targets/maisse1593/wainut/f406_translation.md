## In clear on f. 406

Monsieur de Maisse, I have always answered your despatches as I received them and noted their dates in my letters, so as not to leave you in doubt, and so that you could tell whether any of them had been lost.

Because of that uncertainty, I approve your practice of sending duplicates of the previous despatch with the most recent ones.

Since the last letters I wrote to you, I have received together yours of 26 December and 1 January; the latter reported the arrival with you of the ambassador[?] with his despatch.

If you found that despatch out of season in some respect, you will nevertheless have understood that this was not for want of his being sent back early enough to reach in time the place where he must go, had he not met extraordinary hindrances on his journey.

Had the remedies for these been in my power and had I not provided them, I would regret this inconvenience the more; but since I could do no more than I have done, one must still do what one can for the rest and wait, as in all other things, for the fruit that it will please God to bring from it.

I believe that in the said despatch you will have found an answer to all the points on which you thought an answer from me was needed concerning my affairs over there.

Or if you saw anything lacking, you will have supplied it, as I left it to your judgement; and I am confident, as regards the Pope, that you will have seen to it that it was not delayed on that account, as your letter gave me hope.

Once this step is taken, we shall wait for what the first news after the receipt of the said despatch brings us from that quarter.

Meanwhile, having thought it fitting to make some reply to the letters I received from the sieur de Brèves, I have by the same means chosen to write a word of thanks to the Grand Seigneur, and likewise to write to his chief ministers about the refusal to let the ambassador of Spain go to Constantinople, which they say in their letters, that you sent me earlier, was made out of regard for me.

If you have kept the copy of the cipher that I sent to the said sieur de Brèves, as I understood you would, you will see what I write to him; for I wish you to know all that comes and goes in that quarter, because of the connection between the two charges, and because of the value I set on the good advice you can give in it.

## In cipher on f. 406 and f. 404 (from "Le surplus")

The rest of your letters concerns the affairs of Rome, the journey of the marquis de Pisany and the war in Piedmont.

I was very glad to hear of the said marquis's arrival in Italy, as I saw from your said letters, having still had nothing from him myself.

I am waiting for the outcome of his said journey, and likewise of the new means you have sought for the relief of my said affairs in Piedmont.

In which, although I know that you do what you can, yet with the other despatches I sent to the sieur de Lesdiguières by those who had come to me from him, I sent him a letter that I wrote to you specially on that matter, so that he can forward it to you by whatever route he thinks fit.

And I have nevertheless[?] told his people what I saw in all your letters of the care you take there, so that they might bear witness to the said sieur de Lesdiguières that, without being asked, you were doing of your own accord what he could wish.

Still, so as to leave out nothing that may depend on me, I have chosen to add this further charge, which he will use if he sees fit, so that he can see even more clearly what he may expect from it.

The sieur de Gondi arrived at Nancy some days ago; I have sent him the passport he asked of me for the rest of his journey.

But since I need to [...] at Tours[?], which I cannot put off, I shall go down as far as Saumur, where my sister arrived some time ago; I shall not be able to see him until my return, which may be towards [...]
