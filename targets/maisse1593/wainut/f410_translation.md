## In clear on f. 410

Monsieur de Maisse, since my last letter of the 29th of last month, I have received yours of 20 February and of 7 and 23 March; the bearer of the last arrived sooner than the sieur de la Clielle[?], whom my cousin the duc de Nevers had kept with him for some days in Champagne.

You show me so clearly in your despatches the disposition and state of all things over there, even in what concerns my service, that I take much satisfaction from it, and I recognise more and more the usefulness that your good offices and the continual care you take there bring me.

So I beg you to be assured that I hold your merit towards me to be such that your absence on this occasion will make you lose nothing of what, by serving so well and worthily, you may rightly desire of my good grace and favour.

## From "L'experience" on: in cipher on f. 410 (where the cipher block ends is not fixed)

Experience has shown that you were in no way mistaken in the judgement you formed long ago of the affairs of Rome.

Yet this proof was needed to enlighten those who would otherwise have remained of the opinion that it was a mistake not to have tried it; they will now have more reason to agree to take another way and remedy for the affairs of this kingdom, when it is decided upon.

And although there is already justification enough for doing so, I have not wished to reject the advice given me not yet to let Cardinal de Gondi or the marquis de Pisany come back.

It is thought that this may somewhat hold His Holiness back from the extremes to which some would push him, when he sees from this patience that no occasion is being sought to break with him, as the enemies would like to persuade him.

In any event I shall be glad if they go forward or draw back in this according to the advice the Grand Duke sends them[?], and not otherwise, s[o?] that they have no other news from me; I believe that, coming from his affection and prudence, that advice can only be well considered and tend to the good of my affairs.

And here I think that what I gave the sieur de Gondi to understand of the regard I have for the said Duke's counsels may greatly encourage him to embrace warmly all that can serve my establishment, according to the promises that the said Gondi made me on his part.
