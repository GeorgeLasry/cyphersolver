Status. Solved: about 90 % certain; a few signs and one damaged word open. 15–17 October 2026, by
George Lasry with CTTS and Claude: transcription by Claude and key recovered by Claude (crib analysis).
References

Ciphertext
The cipher consists of 393 symbols in 10 lines on P2 (block A, 206 symbols in 5 lines; block B, 187 in 5 lines),
most of them homophones for single letters, plus nulls and two literal dotted numerals.
Key
57 distinct signs. Homophonic substitution: three to five signs for each vowel, one to three for the
consonants, a few nulls, and the numerals 80 and 7 written as dotted numbers; no nomenclator.
Signs as cut from the manuscript, with their number of occurrences; homophones stand under their letter.
Decryption

Cipher in bold, clear text plain; nulls omitted; 80 and 7 are numerals written in the cipher; two probable encipherer’s slips are
marked.

Contents
From: Donato Acciaiuoli, Florentine orator
To: Lorenzo de’ Medici
Place: Rome
Date: 13 June 1478 — dated in the clear text
The letter is in clear except for two cipher blocks on P2. Block A continues a clear sentence about the briefs
being written to the lord of Forlì: the same is to be written to the lord of Faenza, that they will be content,
should stay in their own lands and each send two squadrons of men-at-arms into the field; Acciaiuoli adds
that he marvels at Faenza, having had news that something (one sign is lost in a hole) was given several days
earlier without his knowing it. Block B, which follows the clear words 'et della legha', reports on Giovan
Francesco Gonzaga: he has freed himself from his other negotiations and will gladly enter Lorenzo's service
with at least 80 men-at-arms and no foot soldiers, asking 7,000 ducats for these troops and 1,000 for his own
person; a certain Baccio(?) is on his way and will explain the matter by word of mouth.
Historical context and dating
The letter was written seven weeks after the Pazzi conspiracy of 26 April 1478. On 1 June Sixtus IV
excommunicated Lorenzo de' Medici and, with Ferrante of Naples and Siena, went to war on Florence;
Alfonso of Calabria's army entered the Chianti from Sienese territory in July [Rubinstein, Lettere di Lorenzo
de' Medici III, 1977; Martines, April Blood, 2003]. Donato Acciaiuoli (1429-1478), the Florentine orator in
Rome, was next sent to Louis XI and died in Milan on the way (August 1478) [D'Addario, DBI 1, 1960].
Florence meanwhile pressed its Romagnol clients, Pino III Ordelaffi of Forlì (Girolamo Riario took Forlì only in
1480) and Galeotto Manfredi of Faenza, and hired condottieri [Mallett, Mercenaries and Their Masters,
1974]. Block B's Gonzaga is most probably Gianfrancesco Gonzaga (1446-1496), third son of Marquis
Ludovico III of Mantua, who died on 12 June 1478, the day before this letter [Lazzarini, DBI 57, 2001];
whether the condotta was concluded is unchecked in the published Medici letters. How a letter to Lorenzo
came into the Sienese Concistoro's cipher file is unexplained; Siena was Florence's enemy in 1478, and
interception of the courier is the obvious hypothesis, but the file gives no evidence.
