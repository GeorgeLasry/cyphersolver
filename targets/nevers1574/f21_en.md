# Renato Birago to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 28 September 1574 (BnF français 3315 f. 21r): English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `f21_transcription.md`, the contemporary clear fair copy found under the
cipher flap. One doubtful phrase, `[?Salvato] suo` (written *saluato suo*: a name, or the participle "saved"). Italian.

## Translation

Most illustrious and most excellent lord,

I wrote to Your Excellency these past days what was needed for your [?Salvato], and I believed you would be here by
the end of this month to keep the feast of Saint Michael; but since you have not come so far, I hope you will come
soon, though it will not be as soon as I desire. For the rest, the occasion for which the present bearer has come
here has been ill-timed and out of season, more than could be, because the affairs of this kingdom are today in such
a state that one would sooner think of anything else in the world than of that. And so he returns with such a
despatch as Your Excellency will learn, on which I have nothing more to tell you.

And as to the affairs of Piedmont, you will likewise learn how they have passed, on which it does not seem to me fit
to enter into any other discourse with Your Excellency for now, leaving it to speak to you of it more minutely by
mouth, when God grants me the grace of seeing you. Only I will tell you that Their Majesties are of very good will to
provide Your Excellency with a government, and with every other thing, as you deserve. In which (although I know it is
not necessary) I have not failed, nor shall I fail, to do for you every good office that a true servant and friend of
yours should do.

And with this, after humbly commending myself to Your Excellency's good grace, I pray Our Lord God to grant you a long
and happy life. From Lyon, the 28th of September 1574.

Your Excellency's most affectionate servant,
Renato Birago
