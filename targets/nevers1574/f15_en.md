# Henriette de Clèves to the duke of Nevers, Lyon, 16 September [1574] (translation)

## f. 15r

My lord, I would not fail to let you know that today Pinerolo and Savigliano were handed over to Monsieur de Savoy, to the great satisfaction of the King and of the Queen Mother, who have done this fine masterpiece. Having learnt this, I went to the Queen's council and said to her: "Madame, my husband wrote to me in the last of his letters that on his way back here he would pass through his government; he was planning to stay a week to put things in order there, which he no longer needs to do, since he no longer has a government. Will you allow me to tell him so, so that he wastes no time [?and receives] a [?...]."

She answered that I would do very well to do so; and then she corrected herself and said to me: "Why should he not go there? He does not lose his government because of this." I answered that, left as small as it was, I [?hold] it as good as lost, because you had too much heart to keep what a mere gentleman would refuse. She added that it was true that it was left very small indeed.

When she had gone to bed she told me, because she saw me weeping, that I must not torment myself over this, and that the loss is not my husband's but entirely the King's. I told her that I was not weeping for the King's loss, but for your misfortune, that after serving

## f. 16r (cipher, lines 1-19)

answered her that it was a thing you had [...] never [asked] anything but the offices of men still living, that one was too close a kinsman of yours and that the other had too much means of making his own settlement. That is how everything went, which I wished to tell you word for word so that you may decide what you have to do about it.

But I cannot keep from telling you that all your friends and servants say that if you keep your said government in the state it is left in today, you would do yourself great harm; yet I cannot [...] you on these matters, because they are beyond my reach. I could not express to you the [...] and the satisfaction they have at the said surrender, and I advise you not to send them anything about it, for fear they take it badly.

## f. 16r (clear, in the duchess's hand)

I beg you to excuse me if I do not write to you at greater length, for it is midnight and my eyes hurt me so much from weeping that I cannot [...] you so; I have nothing else to tell you for now, except that, seeing the way we are [?led], I would wish to be in paradise. I pray God, my good brother, to give you patience. From Lyon, this 16 September.

Address: To Monsieur, Monsieur the duke of Nevers, peer of France.
