# No. 1, Charles VI to Count Windischgrätz, Vienna, 3 February 1720: English translation

Dear Count Windischgrätz. Your letters, which you sent me privately through my Master of the Horse, I have all received
safely one after the other, as you will have learned through him, the Master of the Horse, as also my gracious
satisfaction; but I have not yet answered you particularly, because they consisted partly only of narratives, which I
have had approved for you, and partly you will have received the explanation and orders through the usual chancery
rescripts. Now I have nevertheless not wished to omit writing these lines to you myself, and to show you my satisfaction
with your zeal and with the conduct you have shown up to now both at Hanover and at The Hague, as also that your secret
letters are welcome to me, and that you are to continue with them, especially where there is something more secret, or
where you should find it necessary on some difficulty or query about the chancery rescripts, or otherwise in important
business. Now, to answer only some principal points: I send you herewith (if it is ready) or by the next opportunity the
cipher which you are to use with me, since the (?) of the Court Chancery could serve. Secondly, as I do not doubt, you
will with all diligence stay in good understanding with my ministers in France and England, and communicate to them all
you learn for my service, as they have also been ordered; especially report precisely to ⟦Pentenriedter⟧ everything that
you only will or the least ⟦suspicion make of France and the Regent⟧, which however set it apart with the necessary
caution; especially that you, graciously (?), where you believe you can well do it, also ⟦open yourself confidentially to
Cadogan⟧, but in no way show ⟦towards France the least suspicion either⟧. Concerning the Tories nothing has been
verified; but as for the ratification of the convention, I believe they will all long since have been exchanged, and you
can send them to me by a safe opportunity. That nevertheless on that account not (especially ⟦now that Alberoni has been
expelled from Spain⟧, ⟦[to] fear that France may perhaps seek a separate peace with the Duc d'Anjou through Monsieur
Morville⟧) is fit to be considered, and so it is also necessary to be watchful on one's guard, yet ⟦without the least
suspicion⟧ to look on. As for the Dutch aversion, you will learn the rest from the usual rescripts. The good understanding
and confidence ⟦with Count Tarouca⟧ is to be praised in every way; you will also continue and cultivate it further as far
as possible. We already find without that ⟦from Portugal⟧ his good inclination; thank him for it too; you will be able to
make a gracious expression; you can also say that 50 (?) it vexes ⟦his King me the Alliance⟧ and the ⟦publico no more
about it⟧ you could do than to bring his ⟦King into the Alliance⟧, on which also to proceed in every way because of his
own description (?), but you will be further mindful, for it is not yet the time; but on the above, ⟦think of Portugal⟧
for me; that will not well ⟦be, measures [?ere] it takes, and accession [446] one of Spain suppressed⟧ can be. The news (?)
gladly sent me in cipher are very dear to me, and so you will continue with all diligence and attention. As for the rest,
it is to be awaited and to be on one's guard; and you will seek in every way to keep ⟦Cadogan⟧ on side, and let him know
how I rely entirely on him and his zeal for the common cause, and thank him, and make known the injustice as well as the
harm ⟦to all the allies, yes, to the English themselves⟧, if one ⟦in the future peace⟧ in the least against ⟦the treaty
made between us should be altered⟧; that giving ⟦Sardinia to the Duke of Lorraine⟧ in the meantime is very ideal and not
well feasible; finally also ⟦as the [?] to the good faith of the treaty⟧, so it is not well to be thought of, that
otherwise ⟦the equivalent of Montferrat for me⟧ is most burdensome, not without, if also most just, to press for this
too, that in ⟦the future peace⟧ this burden should not fall on me, but, where possible, be cut off for the ⟦Duc d'Anjou⟧
or for him so ⟦great [to] [638] the benefit⟧. The rest you will learn otherwise, and I have nothing else to remind you of,
except that you continue with your diligence and application to give me all exact news, especially to see rightly
whether (as is after all to be believed) ⟦the Duc d'Orléans⟧ thinks ⟦of making a particular peace with the Duc
d'Anjou⟧; and that I am still most graciously well satisfied with your conduct, as I remain your most gracious lord.

Charles. Vienna, 3 February 1720.
