# The "Tampico message": Riverbank reports it cannot solve it, 27 Aug 1917 (Unsolved Catalogue no. 674)

Status: no write-up. Blocked on access: the War College Division file 10020-60 is not scanned; only its M912 index card is online.

Blocked on images: the file is not digitised and has to be ordered from NARA College Park. Nothing below rests
on a sight of the message.

## The document

- NARA RG 165, War College Division general correspondence, file 10020-60, 27 Aug 1917 (microfilm M1024).
- Indexed in M912 (Name Index), roll 34, frame 0947 (naId 221122451). The index card covers 10020-41 to 10020-64
  (July-Sept 1917): "repts. re inability to solve Tampico message" (10020-60); "fwds two cipher code messages to be
  translated" (10020-61); "fwds. monthly report of Sept. 1-17 undeciphered messages" (10020-64).
- Correspondents: Riverbank Laboratories (Fabyan, Friedman) to the War College Division / MIS. The message itself
  came from an unknown sender at Tampico; German agent traffic is presumed, not attested. Language unknown (German
  or Spanish expected).

## Access (checked 27 Oct 2026, catalogue record)

- M1024 roll 310 (files 10018-10049; naIds 267255625, 267257126) was fetched and searched when the catalogue entry
  was made: frame 0051 is a withdrawal card, "File 10020 ... Transferred to: Military Information Division". The
  file went to the MID correspondence (RG 165 entry 65, 1917-41), which is not digitised.
- So the ciphertext is not on any public scan. The index card gives only the subject line.

## Prior art

None found. Web searches on 27 Oct 2026 (Riverbank + Friedman + 1917 + Tampico; War College Division 10020 / MID /
undeciphered messages) gave only the general history: Friedman's own accounts say that nearly all the traffic sent
to Riverbank in 1917 was Mexican government traffic, which makes a Mexican (Spanish) message at least as likely
as a German one. No source names a Tampico message, solved or not.

## Remaining gaps

- The whole message: needs-physical-access. No scan exists; it cannot be counted, transcribed or attacked. The
  system is undetermined.

## Escalation

- Siblings: 10020-61 and -64 (the cipher-code messages and the monthly lists of undeciphered messages) are in the
  same MID file and may carry the Tampico text or later solutions. Not digitised.
- Known keys: Mexican government ciphers of 1916-18 broken at Riverbank and MI-8 (Elizebeth and William Friedman's
  Mexican work) are the first keys to try once the text is in hand.
- Print / other copies: the George C. Marshall Foundation Friedman collection and the NSA Friedman documents
  (online, searchable) may hold Riverbank's own copies of the 1917 monthly reports. Not yet searched item by item.
- Next step: order MID file 10020, items 10020-41 to -64, from College Park (RG 165 entry 65). Then transcribe, test
  the known Mexican keys, and only then a ciphertext-only attack with a `lang/` model and a same-length control.
