# No. 65, Sormano and de Vaulx to François I, Ferrara, 23 February 1529 (BnF fr. 3096 ff. 119-120): English translation

### f. 119r

Last Thursday I, Gian Joachino, arrived here and went straight to the lodging of the ambassador messer Gaspar Sormano,
where I am; and having communicated to him Your Majesty's intention and commission, and shown him its instruction and
mandate, and having discussed at length between us and settled the way to proceed *with this* ◎, *so that a conclusion
might be reached according to Your Majesty's desire: having paid our respects to Madame Renée and to the lord Don
Ercole, we were with the said* ◎, and having presented Your Majesty's letters of credence with such fitting words as
seemed fitting to us, we set out our credence, and in Your Majesty's name *we persuaded, encouraged, and earnestly
exhorted and begged His Lordship to be willing to accept what he, as his good and affectionate son, should ferisse(?)
and resolve: to make the enterprise of Naples in the manner and on the conditions contained in the said instruction*;
and here, with many most effective reasons, we showed at length *the establishment of His Lordship, his state and
posterity, the honour and the profit that the one … bring back, and Your Majesty's satisfaction, and finally the good,
and, with great glory to His Lordship, the final liberation and quiet of Italy*; nor was he left untold of the great
good work that, for the benefit of *his person, state, house and succession, Your Majesty had done with … and the
almost firm hope that she held of bringing His Holiness wholly round … agreements and conditions that … were spoken of
and His Excellency could desire* — and all of which things, as he wished, were related to him at length.

Sire, although in that discussion, which was quite long, *concerning the affairs of the kingdom* ◎ *answered in such a
way* that from then on we could have written what will now be said, nevertheless, as he concluded that the matter *for*
◎ *… great but his … required time to think it over better, we decided to await his final answer; and meanwhile we were
again with the lord Don Ercole and let him understand that now was the time to work in Your Majesty's service, and that
his house … and his … state concerned him so much …* 《f. 119r L32-36: the run on Ercole's own interest — his house, his
person, what he might hope for — is only partly legible》

### f. 119v

*… we advised that I, Sormano, should go alone, as I went, to the said* ◎ *to show him how to a … servant the … that His
Excellency do … when … what is said. … the following day we were asked by His Lordship* ◎ *… and after many reasons that
seemed to us most sufficient, … only to remove him from every suspicion alleged earlier by him, … as … naturally … said
and … but also to dispose him to accept with … good heart what was offered him by Your Majesty*: finally, thanking her
for *so great and honourable an offer as it had pleased her to make him, he concluded resolutely that for nothing in the
world would he accept the kingdom for himself in any way, still less make the enterprise in his own name*; and his
speech was such that *I, Sormano, seemed to myself almost cleared up about what I always suspected, namely that His
Excellency, although he had given ear and answered* in the way I wrote to Your Majesty, truly had no *mind to undertake
the captaincy, of which many days ago he had been sounded.* He did say that he not only was content but begged Your
Majesty with all insistence to be willing *to negotiate, between her and him(?), the settlement and agreement, about
which he said he would accept whatever Your Majesty did, persuading himself that he would not be constrained by her to do
anything but what is fitting and reasonable*.

Sire, because of the great desire we have that *this lord Duke should satisfy Your Majesty's expectation*, and not
wishing to omit any work or office in this, nor leave anything untried, notwithstanding *this said answer of his, we were
again with the aforesaid lord Don Ercole, truly desirous to do Your Majesty service, and … not … content …, showing him
that such an answer conceded(?) little to any profit of his; at which he too, lamenting with us the quality of his
father's answer, showed himself little satisfied; we urged him to be with His Excellency again, hoping that he would not
want to lose this so fine occasion that time and Your Majesty's liberality offered him, as any other could be offered
him … his state … with little and small danger to him … his house and his succession … Italy, and to satisfy Your
Majesty*; besides this, I advised that *I, Sormano, another time …* 《…》 ◎ 《…》

### f. 120r

*… in conclusion he confirmed himself in the answer written above; and so, ill satisfied, withdrawing myself … to a
person … the lord Don Ercole, who, telling me how the lord Duke in no way [would] undertake … for himself; and as for the
captaincy, notwithstanding what had been said and … answered in the first discussion written above and afterwards, he
said that His Excellency persisted in the … form and manner of the answer given to me, Sormano, in these days …, if said
I wrote it in that very manner, and so little … resolved and little clear as the … was given, touching … they will make
… agreement … to compose his affairs with the …, telling me as much as above.*

Sire, from the above-written discussions held with this lord Duke it seems possible to conclude that *no … foundation can
be made that in effect the … wish to enter or accept the captaincy, on which … provision, circumstances and their
dependencies on His Excellency's side … the difficulties; and in our judgement, for … the thing together with its
provisions … dependencies so difficult that it seems … of difficulty, and not the will be … what makes him not accept …
this perhaps vain opinion of ours* it did not seem to us right to keep silent from Your Majesty.

I, Joachin, will await the lord Marshal Trivulzio here, and we are of the opinion that His Lordship, *much esteemed by
the aforesaid lord Duke, will exert himself with His Excellency for the benefit of this matter*, which, for the good of
this business and Your Majesty's service, we are certain he will very willingly do. From Ferrara, the 23rd of February
1529.
