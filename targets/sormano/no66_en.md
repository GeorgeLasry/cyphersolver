# No. 66 — Sormano and de Vaulx to François I, Ferrara, 23 February 1529 (English)

Translation of `no66_reading.md` (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026), with the same holes. *Italics* = passages that
stand in cipher on the leaf. [word] supplied, [?word] uncertain, […] unread. Modern spelling here only.

### f. 121r

Last Thursday I, Joachim, arrived here and went straight to the lodging of the ambassador Messer Gaspar Sormano,
where I am now staying. I told him Your Majesty's intention and commission and showed him the instruction and
mandate, and between us we discussed at length and settled how to proceed *with the [Duke] so that a conclusion might
be reached as Your Majesty desires*. After paying our respects to Madame Renée and Lord Don Ercole, *we went to the
aforesaid [Duke]*. We presented Your Majesty's letters of credence with fitting words and set out our credence, and
*in Your Majesty's name* we persuaded, encouraged, earnestly exhorted and begged *His Excellency to accept what Your
Majesty, as his good and affectionate kinsman, was offering him, and to resolve to accept it: to undertake the
enterprise of Naples* on the terms and conditions in the instruction. With many very strong arguments we showed him
at length *the security of His Excellency, his state and [?posterity], and the honour and profit he would draw from
it*, Your Majesty's satisfaction, and finally *the good, with great glory to him, of the final liberation and peace of
Italy*. Nor did we pass over the great work Your Majesty had done for his person, state, house and succession *with
the [Pope], and the almost firm hope he held of bringing His Holiness entirely to the agreement and conditions* that
were being discussed and that His Excellency could wish for. All of this, at his wish, was told him at length.

Sire, although in that discussion, which was very long, His Excellency answered *on the matter of the kingdom* in such
a way that we could have written then what will now be said, nonetheless, since he concluded that so great a matter
needed time for more thought, we decided to wait for his final answer. Meanwhile we went again to Lord Don Ercole and
made him understand that *now was the time to exert himself in Your Majesty's service*, and in what: in matters *of
honour, profit, security and exaltation of the name, house and state of Ferrara*, which from now on *could be called
his*. He promised us wholeheartedly, and we believe he has given all his effort. Not content with this, we advised
that *I, Sormano, should go alone, as I did, to the said Lord Duke, to show him as a loving servant the error His
Excellency would make if he did not accept what is proposed*. The next day we were sent for by His Excellency, and
after many reasons that seemed to us more than sufficient, not only to free him of the *suspicion, as he alleged, of
the [Pope] and of the [?Emperor]*, naturally, as he said and still says, *his enemies*, but also

### f. 121v

*to dispose him to accept with good heart what Your Majesty* was offering him, he finally thanked Your Majesty for
the great and honourable offer it had pleased you to make, and resolutely concluded *that for nothing in the world
would he accept the kingdom for himself, still less undertake the enterprise in his own name*. His speech was such
that I, Sormano, felt almost *certain of what I had always suspected*: that His Excellency, although *he had given
ear and answered in the way I wrote to* Your Majesty, truly *had no mind to take up the captaincy*, which I had been
asking of him for many days. He did say that he would not only be content but begged Your Majesty with all insistence
*to negotiate between the [Pope] and himself the settlement and agreement*, and as to that he said he would *accept
whatever* Your Majesty *did*, trusting that you would not compel him to anything but what was fitting and reasonable.

Sire, from our great wish that the [Duke] *should meet Your Majesty's expectation*, and wanting to leave no effort or
office undone and nothing untried, despite that answer we went again to the aforesaid Lord Don Ercole, who truly
wishes to serve Your Majesty, and on *our great discontent* we showed him that *such an answer brought him little
profit*. At this *he, lamenting with us the nature of his father's answer, showed himself little satisfied*, and we
urged him to go again to His Excellency and work so that *he would not lose this opportunity, which time and Your
Majesty's liberality were offering him, as fine as any other that could ever be offered him, both to secure his
affairs and to exalt, with little and small danger and less expense, his house and succession*, and *to free Italy
and to satisfy* Your Majesty. Besides this we advised that *I, Sormano*, should once again *find myself, as I did,
alone with the said [Duke]* to warn him again of what seemed to me fitting for this business. Having debated the case
with him at length, His Excellency in conclusion confirmed himself *in the answer written above, and so, ill
satisfied*, I returned. Shortly after, Lord Don Ercole came to us and confirmed that *the Lord Duke would in no way
take up the kingdom for himself; [?as for] the captaincy*, despite what had been said and answered in the first
discussion and afterwards, he said that His Excellency persisted *in the form and manner of the answer given these
days to me, Sormano, which*, as said, I wrote in exactly that manner, *as irresolute and unclear* as it was given me.
As for *making an agreement and settling his affairs with the [Pope]*, he confirmed what is said above.

### f. 122r

Sire, from these discussions with this Lord Duke it seems one can conclude *that no great reliance should be placed
on his really being willing to enter into or accept the captaincy, on which all the difficulties are put forward from
His Excellency's side concerning provisions, circumstances and their dependencies*; and in our judgement, *taking the
matter together with its provisions and dependencies so difficult, it seems that it is the difficulties, and not the
will, that keep him from accepting; and this perhaps vain opinion of ours we did not think fit to keep from Your
Majesty*. We will not fail to press His Excellency continually, and if we can obtain anything else from him it will be
reported.

I, Joachim, will wait here for the Lord Marshal Trivulzio, and we think that His Lordship, much esteemed by the said
Lord Duke, will exert himself for this cause, which we are sure he will gladly do for the good of this business and
Your Majesty's service. Ferrara, 23 February 1529.
