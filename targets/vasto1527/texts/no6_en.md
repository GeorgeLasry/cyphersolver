# BnF fr. 3022 no. 6, the Marquis del Vasto to Charles V, [Rome, early December 1527]: English translation of the cipher runs

… […] […] I believe this is more likely, because, according to the [dnh], they will not follow it in coming so far
forward, since there is no army of theirs remaining in Lombardy strong enough to resist a large band of Germans that the
King of Hungary could, by Your Majesty's order, send down into Italy. And if that happened, and they found themselves so
far advanced, I believe they would find it hard to go back, and harder still to stay and [bnq], and in the end they could
only expect great loss; and for this reason I think they will want first to finish the business of Lombardy rather than
take another road.

And … is to win [rar] on the way and put it under Your Majesty's command, which in these times is […] that one must most
work to finish, since it will suffice to sustain the army, which is the most important thing, and the one Your Majesty
must most desire. The Duke of Urbino […] another army is at Narni and Todi, lands of the Patrimony; they are few men. And
my opinion is, first, that before the army goes out Siena should be secured, which is very devoted to Your Majesty, and
in this enterprise it will serve […] thousand [cny], for in truth we hold them so as to keep it safe. And when the army
goes out it will go […] road, because victuals will not be lacking, and it will strike fear into [car] and give occasion
to these enemies whom we have so near to make them make [bny] a move; and if they see that we are going against Florence,
they believe they will go to relieve it, which they can do; and if they gather them up, which might not be, the army
can go towards Perugia, which will be found unprovided, because they have not the men to defend Florence and leave the
said Perugia garrisoned, which is a very rich and very populous land of the Patrimony, and the chief of […] country,
which will certainly be taken. Finally, leaving it provided, one can go to the state of Urbino and take it, in which
little resistance will be found, and pass into the Marche, where victuals cannot be lacking.

And besides, it will be possible to see what the army in Lombardy does: if it marches, in [cnt] part one can expect […]
more advantage, because it is outside the Kingdom and in a part that is guarded as if the army were inside […]; and if it
persists in the business of Lombardy, one can more easily go to relieve it [beb], because the road is better and more
abundant in victuals. Finally, if the Swiss do not take refuge, [rar] it will be possible to make an agreement […], and
besides they would pass into Lombardy. And in all this I shall not fail to tell Your Majesty that the army cannot in
future be sustained in this way, and that Your Majesty should think, as I have written in other letters, either of some
means of money or of making war on them in the way it ought to be made, putting them in such straits that they ask for
it; and let Your Majesty believe that, with so many wants as there are in this army and so many miseries, it is
impossible for Your Majesty's service to be done as we your faithful vassals desire, and …
