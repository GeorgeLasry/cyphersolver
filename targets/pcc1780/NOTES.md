# Papers of the Continental Congress, Miscellaneous Papers, cipher pieces 1780-1782 (catalogue 631) — NOTES

Status: no write-up

**Verdict: closed. Every "communication in cipher" in the volume has its plaintext, either on the page or in
print.** Session 2026-10-27. The sweep entry ("keys ... and communications in cipher"; which pieces are decoded
"not given") is answered by the Fold3 item list of PCC item 59 vol. 1 and by viewing the images. The cipher pieces
are not intercepts. They are the Secretary's and the Foreign Affairs office's own **encipherment worksheets and
file copies**, with the English written under or beside each number group, and the texts are printed (Journals of
Congress, *Papers of Benjamin Franklin*, *John Jay Papers*). Nothing is left to decipher. One textual variant
against the Franklin edition is recorded below.

Images: NARA catalog, PCC item 59 vol. 1 (naId 475671573, 422 images, from M247 roll 72 via Fold3). The image
number is the 0-based index into the record's `digitalObjects` list; files are
`https://catalog.archives.gov/medialz/partners/dc-metro/rg-360/M247/pcc_172591_0001/<172593+index>.jp2`.

The Livingston worksheet and the 1782 resolves use one and the same numbered code (19 = the, 141 = be,
379 = to, 495 = will, 606 = of, 597 = France in the Livingston sheet; 19, 379, 606 the same in the resolves).
It is the Secretary for Foreign Affairs' code that the Franklin editors call Cipher No. 4. The 1780 Thomson
note uses a different system (a dictionary code).

## The key sheets in the same volume

Images 95-99, 104, 107-115, 122 (PCC pp. ~117-146). These are key material, not messages:

- 95: "Cipher with keywords" (naId 631635424, pp. 141-142 in the NARA description). Lovell's column cipher.
- 96-97: Dumas's cipher; docket "Dumas Cyphering used by Dr Franklin and the Comee of Secret Correspondence".
- 98-99: "Jay cyphers propos'd": a note (p. 121), "finding it extremely difficult to send you a cypher free from
  inspection", altering the existing cipher by advancing every number one unit through the first page, and adding
  Washington 640, Green 641, Morris 642 and syllables be 600 ... me 652.
- 104: a column-sequence sheet ("This Column is given to shew the Sequence of the Letters ...").
- 107-108: Dana's cipher: Lovell-type three-column alphabet with a name list (Washington 40, U. States 20,
  Congress 26, ... Franklin 41, Jay 43 ..., Great Britain 50, Russia 51, Court 52).
- 109-115: unlabelled cipher sheets; Franklin's cipher; a table of alphabets headed Adams, H. Laurens, Franklin,
  Dalgey(?) and Jay, docketed "Cyphers: Adams, Laurens, Franklin, Dalgey, Jay".
- 122: Dumas's cipher, printed copy.
- Separately catalogued: "Ciphers Belonging to the Various American Peace Commissioners" (naId 2450021).

These keys are the ones the editions describe (Weber, *United States Diplomatic Codes and Ciphers 1775-1938*,
covers the Lovell, Dumas and Thomson systems). Nothing here is undeciphered.

## Variant against the Franklin edition

In the Livingston worksheet, PCC p. 171 (image 134), the encoded sentence reads, group by group:
"is less than one Month's continuance of the War will cost France" (`149 397 [struck] 194 338 / 190 19
250.50.416.631 606 19 658 / 495 456.250 597`). *Papers of Benjamin Franklin* 36 prints "is less than one years
continuance of the war will cost France", and its note 8 says the passage was encoded. Either the sent letters (or
the copy the editors used) changed "Month's" to "years", or the edition misreads. Open for anyone with the HSP
duplicate: decode group 190 there. Not decided here.

## Bearing on other catalogue entries

- Catalogue 636 (Livingston → Jay, instructions "in a modified Thomson code"): the Thomson-Jay dictionary code of
  1780 (image 100 here) and Jay's proposed alteration (images 98-99, "advance your cyphers one unit") are the
  likely parents of that modification. Worth looking at before attacking 636.
- Catalogue 485 (Dana → Congress, St Petersburg): Dana's key sheet is images 107-108 here (names numbered 20-52).
