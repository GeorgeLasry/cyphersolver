# Additional key evidence, 28 October 2026

9 January 1865: THE NAVY OUR PRIDE (THENAVYOURPRIDE), ordinary Vigenere,
A=0, P=(C-K) mod 26. Start at key offset 0; advance over cipher letters
only, including across clear prose. Hyphens, word breaks, and clear prose
do not consume key letters.

Recovered by matching the manuscript against the printed plaintext in OR
I/45/2 p. 772. It was NOT obtained by a ciphertext-only attack. The key is
absent from the three-key candidate set in the original target notes.
The first 39 letters contain the candidate key; on the remaining 55 letters it
predicts 51 raw candidate letters without alteration. This partition was checked
retrospectively after key/transcription refinement, not as an independent blind test. All eight discrepancies
across the whole message are explicitly recorded; the corrected stream is
re-enciphered from the printed plaintext and must not replace the diplomatic
transcription silently.

The historically related phrase OUR NAVY OUR PRIDE is documented for the
Chisolm/Beauregard cipher apparatus in Richard Rabinowitz, The Civil War in
50 Objects, object 20. It is a DIFFERENT phrase. Our evidence for THE NAVY
OUR PRIDE is the repeated C-P alignment here; no contemporary key sheet
bearing this exact phrase was located. NYHS MS 670.5 holds Chisolm's apparatus:
https://findingaids.library.nyu.edu/nyhs/ms670_5_alexander_robert_chisolm/all/
Do not cite that finding aid as direct evidence of this exact key string.

18 January 1865: COMPLETE VICTORY (COMPLETEVICTORY), same arithmetic and
running convention, supplies substantial plaintext. Several manuscript
letters remain uncertain. See the provisional reading, not a complete claim.

## Dated local change order found in the third session

NARA vol. 196 frame 0055, p.105 has two 9 March 1865 orders to Wirt Adams
and D.W. Adams. Both say to use COME RETRIBUTION "after this date" and are
themselves encrypted with COMPLETE VICTORY. The Wirt Adams copy is fully
annotated in pencil with a decipherment, including the new key phrase;
the annotator and date are unknown. See `KEY-CHANGE-1865-03-09.md` for the
full evidence, transcriptions, distinct-recipient coverage and limits.
Do not turn this into a department-wide or Confederacy-wide first-use date.
