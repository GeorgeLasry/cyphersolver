# Taylor to Maury, Meridian, 12 March 1865: the telegram as sentences

Words that were in cipher are in capitals. The telegram is in English, so no translation is given. Abbreviations
are written out; the decryption against the transcription is in v196-0061_decryption.txt.

## Reading

Major General D. H. Maury, Mobile, Alabama.

Telegraph in cipher what CAVALRY YOU HAVE ON EACH SIDE OF BAY, its STRENGTH AND NAME OF COMMANDER. Direct CAVALRY SO
KEEP CLOSE as possible TO ENEMY, and to arrange to KEEP IN CONSTANT TELEGRAPHIC COMMUNICATION WITH General D. W.
Adams and with these headquarters. Also indicate in cipher [?west] POINT you have decided upon OUTSIDE THROUGH WHICH
TO COMMUNICATE WITH these headquarters, SO COURIER LINE may be established. Answer.

By order of Lieutenant General Taylor, W. F. Bullock Jr., Assistant Adjutant General.

## Notes

- [?west]: the cipher gives WEST once one glyph is corrected (IIRX read, IIJX needed). "What point" would fit the
  sense better, but WHAT does not fit the cipher letters. Taylor had just come back from West Point, Mississippi
  (his telegram to S. D. Lee on the facing page, p. 116), so the clerk may have enciphered the wrong word. The hole
  marks this doubt.
- "Direct CAVALRY SO KEEP CLOSE as possible": the cipher gives SO here; the sense is "so as to keep as close as
  possible".
