# R9955, Charles V to the Empress Isabella, Regensburg, 30 April 1532 (English)

## Translation

Most serene, most high and most mighty Empress and Queen, my very dear and much beloved wife. {The needs and expenses that arise for me here are so great and so pressing that, since the hundred thousand ducats provided for my departure from here are not enough to meet them, and seeing those that you also have there, and there being no other way to do it,} I have ordered a contract to be made here with Messer Ansaldo de Grimaldo, Genoese, by which he has bound himself to help me and lend me one hundred thousand escudos, half at the end of May next of this year and the other half at the end of June following. For these I am to have him paid, in the same months of the coming year 1533, one hundred thousand doblas, half in each of them; and for this I have ordered my bills of exchange to be given him on Alonso de Baeza, so that he pays them, and it has been agreed that Baeza will accept them at once in order to pay them at those terms.

Besides this, as security, I have ordered our warrant (alvalá) to be given to the said Ansaldo so that the contadores mayores give him at once a privilege for the amount of redeemable juro that the hundred thousand doblas come to, charged on the revenues of those our kingdoms, so that if he is not paid at those terms he shall enjoy the juro from then on for whatever part of it has not been paid him, as you will see in more detail from the copy of three articles of the contract which goes with this, initialled by the Comendador Mayor of León, my secretary. Also, for what he serves me in this and has served me before in other matters, and for the interest on the money, it was agreed that he should be granted a certain life grant, which I have made him in the Kingdom of Naples {so as not to load everything onto what is there, as we would have wished, if it could have been done, for the principal. I know well the needs there are in those kingdoms and the poor means and difficulties of providing what is needed; but those that arise for us here are so pressing that it is necessary to look for means of getting out of them in whatever way it can be done, and for this reason the said contract has been made; and because without this help, even though the other hundred thousand ducats have been provided for our departure from here, it would be impossible to fulfil what in no way could be avoided.}

I affectionately beg you, Madam, to order that the said Alonso de Baeza accept our said bills of exchange as soon as they are presented to him on behalf of the said Ansaldo de Grimaldo, and likewise that the privilege for the said juro be given and dispatched to him at once, all in accordance with what is settled in the said articles, so that they are fully kept with him, so that he holds it as security for payment as has been said; for in the meantime, between now and the terms at which he is to be paid, there will be money from the servicio or from something else with which he can be paid, and then, payment being made, the privilege given him will be torn up. {And because the fulfilment of this matters to us more than can be said, and it is fitting that there be no failure in it, I once again commend to you that you order it fulfilled at once.}

Most serene, most high and most mighty Empress and Queen, my very dear and much beloved wife, may the Most Holy Trinity keep and commend you in its special care. From Regensburg, 30 April 1532. I the King. Cobos.

Address: To the most serene, most high and most mighty Empress and Queen, my very dear and much beloved wife. Docket: Regensburg 1532, to Her Majesty, [?Dilenga], about the hundred thousand doblas.
