## Translation

Baron de Mercy, my Sumiller de Cortina: I wish to have a resolution of the negotiation on which you went, because it
is fitting to know what can be expected from it with regard to the operations that must be known: 24 ⟨these armies
being in the field⟩; and so I shall be glad if you tell me what you have learned on this subject ⟨of the Duchesse de
Chevreuse and of <Saint-Ibal>⟩. And because any hour of delay in the present juncture is of the greatest harm, you
should try to obtain a categorical answer on the matter, and ⟨that <Saint-Ibal> come with you so that he may inform us
of it and we may know⟩ on good grounds what we can promise ourselves. And in case it is not ready, and it is necessary
to wait some time to be able to say definitely what footing this negotiation has and how far it can be trusted, ⟨you
will go on to Cleves to see the Elector of Brandenburg and Konrad von Burgsdorff, his chief chamberlain, for whom
letters of credence are sent you, which go with this, and you will propose to them whether it would be permitted that
three thousand men of infantry be raised in that country, in two or three regiments, and on what conditions; and to the
chief chamberl[ain] whether he will be willing to take charge of it and have it go through his [ha]nd; the dire ?t
non, and if iu ?[?]a will be more fitting to [?] as many people again of [?] that has raised, pay[in]g it, and in its
place rai[s]e another⟩; and in case it seems to him that this matter should not go through his hand, you will ask him
to what person it could be entrusted, [so that] one could proceed in it without losing time ⟨and obtain the fruit of
having [so mu]ch people⟩. In everything I charge you with speed, because any delay there may be is extremely harmful;
and from your zeal I trust [you will] atten… …to it with the care that is fitting. I charge you. Barneton, the sixth of
June 1648.
