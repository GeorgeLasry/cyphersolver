# Galeazzo Visconti to Francis I, camp at the pieve de Locate, 15 June 1529 (BnF fr. 3096 f. 91r): English translation

*Sire: Knowing* the fear the Venetians have of the peace, chiefly so as not to be bound against the Turk, which they will
never do, [rr] yesterday, with the Duke of Urbino and the provveditore, did the office of your good servant in assuring
them that Your Majesty will never abandon their Signoria; and as much he said of the Duke of Milan, who will never, [with]
great pain, be reassured, even with the going of [48]. At least the two lords, I promise you, remained well satisfied(?).
[rr] I will say indeed that I carry on dealings on my own to good purpose, from which I shall never cease as long as I
live; nor do I wish to keep silent that it was [......](?) [cherì](?) seemed good in council, that if it had been
concluded to try to s[ρ]force Milan. My opinion is that it will(?) be done for them through their own failing, raising
difficulties in the field at all hours, for the [..] which last, that we are those who speak first. If it is not done,
it will not go there: done se[Λ?] [48] ne(?) believes how(?) [I shall hold](?) it will do, and the whole thing, Sire, is
to no good end.

*Sire, I beg Your Majesty ... Given in camp at the pieve de Locate, the 15th of June 1529. Your humble and obedient
servant and subject Galeazo Vesconte.*
