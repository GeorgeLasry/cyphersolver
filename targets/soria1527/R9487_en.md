# R9487, Lope de Soria to Charles V, Mirandola, 13 December 1527: English

Translation of `R9487_reading.md`, same holes.

## Translation

A. I at once had the three thousand ducats paid to the lord of Monaco that Your Majesty ordered by your letter, which
had remained in the merchants' hands, [...] another thousand, notwithstanding that I have given them receipts for the
whole sum of the exchanges, which was done for good reasons. Your Majesty will order what I am to do about them, and
about the jewels that were Bourbon's, which were pawned at Genoa.

B. Yesterday I received a letter from Andrea de Burgo, written at Ferrara the day before [...], and he tells me that the
hostages who [?were] [?held] at Rome in [soc] of the [...] had got loose and fled to Narni; if that is true, it would be
[xad] of [?inconvenience]. [...] it is no wonder at any disorder that has [pur] in the said [yal]; [pib] the troops are
without obedience and with some divisions [yod] [ync] [...] as befits his service. There had arrived the [?prince]
[?of Orange] [...], the [?marquis] del Vasto, and he says that the [...] were coming with the [yal].

C. The said dukes and marquis say that [?served] [...] [...], but that well [...] [...] that, but they [?should] not use
[?disrespect] in [...] against [par] and make a marriage with France [...] the Emperor they will be with Your Majesty so
as to come to no harm, for they are people who know how to sail with every wind.

All the enemy have withdrawn from near Milan, so that Antonio de Leyva is now master of the field and rides where he
will; but inside Milan there are very few victuals, and the country outside is [...] and destroyed, so that they need
provision to be made to supply the city.
