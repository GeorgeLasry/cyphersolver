Two days after the departure of the last ordinary [post], with which I informed Your Eminence of the
resolution taken in the Parlement against Mazarin, the arrêt was printed; I send it to Your Eminence with
the present letter. It contains the death penalty and the bounty of one hundred and fifty thousand livres
placed on his head. It declares him [2149: to have?] incurred them by violating the prohibitions issued by
[2517: His Majesty?] the King against his returning to [2416: France?]. It is grounded in the earlier
arrêts, which stipulated that, if he contravened them, he would fall into the penalty of lèse-majesté.
They claim he is guilty of this crime [2148: since?] he was lodged at Sedan on the twenty-[2698: ?] of
last month. [They also claim] that the Parlement is the competent judge of the crime of lèse-majesté of
any person whatsoever, [2400: even if?] constituted in the highest rank.
And I told those who spoke to me, in plain conversation, that neither the Parlement nor any temporal
[2560: power?] has this faculty, which is entirely reserved to the Supreme Pontiff. And [I told them] that
I hold it for certain that the King will annul it.

Historical check
----------------
The Parlement of Paris's arrêt of 29 December 1651 set a price of 150,000 livres on Mazarin's head and
declared him guilty of lèse-majesté for re-entering France against the earlier arrêts (February and
September 1651). Mazarin re-entered France at the turn of the year 1651/52 with troops raised in Germany,
by way of Sedan. Gaston d'Orléans ("Monsieur") and Condé ("il Principe") were then allied against him.
This historical setting is consistent with the proposed reading. It does not independently establish the
unresolved date or the personal names.
