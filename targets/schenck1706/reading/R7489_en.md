[166]'s recovery has taken a great sorrow from [187]; he asks to offer his heartfelt congratulations on it. In the fief affair [?two] royal autograph letters reached His Imperial Majesty today, and everything is now to be observed in the best way, on which a most gracious decree was again issued today, whose delay [190] once more took upon himself; I do not know to what end he pressed it. What came in from Count Lagnasco by today's post is sent herewith.

{The} [562] [181] [187] has great desire for many very important reasons. The particulars of the latest action at Montechiaro have now also arrived: 6,000 Germans held a brave stand against 25,000 French for several hours and cut down 3 brigadiers, 400 officers and over 600 common soldiers of the French, but in the end had to leave even the field and the field-pieces with some baggage. The blame falls on Count [?Reventau], who has now become a major general; whether he will […] at the [?inquiry] [is] very [?doubtful].

[…] and Hungary want to keep the prospects of peace favourable, and many hope, since His Imperial Majesty has allowed Rákóczi to send a courier to his wife, that this will not be without a happy effect. From Spain the news runs so differently that one hardly knows what to believe awaiting […] the arrival of a courier. Europe has surely never been in a greater upheaval than now. Denmark [?is said to] have fallen into misunderstanding with Sweden over the [?Holstein] affair; but […] holds maxims as to stake its fortune on the hazard of war.

When {Baron Schenck} gave the commission for the books [131] wanted, [187] praises it and [?asks for] news, since he was asked about it. Another thing is impossible for me because of today's business in Vienna, except to add that the patents of the ban against the brothers, the Electors of Cologne and Bavaria, have been very strictly forbidden to be sold; but in the end, [?although] […] an hour after […], I handed one at the post to Count Wackerbarth, and have already received a copy, which a confidant wanted to enclose; [?it was] brought in by a strange chance, […] [?so] […] {King Augustus} does not yet [?have].

Royal […] will be, but {Baron Schenck} hopes […], be it at {King Augustus}'s pleasure. I remain […] most obedient Sidon. Vienna, 8 May 1706.
