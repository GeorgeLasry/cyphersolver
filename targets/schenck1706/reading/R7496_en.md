## Translation

The enclosure will show [132]'s distress at the unexpected blow. It also seems as if [181] were dismayed by it, since they may not promise the best from [193]. "Let it not touch him," said [187]. One must let it go. Meanwhile [132] might find himself somewhat consoled if he received an {order} {from the} [307] only, and first, at once.

The happy news grows daily and shows that this progress comes from divine direction. It is said that {the Imperial court} has itself recognised this and therefore resolved to lead a life <e.> removed from vanities. What this means is not hidden from {Baron Schenck}'s penetration.

As to the reflections lately opened by [187] on the advantages that {King Augustus} intends to make {at} <a.> [809], it would chiefly be worth considering that [180] should in good time, even if this negotiation were to go forward only within a year, choose a person whom he thought fit to use for it, and entrust it to him under his own high hand, but under the seal of the most sacred silence, and at the same time order him to think for some hours daily on what must be done and to note what would have to be observed throughout in so very important an affair. The French indeed were accustomed, in their more flourishing state, to observe the chance too, which [187] could verify from various memoirs. The late Great Elector of Brandenburg also happily provided for such things; for when such matters are broached without mature preparation, […].

[?The] hurrying post does not allow more this time than that, with due recommendation to [166], I remain [?for life].

Close: Monsieur, Vienna, 16 June 1706, your very humble and very obedient servant, Sidon.
