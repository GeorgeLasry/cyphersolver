## Translation

The post again arrived very late today because of the continuing snow and rain. Nevertheless [181] has presented everything that you reported concerning the […] […]; but they already had a copy of it. Nothing troubles [187] more than that the misunderstanding between [181] and [190] does not lessen but grows, and [187] hardly knows, hardly believes himself able, to find any temperament or remedy for it, especially as it seems that {King Augustus} too no longer has any particular confidence in [190]. Meanwhile some […] proofs are showing themselves concerning the […] […] <d> [314] […]mann {with} {the Emperor}. No […] […] positis ponendis.

A newspaper is going about saying that General Rehnskiöld has proposed to the King of Sweden that, as a token of magnanimity, he should set free for the royal prince one regiment and each […] taken prisoner, have it brought to strength from the remaining prisoners and [make] it into a life guard for him […], but should stipulate that it never serve against the King of Sweden [?again]. What there is in this [?regiment], and with what intentions it is done, time will give the best [?solution] of it.

[181] is much surprised that nothing has come in from [186] for some time, on which [187] remonstrated: it is [186]'s way that when he owes an answer, even to his own orders, he stops writing [?further]. But [181] thought that [186] knew of his overwhelming occupations, and that accordingly he should make a distinction with him over other correspondences. I consider it […] that [186] should remit something of the rigour of his otherwise not unjust principle. It is certain that the [?signing] of the Landgrave of [?Hessen]berg takes much of his time, and public business and His Royal Majesty's service still more; and besides, when some […] appears, which one forestalls beforehand as far as possible, and the strength thereby enjoyed cannot be improved.

The correspondence with {Baron Schenck} can now be arranged […] better. The letters by the courier have all come in, and [181] has likewise sent one to {King Augustus} in these days, for whose return [181] waits with great longing. [187] doubts whether [181]'s return can be hoped for before the holidays, and he dare not venture to make further remonstrances, since [187] is persuaded that whoever now advises him against it is his enemy, until he has an answer from {King Augustus} to his representation.

## Close
