[The rest of f. 304v, on the tumults, submissions and an affirmative and negative answer, is read only in part.]

The happy relief of Barcelona is confirmed, but some circumstances vary: whether the Count of Toulouse was beaten at sea, and 2) whether the Duke of Anjou withdrew to Roussillon. The first and the last are made doubtful, [it being said] that Toulouse, by order of the French court, took himself to safety in the harbour of Toulon without hindrance from the English; but that the Duke of Anjou found no way open for his retreat and was so shut in that a retreat would be impossible for him. At the French court there is said therefore to be great dismay, especially since Mons. Berwick's army has mostly surrendered to Mylord Galloway […]. Mylord Galloway is said to have entered Madrid with great solemnity, everything acclaimed for King Charles on 24 May, and the Duke of Anjou to have fled from there altogether.

In Hungary the hope of peace [?grows], for next Monday His Grace the Bishop of Olmütz with Count Wratislaw, His Excellency, will go to Pressburg, where the congress is to be. [The rest, on a tumult at Vienna, Villars's weakened army on the Rhine after Marsin's march to Spain, a liberation the Emperor has at heart, General von Gross's retreat to Liegnitz, and [166]'s report not yet presented, is read only in part.]

Close: Monsieur, your very humble and very obedient Sidon. Vienna, 29 May 1706.
