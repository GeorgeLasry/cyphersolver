## Translation

The campaign looks very bad and very contrary for the just party, both in Italy and on the Upper Rhine. You will no doubt already know that Landau has been invested with 80 battalions and 140 squadrons; and since, out of a security so fatal to the Germans and now almost incorrigible, it is duly provided neither with a needful garrison nor with the other indispensable requisites, and the brave General Count Thüngen is not in it either, its loss would hardly be in doubt any more, since Haguenau and Drusenheim are lost at the same time. The good Count Wackerbarth is therefore much dismayed, since the best part of his regiment lies in Haguenau and the poor men would be made prisoners of war at its capture.

From Spain much good hope is still given that no misfortune need be feared for Barcelona, since it is believed that the French will be forced to raise the siege very soon through the great weakness of the English and Dutch. And some claim to have news that Sir John Leake has entirely beaten back the Count of Toulouse from Barcelona.
