# R4232, Georges de Selve to Francis I, Venice, 5 July 1536 (BnF fr. 3045 ff. 42-49): English translation of the cipher passages

## f. 43v – f. 44r: the Emperor's design on Marseille

[…] [the town was] in very great danger, although the number of the besiegers was beyond comparison smaller than the
one he means to bring there. And since the said town is takeable, as he reckons, being a key of your kingdom as
important as it is, it seems to him that, seeing it in danger, you will want to draw near with your forces to relieve
it. [Tourlucan(?), as his(?)] going to Marseille will give him the occasion of the battle [that he desires, to get out
of this country with which he finds himself burdened; or else your forces will keep him] far off, without relieving the
said town, so that he will get the better of it and conquer it, [which is indeed the thing he most desires, for it would
make him] master of Provence, and there would not remain much for him to gain to have free passage by land from Italy
to Spain, an advantage so great for him that he could not buy it too dear; and it would take from you the navigation of
the Mediterranean Sea, which would secure him Genoa, Naples and Sicily.

[And besides the aforesaid reasons that] invite him to the said enterprise of Marseille, it seems to him that, your fleet
being in the harbour of the said town, it cannot escape him, as he has a more powerful fleet which will be enough to
keep it from coming out. [And the said] harbour being so exposed to the battery of those who will be posted on the side
of the hill of La Garde, that there will be no vessel that could save itself inside it.

[… to the Sieur] Monsieur de ⟨name code, glossed "Rang…": Guido Rangone⟩ [fugitives(?) …]

## f. 44v: the galleys

[Sire, the said Sieur] de Vaulx is still of the opinion that for the safety of your galleys [… two clear lines …] to put
your fleet out and send it towards Naples. [Which … alone could] preserve it for you, putting on board a sufficient
number of men of war.

## f. 45r: the delay; La Forest; the breach

[…] the delay has not been a cause in your affairs; the rumour that has been made over there, which in my opinion may be
quieted. [… to the said] sieur(?) de la Forest of the [N]th of May [… will be done …] to the express breach of the war
between you and the Emperor, [… breach of war …]

## f. 48r: the Pope and the tenths

[…] the Pope had assured him that the Emperor would have no tenths from him for this year, whatever landing and fleet
Barbarossa may make.
