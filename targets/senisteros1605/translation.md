# English translation of the cipher passages (NA 3.01.14 inv. 2442)

Translated from `decoded_A.txt` (pp. 1–4L) and `reading_B.md` (pp. 4R–10R). Clear passages are summarised in
[brackets]. `[?]` marks words not read.

## p01

[With great pleasure I received Your Lordship's letter of 22 October, glad you are well and that the visitation
and journeys are over; God grant it ends soon, as I wish and hope.] The secretary Francisco Gonçalez has given me
very good hopes of this. Since the Sigüenza business came out I have not been able to find the letter to send to
Your Lordship with the very words, which are particular, and more for his character; for being [?] I tore it up.
From which I have gathered that when the see of Sigüenza was filled, the Duke [of Lerma] would say something
about this, discussing Your Lordship and the consultations. God guide it as may best serve Him and suit us.

## p02R

[...] Her favour can be of importance, and I have told her that I wish to see Your Lordship here and she wishes it
greatly, and she laments much to see the times that run, and she sent me [?] about Sigüenza, because she had
given me very good hopes; and many times they cannot, and other times they dare not speak to the Duke about it,
because all have their own claims and go about displeased, and his own [relatives] many times dare not say
anything to him; and so there is nothing for it but patience, and to do what we can as the times allow.

## p04L

[...] I owe the acknowledgement that is due. Afterwards I went to visit Agustín Mexía, the regent's
brother-in-law, and gave him the letters and told him what had been done and how the Cardinal had shown me
favour; and when he heard it he went to the devil, saying the canonry was to go to Doctor Cetina or to Doctor
Sosa, his relative, and that he did not know how these powers had come into my hands, making great show of it.
I softened him as best I could, and fearing that he would undo it or give me some trouble, I would not speak to
him or give him the letters until my business was done, as it was; and if it had not been so well contrived, no
doubt he would have [done] some harm.

## p04R

And it is that the first time I went to Toledo, Doctor García asked me whether I brought a letter from Your
Lordship for the Cardinal, and I answered that it had not arrived. On the other side Horacio Doria asked me the
same and I gave the same answer, and he told me it would matter a great deal that it should arrive, because,
speaking to me as a friend and in secret, he did not fear [?] — unless the Cardinal and Garai should hesitate
whether it was right, having made the inquiry into his nephew, and so as to give nothing to talk about; and for
the same reason, since Doctor Capata had been rector that year and some had begun to talk when he sought the
curacy [...], nobody but a middling man [was chosen] to stop the talk; but that reason was secret and for me
alone. So it mattered a great deal that Your Lordship's letter should arrive, so that it would be understood
that it was done for Your Lordship and not for [?]. And I found myself so pressed that, thinking it would please
Your Lordship, I made a signature of Your Lordship's on a sheet of paper as best I could, and had a trusted
servant of mine write the substance of the letter, saying that Your Lordship had left me some signatures in
blank

## p05

I took care that the letter should be in Your Lordship's style, and it only said, to remove every objection, that
the regent, thinking he could not freely come to reside in his canonry, had decided to resign it into the hands
of His Most Illustrious Lordship [...]; and that Your Lordship begged him that, as I was there to serve him in it,
he would do me the favour, which Your Lordship would value as the greatest. [Then came my brief from Rome to hold
the rectorship with the canonry ... the visitors of the college, the abbot and the vice-chancellor are the
electors ... I asked Francisco Gonçalez for two letters to the abbot and one to the vice-chancellor, which he
wrote at once and very pressingly.] And so, seeing myself so well placed at this time, it seemed to me that I
should make another letter in Your Lordship's name for the abbot, saying that I had written to Your Lordship of
the favour he did me, and that on his advice the brief had been sent so that I could hold the canonry with the
rectorship without any disability, and that Your Lordship was most grateful

for it and kissed his hands. These things I have done because they were so fitting on this occasion, and I think
Your Lordship will approve; and if not, forgive me, I beg Your Lordship to pardon me; whoever tells it to Your
Lordship when he could keep silent — Your Lordship will see the truth with which I act and with what desire to
serve Your Lordship and please you in everything. [The Cardinal said he would answer from the road ... the see of
Sigüenza was given to Fr. Mateo de Burgos, and Pamplona, which he held, to don Antonio Venegas of the Supreme
Council of the Inquisition.] And though I do not believe, nor is it to be believed, as Your Lordship says, that
they used bad means such as people say, I much fear that some occasion must have been given for the rumour; and
some, I think, hold that by [?] and good diligence of the minister one can be given whatever seems just, and this
road has been made so wide by those who know little that they [?] suffer.

## p07L

[I wrote to the secretary that ... he and I had in mind] to aim at the abbacy of Lerma, where the Duke is now

## p07R

[On the students:] Chaves, his long gown being over there [and his travelling cloak], and one without it, so it
has been necessary to make him a gown like the others, because he says he made [?] of the cloak, and the rest
[?], and he made a little cassock of black woollen cloth, and he asked me not to write to Your Lordship that I was
making him more than a cassock because it was summer, and that for the cloak I should put up the money until his
mother had money. Then he told me, when it came to counting the money he brought, that he had taken from the bag
forty reales for some boots and other things, and that he would send them to her; and that I should also tell
his mother. These are trifles that have annoyed me a little, though I have not let him see it in any way, for it
is enough that he is the nephew of whom he is, and the good turn the Regent has done us in this matter of the
canonry, that we should do what we can for his affairs. So Your Lordship will see what you order to be done about
recovering from [?] the cost of the cloak and the forty reales; given that he does not know that Your Lordship
knows it, he even asked me not to write it, as he wanted to [do it himself].

## p10R

[...] the man from La Poveda would have arranged it if he had had an answer from Your Lordship on a particular
point I wrote about, which I dare not decide myself: it is that this lady and the Inquisitor Salcedo have for
grandmother a daughter of a cleric and of a woman married to a smith, who is reputed a converso; and since it has
been proved so clearly that the smith was away when the cleric fathered her on the woman, and that the cleric kept
her in his house and raised her as his daughter, they must have made the inquisitor a collegial at Oviedo and then
inquisitor; and since then brothers of gentlemen who have been collegials have married into these people, and now
I am told that don García de Medrano, collegial of Santiago and Salamanca, a native of Soria, of the habit of
Santiago and of the Royal Council, has married an elder brother of his to a sister of this young lady; and an
oidor of the Chancery has married, because she was a rich woman; and with all this, since the smith's wife was a
married woman and so many things about it are said so plainly, I dare do nothing without Your Lordship's opinion.
