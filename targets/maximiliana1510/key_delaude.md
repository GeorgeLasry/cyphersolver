# Paulus de Laude's cipher (Blois, Feb 1512): working key (partial, 27 Oct 2026)

Rebuilt from the contemporary interlinear glosses in 26-4-6 (img 05), 26-4-9 (img 04) and 26-4-10 (img 07).
Crops: `img/small/dl_0605_*`, `dl_0904_*`, `dl_1007_*`.

## System (as observed)

A letter cipher, one sign per letter, word gaps kept. **The writer drops many letters, mostly vowels**, so a
cipher word is often a skeleton of the plain word: `aliquid` = a l q u i d, `dubitandum` = d b t n d m,
`vobis` = v o b s, `suas` = s a s, `generalis` = g e n r l s, `melius` = m l u s, `multas` ends t s.
Short function words are usually written in full (`quod`, `quia`, `ad`, `esse`, `ut`). Several letters have
two or three signs (d, n, r, s, m, t). Word signs: `RO` = et; `93` = Cesar; underlined `CC` = Andreas de
Burgo. The gloss runs as a continuous line over the cipher and drifts right of the cipher it translates;
when a cipher line is short, the glossator put the next line's gloss at the end of the gloss above
(26-4-10 07 ll. 28-29, see `reading_delaude.md`).

Sign shapes are close to one another (several crossed-t / crossed-d forms). Names below are ASCII.
Confidence: **firm** = two or more glossed words; **one** = one glossed word; **tent.** = fits a
skeleton only.

The "Cesar" question: capital C is **m**, not a Cesar sign. `C' FF c U ...` is m n c u . . (a word beginning
m-n), not Cesar; Cesar is always `93` (and once written in clear). The gloss "Cesaris" near `C' FF c U FF O`
on 4-9/04 l.31 stands between that group and a `93`; given C' = m it must belong to the `93`.

Settled: **capital C (`C'`) is m, and `C' FF c ...` is not a Cesar sign.** Glossed `mandatum` = C' FF c ? ꝺ
(4-9/04 l.35, gloss directly above) and the target's `C' FF c U I T` = m n d v i t = *mandavit*. Cesar is
always `93` or written in clear (the target itself has *Cesar* in clear in D3).

Homophone groups now known: a (A+, ‡); d (D+, X, v, c, 5); m (B, MO, C', ꝺ, Gm?); r (4R, ψ, q, ʒ, R~);
t (T, T4, O:); s (S, SO); n (FF, TTT, L); l (W, 2A); i/e (I, E). Vowels are mostly omitted.

**Same cipher?** Every value that could be checked in de Burgo's letters (quod, et, aliam, in, `93`, `C'` = m,
`ʒ` = r, `W` = l, `‡` = a, `δ` = s, `6`/`B` = m) is the same as in de Laude's. No conflicting value was found.
So far the two look like one cipher (de Burgo's system, used by his secretary/agent de Laude). This rests on
about a dozen shared values, not on a full comparison.

**Never found under a gloss** (in 26-4-6 img 05, 26-4-9 imgs 01 and 04, 26-4-10 img 07, 25-2-25 fol. 40v,
26-5-31 img 01 top): `⊙`, `ㅁ` (box), `ω`, `9‡`, the pair `ꝶ̊ f`, the plain stroke `l`. 26-4-10 (other images),
26-4-15, 26-4-24 and 27-1-5 were **not** searched sign by sign in this pass, so "never" applies only to the
pages listed.
