## Notes

* Opening line (227 81 102 312 85 108 131 56 102 124): does not read; perhaps a date or place line, or the
  copyist's heading. Not counted as part of the letter's sense.
* Nulls: 3, 7, 12, 30 and 50 are word spaces ('_') throughout Lasry's own interlinear of R924 (D3091); here they
  fall inside "a sence" (3-a-7-s-e-n-c-e), "lay a" (l-a-y-12-a) and before "God's" (30 50).
* "a sence": 81 = c from this word only (81 is not in R924); "[?true]" (784) is a conjecture from the T-section and
  R924's "if this be all 784, the consideration of".
* "[?who] [?can] [?express]": 116 is BROUGHT in Lasry's key file, but all three R924 contexts read "can"; 513 =
  express from R924's "513-ions you use"; 331 = who is from this sentence only.
* "[?whatsoever]": 334-so-136, with 334 = what from R924 ("had seen 334 might have been") and 136 = ever (E-section).
* "[?perswasion]": the figures give p-e-s-w-as-ion; the r is not written.
* "[?God]s [?blessing]": 548-s b-132-m-l-e-s-s-ing; 548 = God is a conjecture (G-section, before HUMBLE 550), and
  132 and the m inside "blessing" are not explained. Read as the closing blessing with doubt.
* "And [192] you shall be made believe": 192 = let reads the second clause ("let the Queen be iudge"), not this one;
  it is left as a code here.
* Sense: the King commands the Prince, who is with the Queen, not to let himself be persuaded or threatened into
  [coming to England?] before his father sends for him; any summons he has not really sent is to be judged by the
  Queen. This fits the Prince's stay at Paris from July 1646 and the King's captivity with the Scots at Newcastle,
  but the letter carries no date.
