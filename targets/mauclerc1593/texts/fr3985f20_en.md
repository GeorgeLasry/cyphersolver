# Dr Mauclerc to M. de Creil, Rome, Paris 3-4 Aug 1593 (BnF fr. 3985 no. 7, f. 20): English

Translation of `fr3985f20_reading.md` (the contemporary decipherment, f. 7). Same holes; Latin translated.

Sir and master. Since my last, very full letters, as [...] you know, horrible things have happened, by which you will
judge that Mayenne is still [?set against] Guise, as you judged. I fear nothing so much as that what you wish should
not come about. Be that as it may, to begin where I ended: seeing the Spaniards' resolution in favour of the Duke of
Guise, an attempt was made to [?strike] at the authority of the Estates by a [?fine] pretended decree. But Mayenne's
people, finding themselves pressed, opened the way to the advancement of the Duke of Guise. He made a show of wanting
it badly, and that he wished to secure his nephew's establishment, and for that he wanted to see the Spaniards'
powers, thinking to drive them into a corner [...] to ask [...] whether they had no general power. But the Spaniards,
warned that this was the only thing that could stop them, were very glad of [...], and the Legate and then the
Cardinals [...] drew up a special power which they had from their Majesties to marry the Infanta to the Duke of Guise,
to name him king jointly with the said Infanta, which was the happiest news we could hope for the Catholics.
Monsieur de Mayenne made a show of being very glad [...] of this honour done by so great a monarch to a prince of his
own house. But after agreeing the general point, when it came to agreeing the particulars, he asked for things so
impossible, or at least so difficult, that [...] he plays the declared good Frenchman: that notwithstanding the will
of His Holiness, of His Catholic Majesty, the consent of all the Estates and the princes, the prayers and the wish of
all good men, he would have no other King of France. The Spaniards complaining [...], the good men likewise, the
modesty of the prince who is called to this honour shows itself admirable through all these crosses. Mayenne's people
[?swore] by God to beg him to give up this honour to his nephew. Yet his mind remains unmoved. He is beaten, [...]
[?and] begged by all the cardinals, prelates, princes and nobles present. I do not know what to answer. Yet he has
gone back to the stubbornness of the heretics, who can be beaten but not persuaded. He said he would [?rather be
lost] than be forced to what he did not want. And he who is [...] of him stood alone on Mayenne's side. In the end,
the stratagems of some have managed to turn the election of the king into a truce, to the great dishonour of the name
of the Estates General. For this [...] swallowed [...] so loved as the outcome of fine promises [...] not [...] but a
king [...] who has broken faith so often that it is too much to believe him any further. I [?swear] to you, under seal
of secrecy, that Mayenne, Guise and all the princes swore before the Legate on the Gospels never to make peace with
the King of Navarre, and confirmed this oath with a signed bond in the said Legate's hand. The truth is that at the end
of the three months no other device will be found to prevent the naming of a Most Christian King, and that the
Spaniards have named one who will be to the liking of all good men. The Duke of Guise's people told me today that
nothing will be spoilt provided His Holiness keeps what he promised the Duke of Sessa, envoy of His Catholic Majesty.
But the King of Navarre is trying to shake him by the embassy of the prince he is sending. If the doors are shut on
him as on Cardinal Gondi, all will go well. This Legate and the Spaniards of our parts are fully resolved to do all
they can for the man of destiny. If the forces they promise are ready, within three months a King will be created,
even against Mayenne's will. I shall be able to write to you [?again] in two days something else of consequence, if
I can be sure of it. Paris, 3 August.

Mauclerc
