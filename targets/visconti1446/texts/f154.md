This is still a partial reading. Donino ("da P[..]mago[?]"; spelled `ꝯ° pı ∂÷ β/x` three times: L1, L12, L29) has
newly arrived at Milan from Sforza. The Duke has heard and understood all he said on Sforza's behalf. In reply he has
written to **Pietro (Pedro) da Pusterla** and **Antonio da Trezzo**, who are to go to Sforza secretly and show him
certain things for the Duke. Sforza may then use whichever of them he prefers, and they will carry out whatever he
commits to them. Pusterla is to meet someone under the blank-label sign `<` and probably the King's man. Men are to
be sent to and from **King Alfonso** ([Re de Ragona]), and the Duke will act "as you think fit". If Sforza thinks
others should be sent, he is to say so at once.

The Duke is waiting for the answers of the **Pope** (Eugene IV), the King and probably the **Duke of Ferrara**. He
trusts they will condescend, and whatever they give will be "d'avanzo" (extra). He will keep pressing them. His
affairs need not only help but the very quickest provision. He therefore begs Sforza to resolve to come to the effect
and to a conclusion without loss of time, keeping the matter secret until the others answer. He has resolved to put
[..] into Sforza's hands, to be disposed of "al vostro modo".

On the verso Sforza is not to leave without licence but to come to Milan, where the Duke will keep him. Letters are to
go secretly and "senza suspecto alcuno" through **Cotignola** (named four times). Sforza is to be told of everything
done at Milan, and nothing is to be done except by his wish ("noma quelo vui voriti"). Sforza is to name the man he
wants: the Duke will gladly send one of his own men to him secretly, and will send no one until Sforza has asked for
the man he prefers. Dated Milan, 12 December 1446, with the full ducal title.

Context: in December 1446 Sforza was in the Marche, short of money and courted by both the Duke and Venice. The
ducal army had been routed at Casalmaggiore in September, and the Venetians held the Cremonese and the Ghiara d'Adda.
The letter belongs to the run of appeals for Sforza to come north (ff. 130, 136, 158). Cosimo de' Medici, the
Dauphin, Asti and Giovanni del Maino are not named in what is read.
