## What they are

- **Not one recurring group.** No two groups agree in sequence or length (6 to 13 signs; 28 on f. 24), so they are not
  a fixed check word, signature or secretary's mark.
- **Not a date or counter.** Each slip already carries its date in words before the title (and f. 23, f. 102 a
  duplicate date, "duplicata die vigesimo quarto eiusdem" / "duplicata [..] vigesimo [..]"). The groups contain no number
  word in R5323 values; f. 5 and f. 8, written on consecutive days (10/11 Jan), do not show a running count.
- **Not a passage in another key, as far as the evidence goes.** The value rows are not random letters: on ff. 5, 23,
  83 and 72 every sign is one of the vowel homophones (÷ ·‖· ⊢ = e; σ- ♀ -o = a; b ϵ 5̄ = i; β ꭓ y = o; 8 ƀ = u). A
  plaintext in another key would not be drawn only from one key's vowel signs. (Not tested against R5324 sign by sign;
  the shapes are R5323's.)
- **Padding.** The groups are made the same way as the longer fillers after "Dominus" on ff. 30, 31, 36, 37, 64, 136,
  which run through the cipher table in alphabet order: here the clerk ran through the vowel homophones (a e i o u, in
  rough order: f. 5 e a i i e i o o u u; f. 23 a e i o o | a g e i i e; f. 72 e o i i o o o a) or a short stretch of the
  alphabet (f. 8 e a i l l p; f. 94 a e e c b c c o r ? a, roughly ascending). **f. 24's 28-sign group is the full
  alphabet run** (it ends h i l m n o .. t z z), standing where f. 23 has the duplicate note and the title. They fill
  the end of the slip so that the cipher does not stop at a predictable word (the title), and the 18th-c. copies
  (ff. 82-85, 92-93) rightly leave them out.

Conclusion: **padding (filler) signs, drawn from the R5323 table, mostly its vowel homophones**; not text. In the work
files they are now marked `{null:pad}` on ff. 23 and 24 (worker E); on ff. 5, 8, 72, 83 and 94 they are still `[..]`
(so the read shares of those slips are slightly understated: f. 5 10, f. 8 6, f. 72 10, f. 83 11, f. 94 11 tokens).
