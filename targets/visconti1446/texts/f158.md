## Summary

The Duke tells Sforza how his affairs stand. When the ducal men-at-arms were routed ("furono rote": the defeat at
Casalmaggiore, 28 Sept 1446), he sent one Thomase (Tommaso; "da [Roma]", the name sign N) to France to ask for help.
Thomase first went to the Duke of Savoy and got nothing but words; then he went to the Dauphin (Louis, the future
Louis XI), whom he found very well disposed to give good and large help. The Duke had it offered that, in return for
that help, he would put **Asti** ("Ast") in the Dauphin's hands, and he believes Thomase concluded on those terms in
his name. He adds that the French are trying to get [..] and he is almost certain that, as things stand, it will
fall into their hands; he asks Sforza to send his opinion on all this. Cusago, 31 December 1446.

"Thomase da [Roma]" may be the ducal envoy Tommaso (Morroni) da Rieti; the cipher has the name sign N (= Roma in the
key copy), so the reading "da Roma" is kept and the identification left open.
