Of the 205 read tokens of the sample, 202 agree with the clear text; the 3 disagreements are `gto` (qui, omitted by the
copy), `ħ̊` (ff/g in Maffeo/Mageo) and `fa` (mandasiti/mandasti): **202/205 = 98.5%**. Single/double consonant variants
(mile/mille, contentisimi) are not counted.

**Conclusion: the neat copy (f. 92 in Mazzatinti, "copia moderna: manca l'originale") is the text of f. 94**: the
"missing original" is the cipher slip two leaves on, of which the copy is a decipherment. The copy is not
contemporary: same 18th-c.-style italic as ff. 29, 35, 63, 82 (no abbreviations, modern accents, punctuation and
capitals). Its one name slip ("Mageo" for the cipher's Maffeo) and the dropped "qui" show it was made from the
cipher (or from a working decipherment), not from a clear original.

## English summary (from the clear copy, confirmed in the sample)

Now that Gasparro degli Ubaldini has arrived we have your brief about Messer Nicola Maffeo [copy: Mageo], whom you sent
here. We are very glad and content you sent him; of all those outside your household no one's coming could please us
more, for we have always known him as a worthy man devoted to you, wanting nothing but that you and we be one. We
commend your conduct in writing to the College of Cardinals and the governor of the March, in stopping hostilities and
making the truce with the Malatesta lords: you have acted most prudently, we thank you and ask you to persevere. No
time is lost over raising the money. Tell us how we should write to you from now on: by simple briefs as before, or
with superscriptions, subscriptions and seals, and we will do as you say. Milan, 18 March 1447.
