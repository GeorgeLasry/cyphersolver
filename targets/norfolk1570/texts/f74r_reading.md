I have, my oun good lord, {3̲2} your friendli {ꝗ} {ʃ̊} no [?lentle] satisfaction, and fore I will never tak anithing in hand {⊕} some ver good mey come of it to me, anithing except I var sur it var to your veil as for myn; and if I mocht be so to us both, I would be sur al schould not be in denger.

For I have experiance {⊔} that [?favour] that me oune f[…]s […] but no[t], my oun lord, that your {a—} and myn [?friends] would not belive so to [?ues] a vey kan in nothing content the Queen of England.

{blot} I trust God he [?will] help us, as he begen […] alredy in delyvring us of our {o—} [?Murray].

The Queen of England hes spokn […] courtusli to the B. of Ross of you […] schued; and if I would caus schut her friend a harkebus-schut, would not hurt me, God be iudge, betvix us.

I am asured that sche [?geves] me that veyt of Murray death {ø}; but God knoueth if […] her mynd would […], at le[?a]st you knou the treuth of this.

God will, I trust, prosper us both, the better that ve […] {Ш—} […]les.

That brut is that Murton is gon to […]; but not for treuth it I vreit [?ane] this: nicht to me Lord Talbot {∂}, not for treuth, it cometh from York.

[…] I will end, and prey you not to constren you or hasard you to vreyt your self, but caus {⊗} but vreyt to ani uther in your nem, but you ar vel; and remembers your oune.

I desyr no mor, for and I hear nothing, I fear ever that som teales mak you ofendet at me.

I schal, God veling, never mak caus, but sens the last […] I ever am in doubt of the lyk.

I prey him to preserve you from feynit friends, and send us mor confort; an[d] [?yet] vi have had in this meantym in vel or vo, I schal ever remen yours feithful til death.

{Mary}. The tventi of this instant.
