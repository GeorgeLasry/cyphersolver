# Charles IX to Philibert du Croc, undated (1565-67 or 1572): English translation

I know […] you know […] well the man […] those with […] you have to deal; and being assured of the affection you have
for my service, [I know] there is no need to give other advice […] you will have to deal in Scotland [for] the good of my
affairs, also referring myself to the instruction that was given separately and to the one now sent […] you will well
know how to follow likewise in what concerns the good of the peace of the said country; since […] at this hour no
instance can be made for my sister […] for fear of harming her, and […] I shall say […] opening it. I wish that my nephew
also, above all things, be well brought up; that he should always, in imitation of his predecessors, bear his principal
affection to me and mine; and that you conduct yourself […] well in the things you will see that are of importance in
the negotiation of the establishment of the said peace, order, government and council of that country; and you will
see to it, without showing […] that it comes from my part or from me, that he does not marry […] before I am first
informed of it, as will be easy by way of […] or through Normandy […] to see that there is safety, so that he may send
him my intention before the conclusion is reached. But above all act so that […] can have no distrust of your actions,
and nevertheless do not fail to serve me and always win the hearts of the Scots, so that they lessen nothing of the
affection they have been accustomed to bear my predecessors and me.

What you have principally to do over there is to give order that the English encroach on nothing in Scotland, but that
[…] remain always entire. You are so well advised that you will know how to conduct yourself very prudently in this as
is needed, so that the Scots know that […] desires their preservation, and that […] and her ministers cannot take any
bad occasion from it that would hinder the effect of our friendship. […] The ambassadors of England have been satisfied
according to the […] […] instruction only, at which I have been very glad […] will come to conclude […] treaty that you
will make […] wishes […] you have one; that will serve you as a pretext to send to ask me for one; and meanwhile you will
inform me of everything […]
