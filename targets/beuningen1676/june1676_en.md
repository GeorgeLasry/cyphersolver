# Van Beuningen to Fagel, Westminster, 23 May / 2 June 1676 (NA 3.01.18 inv. 249 scans 131-132): English translation

After my last of the 29th of last month, New Style, had gone off, I received Their High Mightinesses' resolution of the
26th before, containing their orders on the subject of the design against Brielle and Hellevoetsluis reported to me,
by which, as far as it concerns me, I shall punctually regulate myself. The informer continues to assure me that he
will be found to be sincere and well-intentioned, and he has been to tell me that yesterday Du Plat and the two
Frenchmen who came over were to cross the sea and go on to Bruges to hire [or buy] the bylanders. I am not without
strong distrust that people may be out to mislead me, and that perhaps the projected design has not been taken up by
France, but that it is feigned in order to draw some reward; and that not only because many improbabilities show
themselves to me in what has been reported, but also because the man insists very importunately on the reward. I have
refused to give anything before I should have had proofs as well as [?words] and [?promises] which one can [...]; but at
last I have handed to a known burgher, whom he has made a sharer in his secret, [a sum, uncertain: two hundred?]
sterling, to be paid out to the said informer, the said burgher binding himself to restore it to me if the truth of the
report is not further established. Du Plat has meanwhile fetched a pass for one Thomas Straught and [?thirty] men,
which I have had engrossed on such large paper as goes herewith, so that it might be recognised; although it would be
enough that only the name of the [?said] person in whose name the pass stands should be [?known] at the place where it
is meant to be misused, if it were not to be feared, as I have been warned, that my passports will be forged; that
being so, warning should be given that no one let himself be deceived by my passports.

It is not without great reason (in my judgement) that Their High Mightinesses consider whether the matter should be
kept secret in order to catch the plotters. For those whom they would bring in the [?vessels] will be English; and
apart from a few officers who have a part in the plot, they will consist of soldiers who will not be [?instructed]
before they are so far that they cannot turn back. I also learn from the letters of persons outside the government at
Amsterdam that there is already a strong rumour there of instructions sent by Their High Mightinesses to prevent a
design on Brielle, and among other things that the fleet destined for cruising under Lieutenant-Admiral Bastiaensz
would be held back on that account, which however I will not hope.

If the orders given there to prevent the said design cannot be kept secret, I leave it to Your Honour's consideration
whether one ought not to write to Brussels to [?seek] and catch the said Du Plat with the two Frenchmen at Bruges, and,
to find out their lodgings, have enquiries made from afar at the house of the merchant van der Straeten mentioned in my
last; [struck through: ...] if only what has been reported to me is true. With which I remain.
