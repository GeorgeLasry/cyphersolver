# R8565: Ghinucci, Lee and Poyntz to Wolsey, Valladolid, 17 July 1527 (BL Cotton Vesp. C IV ff. 174-177), reading of the cipher passages

Re-read 23 Oct 2026 (Claude Opus 5.5, REREAD_BRIEF batch b31); log in `R8565_reread_log.md`. **Rests on the contemporary
decipherment written over and between the cipher lines** (darker ink, a different hand from the despatch; L&P IV 3271:
"The cipher deciphered by Tuke"), read on the full-resolution DECODE images `IMG_R8565_I39496_P1..P7` (6600 x 9300 px,
fetched logged in 23 Oct 2026). Each passage was checked against the cipher signs with S. Tomokiyo's Lee-Worcester key
(the DECODE document on the record); every check agrees (Notes). The despatch is clear English; only these five passages
are in cipher. The clear words that lead into each passage are in the Notes, not in the reading.

Conventions: plain = carried by the decipherment and the signs; `[?word]` partly carried; `[word]` supplied; `[…]`
unread. Spelling as written; *p̃*, *ye*, *yt* expanded to *the*, *that*.

## Reading

Yet [?bere] the frenchemen, and that possible had not ben for them to have brought hym to shewe any demaundes, but
wolde have stikkyd upon the treatie of Madrill; but evermore, aswell in thair presence as oute, for the kinges highnes
he wolde doo all thinges.

That the kinges highnes [and] your grace be as hoole his as ever you have ben. [?Wher] he [?fearith] lest the frensh king
wil not be brought to raison, wherfore it shall be as well meritorious as honorable and high deed to wynne them in peace
and amite, wherof yf the kinges highnes and your grace do not, we thinke it wolde be long or it be doone.

What suretie shal we have that he shal not rendre the duchy unto the frenche kinges handes? We can not doubt but that he
wol. And therfor it is better that the Emperour bestowe it upon some of his frendes, that knowes non so great as the
kinges grace, and therfore [?yf] [?he] [?wolde] [?geve] the doughter of Portugalo for the duke of Richmont, and the duchy
of Millayn with her, how say yow, [?how] doth this like yow? That thinges were better to gyve to oon of his frendes than to
this man that is nothing mete for it.

De Tarbes hath nowe shewed us the fourth instruction. He kept it from us for that it [?is] oon devise that he shal saye
to the Emperour, when the ambassadors of England be about, that they desireth his amyte more than of any other prince.
