## Translation

**p. 277.** At Słupca, 15 October. Monseigneur, Your Highness will have seen from my last two letters, from Görlitz
in Saxony and from Lübben in Silesia, in what manner the affair of the Swedes with the Emperor has been untangled. He
[85] all at present here, 8 leagues from Poznań, where they are waiting for the recruits and the munitions that
have landed at Stettin. Their confidence in the English and the Dutch must assure us that they will not stop[8]
here to watch the conduct this Prince will keep as to the execution of the articles agreed, they aforesaid. I
persuade [120] that the Court of Vienna will not break a word confirmed and strengthened by a guarantee as
accredited in the league, and with them, as that of these two powers into whose hands it seems they have put the
fate of affairs [224 264 174 226 37 / 324 125 104 589 4].

I have also had the honour of giving you an account, Monseigneur, of the impression that [297] letters of Monsieur
Ráday to General Rehnskiöld had made on minds here. I [363]s that perhaps in [129 436] said as frankly in April, as I
owe it, that it has deprived you of your best friends at the Court of Sweden, and that it will take [860] and much
time [95] much skill to repair the incredible harm it has done Your Highness. That is why I would advise you,
Monseigneur, if you believe [120] that it were also in your interest to remedy this, to send secretly, or to seek
here, a person who had the qualities needed to succeed in it. I confess in good faith that I lack them, [9] besides
which the misfortune I have had of being suspected by Your Highness, or by those [?who may] belong to you, makes me
understand that the continuation of my services would not be agreeable to you. I have nevertheless not yet
slackened, up to now, in my zeal towards you, and I have made every effort of which I have been [194]pable, both at
the Swedish chancellery and at the Court of Poland,

**p. 278, lines 1-9.** [121] to destroy at once the rumour that was going about, or rather the opinion people had,
that Your Highness had had arrested the children of the Palatine of Russia and the envoy of the King of Poland, who
was coming back from Prince Constantine [27] (?Constanti)nople, [129] and was bringing with [264 89] a deputy of the
Porte to the King of Sweden. I do not know whether my efforts, joined to this information, more exact than what
people had had, have effaced that opinion; but I can say that if it is not yet entirely dispelled, it will not be
denied that too much faith was lightly given to reports and prejudices which

**p. 278, lines 10-18.** gave rise to it. For the present they confine themselves to complaining of Your Highness's
conduct with regard to the affairs of Poland, and of certain remarks you are said to have made in the presence of
people who have reported them, asserting that you had said that, taking the interests of Poland as much to heart as
those of Hungary, you were resolved to sacrifice yourself if need be to free Polish liberty, oppressed by the Swedes
in favour of King Stanislas. I am, with a very deep respect, …
