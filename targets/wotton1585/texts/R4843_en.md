To the Secretary, 1 September.

Sir, to carry out the proposal of {39 = the Master of Gray}, of which I have already told you, he and the Prior of Blantyre think it right that the Lords should by all means come to an agreement, quickly and secretly, with {20 = Lord Maxwell}; that {20 = Lord Maxwell} should then make a raid on the Laird of Johnstone, with whom he has a feud; so that, under colour of helping the Laird against {20 = Lord Maxwell}, the L. B., the L. H., S. J. H. and S. can gather their forces and join {20 = Lord Maxwell}, while the Lords send five hundred of their best men as fast as possible to seize {10 = the King} and {19 = Arran}. These five hundred are to be led by the Lord of Arbroath, the Earl [?of Mar] and the Master of G[lamis].

{39 = the Master of Gray} says that if he can quickly have enough money out of [36] to keep a hundred men for a short while, he will guarantee that the Lords are so strong inside {10 = the King}'s house that if {10 = the King}, on hearing that the Lords are coming into the country, tries to cross the water into Fife or to take refuge in [32], he will stop him by force and hand him over to them.

You can well judge how necessary it is to have a friend inside the court who can (should the surprise attack of the five hundred fail) keep {10 = the King} from slipping away until the Lords' whole force arrives. So in my judgement it would be no bad thing to grant {39 = the Master of Gray}'s request. He is now so far committed that, unless this succeeds, I consider him utterly ruined; and so he can be trusted all the more.

{39 = the Master of Gray} has had long talks with the Prior of Blantyre about this, and asks you to tell the Lords from him that whatever the Prior says to them when they come to the Borders, they should believe it, for he will certainly carry it out.

I have held back from telling the King the part of your letter about postponing the commissioners' meeting to a later day, [..] [..] until such a day, having been told by the Secretary that his commissioners cannot be ready so soon. They want one of the commissioners to be an earl, one of the men of highest rank. I made a show of disliking his proposal, but told him I would report it, and asked that his commissioners should have full power and authority to do and conclude whatever the purpose of the meeting requires, which he says they will have.

If the violent course is decided on, it cannot be consistent with {3 = the Queen's} honour for me to be in this country at that time. I beg you therefore to ask the Queen to recall me quickly, for I am in great danger; though I am unfit to be her ambassador, I may still live to serve her some other way. My sword cuts as well as any man's, and I have some friends in the part of the country where I live.

Note. The matter proposed by {39 = the Master of Gray} is being kept from {40 = the Justice Clerk} and [27], because we here think {40 = the Justice Clerk} would reveal it to [27], whom we do not take for any great friend of the Lords. I beg that the matter be handled so that {39 = the Master of Gray} can be sure the Queen places great trust in him; otherwise he may out of spite reveal the whole thing and wreck it.

As long as I stay in this country, people [?will] imagine that the Queen wants the league to go ahead and does not mean to support the Lords; that belief may do much harm and make their friends here hesitate.
