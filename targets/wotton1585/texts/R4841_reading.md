To Mr Secretary, the 25th of August. The King being at hunting in an yland of the Duk of Lennox.

Sir, {39 = the Master of Gray} sayed to me yesterday that, notwithstanding he had written to your Honour that he would forbeare to deale any more in the course undertaken by him til he hearde out of England that her Majesty did rest fully satisfied of his doinges, yet considering with himself that in matters of importance protraction of tyme is dangerous, he thought good to imparte unto me his opinion touching the state of matters heere, to the end that your Honour, being therof advertised by me, might acquainte her Majesty with it.

His discourse tendeth to this end: that he now findeth how he and [the] rest of his party have runne al this while a wrong course in seeking to disgrace {19 = Arran} with {10 = the King}, whose love towarde him is so extreeme as [he] wil not suffer one hair of his head to fall to the ground if he may helpe it; that as long as {19 = Arran} shal continew in favour with {10 = the King}, it will be a thing impossible to bring home the Lords; that unles they may be restored the league cannot be sure, nor {39 = the Master of Gray} and the rest in safety. For {19 = Arran}, receaving the King presently, then wil he with his credit be able to ruine them and diverte {10 = the King} from {3 = the Queen of England}; or if, that finding the King constantly [in] affection towarde {3 = the Queen of England} [?immoveable], [?stick], he wil not fail to convey him into France.

To obviate therfore al thees inconveniences, his advise is that her Majesty, having so good occasion ministred unto her by the death of my Lord Russel, [should] take the matter very highly and hold [the King] from concluding the league for this time; that she lette slippe the Lords, who, if they may have from her some supporte of money, wil by their frendes heere and suche as they may levy for money be able to take {19 = Arran} and possesse [and] seize {10 = the King's} persone; wherunto they shal have the best helpe he can give them by himself and his faction.

If he may perceive that this overture be liked of, he wil direct a spetial frend of his and theirs into England very shortly, who shal conferre with the Lords particularly about the execution of this plotte.

This was the effect of his whole speeche, saving that in the end he saied, in answeare of an objection which I made, that he would undertake (this thing being once done) to bring the league to a perfect conclusion, and that the King shal notwithstanding al this be wrought to assente unto it with a [..] heart, as wel as to confirme it with his hand and oath.

It is suspected that {19 = Arran} and {24 = Ferniehirst} be secretly agreed, and some heere feare lest {24 = Ferniehirst} by consent of the other take the King and convey him into France. It is now very constantly reported that {24 = Ferniehirst} payeth his men with crownes of the sun, and that the greatest parte of the six thousand [crowns] which the Jesuites brought fell to his share; which, if it be true, is a shrewd token.

Postscript. Article: if her Majesty shal like of the Master's overture, I could wishe the matter so handled that {10 = the King} might first waxe colde in the matter of the league [and] refuse the league, which I suppose might easely be compassed if her Majesty do meet with that article which is proposed touching the King's title; she would inserte another for the home comming of the Lords.

[Endorsed:] Copie. To Mr Secretarye, 25 Auguste.
