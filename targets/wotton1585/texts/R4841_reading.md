To Mr Secretary, the 25th of August. The King being at hunting in an island of the Duke of Lennox.

Sir, {39 = the Master of Gray} said to me yesterday that, notwithstanding he had written to your Honour that he would forbear to deal any more in the course undertaken by him till he heard out of England that her Majesty did rest fully satisfied of his doings, yet considering with himself that in matters of importance protraction of time is dangerous, he thought good to impart unto me his opinion touching the state of matters here, to the end that your Honour, being thereof advertised by me, might acquaint her Majesty with it.

His discourse tendeth to this end: that he now findeth how he and [the] rest of his party have run all this while a wrong course in seeking to disgrace {19 = Arran} with {10 = the King}, whose love toward him is so extreme as [he] will not suffer one hair of his head to fall to the ground if he may help it; that as long as {19 = Arran} shall continue in favour with {10 = the King}, it will be a thing impossible to bring home the Lords; that unless they may be restored the league cannot be sure, nor {39 = the Master of Gray} and the rest in safety. For {19 = Arran}, receiving the King presently, then will he with his credit be able to ruin them and divert {10 = the King} from {3 = the Queen of England}; or if, that finding the King constantly [in] affection toward {3 = the Queen of England} [?immoveable], [?stick], he will not fail to convey him into France.

To obviate therefore all these inconveniences, his advice is that her Majesty, having so good occasion ministered unto her by the death of my Lord Russell, [should] take the matter very highly and hold [the King] from concluding the league for this time; that she let slip the Lords, who, if they may have from her some support of money, will by their friends here and such as they may levy for money be able to take {19 = Arran} and possess [and] seize {10 = the King's} person; whereunto they shall have the best help he can give them by himself and his faction.

If he may perceive that this overture be liked of, he will direct a special friend of his and theirs into England very shortly, who shall confer with the Lords particularly about the execution of this plot.

This was the effect of his whole speech, saving that in the end he said, in answer of an objection which I made, that he would undertake (this thing being once done) to bring the league to a perfect conclusion, and that the King shall notwithstanding all this be wrought to assent unto it with a [..] heart, as well as to confirm it with his hand and oath.

It is suspected that {19 = Arran} and {24 = Ferniehirst} be secretly agreed, and some here fear lest {24 = Ferniehirst} by consent of the other take the King and convey him into France. It is now very constantly reported that {24 = Ferniehirst} payeth his men with crowns of the sun, and that the greatest part of the six thousand [crowns] which the Jesuits brought fell to his share; which, if it be true, is a shrewd token.

Postscript. Article: if her Majesty shall like of the Master's overture, I could wish the matter so handled that {10 = the King} might first wax cold in the matter of the league [and] refuse the league, which I suppose might easily be compassed if her Majesty do meet with that article which is proposed touching the King's title; she would insert another for the home coming of the Lords.

[Endorsed:] Copy. To Mr Secretary, 25 August.
