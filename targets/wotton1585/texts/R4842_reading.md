To Mr Secretary, the 30th of August 1585.

Your Honour's of the 21st came to my hands the 26th of the same, by which I perceive that {39 = the Master of Gray} hath complained of the alteration of my countenance toward him. My answer thereunto is that I never, what cause soever he gave me to the contrary, altered my countenance, but used him always with as great familiarity and courtesy as I did [..]. Marry, the truth is that after he had spoken to {10 = the King} for the enlargement of {19 = Arran} without the knowledge of {40 = the Justice Clerk}, [27] or myself, he then for three or four days abstained from his wonted manner of visiting me, so that the alteration of countenance was on his part and not on mine. This proceeding in that matter I will now interpret in the best part, seeing he hath so well taken up himself, and [..] again.

For [27] and {40 = the Justice Clerk}, I never saw cause to suspect them to be in the [11] course, neither do I suppose that {39 = the Master of Gray} in his conscience is so persuaded of them. Yet can it not be denied, as your Honour writeth, [but] [27] was greatly hers, and is a great favourer of the Hamiltons, and as yet no earnest friend to the Lords; [?howbeit] in all matters concerning my negotiation wherein I have had to deal with him I have found him fully as forward as any of the other two. Nevertheless I mean to follow your Honour's grave advice in dealing warily with him and all others. For by that little experience I have of this country men, I think it dangerous to trust any of all their [..] further than it shall stand with his own particulars, as they term it here.

So that in my simple judgement, if her Majesty will procure herself fast friends in this country, she must both have a consideration to their particulars and so embark them in her course [..] that necessity [..] [..]. [Men that] are bound will quickly for greater gain free themselves. To feed them with money is good, but to no great purpose unless you do so embark them as [they] may not hope to leap ashore without [?drowning].

{19 = Arran} yet remaineth at Kinneil, where, under a colour [or] pretence of fear to be suddenly assaulted by his [enemies], he is, as I hear, very well accompanied. To keep him from the court longer than the meeting of the commissioners, unless he be found by them guilty of the [?late] breach on the Borders, will be, as I suppose, very hard. If he recover the King's presence, I cannot see how {39 = the Master of Gray}, {40 = the Justice Clerk} [and] [27] can live safely in the court; and if {19 = Arran}, being there alone do fac totum, we may easily conjecture what success matters are like to have. For my own part, I am too young to find out a remedy for this mischief, and therefore leave it to the consideration of her Majesty whether she will hearken to the {39 = the Master of Gray's} offer or no. [I] expect daily her Majesty's resolution touching the overture made by {39 = the Master of Gray}.

This morning I saw a letter written to the Prior of Blantyre from the Laird of [?Jamington's] brother, by which it seemeth that {20 = Lord Maxwell} [is] very desirous to make his peace with the King by the means of {19 = Arran}, with whom I suspect he is secretly already contracted; and therefore I will do what I can to hinder his reconciliation with {10 = the King}.

Earl Bothwell is by the Master's means newly come to the court, to the great grief of the Arranists. It is very certain that Hay and Dury are in this country. The King seemeth very desirous of their apprehension, and hath laid that charge on {39 = the Master of Gray}, who hath promised to perform it. I cannot perceive any change in the King, but that he seemeth still as desirous of the {9 = English} friendship as ever he was.

Ticket to my Lord of L[eicester]. This morning Sir William Stewart told his Majesty that Cuthbert Armorer is now [..], [and] his brother was advertised that her Majesty is resolved to banish the distressed Lords out of England for a few days, [and] that her meaning is to send them shortly into this country to shuffle the cards. Whether there be any such meaning or no, your Honour best knoweth. But in the mean season this companion playeth his part very kindly. I wish therefore some order might be taken that no Englishman without special licence pass into this country. I wish some order might be taken to stay his repair into this country.

[Endorsed:] Copy. To Mr Secretary, 30 August.
