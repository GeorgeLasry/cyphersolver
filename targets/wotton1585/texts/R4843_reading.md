To Mr Secretary, the first of September.

Sir, for the better effectuating of the overture made by {39 = the Master of Gray}, wherof I have already advertised your Honour, it is thought meet by himself and the Prior of B[lantyre] that the Lords in any case doo agree, and speedely, secretly, with {20 = Lord Maxwell}, and that then {20 = Lord Maxwell} make some incursion upon the Laird of Joh[nstone], with whome he hath a quarrel; to the end that, under a colour of aiding the said Larde against {20 = Lord Maxwell}, the L. B., the L. H., S. J. H. and S. may gather their forces and ioyne with {20 = Lord Maxwell}, the Lords sending five hundred of their best men with greatest expedition possible to possesse themselves of {10 = the King} and {19 = Arran}; thees five hundred to be conducted by [the] Lord of Arbroath, the Erle [?of Mar], [the] Master [of] G[lamis].

{39 = the Master of Gray} sayeth that if he may have with speed so muche money out of [36] as may suffise to maintain a hundred men a convenient shorte time, he wil assure the Lords to be so strong within {10 = the King's} house as that if {10 = the King} would, upon the bruite of the Lords' comming into this contrey, passe over the water into Fife or seeke to convey himself into [32], he wil by force stay him and deliver him into their handes.

How necessary a thing it is to have a frend within the court who may be able (in case the sudden enterprise of the five hundred shoulde faile) to hold {10 = the King} firme from starting till suche time as the whole forces of the Lords shall be at the place, your Honour can wel consider; and therfore in my iugement it were not amisse to satisfye {39 = the Master of Gray} his request, who is now so farre embarqued as, unles this matter have good successe, I esteeme him utterly ruined; [and] he may be the better trusted.

{39 = the Master of Gray} hath had great conference with the Prior of B[lantyre] about this matter, and he prayeth your Honour to advertise the Lords from him that whatsoever the said Prior shal say unto them when they shal come to the borders, he desireth them to beleeve it, for he wil undoutedly performe it.

I have forborne to acquaint the King with that parte of your lettre which concerned the adiourning of the meeting of the commissioners to a further day, [..] [..] till such a day, being informed by the Secretary that his commissioners cannot be ready so soon. [They] desire that one of the commissioners may be an erle, one of the men of greatest ranke. I seemed to mislike his motion, but yet told him that I woulde advertise it, and desired that his commissioners might have sufficient power and autoritie to doo and conclude whatsoever the cause and purpose of their meeting shal requyre, which he sayeth they shal have.

If the violent course be resolved on, it can no way stand with {3 = the Queen's} honor that I should be at that time in this contrey. I beseeche you therfore to be a meane to her Majesty for my speedy revocation, for that I am in great danger; though I am unfitte to be [her] ambassador, yet may I live [to] doo her service some other way. My sworde can cutte as wel as another man's, and some frendes I have in the contrey where I dwell.

Ticket. The matter proposed by {39 = the Master of Gray} is concealed from {40 = the Justice Clerk} and [27], bicause wee here think that {40 = the Justice Clerk} would reveale it to [27], whome wee esteeme no very great favorer of the Lords. I beseeche that this matter may be so handled as {39 = the Master of Gray} may assure himself her Majesty reposeth great trust in him; otherwise in a despite he may reveale the whole matter and overthrow it.

So long as I shal continew in this contrey, it [?will] be imagined that her Majesty desireth the going forward of the league, and that she meaneth not to favour the Lords; which opinion may doo muche harme, and make their frendes heer irresolute.

[Endorsed:] Copie. To Mr Secretarye, the first of September.
