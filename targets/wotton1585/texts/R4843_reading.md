To Mr Secretary, the first of September.

Sir, for the better effectuating of the overture made by {39 = the Master of Gray}, whereof I have already advertised your Honour, it is thought meet by himself and the Prior of B[lantyre] that the Lords in any case do agree, and speedily, secretly, with {20 = Lord Maxwell}, and that then {20 = Lord Maxwell} make some incursion upon the Laird of Joh[nstone], with whom he hath a quarrel; to the end that, under a colour of aiding the said Laird against {20 = Lord Maxwell}, the L. B., the L. H., S. J. H. and S. may gather their forces and join with {20 = Lord Maxwell}, the Lords sending five hundred of their best men with greatest expedition possible to possess themselves of {10 = the King} and {19 = Arran}; these five hundred to be conducted by [the] Lord of Arbroath, the Earl [?of Mar], [the] Master [of] G[lamis].

{39 = the Master of Gray} sayeth that if he may have with speed so much money out of [36] as may suffice to maintain a hundred men a convenient short time, he will assure the Lords to be so strong within {10 = the King's} house as that if {10 = the King} would, upon the bruit of the Lords' coming into this country, pass over the water into Fife or seek to convey himself into [32], he will by force stay him and deliver him into their hands.

How necessary a thing it is to have a friend within the court who may be able (in case the sudden enterprise of the five hundred should fail) to hold {10 = the King} firm from starting till such time as the whole forces of the Lords shall be at the place, your Honour can well consider; and therefore in my judgement it were not amiss to satisfy {39 = the Master of Gray} his request, who is now so far embarked as, unless this matter have good success, I esteem him utterly ruined; [and] he may be the better trusted.

{39 = the Master of Gray} hath had great conference with the Prior of B[lantyre] about this matter, and he prayeth your Honour to advertise the Lords from him that whatsoever the said Prior shall say unto them when they shall come to the borders, he desireth them to believe it, for he will undoubtedly perform it.

I have forborne to acquaint the King with that part of your letter which concerned the adjourning of the meeting of the commissioners to a further day, [..] [..] till such a day, being informed by the Secretary that his commissioners cannot be ready so soon. [They] desire that one of the commissioners may be an earl, one of the men of greatest rank. I seemed to mislike his motion, but yet told him that I would advertise it, and desired that his commissioners might have sufficient power and authority to do and conclude whatsoever the cause and purpose of their meeting shall require, which he sayeth they shall have.

If the violent course be resolved on, it can no way stand with {3 = the Queen's} honour that I should be at that time in this country. I beseech you therefore to be a mean to her Majesty for my speedy revocation, for that I am in great danger; though I am unfit to be [her] ambassador, yet may I live [to] do her service some other way. My sword can cut as well as another man's, and some friends I have in the country where I dwell.

Ticket. The matter proposed by {39 = the Master of Gray} is concealed from {40 = the Justice Clerk} and [27], because we here think that {40 = the Justice Clerk} would reveal it to [27], whom we esteem no very great favourer of the Lords. I beseech that this matter may be so handled as {39 = the Master of Gray} may assure himself her Majesty reposeth great trust in him; otherwise in a despite he may reveal the whole matter and overthrow it.

So long as I shall continue in this country, it [?will] be imagined that her Majesty desireth the going forward of the league, and that she meaneth not to favour the Lords; which opinion may do much harm, and make their friends here irresolute.

[Endorsed:] Copy. To Mr Secretary, the first of September.
