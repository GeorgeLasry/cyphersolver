To the Secretary, 25 August. The King is out hunting on an island belonging to the Duke of Lennox.

Sir, {39 = the Master of Gray} told me yesterday that, although he had written to you that he would stop dealing in the course he had taken up until he heard from England that the Queen was fully satisfied with what he had done, he had concluded that in important matters delay is dangerous, and so decided to give me his view of affairs here, so that you, informed by me, could pass it on to the Queen.

The gist of what he said is this. He now sees that he and the rest of his party have all along been going the wrong way about it in trying to bring {19 = Arran} into disgrace with {10 = the King}, whose love for him is so extreme that he will not let a hair of his head fall to the ground if he can help it. As long as {19 = Arran} stays in favour with {10 = the King} it will be impossible to bring the Lords home; unless they are restored the league cannot be relied on, and {39 = the Master of Gray} and the rest will not be safe. For {19 = Arran}, once he has the King again, will use his credit to ruin them and to turn {10 = the King} away from {3 = the Queen of England}; or, if he finds the King's affection for {3 = the Queen of England} [?unshakeable], he will not fail to carry him off to France.

To head off all these dangers, his advice is that the Queen, given so good an occasion by the death of Lord Russell, should take the matter very seriously and hold the King back from concluding the league for now, and let the Lords loose. If they can have some money from her, they will be able, with their friends here and the men they can hire, to take {19 = Arran} and seize the person of {10 = the King}; and he will give them all the help he can, himself and his faction.

If he sees that this proposal is liked, he will very shortly send a special friend of his and of theirs to England to confer with the Lords in detail about carrying out the plan.

That was the substance of his whole speech, except that at the end, answering an objection of mine, he said he would undertake, once this was done, to bring the league to a full conclusion, and that the King would nonetheless be brought to agree to it with a [..] heart, and to confirm it with his signature and his oath.

It is suspected that {19 = Arran} and {24 = Ferniehirst} have come to a secret understanding, and some here fear that {24 = Ferniehirst}, with the other's consent, may seize the King and carry him to France. It is now firmly reported that {24 = Ferniehirst} is paying his men in French gold crowns, and that most of the six thousand crowns the Jesuits brought went to him, which, if true, is a telling sign.

Postscript. If the Queen likes the Master's proposal, I would wish it handled so that {10 = the King} first cools towards the league and refuses it. I think that could easily be managed if the Queen answers the article proposed about the King's title by inserting another one for the return of the Lords.

[Endorsed:] Copy. To the Secretary, 25 August.
