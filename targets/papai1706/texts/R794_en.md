# R794, Pápai to Rákóczi, early 1706: English

Translation of `R794_reading.md` (23 Oct 2026), same holes.

## Translation

[…] all such beginnings as will fulfil Your Highness's wishes; may God grant it! [200] […] János Pápai.

[P.S.] The propositions must [?…] all in Wallachia, [?…] only that they allow the recruiting here, which [?…] the
recruiting of four thousand horse costs us a great deal, since beforehand thirty thalers' discretion must go to its
officers separately, [?and] to the [?common soldiers]. But since Your Highness has enough cavalry besides, foot is more
needed. When we hand the Vizier our memorial about Muscovy, we make no specification either of horse or of foot, but
only asked permission to recruit; and if Your Highness wants more foot, it will be easy, horse or foot. [?…] to make a
disposition about money quickly, since the ambassador will not have it; without it we get nowhere in the recruiting.

As I humbly report to my gracious lord, so that I may inform Your Highness of the matters in hand: when some days ago I
wanted to send my servant off with the Moldavian couriers, I had already sent to the lodgings for some boyar, seized
for his tax; my servant too has had to wait here until now. [?…] Yesterday, very secretly, early in the morning, he
sent his son, who is now interpreter at the Vizier's gate, summoning me; he wished, [?…] the order being, first to meet
the Vizier's kiaya [?…] very secretly, [?…] without pomp, since he so wished, and I asked him to obtain an audience with
the Vizier; he much obliges Your Highness's [7] too, so that when, having taken leave of the Vizier, I went towards
evening to the ambassador, I asked: the affairs are now starting, money is needed for the discretion and for
recruiting Turks [?through] the Albanians; to which he answered: [?…] the money due from the King for Your Highness is
assigned to it, [?but without] his order [?he cannot] give cash, and without it he cannot give his own, having spent
more than ten thousand thalers of his own not for the promotion of Hungarian affairs, and the King has not refunded
even that; and we cannot want him to make himself a debtor and a beggar; though what he spends for Your Highness is
surely refunded, without the King's order he cannot give, [?not] up to three thousand thalers [?…], from which the
discretion is not even covered. [?…] through lack of money a damaging confusion falls on our affairs. [?…] Kassa [?…]
serve; he had to be summoned to him by the [?Vizier's] order; he [?said] I should put in writing the matters relating
to the propositions and take them to him, so that he may have them turned into Turkish [?…] tomorrow [?…] to him.
