## Translation

Therefore, whoever [?…] for this [?end], Your Highness's gracious will [?…] is ordained, as [?…] [200] [?…] in this Polish
situation too, our duty being obedience with pure and true loyalty, [?…] we do not dispute how such affairs should go,
but further commend ourselves to Your Highness's gracious disposition. We went to the ambassador of France according to
Your Highness's command and made an approach about the recruiting, [?…] that nothing else is left but that he
[?report] to the King: the Sublime Porte permits the recruiting, let him obtain money for that need. He answered [110]:
I am writing to the King just now, as I had already written before, that the present Vizier [?…] Poland [?…]; let His
Majesty only send money; and if the Vizier changed, his successor might be difficult in this, and the money would stop
here, or he could more easily dispose [120] the men with money; [?…] that the Prince too should write to the King; it
is needed, since [110] the common rumour here among the Turks too is that the French King wants peace. Indeed they say
the English, even in the preliminaries, [?…] so much that they would not have thought of it for nothing; to which end
a French envoy has been staying secretly with the Dutch for half a year, which the Venetians [?…] taking the mediation
upon themselves [120] are accepted [110]; to that end they also have envoys in England and Holland. We said: lord, the
gracious Prince, [?…] nevertheless [110] wishing to show his sincere attachment to the King, continues [?the war]
without ceasing; we fear the case of the late Prince Imre Thököly, when he was [?excluded] from the peace, though the
King was then triumphant. He answered: my king is most generous, he will by no means let it happen that he excludes the
prince, even if it were to happen [?…] [180] I do not believe it. We have no doubt of this about the gracious Prince,
but sometimes necessity and the ministers' suggestion are wont to change kings' minds. He answered: my king loves and
esteems the prince greatly; [?…] the late prince Thököly [?…]; even if he treats of peace, he will either include the
prince or give [?…]; at that time he will be able to do more [?…] will have peace. We reminded him of the gunpowder
too; he promised to obtain a passport in such form that sixty or seventy [?camels] [?…] from France could be carried to
Hungary; under that pretext, with that passport, through the Pashas' goodwill, more could be carried. Communicating
this matter to [?Golopali], he [?said]: if the Sublime Porte has a mind to treat with us, [?…] otherwise he does not
hope. The Vizier asking [?…]'s opinion on the present European and Muscovite affairs, he has already handed it in, so
that he wants nothing more from the Sublime Porte [?…], as he urged war against Muscovy, which he also read before us;
we have it written in Italian, but could not send it without cipher. But since they announce that the Swede has made
peace with Augustus, this too causes great confusion in men's minds here [?…]; nevertheless we hear that the Swede
wants to go on making war with Muscovy; we too announce it so, but the truth will soon reveal this better. The
ambassador communicated to us the specification of the administration made so far to Your Highness; he promised to
communicate it further. To compare the course of money [?…] with the thaler current here and judge of it, or to learn
[?their economy] from the ambassador: he hardly allows it. Until the Sublime Porte's resolution about the envoys we
looked for an interpreter, but have not been able to get one so far; [?…] says one cannot get one, but the ambassador
promised a Frenchman, who could at the same time be Your Highness's French secretary. We tried to have the ambassador
send out Mr Brü, but our intention did not succeed, though Mr Brü offered himself. We reported Your Highness's
intention about the voivode of Wallachia. He answered: you know, it is [?…] years since I have wanted to promote [?…]
voivode to that voivodeship, who is now here in the palace, yet I can get nowhere; even now the [?…] [210] voivode has
sent three hundred purses of money here to have him seized, because of which he dares not leave here. I wrote to the
King, I await His Majesty's letter to the Turkish Emperor about the removal of that voivode from office, but it is hard
to accomplish anything against him, since having much money he forestalls our small opinion with it. As Your
Highness's affairs are not yet in such a state here at the Sublime Porte, let Your Highness put off this intention.
The burdensome state of the late Prince Thököly [200] was not a little aggravated by the earlier quarrel with him, as
once [?…] he did not promote [200] the Transylvanian Ferenc Gyulai to the Principality. Here, gracious lord, that
voivodeship goes to the highest bidder; though others have often tried to oust him from that office, he always
prevailed with much money, so that once in such a state he paid even a thousand purses. It is better for Your Highness,
even if he has injuries [?…], to put it off to a more fitting time and meanwhile dissemble, for whoever is in the way
hurries to his goal; one must watch the time on his way too, for being offended he sometimes causes great obstacles
here; he does not accommodate himself well to us; his steward and kapi-kiaya too will not be to Your Highness's harm if
[you keep] true neighbourliness with him rather, as the affair of those Wallachian boyars [240] is some deep-rooted
faction stirred up with the neighbours, [?…] so that that quarrel [?…]. Going to the kiaya, we communicated the news to
him; [?…] Poland [?…], that it is not true either that Your Highness is treating [?…]; though we reminded him of his words
when he said: your presence is not unwelcome to the Porte, [?…] he does not willingly converse with you in the presence
of the internuncio, he only answered: gentlemen, [?…] the resolution of the Porte; [?…] the Sublime Porte wishes
religiously to observe the holy peace, from which it will not withdraw unless it is opposed from the other side. [?…] I
say now: you are not unwelcome, no one hinders you, [you are] safe here [?…]. At once he mentioned the confederates'
progress everywhere [?…] Poland [?…] France against Muscovy; if France had not besieged Turin and Barcelona almost at
the same time [?…] victory; he advised continuing the peace with the Roman Emperor. I asked: is [?…] the man
[?Vainovics] Your Highness's, are there troops with him? As Mr Gáspár Pápai fortunately wrote that Vainovics is going to
the Serbian to Banja Luka, being informed [?…] Poland, we said: [?…] Your Highness's faithful man, but whether he has

[?…] are our enemies; yet let the Porte do [?…] fitting to [?its] inclination. [?…] Prince Mihály [?…] still has at
Vienna [?…] his wife, and whose daughter [?…] lives, has not changed religion. [?…] that he knows everything well even

Nevertheless we heard that, gracious lord, giving no occasion for it [?…] to bring it up; as so far we have not even
