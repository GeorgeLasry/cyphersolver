# R793 — English (János Pápai to Ferenc Rákóczi II, Constantinople, 16 March 1706)

Same holes as R793_reading.md. Modern spelling; codes as names.

Gracious lord! [I humbly ask?] Your Highness to put it down not to my sloth that I cannot report often, but to the lack
of a sure opportunity. [Though?] we find [?…] a disposition towards us, we do not cease [to carry out] Your Highness's
orders, but so far we have not been able to bring our affairs begun to effect. In recent days we approached the
Sublime Porte with [new writings], which we presented in two parts: one on the Muscovite business, but very secretly,
through the chancellor and the mufti, so that, as was necessary, his co-religionist should not notice; upon which
[…], having arrived, reported on the settlement of the frontier disputed between the Muscovites and the Sublime Porte,
on the frontier by the Tana [Azov]: it has not been settled, […] the envoy; the peace with the Muscovite Tsar has not
been fully ratified. [It is time] for the Sublime Porte to take a hand, since with the Sublime Porte on one side and the
King of Sweden on the other, if he attacks, Poland could not hold out; the Roman Emperor's affairs being such that he
could not act against our Sublime Porte with all his strength; indeed, if the Roman Emperor wanted to attempt
something, his war with the French and the Hungarians [would prevent it]; and if our Sublime Porte allies itself with
the Hungarians, the King of Sweden being an ally of the Hungarians, Prince Rákóczi will bring it about that he allies
himself with the Sublime Porte on certain conditions.

The Muscovite, under colour of the peace, already [holds?] the Astrakhanians, the Circassians, the Mingrelians […] of
the Sublime Porte; among the [Kakhetians?] he has already sent two hundred priests, armed […], he leaves that mouth
[of the river] there, which [threatens] the Sublime Porte, there being harbour enough in Circassia and Mingrelia on the
Black Sea, directly against Constantinople on one side, and on the other in that […] Black Sea […] with Moldavia, the
Roman Emperor [and] the confederates' gatherings too. When they understood this, the Vizier, informed of the
withdrawal without its being noticed, so moderated his views on all this; they presented it in writing. Here the
Vizier soon called me and asked me: do you think the Hungarians would ally themselves with the Swede? In this with
them […], but I believe they too have an interest in it. Meanwhile the chancellor explained our project on this to the
Vizier: that both Your Highness and the Transylvanian King, through Your Highness's mediation, wish to ally with the
Sublime Porte, the other with other reasons, which demonstrated the harm to the Sublime Porte that would follow from the
confederation to be made in Poland. This had to be [delivered] with the manifesto, which we gave to the Vizier already
translated. The envoy himself is persuading the Vizier. We hope for the desired answer in a few days; if these two
writings of mine do not move them, after that we see nothing else. They would gladly be friends with us, as [appears]
from the chancellor's words, except that the German too [is] much despised […], all the more since the German has now
retaken Transylvania. Those who are in Moldavia, about them [the envoy] wrote to the voivode; he is waiting meanwhile
until the firman goes off. The Vizier too inclines towards us; we shall obtain it through the envoy, even if it
prejudices the pacification. About money: the envoy gives not a penny. And I remain Your Highness's humble servant,
János Pápai. Constantinople, the sixteenth of March, one thousand seven hundred and six.
