# R757, [Pápai] to Rákóczi, 16 May 1708: English

Translation of `R757_reading.md` (23 Oct 2026), same holes. The Latin passages are translated with the rest.

## Translation

The ambassador of Muscovy sent me word thus: those Hungarians have not been [to me], I have not seen them, but if they
make themselves known I shall hear them and give them an answer or not; it is in my power. I cannot send money from
here, since by the religion of this Sublime Porte the canon is that whoever comes here is admitted [?…]. Before the
ambassador of Muscovy and through a request at the Sublime Porte we also called Mr Hentér, but he did not come; doing
our duty, we went to his request. [?…] said: I shall tell you the resolution of the Sublime Porte [?…] you are joined
with Mr Hentér: [?…] the steward of the court had resolved to admit you three to his presence, but he put off that
intention; yet [?…] to the Porte [?…] the kiaya will resolve; we shall let you know. This present steward of the court
is a prudent man, more so than the former; Hentér brought those [?…] together with your propositions; he went through
them from head to foot and examined them. From the start we asked: [?…] safe conduct? He answered: yes, but not
[?…]. We answered: yes, but with arms. He answered: yes, and not [?…]. Going to the kiaya, the chancellor was present
too; kissing his robe, we congratulated him on his new office; he seated us with respect [3000]. Then he asked Mr
Hentér: did [?…] Your Majesty and the Estates [?…] come? He answered: yes; when the Prince was inaugurated to the
Principality, he took the country's homage to His Majesty, and His Majesty too swore to the Estates. He asked him:
whether the Prince now [?…] the seat at Pozsony [?…] outside others' realm? We answered: Sibiu is not the seat of the
Princes but [Gyula]fehérvár, where His Majesty's general actually is; the Estates of the country are in His Majesty's
loyalty [?…] on Rabutin's entry, those of Sibiu and Brașov [?…] obeyed the German; notwithstanding that, the Estates of
the whole country are in His Majesty's loyalty. Mr Hentér briefly proposed the wish of Your Majesty and the Estates
about the admission of the [?…] envoys. The kiaya answered: the letter he brought from the Prince and the Estates of the
country [?…] refer to you, that we should credit your words; [?…] as we now understood, that solemn envoys might come.
I ask this: does the Prince hold all Transylvania? Mr Hentér: I cannot say so, for the German holds Brașov, Sibiu,
Făgăraș. We said: if [?…] we may speak more fully in this matter, the Sublime Porte well knows that when Bocskai,
Gábor Bethlen and other princes [3000] afterwards turned to the Sublime Porte, before they held all Transylvania and
those towns, the Sublime Porte [?…] their wishes and protected them. The [?…] kiaya answered: the Sublime Porte held
Buda, [?…] our faith [?…] the canon; we hold that [?…] we accept envoys from the Prince; a Prince [?…] [160] outside
another's co-possession, for a principality consists in the country being [?…] in his jurisdiction as absolute
[?dominion]; to such envoys we must give their due honour and send [?…] with pomp; but in the [present] state of the
country we could not give the honour to the envoys of the prince. Pozsony is far; [let him correspond] by the Prince's
order, either by word or letter, with the Pasha of Belgrade; Poland [?…] are close to one another; let him keep the
Sublime Porte constantly informed [?…] the head [?…] of Kassa [?correspond] with the Pasha of Belgrade; [?…] until God
turns [?affairs] to the better, and both we and the Prince will have a better way of talking with the Pasha of
Belgrade. Wherever the Prince [?…] the Prince's trusted man is, there too, if you propose anything, we shall listen. In
so great a matter we must proceed by degrees; it will be better for the Prince too. Mr Hentér was sent back [?…] a
firman is given, I take it myself before the Vizier, I procure the answer. He answered: we know that, but we do not
speak, since until further disposition for the two of us the Prince requires us to stay here; we have recourse with
that, let him admit [us]; yes, tell me, I report it to the Vizier; once [?…] they will give an answer by letter [?…].
There will be a firman for safe conduct [?…]; are you content with this resolution of the Porte? God will soon give
better. The secret treaty: but since he ordered us to suspend Your Majesty's intention, we kept silent. Explanation:
the kiaya seemed to attach [us] to himself, so that this should not change any more. [?…] the Vizier [?…] may judge
from which the Sublime Porte finds difficulty; accordingly he moderates the Transylvanian operations, likewise the
operations with the Pasha of Belgrade, further on. Through the kiaya's favourites too he [?invited] us to a secret
colloquy with the kiaya, so that [?…] the kiaya's answer: he is not an absolute Prince as long as he does not hold it
entirely; [?…] they do not acknowledge such; now they could say nothing else about the admission of the envoys to the
Sublime Porte. [?…] your affair, for it has got on its feet; you have not [?…] in vain so far. The Kecskemét case [?…]
the Turkish Emperor surely keeps it in hand; [?…] not we but the Roman Emperor broke the peace; your affairs have been
entrusted to the Pasha of Belgrade; from there believe the Porte's real will. The Sublime Porte has taken your affairs
so much to heart that the Turkish Emperor too had all your propositions put before him in writing word for word and has
read them. [?…] your many labours will not be in vain; I know the affairs, I knew too what your [?…] will be to the
kiaya, to whom it pertains, I said: it concerns [?…] the King of Sweden's march to the Bohemian border against the
Roman Emperor, [?…] we can carry it through, both the Sublime Porte and the Hungarians [?…] this is the truth; let
Sweden or Poland think of it beforehand [?…], so that he can come to the Sublime Porte, to the serdar of Babadag, about
the Sublime Porte.
