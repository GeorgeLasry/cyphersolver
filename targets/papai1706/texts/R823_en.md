# R823, Pápai and Horváth to Rákóczi, 19 December 1706: English

Translation of `R823_reading.md` (23 Oct 2026), same holes.

## Translation

[…] the King having gone, we at once made ourselves known, and being admitted here without asking on the twelfth of
this month, we reported our long waiting and asked for the promotion of our affairs. He answered: remember my words
which I said to you before, namely that the Sublime Porte does not willingly converse with you in the presence of the
internuncio; your presence, however, is not unwelcome to the Porte. But it is certain that the Prince is treating anew
with the German: for they have come from Vienna [?…] to confer anew on those propositions which the Archbishop of
Kalocsa presented anew; so if you think it is in the interest of your affairs to stay here longer, no one hinders you.
We said: we have no news of this. He asked whether we had received Your Highness's letter. We said: no, since only the
other day the Pasha of Belgrade's courier [?…], bringing Your Highness's letter as far as Adrianople, 465 [?…] his
master's [?…] returned, and that too [?…] Belgrade they hinder. As we [?…] in discourse, he brought up: see, the
Hungarians ought not to shrink so much from hereditary rule, provided it were under laws; the hereditary kings of
England, Denmark and Sweden are nevertheless subject to laws; Hungary too would bind him by law if he became hereditary
King, and so you would keep the fruit [?…]. We answered: lord, the condition of those countries and kings is
different: each of them is a King from their own nation, rules one coalesced nation, governs lawfully, not lawlessly;
but [?Joseph] is German, and we are Hungarians; he has a great empire besides Hungary; in case of [?succession] we
cannot always [?…] against him. It is our perpetual law that we do not acknowledge a hereditary king; and if once we
ourselves infringe that law, then, a breach being left in it, [?invading] through that breach into all our liberties,
he can more easily trample on them. Other kings govern lawfully by the counsel of the head of that country, but us they
oppress lawlessly at their pleasure with Germans. Therefore, the time being now come, through the goodwill of other
confederates, for the Hungarian nation to restore its old liberty, [?…] lose [?…] help or [?win]. He then said: I bring
this up only for discourse; I leave it to the more prudent; I know there are minds in Hungary too. [?…] in the
ministry, always resolving so, the Sublime Porte has noticed the affairs, all that our being here is for. What we are
to do further we humbly await Your Highness's command, for the Porte [?…] Your Highness's affairs so far that here we
[?do] nothing but idle and spend. Having had enough of our service here, may it please you to make such disposition
and provision for our departure that we may part honourably from the Sublime Porte. We [?again] beg Your Highness, for,
gracious lord, with the procedure so far the Sublime Porte will never take Your Highness for anything; [?…] Your
Highness [?…] they penetrate the matter, and they throw people's hearts in our face. If Your Highness indeed wants to
treat with the Sublime Porte, [?Temesvár] [2] why has Your Highness had no man, first at the Porte, on the frontiers,
nor has one now with the Pashas of Belgrade and Temesvár? Of Your Highness's letter written on 31 August we have
received nothing else so far. The letters [?…] here are all worth considering; we [?shall bring] them to Your Highness's
remembrance on the occasion of our departure. To what should we accommodate ourselves in this? [?…] […]
