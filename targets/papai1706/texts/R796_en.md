# R796 — English (János Pápai to Ferenc Rákóczi II, Constantinople, 20 February 1706)

Same holes as R796_reading.md. Modern spelling; codes as names.

Gracious lord! I received with due obedience on the eleventh of this month Your Highness's letter sent undated from by
the Tisza. […] From the letters with it Your Highness can understand at length how matters have gone so far. As for the
false news spread by the Germans, since we have not neglected our duty either, we have carried it out: not only did the
Turk not believe it, but even the confederates despised it, knowing besides that they are wont to multiply the scant
[?truth] of their affairs with many lies. We did not fail to plead for those trapped in Moldavia; but since it
prejudices the articles of the armistice, I see they are reluctant to give a firman to the voivode about it;
nevertheless I shall not cease to [press] this request further, or at least that an order be given to the voivode
through the Moldavian kapukehaya here, whose [260] I have written to his principal; only let Your Highness meanwhile
keep in correspondence with that voivode. I know he protects those exiles anyway; it will be all the better if Your
Highness writes to the serdar of Babadag, who was pasha of Belgrade, since they were moved from there and sent to
Babadag, and he commands Moldavia.

The coming in of the Transylvanian [envoys?] they do not [?…]; [only] in the present state of things do they admit them,
since the Turk regards Transylvania as [his own]. We did not fail to press the Sublime Porte with [the treaties?] of
peace, and we press it still; but it must be admitted that the German never frightened this nation as much as on the
occasion of the last war; that is why it is so reluctant, and does not willingly wish to prejudice the armistice.
Besides, neither the Vizier nor the Turkish Emperor are [martial?] men, but [take] such good news for their pleasure
[…]; if Your Highness writes to the Vizier's chancellor and to the mufti too, [about] the standing of the armistice.
Nevertheless let Your Highness not think that the Sublime Porte will give him help, since by its nature it hates [the
idea] against itself for the future too, and does not wish to settle on the proposals submitted. Until now it has been
good […], but there is no resolution; […] only hope too […]. And I remain Your Highness's humble servant, János Pápai.
Constantinople, one thousand [seven] hundred and six, twentieth of February.

[P.S.] [The kapukehaya?] sent to Albania with Mr Ferenc Horváth […] would remain to go; whoever does not stay here […].
