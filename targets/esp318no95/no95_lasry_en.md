# BnF Espagnol 318 no. 95, f. 122r, 8 January 1497: English translation of George Lasry's edited reading

[null?] Of matters here there is nothing to say, referring myself to what has been said, except that the dearth grows
every day, and it is expected that bread will not be had for money or without it. And the French are bringing many
victuals, and licence for those who make silk, who are a third of this [city], to trade in France; and with these two
things they think to bring these [people] round to their opinion. And together with this they say they are coming with as
many [men] as they can, and money too; and [although?] the [king] provides as he usually does, very late and very little,
he wants to fulfil everything with words. And the Signoria seems to be disposed in this, because it concerns it greatly,
but it waits for the [king] to act. [and?] With this, things are in no good state.

And we have brought Messer Juan Luys here with great trouble, and he shows that he is an enemy of France. And the [King
of the Romans?] deals every day with those who govern here and promises them very great things, but I do not believe they
will come to terms, since they tell me everything, although they hide from the [king]. And it seems they wish to govern
themselves as [Name1?] may please, and in other things (?) [they have] great evil thoughts against the [king]. I do not
know whether they will put anything into effect. And I believe Claver will have sensed something; he will write it to
[Name2?]. And I hinder the [king]'s harm as much as I can, but I cannot speak of it except with great difficulty.

And the [pope] says he wants the [cardinal] of Santa Cruz to go to France to procure peace. And certainly, to achieve it as
it should be, the best would be for the League to do what it ought, so that [Name3?] over there might do what he can, and
so the [King of the Romans?] would be begging those who beg him; otherwise I think this will do more harm than good.
