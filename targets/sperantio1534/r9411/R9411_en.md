# R9411, King John (Zápolya) to the dukes of Bavaria, Buda, 6 Feb 1534: English translation

Translated from `R9411_reading.md` (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026): the whole letter is cipher, read with the
letter's own contemporary interlinear decipherment and the key rebuilt from it. Holes as in the reading: `[..]` not
resolved; a word in [ ] supplied or doubtful. "Your Lordships" renders the reading's *Vestre* / *Vestras*
(Vestrae Dominationes / Illustritates).

## Translation

Greeting and every good thing. Most illustrious princes, lords and dearest brothers. We have received Your
Lordships' letters, and the things which [..] we have also understood, which [..] they wrote [to?] their secretary.
May Your Lordships be persuaded that whatever we can do for the advantage and dignity of Your Lordships and of
their brothers and friends, we shall always do it with the greatest goodwill. And [..] in the business [..] for which
they now write, we shall gladly work; [..] it seems, however, that the matter must be communicated to the
illustrious lord governor of our kingdom, who [cumis] will shortly be here with us. For it seems better to us to
take his counsel on this matter than to try it abruptly with the most mighty emperor of the Turks [..]. Of our own
affairs we [have] nothing else to write to Your Lordships than [now] the same as we have [always] written, whatever
our adversaries may spread abroad. For the rest, Your Lordships, through the magnificent [Caspar ..], our captain and
councillor, while still with us on his first coming to us, concerning the mines of gold, silver and other metals,
which matter has been most dearly at our heart, [..] we have decided [..] with God's help to take the matter in hand.
Nor do we doubt that not only we but others too who are willing to take up this care will gather no small profit
from it. We have now sent this our servant Nicolaus Freeberger to Your Lordships, so that from them [kindly they
ask] the state of such mines; [..] for we had sent to survey those places. And we should gladly see Your Lordships,
too, willing to take up the labour and working of such mines, from which we believe Your Lordships would gather no
small profit. It will also be most welcome to us if Your Lordships send [to find] such things men and masters
skilled in such matters, as the same servant of ours Nicolaus will report more to Your Lordships on this matter in
our name, whose words we ask Your Lordships to believe [in this] respect. Whom we wish to fare most happily. Buda,
6 February, in the year etc. 34.

The said servant of ours [has drawn up] for us an ordinance on mining matters, which he will show to Your Lordships;
also [..] [..] may Your Lordships deign, in friendship, to have it judged by their men skilled in mineral matters
and to send us their judgement on it in writing. We also ask Your Lordships to send us, by a sure messenger, the
mining ordinance which they have [decreed] in the town of [..] and well [..], whereby we may be able to repay [it] in
greater things with our mutual goodwill towards the lords, our dearest brothers and friends. Given as above.
