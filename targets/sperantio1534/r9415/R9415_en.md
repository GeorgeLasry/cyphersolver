# R9415 (BayHStA KAA 4591 ff. 260-261), King John's side to the dukes of Bavaria, 18 April [15]33: English translation

Most illustrious princes, lords, as it were most renowned brothers, greeting and continual increase of perpetual
happiness. Your Lordships' servant Georgius ⟨.10.⟩ ⟨Marijo⟩ has come back to us, who in Your Lordships' name reported to
us the three chief reasons why he had so greatly urged that a concord be made with us at the diet of Pozsony. ⟨And indeed
all these things, which we have now also⟩ experienced in the event itself, under a certain feigned appearance?, whose
words Your Lordships' letters, brought to us afterwards by the Jew? ⟨.33.⟩? Marcus?, sufficiently confirmed. For the
[due]? goodwill of Your Lordships' mind towards us we give and hold great thanks to Your Lordships as to our most dear
lords and brothers, and, if ever an occasion of deserving it should offer itself, we promise our service and indeed all
our zeal in return. ⟨But⟩, most illustrious lords, ⟨how⟩ so sudden a transformation ⟨of all things⟩ has arisen both in
our kingdom and in other places of the earth, we have thought it superfluous to write to Your Lordships in a few words.
⟨How the k(ing)⟩, our enemy, on one side began a treaty with [...] with us, and on the other side secretly begged the
Emperor of the Turks for peace, we believe Your Lordships have already found out. ⟨Of which indeed⟩ secret business we had
[true]? knowledge in good time through letters of the Emperor of the Turks sent to us. ⟨Wherefore⟩ we at once recalled
our ambassadors, distinguished ones indeed. ⟨Moreover⟩ our governor, who had likewise been asked of us by letters of the
Emperor of the Turks as our councillor, with whom the Emperor of the Turks might consult about peace, we have sent. ⟨Hence⟩,
if any news is brought to us, we shall not hide it from Your Lordships, but shall report the whole matter fully, however
it stands, through Georgius. ⟨And hence⟩, although our enemy boasts much and brags that he has obtained peace, and, in his
usual way, consoles his subjects publicly with letters obtained, yet whether he has a certain peace, and on what
conditions, he alone does not know. ⟨Wherefore⟩ we have thought it not amiss to forewarn Your Lordships, lest you embrace
so uncertain a report; ⟨although otherwise⟩ you have his tricks and wiles well explored by us. We, however, who, God as
witness, have always wished to act sincerely and rightly, have nevertheless so far not been able to have an opportunity
with his brother and all the other Christian princes, while he himself has assiduously persecuted us with countless
injuries. ⟨But now all the⟩ blame that he had ascribed to everyone is turned back on him by right?. ⟨It remains⟩ that we
do not wish to hide from Your Lordships what the envoy of the Emperor of the Turks has now done with [...]?. ⟨Although all
these things come forth known to no one⟩ [...]? at Vienna, yet they are not uncertain to us; in the matter [...]? he did
this? alone, namely, that the ambassador whom he now has in Turkey should be his ambassador, and that his ambassador's
words? should be his words, all of which he affirmed; nor do we doubt that with this Turkish friendship of his, once
entered into, he will have wished to bring all the princes of Germany [...]? in the matter to his nod. Still, we could
write to Your Lordships more fully? of [...]? shortly, asking you that? you write to us any news you may have, which we
shall esteem as a mark of great friendship. We therefore commend ourselves to Your Lordships, whom we wish long to fare
well. Given ⟨18 April 33⟩.

Address (clear): ⟨To the noble and magnificent lord ..io Aug..rello?, into his hands⟩.
