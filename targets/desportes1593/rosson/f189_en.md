# BnF fr. 3984 f. 189 (no. 90): Baudouin-Desportes to Girolamo Frachetta, Paris, 22 July 1593: English translation

⟨[J]uly 1593⟩

Our will was good, and our power great enough to make it succeed; but whether we could have held out in the long run,
that is what made us keep still. And meanwhile our friends showed themselves not only neutral but open enemies, and did
what they could to bring about the ruin of the [?friend] of the man of the College, and yours, sparing nothing, putting up
now one candidate, now another, seconded by our Doctor Gratian, who played the part better than any of the others, so
far as to stake his master's authority on it, indeed to compromise it and do it much harm. If our uncle had been willing
to speak according to the promise he had made to the man who went to see him, and if the man of the chain had given other
assurances than words, we would have gone in head down. But believe me, there was no thinking of it: ⟨a man who is far
away speaks of it as a blind man speaks of colours. [?Now]⟩ the best that could be done was protection and to gain time.
That was not done; the truce was necessary. The imprudence of the monarch and his people having been such that he had
made no preparation, or so small a one that the least gentleman in a quarrel would have done more. And on your side, the
disbanding that your [?auditor] carried out was another fine deed. I [?want] to believe what you write me, that there is
no change; yet it is a strange thing that our Poquinteste [a proper name] brought us back nothing in particular, and that
they told us what they pleased; and it was only words, the most extravagant I could have imagined. To lose everything,
even to be deprived of paradise: I would have gone much further; to toil, to ruin oneself, to do what [can] be done, so
that without our prudence we would not last an hour; and for reward, to build a ruin. Now I would not be so charitable,
I confess. If our friend of the College would speak of it to the man of the chain, he would infinitely oblige our friend,
who is wholly his. As to what concerns your pension, that cannot be done, for no one supports it but the prelate to whom
the benefice is assigned, and this is not the time to take anything from him. That was your bad luck and mine. Courage;
other occasions will come. I hold that we shall have the truce in a few days. The journey of [an unidentified sign] has
been the most pernicious thing in the world: it has made [the King of Navarre] change his religion, and offers [?five
hundred] thousand écus. Their party was ruined without this change. Our legate did what he could to cover the Spaniard;
he took up their business with such ardour that he neglected what was of his master's service, so far as not to have
delivered a single one of our [?briefs], which would have done infinite good; but that was not what he [?wanted]; on the
contrary, [he wanted] to hasten the dismemberment of our state. From the letter of [?His Holiness] you will learn the
true general state of our affairs.

⟨I kiss your hands and pray God to give you a most happy and long life. Paris, 22 July 1593. Your servant [paraph].⟩
