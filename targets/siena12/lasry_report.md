Contents
From: the bishop(?) of Fermo (signed “Vescovi de Fermo”?)
To: Sforza Attendolo, “magnifico capitanio”
Place: Naples
Date: 10 March 1413 — written “a dì dieci de marzo, sesta indictione”; the sixth indiction with a king on the 
throne of Naples and Migliorati alive gives 1413
Carlo di Ludovico da Cesena has come to Naples and reports that the King is willing to make a marriage
alliance with Sforza. Two earlier matches fail — one girl was promised to the brother of messer Ludovico
(Migliorati) da Fermo and cannot be withdrawn, the other is only about ten — so the King offers two girls of
his own house: the daughter of Ludovico di messer Gianni de’ Malatesti, eighteen and legitimate, or his
niece, daughter of Niccolò Filippo Brancaleoni of Castel Durante; Sforza may ask for whichever he prefers.
The writer, who has written three times already, urges him to accept and will do as he is told.
Historical context and dating
The letter is dated at Naples on 10 March of the sixth indiction; with a king on the throne of Naples and
Ludovico Migliorati (lord of Fermo 1405–28) alive, that is 10 March 1413 — three weeks before the Perugia
letter of 30 March and the royal memoranda of April 1413 (nos. 20 and 23 of this fascicle), when Ladislaus
was gathering Sforza, Tartaglia and his other captains against Paolo Orsini in the Marche [Siena Concistoro
2308 keys and sources, § dating]. Sforza, a widower since 1411, married Caterina Alopo in 1415, so the royal
offer came to nothing; the Malatesta and Brancaleoni girls are not yet identified (a Sogliano Malatesta and a
Brancaleoni–Durazzo kinship are guesses).
Open points. Unidentified: the two name signs, the signer (“Vescovi de Fermo”?), the girls’ fathers Ludovico
di messer Gianni de’ Malatesti and Niccolò Filippo Brancaleoni of Castel Durante.

Translation
Letter
Magnificent captain, know(?) that Carlo di Ludovico da Cesena has come to Naples, and he has told me that the
lord King is content to make a marriage alliance with you. But of the two girls(?) of [?] [?] [?] nothing(?) seems
possible, because the one who was promised ? to the brother of messer Ludovico da Fermo cannot be undone;
as for the marriage, her sister ? is perhaps ten years old, so she would not be suitable(?).
Now it is new(?), but the lord King says that he has [?] two beautiful girls of his house: one is the daughter of
Ludovico di messer Gianne de' Malatesti; she is very beautiful and legitimate, eighteen years old. The other is a
niece of the lord King on the side of [?]; she is the daughter of Niccolò Filippo(?) di Brancaleoni of Castel
Durante: [she is] very beautiful and legitimate. The lord King wants to give you whichever of the two you like,
provided that Your Lordship(?) has her asked for.
Now that is all. This I have said to Your Lordship(?). Now it rests with you: if you write to me, I shall do what you
tell me. Now it rests with you. I for my part advise and beg you to it. I have already written it to you three
times. Given at Naples on the tenth day of March, sixth indiction. Vescovi(?) de Fermo, wholly yours in soul and
in body.
Address
To the [stre]nuous man Sforza, [magn]ificent captain, lord ... let it be delivered : [?] lord [?].
