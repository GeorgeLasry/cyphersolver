# R1026 — Van Reede to Van de Spiegel, Berlin, 29 December 1787: English

Translation of `R1026_reading.md` (same holes; modern spelling of names: Hertzberg, Finckenstein).

## Translation

Berlin, 29 December 1787. Sir,

The day before yesterday the two Ministers put to the King the project of the alliance as you have it, Sir. As they
went on differing in opinion, I asked Monsieur de Hertzberg to set out the views separately in the report and to give
the reasons for his own, and he did so. The answer arrived yesterday evening and it seems good to me. There is to be a
conference on this subject again this morning at ten, between the King and his two Ministers. I hope there will be no
change. The only point [?raised] so far is that the King said he could not guarantee our possessions overseas. I did
not ask for that, but only that, should they be attacked by a European Power, the subsidy [?be furnished] in money.
Perhaps His Majesty understands by "guarantee" furnishing the subsidy; but even if that were so, I do not yet despair
of obtaining this point. Article seven, which I had the honour of sending you separately, is suppressed as
disagreeable to England, at the request of Count Finckenstein. Monsieur de Hertzberg has made the Treaty temporary and
fixed it at twenty years. That is, so far, the only difference between what was settled yesterday and what I had the
honour of sending you.

Since this was put into cipher, Sir, I have been, on coming out of a Conference that the two Ministers had with the
King, on a visit to their Excellencies, who told me that my audience could not take place before Wednesday. The
ceremonial has been settled in a fitting manner, and although I am little versed in forms, I [?consider it] the most
[?esteemed] [?here] to acknowledge the attention of Their High Mightinesses with the greatest courtesies. The King
was pleased to crown it by deciding of his own accord, although this was not done in the time of Monsieur de Tessin,
Ambassador of Sweden, that I should have [?that] day the honour of dining at his table.

I learn that it was decided at this morning's conference that, without spelling out the words "possessions overseas",
it would simply be said that if the Republic were attacked by a European Power the King would furnish it the
stipulated aid, thus leaving the matter as it were undecided. However, if Their High Mightinesses order me to insist
on having the article [?stated] more clearly, I believe I shall be able to obtain it, from the way this was said to me.

If I have understood rightly, I need not expect other changes. I have the honour to be with a very profound respect,
Sir, your very humble and very obedient servant, A. W. van Reede.
