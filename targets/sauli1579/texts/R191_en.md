# R191, Portogallo 8 ff. 98r-101r: Cardinal Riario, legate, to the Cardinal Secretary, [Elvas, mid-January 1581]

## Letter

After I had written to Your Lordship, His Majesty's ministers brought me the {second report of the destruction of those
poor Italians}, attached and marked A, {written by} His Majesty's ambassador {who is in England}; and almost at the
same time I was given in His Majesty's name, as a final decision in this business, the offers that will be attached,
to which I gave the answer Your Lordship will see on the same sheet marked B, in line with what Monsignor Nuncio wrote
to me and sent to negotiate here by one {called Ortiz} expressly. At almost the same time two letters of Signor
Prospero arrived, attached, one to Signor Duara marked C and one to me marked D; and Idiáquez brought me an original
{from the Duke of Alba to His Majesty, saying that the Italians embarked were 700}, and that all were going off
{fleeing by land, and even of those embarked some were escaping}; that they were for the most part {sick} and all
without {arms and clothes}, and so {reduced} that he did not judge them fit for any kind {of enterprise}, and asked
leave to send them back {to Italy}; and that, most of them being {subjects of the Grand Duke, the captains} made
difficulties about going on {any enterprise} without {his licence}.

And the said Idiáquez told me that because of {these reports}, and since I did not accept the offer outright, knowing
that they were plainly being sent {to be slaughtered, all of them}, in the state they are in, His Majesty felt it on
his conscience {to send to destruction this nation} that had served him so well; that he had the best will and
resolve {for this enterprise}, but with due preparation and consideration, showing that greater {forces than those}
were needed [...] to His Holiness, and for this he resolved {that he could not send men for now}. I replied that His
Majesty could take others {with Italians, re-arming them and giving them clothing}, and that as to the difficulty of
the {Grand Duke's licence} I would overcome it with Signor Don Pietro and Signor Duara, as I did; indeed Signor Duara
showed me an original letter {of the Grand Duke} with the attached article marked E; and that I offered myself, with
the other subjects of His Holiness, {captains and soldiers}, begging and ordering them as His Majesty thought best, so
that {they would serve}, and that I would give {patents and the name of His Holiness} in everything, as Monsignor Nuncio
did for the {other soldiers sent}, but that I should not {take charge} for many reasons, nor promise what I had not been
ordered. In conclusion I believe His Majesty has ordered {that they return}, and is about to send {two ships} {to get
information on the state of that island} so as to inform His Holiness; and perhaps, as I hope from the words of His
Majesty and his ministers, to make some good and well-founded decision, which God grant, for the service of God and
His Majesty and the satisfaction of His Holiness; and while I am here I shall miss no occasion to pursue all this.

His Majesty decided to send {the two ships towards Ireland} to get {news of that island} and to make sure of the
{reports}, repeated, {of the loss of those soldiers}, and on that occasion I thought it a good and fitting office to
write {to the Earl of Desmond and his brother} what Your Lordship will see in the copy that comes with this, all to keep
them {heartened} for every good effect; and I have sent {two friars of those whom} Monsignor Nuncio sent before, to get
{surer certainty} of what is happening {in that island}.

And since Your Lordship wrote that I should take the occasion to help the affairs {of the Duke of Sora, my lord} with
these ministers, you will know that I have not failed, with the affection and obligation I owe; and although I hold
that His Majesty has the best intentions toward him, having had occasion to speak of it over the dispatch of a
secretary {of His Excellency} to His Majesty, I find among these ministers that with His Majesty {nothing but many bad
offices have been done against the Duke}, and I have heard it from others too; all come from over there, {may God
forgive those who do them}. But I hope despite all this {that the Duke} will know that I am his devoted, loving and
most obliged servant; and {the office done for His Excellency at the Duke of Alba's request} [...] through the {Dean of
Calahorra} was hardly to the purpose for the service {of His Excellency}, and I know what I am saying.

## Enclosure E: "From the Grand Duke to Duara"

And as for the {soldiers}, if they are {discharged}, as is believed, we shall be glad if not only is it seen to that they
are {paid}, but that they are also given the means to {return}, because His Majesty {will be able to use them} on any
other occasion; whereas if they came to {harm}, we would lose {these good men} and His Majesty would deprive himself of
them {for another time}. But if His Majesty should judge it for his service {to send them to Ireland} or elsewhere, let
him use them as he pleases, provided they are {treated} so that they can {serve His Majesty}.

## Addition (f. 101r)

I have learned {from a good source, as certain, that His Majesty is now about to discharge that Anselmo, to whom his
ministers gave 4,000 scudi a month in pay, and they owe him 4 or 6 months, and are now finding money to be rid of him}.
