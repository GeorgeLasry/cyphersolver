# R194, Portogallo 8 f. 281r-v: Cardinal Riario to the Cardinal Secretary, [late 1580 - 1581]

On the occasion of being written to and having to send {the Irish captains} to His Majesty, as Monsignor Nuncio had
written to me, I invited His Majesty to give me some good and sure intimation of his will {regarding the enterprise}
desired by His Holiness for the service of God, of the Christian religion, and all to the honour and greatness of His
Majesty; who answered that he had the best will and inclination, but that it needed {a great provision of money}, and
that His Holiness not only did not help him with new graces but denied him the usual ones, and that his real aim was to
attend {to Flanders} and then to {this enterprise}. I answered that if His Majesty wished, he could very well attend to
both with {his great forces}, and that, as I understand, it might be more convenient for the affairs {of Flanders} to
begin {with those of England}; and that as to the graces I could not speak, but I hoped His Holiness governed with the
greatest prudence and loved His Majesty cordially, and I believed that when His Majesty resolved to say and act in
earnest he could hope for every reasonable aid from His Holiness. I also tried to get from His Majesty some decision on
the business {of the truce}; he told me there was nothing yet, and that he was trying to free some man of his over there,
but that necessity pressed him, that he did not know what he would do, and that he found the whole weight {of
Christendom} on his back, and that {to maintain the Catholic religion in Flanders} he had spent an infinite amount of
money, of which, by my reckoning {of State}, he could have kept a great part had he been willing to give way {to them}
[...] thus {in religion}. I answered what seemed fitting in this case for the service of God, of His Majesty and my
duty, but could get no other answer.
