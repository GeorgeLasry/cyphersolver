# R192, Portogallo 8 ff. 195-197: Cardinal Riario to the Cardinal Secretary, [Elvas], 4-5 January 1581, with a copy of 29 November 1580

## Letter

Today, the 4th of January, I spoke to His Majesty {about the Piedmont business}, so that His Majesty was very content and
showed himself much obliged to His Holiness, whose feet he kisses a thousand times for this kind {warning}, {telling me
that there is something in it concerning those Anselmi, putting their men into Italy}; and that he would have {the
letters they had on this business} searched for, and would send Idiáquez to me to inform me of everything, so that I
could {report to His Holiness}; so I shall wait and report what happens. But certainly {His Majesty has taken the
warning in very good part, and the Piedmont affair I believe is true, but I believe it will not turn out wholly to His
Majesty's satisfaction}, who readily has the same {suspicion as His Holiness}.

At this moment, on the evening of the 5th, Signor Idiáquez came in His Majesty's name to tell me {that it is true that
the Marquis of Ayamonte, formerly Governor of Milan,} began and kept up {the dealings and encouragement} by some means
{with those Anselmi}, but that it always displeased {His Majesty}, who at the time {the Marquis} died wrote him his mind
frankly, which was that it displeased him; and that His Holiness may be sure that he will not only not procure or
consent {to anything that might disturb Italy}, it mattering to him as much as the {peace of Castile} itself, but that
wherever needed to prevent {any disturbance} that might arise he will employ himself most powerfully {with all his
forces}; adding that {the encouragement the Marquis gave those Anselmi} was on this reasoning, that if they found
themselves abandoned they would not draw {to their aid into Italy a fury of Huguenots from the Dauphiné}, and that in
this business {the late Duke of Savoy had a hand}, who was a great friend {of the said Marquis}; and again His Majesty
kisses His Holiness's feet for this kind {warning}, showing {that such dealings have ceased}.

I pressed {the English business}, as I wrote to Your Lordship, and referred to the decision I sent you before, which was
an answer in writing to Idiáquez, of which he has now sent me the duplicate. Seeing the {general enterprise} all but
excluded, I sought {the aid of 4 or 5 thousand men} with the other necessary provisions, since this had been promised to
Monsignor Nuncio; and others say {that because of the release of Don Antonio that promise was revoked}; so that, {His
Majesty's army} being much reduced, which I do not think reaches {5 or 6 thousand men or more}, and this {kingdom} needing
a great part of it, the business was finally brought to this, that His Majesty would send {all the Italians with
artillery, arms and munitions, victualled and paid for 4 months}, who at the {muster were 3,000 in the pay}, but [...]
little more than {[...] thousand}; and although His Majesty wanted me to take charge of providing for them from now once
{the 4 months} were over, or that Monsignor Nuncio should, having however reduced His Majesty to this, that having sent
them {with the said munitions, victuals and pay, about 2,000 foot}, if within three months they had no other orders they
should come back, without our taking any other care; now that His Majesty wanted {a commander found, and that they go
under His Holiness's name}, and while they held this {relief} certain in this form, to inform His Holiness, so that in
the said term {new aid} could be procured and meanwhile {the enemy} harassed, and {the soldiers} sent there not left {to
perish}, and {the Catholics} heartened, the report came that Your Lordship will see, which has upset everything and seems
to be confirmed by way {of France and England}, at which His Majesty's ministers seem to have {cooled}, and now offer {the
Italians such and as many as} there are, but say they will not get involved in sending or appointing any {commander},
believing rather that none will be found, since they would be going into {clear danger} of all being {cut to pieces, the
Queen of England having ships and men} there in strength, as they say; so I would take charge of nothing except giving
{the name to the commander} they appoint, a man {of His Holiness}, that they should send as many {men} as possible with
the said provisions and a good {escort}, who will see whether they can achieve something, or [?seeing] {the danger plain,
they could withdraw}. I do not know what they will decide. On my side nothing possible will be left undone; I spoke of
all this to His Majesty yesterday, having dealt with this business according to the letter Monsignor Nuncio wrote me, of
which he sent me a copy, which seemed to me very much to the point, and before I had it I was treating it in the same way
as I wrote to His Holiness. I shall report to Your Lordship what happens, and kiss your hands.

## Copy, 29 November 1580

His Majesty's ministers who are sending dispatches to Portugal tonight urgently ask Your Lordship's answer about {the
business of the Italians}, to send it to the Duke of Alba, so it will be needed by tomorrow morning; because, as to {the
enterprise} Your Lordship proposed, I told them at length how much His Majesty wishes to employ himself in everything
that may be for the increase {of Christendom}, as he will do as soon as affairs give him room and do not force him to
keep {his forces} busy {as a remedy} for his own; but Your Lordship already sees that for {such enterprises}, and
particularly {that of England}, the true and direct road would have been and would be to pacify {the states of
Flanders}, His Holiness helping His Majesty in this as largely and liberally as in this {war} which has been waged for so
many years against {most obstinate spirits, to defend the Roman Catholic religion}, after which from {those states} {the
passage into England} would be very easy; and if Your Lordship considers the capital {of money and forces} that {an
enterprise} as great as the one proposed requires, you will see the [?need] and the extraordinary {aid} that ought to be
offered, [...] urges it so, and how far from this His Majesty's due has been put off. Nevertheless, if it seems to Your
Lordship, in His Holiness's name, not out of place for now to send forward {the aid His Holiness} has begun {to give the
Catholics of Ireland}, and you judge that this could in time serve {almost as ports for England}, I say again that His
Majesty {offers him the Italians that remain in Portugal, paid}, and today {embarked in the river of Lisbon} with
{victuals for 3 months and some money} in reserve {to relieve them}, on the understanding that {Your Lordship takes
charge} on His Holiness's part {from Lisbon} {before these men}; and if you will not accept it, nor think it fitting,
they will go at once {towards Italy}, since they wait only for this answer and cannot {be delayed any longer}. From
Ca[?s]a, 29 November 1580.
