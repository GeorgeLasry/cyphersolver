# R190, Portogallo 8 ff. 92r-95r: Cardinal Riario's papers on the papal troops in Ireland, Elvas, January 1581

## 1. To the Earl of Desmond, Elvas, 12 January 1581

I have heard with great satisfaction how valiantly Your Lordship bears the charge there {of His Holiness's soldiers},
and how you do not cease to take the best care of them and to make what progress is possible. Thanking you warmly
for this, for the service of God, which is pursued with infinite zeal {by His Beatitude}, I tell you that here no
effort has been spared to obtain {strong aid} for you; and it has been held back because of the news that has come to
this court {that the fort held by your force has been lost, and part of those soldiers too}. But be assured that every
office is being done with His Holiness and His Majesty so that {notable relief} may be made ready; and meanwhile do not
fail to attend {to the enterprise} with the same valour and vigilance, hoping that I shall always obtain for you from
these two princes all I can to your liking. And so I commend myself to you, praying God for your health and that he
always prosper all your holy actions. From Elvas, 12 January 1581.

## 2. News from court (f. 93r)

While writing this I am told how {a captain from Ireland came to tell the Queen of England that all His Holiness's
soldiers} who were {in the fort} ... {the viceroy had cut them to pieces} ... [?without] their having made {any
resistance}, but letting themselves {be killed like beasts}; and although he does not give the day, {the celebration}
that {the Queen} has made of it makes it held {for true}. And it is not possible that they managed well, since after
{the viceroy} had resolved not {to attack them}, for this [...] the second time to take them.

## 3. Memorial to Philip II (ff. 94r-95r)

In the last {payment} His Majesty made to the {Italian infantry} he paid {3,037 foot}, and as to the number of the
{men fit for service, they come to a thousand} and a little more; and so His Majesty offers to make them up {to 2,000
with Spaniards, victualled and paid for 4 months}, on condition that the {legate takes charge of them} from now,
caring to provide for them and to give them [...] intention for when the said {4 months} have passed; or His Majesty
will give {the Italians such as they are and as many as} there are, but under {His Holiness's name, paid and victualled
for 4 months}, with orders to come back once the three months have passed if they have no other orders, so as to have
that month free to {return to their homes}; and if {Signor Prospero Colonna} will not go as {their commander}, His
Majesty wants the {legate} to provide {a commander to go}, the said {legate} dealing with that {commander}, who within
eight days should take up {the men at Lisbon}. True or not {the report}, I am of opinion, and beg His Majesty for the
service of God, of His Majesty himself and the satisfaction of His Holiness, that {the aid or relief} should go [?in any
case] as strong as possible, {with arms, munitions, victuals, money and artillery}, for as many {months} as can be, so
that there is time to arrange greater {aid}, and meanwhile to hearten {the Catholics of the island} and gain a foothold
{in a port, which Pietro Ortiz} says can be done easily in any case, even if it were the port {of Smerwick}, which was
the worst {port} of all; and he says that even if {the Queen's fleet} were there and met them, they would overcome it
and take {the port}; and since the other is not lost, {those poor men} would be relieved so that they are not {lost},
and would rather make progress. It does not seem good to send {a commander} who could rival the {Earl of Desmond}, who
already holds the title {of General} in the {island}, but to send {a commissary} to hand these {men to the said Earl},
someone not inexperienced in {war}, so that he can {command} on the way if need be; and this will be a smaller
{expense} for His Majesty, making only a {colonel} to serve {under the Earl}, to which officers His Holiness's {patents}
will be given; it being right in every respect to do as soon as possible what must be done, to the praise of God. And
if His Majesty thought fit, it would be good to send {an escort with a zabra} so as to inform {these men} of the state
of things, and also to send with these {soldiers the English and Irish} who have served {in Flanders} and are here,
with some gift {to the Earl of Desmond}; and everything is left to His Majesty's prudence and piety.
