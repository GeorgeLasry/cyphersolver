# R193, Portogallo 8 ff. 236-238: two letters of Cardinal Riario to the Cardinal of Como

## 1. Badajoz, 2 [October?] 1580

Most Illustrious and Reverend Lord. Eight days ago the Secretary, Signor Idiáquez, came in His Majesty's name to tell me
that, as I had pressed His Majesty {for the English enterprise} in His Holiness's name, His Majesty greatly wished to
please His Holiness in what was his satisfaction and the service of Christendom, but was in no position for {this
enterprise}, which needed very great {preparations and men}; and that the aids and graces offered to him in His
Holiness's name, meaning by Monsignor Nuncio (for I have never gone into particulars on this), were weak {for such an
enterprise}, which he had to put off until he was {master of Flanders}, which would be safer and easier; and meanwhile
{for Flanders} His Holiness should give him generous aid, complaining that now, on the pretext that the galleys were in
Portugal, or that the term had not ended, His Holiness was delaying the confirmation of the galley subsidy; and that I
ought to do good offices in this, showing I believed I had done the contrary; and with all that, in His Majesty's name,
he offered me {the Italians for Ireland} as Your Lordship will see in the note marked A. I answered with the hope His
Holiness had in His Majesty {for this enterprise}, with many reasons, and the hopes His Majesty had given, to me in
particular; I showed him that the {aids} they spoke of, although I had no commission for them, were of greater weight
than they made out, and that when His Holiness had seen {His Majesty's happy progress} in this business, one could
believe His Holiness would have done what he could to His Majesty's satisfaction. As for the graces, that was not my
business, and I had done neither good nor harm in it; His Holiness, most prudent and inspired by God, knew what he was
doing. As for the offer {for this Irish matter}, it had not been my negotiation, especially if there was no intention
of going on {into England}, and I would write of it to Monsignor Nuncio if they gave me time; so they sent a courier,
having had it put in writing as seen in the note marked A, and answered in writing as in mine marked B; and I wrote to
Monsignor Nuncio as in the letter marked C, who answered me with that marked D, which arrived last night, where, as
Your Lordship will see, he reasons prudently on this business, but will not take any charge {of these soldiers}, who
they tell me do not reach {1,300, and badly armed}. And I thought it right to accept that offer, but to beg His Majesty
through Idiáquez to wait for this post in eight days, so that His Holiness's mind can be better seen, pointing out that
if His Majesty means to put me in charge {of these foot}, he ought to help {those poor wretches}, his vassals {sent
there}, especially as they have made some gain and give good hopes, by sending them {aid, arms and munitions}, as
Monsignor Nuncio will have told Your Lordship, whose hands I humbly kiss, and God keep you well. From Badajoz, 2
[?October] 1580. Your Lordship's most humble and devoted servant, A. Cardinal Riario.

## 2. Elvas, 1[?0] December 1580 (deciphered copy)

Most Illustrious and Reverend Lord. After sending the dispatch for Monsignor Marco at Madrid I received the attached note
from Councillor Vázquez, from which Your Lordship will understand how far they are advancing on their side in their claims
in this business, to bring it, in my judgement, to leaving the business to His Holiness without jurisdiction, to be taken
from his hands without the intervention of His Holiness's ministers; and the compromises offered by Mr. Montio were not to
be put forward unless they were concluded at the same time; indeed they say (which I do not believe) that he came into
those of the President, except that the preamble declared they should last as long as His Majesty pleased; and this the
said Councillor told me. And from what I know and hear from good quarters, this {President is a stone of scandal and ill
disposed to the Roman Court, and whoever defends and covers him does so out of friendship and perhaps with good intent,
but I think it right to report what I discover and hear, leaving the truth to be found later}. Having just dismounted at
Elvas, I found the enclosed from the Archbishop of Évora with the possessions [...] of Your Lordship's servant; and with
this I end, humbly kissing your hands. 1[?0] December 1580. A. Cardinal Riario.
