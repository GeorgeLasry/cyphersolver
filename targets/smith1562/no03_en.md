# No. 03, Smith to Cecil, Blois, 7 March 1563 — English

⟨The Duke of Guise's death, and the Queen's bold and courageous dealings in paying the Admiral (Coligny) and making
other preparations, had brought them⟩ right into my lap. ⟨You know now what is to be done: you must have⟩ [?musters]
held and captains appointed, ⟨and everything done that can be done⟩ without cost. Let the Admiral know ⟨that you mean
to send a force after him; and if they will not agree to your wishes, that you will⟩ have all Normandy back this summer.
⟨I wish you would show the courage I show them.⟩ I tell them plainly that I know ⟨what money, munitions and captains⟩
they have, ⟨and what men, and what kind of men⟩ they can raise; ⟨and that⟩ the Queen, ⟨now that the season favours it,
will no longer be trifled with.⟩

As for hostages or securities for Calais to be restored later, ⟨I will not hear of it;⟩ no, nor of holding Newhaven (Le
Havre) quietly so long. ⟨I tell them I will never put it to the Queen, for they shall never have love, amity, true peace
or friendship with England as long⟩ as they keep Calais from us. ⟨As they chose their moment for Boulogne, they may be
sure we will choose ours, either⟩ to have Calais back ⟨or something five times as good.⟩

⟨This far we have come: they have, though with great difficulty,⟩ admitted their weakness and their need of our
friendship. They are now beginning to court me, ⟨and⟩ have offered more than I will yet vouch for to you, ⟨because it
has not yet been confirmed to me as I would like.⟩ ⟨What I fear most, and only, is the meddling⟩ of the Queen of Scots.

⟨I gather from you that⟩ the Queen ⟨is very unwilling to grant any more allowance for my expenses.⟩
