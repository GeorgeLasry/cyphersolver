# No. 01, Sir Thomas Smith to the Queen, Chartres, 17 January 1562/3

⟨The 14th came a secretary with letters⟩ from the Cardinal of Lorraine.

⟨I do take it marvellous necessary, that⟩ some man were sent to be with the Admiral who now is chief on the other
side; that your Majesty may be as well advertised what is done there, as you may be what is done here. Of things
done on that side ⟨we can learn else but only such as it pleaseth them here to bruit abroad.⟩ And he would be one who
hath some familiarity, acquaintance, and credit with them; ⟨and⟩ I think Mr Middlemore were a very ⟨handsome and fit
man.⟩ As for help, if your Majesty be not minded to revoke me, ⟨I do not doubt but to do well enough.⟩

⟨I may be deceived; but by all the means, ways, and intelligence which I can learn, they mind⟩ to come to no
agreement nor appointment with your Majesty: ⟨they will rather⟩ agree to all the Admiral's demands. ⟨As for religion,
they make no difficulty here in words.⟩ For the offices; the Constable ⟨shall greatly help to it: who will begin and
doth now, as men say,⟩ suspect the greatness of the Duke of Guise, and consider the ruin of the house of
Chastillon, being his nearest kindred in France, next his own children.

⟨Yet methinks I do see,⟩ that if the Admiral do hold out a while, or give them again but a doubtful battle as the
last was, ⟨necessity will compel them to come to accord.⟩ ⟨What consideration⟩ the Admiral and his sequel will
have of your Majesty, ⟨I am not able to say; with whom I never had conference nor acquaintance:⟩ but if one were
with him from your Majesty as your minister, ⟨I would think, it would stand your Highness to so good purpose, as none
could be more.⟩

⟨Marry, for all that I cannot yet see or learn, best it were for your Majesty to trust unto your own strength, and so
provide, as that⟩ which you would do were done with all speed; to lose no time, so long as you may have help of the
French of that faction which be now at your devotion; to use both their countenance and help, while time may serve
thereto: making of them as confederates ⟨who be soon weary of the travail, where all the profit is not theirs.⟩
