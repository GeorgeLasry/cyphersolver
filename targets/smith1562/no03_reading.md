# No. 03, Sir Thomas Smith to Cecil, Blois, 7 March 1562/3

⟨The Guise's death, and the Queen's Majesty's stout and courageous doings in paying the Admiral, and making such other
preparation, had brought them⟩ even into my lap. ⟨Ye know now what is to be done: ye must cause⟩ [?musters] to be made,
appointing of captains, ⟨and all such as may be done⟩ without charge. Let the Admiral know, ⟨that you intend to send a
power even after him; and if they will not agree to your desires, that ye will⟩ have all Normandy again this summer.
⟨I would ye did show that courage which I do to them.⟩ I tell them plainly, I know ⟨what money, what munition, and what
captains⟩ they have; ⟨and what men, and what manner of men⟩ they are able to make; ⟨and that⟩ the Queen's Majesty,
⟨now the weather doth come in propice for it, will not longer be dallied withal.⟩

For hostages, or assurances for Calais hereafter to be rendered, ⟨I will not once hear of it;⟩ no, nor to keep Newhaven
quietly so long. ⟨I tell them, I will never motion it to the Queen's Majesty: for I assure them, they shall never have
love, amity, true peace, nor friendship with England, so long⟩ as they keep Calais from us. ⟨As they took their time for
Boulogne, they shall be well assured, we will take ours, either⟩ to have Calais again, ⟨or that which shall be five times
so good.⟩

⟨Thus far we have gone: and they have to me, although marvellous hardly,⟩ confessed their weakness, and their need of
our friendship. They begin now to seek upon me; ⟨and⟩ have offered more than I will yet affirm to you, ⟨because it is
not yet so affirmed to me as I would have it.⟩ ⟨I fear yet most, and only, the entermeddling⟩ of the Queen of Scots.

⟨I perceive by you, that⟩ the Queen's Majesty is ⟨very hard to depart with any more allowance for my diets.⟩
