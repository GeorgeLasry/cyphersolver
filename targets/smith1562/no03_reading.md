# No. 03, Sir Thomas Smith to Cecil, Blois, 7 March 1562/3

⟨The Guise's death, and the Queene's Majestie's stowte and courageous doengs in paieng the Admirall, and making such other
preparation, had brought them⟩ even into my lappe. ⟨Ye know now what is to be done: ye must cawse⟩ [?musters] to be made,
appointing of captains, ⟨and all suche as may be done⟩ without charge. Let the Admirall know, ⟨that yow intend to send a
power even after him; and yf they will not agree to your desires, that ye will⟩ have all Normandy again this summer.
⟨I wold ye did show that courage which I do to them.⟩ I tell them plainly, I know ⟨what mony, what municion, and what
captaines⟩ they have; ⟨and what men, and what manner of men⟩ they are able to make; ⟨and that⟩ the Queene's Majestie,
⟨now the weather doth com in propyce for it, will not lenger be dalied withall.⟩

For hostages, or assurances for Calais hereafter to be rendred, ⟨I will not once here of it;⟩ no, nor to kepe Newhaven
quietly so long. ⟨I tell them, I will never mocion yt to the Queene's Majestie: for I assure them, thei shall never have
love, amitie, true peace, nor friendshippe with England, so long⟩ as they kepe Calais from us. ⟨As they toke ther tyme for
Boulogne, thei shal be well assured, we will take ours, eyther⟩ to have Calais again, ⟨or that which shal be five tymes
so good.⟩

⟨Thus farre we have gone: and thei have to me, although marvelous hardely,⟩ confessid ther weaknes, and ther nede of
our friendship. They begin now to seke upon me; ⟨and⟩ have offered more than I will yet affirm to you, ⟨bicause it is
not yet so affirmid to me as I wold have it.⟩ ⟨I feare yet most, and only, the entermeadling⟩ of the Queen of Scots.

⟨I perceive by yow, that⟩ the Queen's Majesty is ⟨verie hard to departe with any more allowance for my dietts.⟩
