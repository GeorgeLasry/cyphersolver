# No. 04, Sir Thomas Smith to the Queen, Blois, 12 March 1562/3

⟨It is muttered privily here, that⟩ the King of Spain's ⟨messenger was detroussed by the way⟩ into Italy, ⟨and his
packet brought to⟩ the French Ambassador at Trent: ⟨who, opening the packet, in reading of the letters did discover a
practice⟩ between the King of Spain and the Emperor ⟨to invade⟩ France: ⟨whereof he hath advertised the Queen here.
This goeth now abroad: and they here do suspect, that⟩ the King of Spain and your Majesty are agreed; ⟨and that he can
be content, ye should make⟩ war with France ⟨to recover⟩ Calais, yea and more too, ⟨if need be. I do not perceive much
otherwise myself, by the familiar conferences which I have⟩ with the Spanish Ambassador. ⟨How so ever⟩ the French ⟨have
agreed within themselves, as hitherto, by all the means I can make, I can learn no certainty.⟩

⟨I perceive, a great stay of the matter⟩ doth lie in the Admiral: who, as I understand, ⟨doth not like the conditions
so well⟩ as the Prince doth, ⟨and holdeth stoutly out; and will first have the King to avow that army which the Prince
made, and all their doings, as done by the Queen's commandment; and that he will have⟩ your Majesty ⟨and their⟩ other
allies of Germany ⟨satisfied of such things⟩ as are promised them.

⟨Yesterday the Chancellor went to the camp. This night, which is the 12th, he will be there.⟩ The Admiral is looked for
to come hither in post, with five ⟨or six horses only: and all is said to stay⟩ upon his coming. ⟨I would to God, he did⟩
work wisely; that he be not attrapped ⟨either by the way or here. I do not like, nor cannot but mistrust, so long⟩ as the
Duke d'Aumale and the Guisians ⟨be so great⟩ about the Queen Mother, ⟨making such a bruit to run⟩ upon the Admiral ⟨as
they have, lest they make him in that manner⟩ to be slain, ⟨as they say⟩ he caused the Duke of Guise to be killed.

⟨Talking with⟩ the Ambassador of Spain ⟨(with whom I am now very familiar) and asking of him, what news, seeing I heard
say, that the day before he had⟩ set with the council in the camp, ⟨and as I will now and then say merrily unto him, that
he⟩ governeth all the council of France; ⟨he would tell me nothing: for he said, they were so dismayed and troubled with
the news⟩ of the rendering of the castle of Caen, ⟨that they wist not what to say or do. And still he was enquiring of
me, whether⟩ the Englishmen had it wholly ⟨in their government, or at the least were able⟩ to master the Frenchmen. ⟨I
said, as yet I had no certainty. Well, saith he,⟩ if you be masters there, ⟨I dare give you⟩ Calais, yea and ⟨three
hundred⟩ of the ⟨five hundred⟩ thousand crowns ⟨which ye⟩ ask for the forfeit, rather ⟨than ye should keep that⟩ castle,
Dieppe, and Newhaven: ⟨and if ye⟩ keep ⟨those in your hands;⟩ your peace is made, if the Queen's Majesty will, ⟨and all
your⟩ requests granted. ⟨But he doth not think, either⟩ the Admiral, or any other Frenchman to be trusted; ⟨but in any
wise he would, that⟩ in all those three Englishmen should be ⟨only, or at the least⟩ able to master all the rest, at all
times.

⟨I have sent your Majesty such⟩ letters out of Orleans, ⟨as ye shall perceive by them from whence they come, and of what
importance they be to understand⟩ the state of affairs as they go here. ⟨They have won⟩ the Prince of Condé: ⟨and I know
he hath promised, that,⟩ granting his demands, ⟨he will be one of the first and chief,⟩ after one or two summons, that
shall help to drive your power out of France. ⟨I have received also letters from⟩ the Admiral and Sir Nicholas from
Caen, ⟨at the last.⟩ Those of Orleans ⟨I take of most importance. One of my men I have⟩ lost there. Orleans is kept as
straight now, in this truce time, as ever it was. ⟨In the rest which came from⟩ Caen ⟨there was no great matter, but of
the rendering⟩ of Bayeux and Falaise. ⟨If⟩ the Admiral ⟨be no trustier⟩ than the Prince, ⟨I do not see but all the war⟩
will be turned thither. ⟨By⟩ the Emperor's messenger, Monsieur Achilles, ⟨I cannot perceive, that⟩ the French King ⟨will
render⟩ Metz, Toul, and Verdun: ⟨and, not so doing, for ought I can learn by him,⟩ the Emperor will denounce war. But if
the Admiral hold out and be trusty, all shall be well.
