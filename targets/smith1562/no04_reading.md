# No. 04, Sir Thomas Smith to the Queen, Blois, 12 March 1562/3

⟨It is muttered privilie here, that⟩ the King of Spain's ⟨messenger was detroussed by the way⟩ into Italy, ⟨and his
packet brought to⟩ the French Ambassadour at Trent: ⟨who, opening the packet, in reading of the lettres did discover a
practize⟩ between the King of Spain and the Emperor ⟨to invade⟩ France: ⟨wherof he hath advertized the Queene here.
This goeth now abrode: and thei here do suspecte, that⟩ the King of Spain and your Majestie are agreed; ⟨and that he can
be content, ye should make⟩ war with France ⟨to recover⟩ Calais, yea and more too, ⟨yf nede be. I do not perceive much
otherwyse myself, by the familiar conferences which I have⟩ with the Spanish Ambassadour. ⟨How so ever⟩ the French ⟨have
agreed within themselfs, as hitherto, by all the means I can make, I can learne no certainty.⟩

⟨I perceive, a greate staie of the matter⟩ doth lye in the Admiral: who, as I understand, ⟨doth not like the conditions
so well⟩ as the Prince doth, ⟨and holdeth stoutly owte; and will furst have the King to avow that armie which the Prince
made, and all ther doengs, as done by the Queen's comaundement; and that he will have⟩ your Majesty ⟨and their⟩ other
alies of Germany ⟨satisfied of such things⟩ as are promised them.

⟨Yesterday the Chancellor went to the campe. This night, which is the 12th, he wil be there.⟩ The Admiral is lokid for
to com hither in post, with five ⟨or six horses only: and all is said to stay⟩ uppon his coming. ⟨I wold to God, he did⟩
worke wisely; that he be not attrapped ⟨either by the way or here. I do not like, nor cannot but mystrust, so long⟩ as the
Duke d'Aumale and the Guisians ⟨be so greate⟩ about the Queen Mother, ⟨making such a brute to run⟩ upon the Admirall ⟨as
thei have, leest thei make him in that manner⟩ to be slain, ⟨as thei saye⟩ he caused the Duke of Guise to be killid.

⟨Talking with⟩ the Ambassador of Spain ⟨(with whome I am now verie familiar) and asking of him, what news, seing I heard
saie, that the daie before he had⟩ set with the council in the campe, ⟨and as I will now and then saie merrily unto him, that
he⟩ governeth all the council of France; ⟨he wold tell me nothing: for he said, thei were so dysmaid and troblid with
the news⟩ of the rendring of the castle of Caen, ⟨that thei wist not what to saie or do. And still he was enquiring of
me, whither⟩ the Englishmen had it wholly ⟨in ther governement, or at the leest weare able⟩ to master the Frenchmen. ⟨I
said, as yet I had no certaintie. Well, saith he,⟩ yf yow be masters there, ⟨I dare give yow⟩ Calais, yea and ⟨three
hundred⟩ of the ⟨five hundred⟩ thousand crownes ⟨which ye⟩ ask for the forfeit, rather ⟨than ye should kepe that⟩ castle,
Dieppe, and Newhaven: ⟨and if ye⟩ kepe ⟨those in your hands;⟩ your peace is made, if the Queen's Majestie will, ⟨and all
your⟩ requestes granted. ⟨But he doth not thinck, eyther⟩ the Admiral, or any other Frenchman to be trusted; ⟨but in any
wise he wold, that⟩ in all those thre Englishmen should be ⟨only, or at the leest⟩ able to master all the rest, at all
tymes.

⟨I have sent your Majeste suche⟩ letters out of Orleans, ⟨as ye shall perceive by them from whence thei com, and of what
importaunce thei be to understand⟩ the state of affaires as they go here. ⟨Thei have won⟩ the Prince of Conde: ⟨and I know
he hath promised, that,⟩ granting his demands, ⟨he wil be one of the furst and chief,⟩ after one or two summons, that
shall help to drive your power out of France. ⟨I have received also lettres from⟩ the Admirall and Sir Nicholas from
Caen, ⟨at the last.⟩ Those of Orleans ⟨I take of most importance. One of my men I have⟩ lost there. Orleans is kept as
straight now, in this truce tyme, as ever it was. ⟨In the rest which cam from⟩ Caen ⟨there was no greate matter, but of
the rendring⟩ of Bayeux and Falaise. ⟨If⟩ the Admirall ⟨be no trustier⟩ than the Prince, ⟨I do not se but all the warre⟩
will be turned thither. ⟨By⟩ the Emperor's messenger, Monsieur Achilles, ⟨I cannot perceive, that⟩ the French King ⟨will
render⟩ Metz, Toul, and Verdune: ⟨and, not so doing, for ought I can lerne by him,⟩ the Emperor will denounce warre. But if
the Admiral hold out and be trustie, all shall be well.
