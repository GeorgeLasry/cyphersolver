# No. 05, Smith to Cecil, Amboise, 13 April 1563 — English

⟨To speak plainly of the state of things here: no one can promise himself any good from them.⟩ The Prince of Condé,
now appointed and sworn into his full authority, is thought ⟨by many of the gentlemen who did most for him and were
closest to him to have become almost⟩ a new King of Navarre (that is, as unreliable as Antoine of Navarre). ⟨They
think he will soon show himself unkind to God, to us and to himself, won over⟩ by the papists, ⟨either by Balaam's
reward or by Cozbi the Midianite.⟩

⟨You will see from my last letters that sending a special envoy to deal with⟩ the Admiral ⟨or with⟩ Monsieur d'Andelot
⟨is useless, especially since you have Mr Middlemore here, who never leaves⟩ the Prince of Condé. ⟨Everything now has to
be done⟩ with the Queen Mother and the Prince.

The Spanish Ambassador ⟨always tells me⟩ that the King of Spain, as long ⟨as the quarrel was about obedience and
rebellion against the King of France, or about religion, was ready to help; but when it becomes a quarrel over the crown,
or over the territory of⟩ England and France, such as Calais or Newhaven (Le Havre), ⟨he assures me he will not meddle,
nor break the old league⟩ with England ⟨for any new alliance.⟩ ⟨If no one were wiser than I, I would not now demand,
but⟩ take Calais.
