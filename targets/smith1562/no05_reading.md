# No. 05, Sir Thomas Smith to Cecil, Amboise, 13 April 1563

⟨To make a plain discourse of the state of things here: no man can promise himself any good of them.⟩ The Prince of
Condé, being now appointed and sworn in his full authority, is thought ⟨of many of those gentlemen which have done most
for him, and were nearest about him, to be waxen almost⟩ a new King of Navarre. ⟨They think plainly, that he will declare
himself, ere it be long, unkind to God, to us, and to himself; being won⟩ by the papists, ⟨either with reward of Balaam,
or else with Cozbi the Midianite.⟩

⟨Ye may perceive by my last letters, that to send any special man to traffic with⟩ the Admiral, ⟨or with⟩ Monsieur
d'Andelot ⟨is but in vain; especially seeing ye have Mr Middlemore here, who departeth not from⟩ the Prince of Condé.
⟨All that is to be done is now to be done⟩ with the Queen Mother and the Prince.

The Ambassador of Spain ⟨telleth me always,⟩ that the King of Spain, so long ⟨as the quarrel was for obedience, and
rebellion to the King of France, or for religion, was ready to help: but when it cometh to be a quarrel of the crown, or
of the territory of⟩ England and France, as Calais or Newhaven, ⟨he assureth me, he will not meddle, nor will break the
old league⟩ of England ⟨for any new alliance.⟩ ⟨If there were no wiser than I, I would not now demand, but⟩ take Calais.
