# R8533: Edward Lee to Wolsey, Valladolid, 20 April 1527 (Cotton Vesp. C IV ff. 109-110) — partial running reading

Rests on: the clear text of the three pages and the cipher runs decoded with S. Tomokiyo's Lee-Worcester key (DECODE

word except where noted. L&P IV 3028 (the 8 April letter, of which this is the duplicate; `lp3028.txt`) is a
calendar summary with some quotations, used to check sense, not as a text.

Spelling as written; abbreviations expanded (p^t = that, w^t = with, yo^r = your). Cipher passages are given in the
same running text; `[..]` = an unread group or sign cluster (the signs are in the reread log), `[?word]` = partly
carried, `[word]` = supplied. Underlined sign clusters that look like nulls are shown as `[..]`.

**Not read**: the sentences hold over most of the letter, but about one in three still has an unread group or an
uncertain clear word, mostly name codes and underlined clusters; see the score in the log.

## Notes

- *Perot* (f. 109r l. 13) and *Perote* (f. 109v l. 1): the cipher on f. 109v reads `† v x + ʒ v`, where the key gives
  *perfte*; the cross + (f) and the inverted T ⊥ (o) are look-alikes, and *Perote* follows with ⊥. On f. 109r the first
  sign is p or f (`†`/`+`). Both kept as [?Perot].
- *[?the King] of Englonde* (f. 109r l. 17): the cross sign is the French King's sign in shape; Tomokiyo's table gives
  a near-identical cross for the King of England, and Tuke glosses the same sign *the King [of] England* on f. 110r
  l. 22. Read as the King here; flagged as a look-alike.
- *don Inachus*: the cipher *-c7÷d4* (twice, f. 110r), Tuke's gloss *marquis*; L&P prints *don Inachus*. The clear
  *don [?Inarches]* on f. 109v l. 7 (L&P: *don Inigo*) is the same person, Iñigo López de Mendoza, imperial
  ambassador in England.
- The *sutcheners* are the Swiss (L&P: *They have prevented all communication between Francis and the Swiss*).
