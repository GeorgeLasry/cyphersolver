# Bienvenut to "Your Highness", Paris, 28 February 1590 (BnF fr. 3623 no. 23, ff. 35r-v): English translation

[f. 35r] Yesterday [Madame de Nemours] walked me through the gallery of the Queen's lodging for more than an hour, where
she talked to me of many things. Among others she showed me several reports she had had from [Saint-Sorlin], which said
chiefly that because of the risings and intrigues beginning in that province, [Monsieur de Nemours] is being called
to his government as urgently as can be. Not for anything in the world will she let his departure be delayed any
longer, for she wants him from now on to set the whole foundation of his fortune on his government.

She complained to me that, speaking to Señor Moreo, she had let him understand that since [Monsieur de Nemours] now
found himself without a master she would wish it to please [the King of Spain] to make use of him; that if it pleased
him to take him into his service, and him [Moreo] to arrange it, she would see to it (?) that he would serve with such
fidelity that His Majesty would be entirely satisfied. And on this she said that Moreo had not answered her a single
word, at which she was very indignant, and she told me, almost in anger, that it might well be [Monsieur de Nemours]'s
good fortune to withdraw into his government and there do as he could, since means were failing him on every side. I
have considered this, and I judge she may mean that through his government he will always have the means to make
himself sought after and respected by one party or the other, should either of them go under.

She also showed me a report covering the siege of Grenoble, the taking of Roccas by Vier (neither identified), the ruin and
demolition of the castle of Gex, and the siege of Bonne, which troubles us greatly.

The Legate finds himself very much hampered amid the affairs of this kingdom, seeing the mortal hatred the
French have for the Spanish name. I have word from a good source, and from a person in a position to know, that there is
a very close understanding between [the Pope] and [the King of Spain], and that since the Legate's arrival it has been
agreed between him, [Mayenne] and the ambassadors of [Provence and Dauphiné, or the towns of the Union] that, to satisfy
France and remove the belief that [the King of Spain] wants to encroach (?) on [Picardy] under pretext either of
protection or of religion, it will be better from now on that most of the help [the King of Spain] gives to this cause
be given through [the Pope], in men as well as money, and that [the King of Spain] will secretly repay him with interest
at the bankers' rate; and in this way the war will be waged in the name [f. 35v] of [the Pope]. And that this is true I
have learned for certain, through the man of whom I wrote to Your Highness in an earlier letter, that the courier the
Legate sent to Rome carried orders to have ready money sent promptly to keep the war going until [the Pope] can send
8 or 10,000 men of war, with whom he may put heart (?) into [Mayenne] and carry them on, without his having to crush
[the King].

If we see [the Pope] resolve on this and send forces from Italy, there is no more doubt that the game will be played out
entirely between [the King of Spain], [the Pope] and [Mayenne] without Your Highness having any part in it, unless you
take your remedy in [Provence and Dauphiné], or else choose to come to terms with the other party, which in my opinion
would be the worst counsel.

I do not know what to make of what Monsieur Panigarola told me of the reasons Moreo gave him for the affront (?) that
Don Bernardino and he dealt me: he says it was because he foresaw that I wanted to put my nose into the council and
learn its business, and that it was not good for so many people to know what is being negotiated. I judge that they want
only to keep me and all Your Highness's other ministers as far back and as far from affairs as they can, for fear that
you (whose great heart they hold suspect) should get wind of the enterprises and plots they are running in [Picardy].

[What follows (?) put in cipher:] This makes me resolve, since I see I am useless to your service here, inasmuch as the
diligence and fidelity of Monsieur de Riddes, who is very practised and very well known here, is quite enough to
keep you informed of what happens, and since I also see that the means Your Highness gave me are already beginning to
run short, to set out at the first opportunity I can have to cross and return, which in my opinion may be at any time in
the coming month of March, when I hear that Monsieur de Nemours will go with some troops to his government. Otherwise I
shall find myself so committed and cornered that I shall be here until next winter, for want of being able to cross in
safety.

[And afterwards in cipher:] If for the war Your Highness now has on your hands you needed 1,000 or 1,200 arquebusiers,
on your sending me word I would get you some of the best of [Picardy, or the Duke of Parma].

Paris, the last day of February 1590. Your Highness's most humble and most devoted subject and servant, Bienvenut.
