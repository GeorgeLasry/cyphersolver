## N04 | June 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai | JACAR B03050089200, PDF page 21

To be passed to our friends […]yu, Longmen, Xitang and Jianqiu: telegram received. The affairs of the North-West rest on you; I admire it. I have had the independent provinces wired to urge them to march north. The Shaanxi army is cornered; without a diversion from elsewhere it cannot hope to advance. I will faithfully pass on your view. As for funds, the Military Affairs Council plans for the whole situation and must have a plan; within my personal means I will also do my utmost. Press forward vigorously. — Xing.

## N29 | 14 June 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai | JACAR B03050089400, PDF page 33

To Mr Shiping (Tan Renfeng): Yuan the rebel has perished of himself, and his remaining followers have lost their nerve. If Li [Yuanhong] can govern according to the law, the seeds of disorder can be checked; should he depart from this principle, I, Xing, will not accept it either. You are experienced and highly respected; at your call, people follow. If you have any great plan, please let me know at any time. Xing.

## N34 | 15 June 1916 | Shanghai → Tokyo | JACAR B03050089400, PDF page 44

The Zhonghua xinbao has just forwarded a telegram from Li [Yuanhong]: To Mr Keqiang: not knowing where you were, I was late in wiring; I am sorry. The country has many troubles, misfortunes follow one another; a man of little ability, I am entrusted with a heavy charge […] like driving with rotten reins at the edge of an abyss — how could I not tremble? I have just received your telegram of the 9th; your patriotism and your exacting demands overflow from it. I admire your good counsel and am grateful for your friendship. The people are exhausted, the finances drained, hard days are ahead, and no one can see where it will end. You founded the Republic with your own hands; your merit is written in the land. In the old days at Hanyang and Hankou we were in the same boat, and I admired your great plans. I still hope you will support me on every side, correct my mistakes and keep me on the right path so that I do not fail. As for the great affairs of State, everything remains to be discussed; let us begin by consulting the nation. Once the arrangements are settled, I will wire you. — Yuanhong. The 14th.

## V4A | 25 June 1916 | Shanghai → Tokyo | JACAR B03050089900, PDF page 4

To Mr Huang Keqiang: the President is in place, but the political situation remains perilous; [joining] forces to set the country right depends entirely on men of worth. […] You built the Republic with your own hands and the whole country looks up to you; we venture to beg you to set out this very day, return to Shanghai and give us the direction to follow. Tang Shaoyi, Fan Yuanlian, Tan Yankai, Sun Hongyi, Niu Yongjian, Wu Jinglian, Zhang Ji, Gu Zhongxiu, Wang Yongtai, Xu Fulin, Ouyang Zhensheng, respectfully.

## V4C | 27 June 1916 | Tokyo → Shanghai | JACAR B03050089900, PDF page 33

Please transmit to Commander-in-Chief Li of the Navy, to the two commanders Lin and Zeng and to the captains of all ships: Yuan has met Heaven’s punishment and Mr Li succeeded him twenty days ago, yet the [old] regime is still unchanged. I have just read your proclamation: stern in word, just in cause, wholly sincere. You hold back the raging wave that had overturned everything and stand as the rock in midstream. Across sea and sky I send my good wishes, with boundless [admiration]. Huang Xing. The 27th.
