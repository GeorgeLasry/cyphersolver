## Translation

p. 111
Monseigneur.
I wrote to Your Grace in my last letter by way of Vienna that new changes keep being made here.
Today [?they agree] [...] spoils the resolution.
The Kapudan Pasha, having become vizier, began to change; some say that he has now been talked round, others that
he is still in Istanbul.
For having confidence [...] of the ferman, and to me this [...] could be.
He [?went] as if to visit the King of Sweden, but since he did not want to be at his quarters, only in a third place,
he came back without

p. 112
seeing the King.
On the next day after, the 24th of April, he held a public divan, at which war was declared sub ignoto nomine [under
no named enemy]; whence non vanus [is] our metus [our fear is not groundless].
And so it was to turn towards Chocim, and us to the Yedikule; having halted at Chocim, he meant to force the Swede ad
agnoscendum Stanislaum [to recognise Stanisław] or eligendum tertium [to elect a third], not wanting King Augustus
for fear of the German power and the Muscovite alliance, which they also put into the ferman.
A prayer for war was also made.

p. 113
On the 26th this same vizier, while with the Sultan, was deposed and strangled, for which reason things are still
in confusion; and although we hope that our affairs will go well, since [112] the Istanbul [...], yet they have no
conclusion with us, so that Your Grace must be on guard.
Yet [it is needed] that Muscovy not be in Poland, that our army not be near the border, and that the King, His
Majesty, not leave Poland.
Time itself [?will show] the longed-for [...] personaliter [in person] [...] I remain Your Grace's and benefactor's
most humble servant Chomentowski.
At Adrianople, 9 May 1713.
