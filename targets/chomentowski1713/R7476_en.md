One cannot [?understand] what they are keeping us for and do not dispatch us, nor give us the freedom that was granted,
nor the meetings with the envoys, nor the visits that are due. Although I lately sent presents to the vizier and his
kehaya, the vizier received them gratefully and declared that the dispatch will come, [?that] we will not be kept
long; but I know [better]. To [?pay] visits to Their Graces the envoys residing at Galata they cannot [?allow]; they did
allow it indeed, but on condition that a memorial be handed in for each visit, which we could not accept for two
reasons. First, that they could allow one and not another, and so make a difference without it; second, that we could
by no means allow this prejudice. Better that it remain their courtesy than our condescension. [?Our detention],
moreover, [comes from this], that they fear [?] - [?lest] it come from this - that they want to [?press] Your Grace's
