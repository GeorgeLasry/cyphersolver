## The Dresden file

Ortloff calls it "Dresd. Arch. Nr. 26" (*Geschichte der Grumbachischen Händel*, vol. 1, source list: "Loc. 4414. 26*.
Grumbachische Briefe und Ziffern. 1564. 1566."). He cites almost every leaf of it in vols 2-3 and paraphrases the
letters. In vol. 4, p. 151 n. 2 he describes Bl. 279-306 as 21 cipher alphabets, 18 without the duplicates, and names
their users.

- **Scans 0001-0346, ff. 1-278: clear letters.** Asch von Holle to Grumbach and Duke Johann Friedrich II, 1564-65
  (ff. 1-67), the Lötz letters about the "heimliche Sache" (ff. 68-142), then further correspondence. Two survey
  passes over contact sheets, with about 45 openings checked at full size, found no cipher. Single code signs inside
  a clear letter could still have been missed at contact-sheet scale.
- **Scans 0347-0381, ff. 279-307: the cipher section.**
  - About 20 key sheets (see the table below). There are no interlinear glosses and no decipherments anywhere.
  - **f. 304r (scan 0377, right): fully enciphered letter**, dated in clear "Datum 3. Martij Anno 1553". It has 8
    lines plus a 3-line postscript, about 311 sign tokens, 23 two-digit code numbers and 9 Latin-script words
    ("dany ob wool", "antworten" ...). The address panel on f. 304v is faint: "Peter? von Guns? ...", "a pierre de B..oire?".
  - **ff. 307r-v (scans 0380 right, 0381 left): clear letter with about 10 cipher runs**, dated "... den 23? ... 64".
    It opens "Lieber und getreuer Freundt". The runs are Latin-shaped letters mostly written without word breaks
    (`gougmydybrzyqeufyb...`, `zygfeboad...` three times). The writer explains in clear that he has written a
    "Zettel". A marginal note reads `uttqmordya`.

## f. 304r: what has been done

- **Key match tried and failed.** The letter's signs (ν, σ, ψ, ω/ϖ, π, λ, з, θ, φ, δ, Δ, η, 8, 6 ...) look like key A's
  and the Baumgartner key's Greek-like rows, and it also uses two-digit numbers, as both word codes do. Both keys were
  read off the scans: key A as a=ρ b=ε c=θ d=η e=γ f=α g=Λ h=ζ i=μ k=ξ l=6 m=н n=λ o=π p=σ q=τ r=υ s=ϑ t=f u=ο w=ϑ'
  x=ω y=χ z=ϖ, and the Baumgartner key's row likewise. Neither gives "und" for the letter's three-times-repeated
  `6 σ ς`, or "-en" for its constant ending `ν σ`. The digit/letter keys (ff. 293r, 294r, 298r, 282r) do not share
  its signs. Ortloff dates key A to 1566, and the letter is dated 1553, so its key is probably not in the file.
- **Ciphertext-only.** Repeated words point to a simple substitution with word breaks, two-digit code numbers, a few
  special signs and some clear Latin-script words. Hand cribs give a consistent core:
  - `6 S G` = **und** (3 times)
  - final `V S` = **-en**
  - `P 8 L 3` / `P Z L 3 V S` = **mach / machen**
  - `Y Z L 3 V S` = **sachen**
  - `H S V L 3 T` = **knecht** (twice)
  - `V F Y C Z F V S` = **ersparen**
  - `S V F S V P V S` = **vernemen**
  - `S V F Q V D D V S` = **ver?ellen** (verzellen?)
  
  That gives e n u d m c h s k t r p, with a = 8 and Z (homophones?).
- **Solver.** `solve304.py` anneals a one-to-one map, or a homophonic map with a reuse penalty, scored on `de-1500s`.
  `seeded304.py` holds the crib core fixed. The best run scores −2.9 per character (real German −1.1 to −1.8). Its
  output contains the crib words and *ersparen*, *erharten*, *vernemen*, *mich*, *im*, *am*, but most words are
  still wrong. **This is not a reading.**
- **Control not yet run.** The solver has not been checked on a self-enciphered text of the same length and system,
  so its output means nothing yet.

## ff. 307r-v: read in part with key A

- **Key.** Key A (f. 279r, copies ff. 287r, 305r) reads the runs unchanged. The work was mapping the writer's cursive
  onto key A's signs. `f307_keyA.md` has the sign table, the nulls in use and the run-by-run table. In summary:
  - ϑ' (flat-topped g) is used for v/w: "wursiegelt" = versiegelt;
  - "tt" with plain stems is н = m;
  - ℓ is ρ = a;
  - the long-tailed q, ꞔ, ◊, ƀ and ꝭ are key-A nulls, used often;
  - c is dropped before h ("auh").
- **Writer.** The letter is signed with key A's sign for **Ernst von Mandelslo** (barred Y).
- **Content.** The Bestallung and the sealed Artikel for the Knechte, the Rittmeister "beider irer aller namen darin
  benent", the names of the Obersten, Johann von Holle's Bestallung, Dornburg. This matches Ortloff vol. 3 § "Grumbachs
  Bestallungen französischer Rittmeister ... Ernst von Mandelslohe in Dornburg" (1566, citing this file Bl. 39-51), so
  the date read "…64" may be 1566.
- **Check.** An independent shape transcription (`f307_runs.txt`, R4 `EozquoyfyubEofq`) decodes through the same sign
  mapping to "auh·rueter laut·", the agent's R4.
- **Share.** About 363 letter signs, nulls not counted. About 82% give sense including tentative words, 71% counting
  firm words only. These are hand counts, not yet measured from a reading file.

## Remaining gaps

- f. 304r: the text is unread. Likely causes:
  - signs merged or split in the transcription (ω/ϖ, η/y, σ/6, 8/ξ/ζ are close in this hand);
  - homophones, since the cribs already need two signs for a and possibly v;
  - two-digit code words (23 groups) with no key.
- ff. 307r-v: read in part with key A. Still open:
  - the "?llaten" group after "ich" (R8, R14, twice);
  - the "gein" group (Λγμλ, R7, V3);
  - the start of V1, the middle of V3, the tail of R13 and the margin note;
  - the name sign after "von" on 307v l. 15;
  - places where б could be c or l, decided by context only.
  
  These are workable. They need a second sign-level pass and a reading file, then a measured share.
- f. 304r: the [control](control304.py) shows the solver works at this length with homophones, so the failure lies
  in the transcription or the system. Code numbers take endings (`57 ν`, `57 νφ`, `57 ψ`). No key in the file fits:
  key A, the Baumgartner key and the f. 281r Latin homophonic key were all tested.
- GStA Rep. 39 Nr. 289 (Jan 1557) and Nr. 58 (1552): not digitised, so they need the archive (an outside blocker).

## Escalation

0a steps, in order:

1. **Siblings and keys in the same file.** Key A and the Baumgartner key were tried against f. 304r and failed. Next:
   transcribe the cipher rows of the other a-z keys (ff. 283-301) into a table and score each against the f. 307
   runs, which are long enough for a key check.
2. **Clear pages and print.** Ortloff vol. 3 (§§ on the 1566 correspondence) paraphrases ciphered letters from Nr. 89
   and Nr. 26, and the Coburg archive (Cob. Arch. Briefw. VI) holds parallel copies of many letters in this file.
   Search Ortloff for a letter of 3 March 1553 and for the 1564 letter on f. 307 (look for a clear-text crib).
3. **Key rebuild.** Re-transcribe f. 304r at full resolution, keeping every doubtful sign separate. Run a homophonic
   solver with a control text of 311 signs. Extend the crib core by hand (wissen?, `W 8 Y Y V S`; handeln?,
   `3 8 S Z V Q S`).
4. **Archive.** GStA Rep. 39 Nr. 289 and Nr. 58 need a reproduction order.
