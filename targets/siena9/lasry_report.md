No. 9 (R4798): Castiglione this winter, with the King of Aragon's assent
Status. Solved (the two cipher passages). 15–17 October 2026, by George Lasry with CTTS and Claude:
transcription by Claude, held in CTTS; key recovered by Claude from the interlinear plaintext written on the
slip in a second hand (“uno altro anno”, “Chastiglone questa vernata”), which fixes the two passages sign for
sign.
References
• Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2 (Lettere in cifra), no. 9
• DECODE record R4798 (Concistoro_2308_52), https://de-crypt.org/decrypt-web/RecordsView/4798
Ciphertext
53 cipher symbols in 3 lines on one page (manuscript lines 5, 6 and the right end of 8), set inside Italian clear
text.
Key
33 distinct signs (31 letter signs and two separators). Homophonic substitution of single letters: five signs for
a, three each for e and o, two each for r, t and u, one for the rest; words are set apart by spaces and dot or
slash separators, one sign is probably a null, and two clear code words (Stella = the King of Aragon, nichil = ?)
serve as a small nomenclator.
Signs as cut from the manuscript, with their number of occurrences; homophones stand under their letter.

Contents
From: unknown

Date: 1440s–1450s? — Castiglione and the King of Aragon (Alfonso V) point to the Tuscan wars of 1447–48 
or 1452–54; unverified
A slip of paper, not a full letter. Four hurried clear lines open it: the writer excuses himself (he has a
headache, 'doglia di testa', and has not written more), addresses 'la V. S.', and speaks of 'questa via de
accordo', this way of agreement. The sensitive part follows in cipher, with the key words disguised: [the
negotiation?] [of?] Castiglione this winter, with the assent of 'Stella' (the King of Aragon), and 'to sustain this
war one more year'. Below, a second hand has written out the decipherment line for line, resolving Stella as
'del re d'Aragona' and nichil as 'la pr...' (la pratica?); this contemporary solution confirms every locked value
of the key. A last cipher group at the right may read 'Francesco'.
Historical context and dating
Castiglione (della Pescaia?) was the Aragonese foothold on the Tuscan coast: Alfonso V of Aragon and Naples
took it in 1447 and held it for more than a decade; his army wintered in the Sienese Maremma in 1447–48
before the failed siege of Piombino in summer 1448, and in the war of 1452–54 the Neapolitan army sent
against Florence again operated from there, with Siena as Alfonso's ally [Ryder, Alfonso the Magnanimous,
1990]. A report that Castiglione is to be held 'this winter' with the king's assent, and the war sustained
'another year', fits either campaign; the winter of 1447–48, when the king himself lay on that coast, fits best.
Nothing on the slip dates it, sender and addressee are unnamed, and the identification of Castiglione with
Castiglione della Pescaia is a conjecture; the date remains unverified.
Open points. The clear code word nichil (la pratica?), the final cipher group 'Francesco?', and the persons
mentioned in the clear lines (a Guido and someone 'da Rimino'?) remain unidentified.
