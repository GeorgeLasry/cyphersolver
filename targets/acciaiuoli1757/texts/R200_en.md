# R200, Acciaiuoli to the Secretariat of State, Lisbon, 28 Nov 1758

The affair of the Jesuits is in the state I describe in the letters in clear. The whole city is for them: the first is
the Infante Don Pedro, followed by the Princesses and almost the whole court; but neither the first nor anyone else
dares to speak.

The passion is all the Secretary de Carvalho's, who is truly despotic, and there is no one to resist him; and it is
said openly that the King of Portugal is afraid of him. Before, he spoke of it for whole hours at a time without anyone
being able to interrupt him, and the Ambassador of [?Spain] and the Emperor's Minister have told me in confidence that
he also spoke of it with them a great deal and always at length; now he speaks of it much less. In the city no one
dares to talk about it; in confidence, though, everyone calls it violence.

With me, besides the long outburst he made to me, which I reported in clear, not only does he no longer speak of it,
but when I deliberately brought it into the conversation he avoids it; as does the Cardinal too, who trembles before him
and depends on him in everything. And this behaviour began with the suspension of the Fathers from confession and
preaching made by the late Patriarch, when I spoke of it with Cardinal Saldanha the same evening, as I have already
reported.

I fear that that Minister there and the Dominican who accompanied Signor Carvalho's son to Rome may write nonsense and
falsehoods to help. The expulsion of Father Torres, carried out without telling me beforehand, is another reason for
distrust towards me and for confusion in the Cardinal.

A lay brother dismissed from the Society by the said Father Torres, then my confessor, has for two or three years been
at Carvalho's all day; he is the one who supplies information, and with it he stokes the whole fire. He was very well
informed, having been an official of the sale of the goods from India that are sold here. This is the key to the
present state of the affair, which I am persuaded, with many others, they would like, with honour, if it were possible,
to withdraw from many steps taken, which are recognised as irregular. The Cardinal is at school and carries out the
precepts of the said Signor, without ever answering back, let alone opposing.
