# R25, Acciaiuoli to the Secretariat of State, Lisbon, 12 Sept 1758

In the despatch I explain the King of Portugal's illness and the cause given for it; but the whole city holds that it is
not the true one, and it is told that the King, coming back from a visit to a Lady (about whom, for this reason, people
have been talking for years) in a calash with Pedro Teixeira, [?Vilasso], his favourite valet (for that reason Knight
of the Order of Christ and under-equerry), three musket shots were fired at the calash, by which the postilion and the
mule of the leader he was riding were badly wounded; Teixeira was wounded to death (it was said on Sunday that he had
received the sacraments), and the King had several wounds in the right arm. And with a single mule, having called the
Marquis of Angeja, the King's confidant, to his help, passing by his house and leaving Teixeira there, following the
mule, the said Marquis took the King to the Real Barraca, not without much noise.

The matter is delicate, but having become public in the mouth of every commoner, I thought I should inform Your
Eminence, so that it should not be told you from another quarter.

The Lady is of the first families, sister of the Marquis of Távora who was Viceroy of the Indies, married to his eldest
son. It is said that the blow comes from her relatives, or from enemies of Teixeira, who is believed to be the
governor [of the King] in the chair. The fact is not doubted. It is held that, struck in the bone, the King's arm is
broken, and that therefore, being unable to sign for a long time, he has entrusted the government to the Queen, as in
the despatch.

The Infante Don Manuel spoke of it to me with the suspicion of the others. The ministers and courtiers say what I report
in the letter in clear; nor can I report more for now.
