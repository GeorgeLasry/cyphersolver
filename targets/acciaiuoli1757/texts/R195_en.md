# R195, Acciaiuoli to the Secretariat of State, Lisbon, 19 Sept 1758

As to the cause of the King of Portugal's illness, the same rumour goes on: it seems [?only] [?certain] that it did not
come from the Marchioness already reported, but from another woman, not noble. The arm is said to be broken, but at
court the answer given is the one I submit in my letter in clear on that point. His companion Teixeira is out of
danger, but is not seen.

The Queen would be against the Secretaries of State, but she will change nothing, since it [her regency] is not to last.

She has given no ground over the Jesuits, but is thought to be inclined towards them. In any case she insisted on the
departure of the fleets held back, with the ships loaded for a year, through the negligence of the Secretary, to the
loss of the King, the merchants and the poor captains and other officers, and [?of the people of those countries], who
receive with the fleets the horses, goods they lack.
