# R9667 — Lope Hurtado to Charles V, Rome, 5 May 1523 — English (partial)

Translation of `R9667_reading.md`, with the same holes. Cipher passages in *italics*. Not a reading: the clear text
reads, the cipher runs only in fragments.

Sacred Catholic Caesarean Majesty,

From the arrest of Volterra the greatest favour has come to Your Majesty's affairs and to all your servants, and it seems
that Pope Leo has not been lacking to the Cardinal de' Medici. His Holiness is content with him and says he must treat
and honour him greatly, and the Cardinal is satisfied with His Beatitude. The adversaries have received such a reverse
that they do not know where they are. The Duke has handled this negotiation very well, and treats the Cardinal so that
he holds himself not Your Majesty's servant but your slave. *[…] the Pope does not declare himself; but in […] nor in
any other thing […] that […] Your Majesty,* and they are now without any hope of good for themselves.

At present everything here is well on its way, as Your Majesty will understand at more length from the Duke's letters.
In these matters *[…] in this […] [?French] that [?was thought] […],* because all are as glad of what the Pope has done
as I am, and so is Theodoric, *who was the one most [?suspected].*

It is reasonable that what the Pope has done and his good will should earn that Your Majesty do them the favour of
having those closest to His Holiness made naturals of these kingdoms; the memorial of them goes with this. Likewise Your
Majesty ought to have the benefices that the Pope gave in Flanders granted to the chamberlain and to Francisco, because
Madame had offered them to them, commending the person of His Holiness, so that they should receive them from Your
Majesty as patronage; for all are displeased to learn that in these kingdoms they would not give a benefice to Pedro de
Roma, saying he was not a native, and that in Flanders *[…] them […] Your Majesty […]; Your Majesty what […] will
[?give] them […]. […] that the Pope has said that Your Majesty […] that of benefices* to his servants. *No that […] in
[…] the […] in his […].* At present it seems to me that this provision would be necessary so that they may be better
disposed in what they are bound to, the service of Your Majesty.

The Pope was very glad to have been paid Bormediano and to know that the houses would be paid him. May Your Majesty have
it provided, and also give him somewhat more of the bull of Saint Peter, since the things despatched have cost so
little, being ordinary ones of good settlement.

I wrote to Your Majesty some days ago that the Duke ought to have some credit here for the needs that might arise. Your
Majesty answered me that you ordered the viceroy to see to it. The Duke says he knows of no such provision made by Your
Majesty, except only that he be repaid what he had spent up to that provision of Your Majesty. It is a thing Your Majesty
must order to be provided, for new things arise here every day and the Duke has no surplus of what he needs for his
necessities, and has trouble enough keeping himself. Your Majesty ought to do him the favour of having him helped, for
the ambassador's salary is little for his expense, which is great and very honourable.

As for me, I have already written to Your Majesty that I have nothing here in which to serve. May Your Majesty remember
to send me orders on what I am to do, and to have me provided with my salary from what is the Pope's, as I have begged
in other letters to which I refer; and in this one I say no more. May Our Lord keep and increase Your Majesty's most
royal person and estate. From Rome, 5 May 1523.

Lope Hurtado

[enclosed list:] Pedro the chamberlain. Francisco. The dean of […], his chamberlain. Pedro de Roma. Juanin of Antwerp,
his carver. Adrian. Juanin, his credenzer. Girart, his steward. The confessor. Antonio. Master Pedro, barber, the
chaplain. The other Master Pedro, cubicularius.
