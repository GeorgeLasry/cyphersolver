# R9846 — Lope Hurtado to Charles V, Rome, 5 February 1524 (ff. 130r-130v, from the clerk's clear on f. 133r) — English

Translation of the contemporary clear `r9846_clear.txt` (the deciphered first third of the letter; the clear stops
mid-sentence where the imaged leaf ends). Uncertain readings of the clear keep their `(?)` as `[?…]`. Written 22 Oct
2026, batch b07.

The Pope has been pleased by the news of the coming of Monsieur de Beaurain. He awaits him eagerly; his coming here at
this juncture will be useful for all business in general, and in particular for the matter of the contribution, for, as
there is little money, every help is needed, until we see whether the viceroy's going out makes the French withdraw,
for where they now are makes one think what is to come. However, the Pope's resolve is that they should go out of Italy,
and if he saw the means to humble the pride of the King of France and to aggrandise Your Majesty, His Holiness has told
me he would like to help in it. And the archbishop of Capua has also told me this, [?and says] he has given his advice
to the Pope that he ought to do so, saying that, to resist the Turk, to remedy the Lutheran matter, for the quiet of
Italy and the memory of his house, the best remedy is to aggrandise Your Majesty.

They are of this mind; but as they have seen the great [efforts] of Your Majesty, and the little harm that has been done
in France from Flanders and from Spain, and the bad provision of money here, and the great cost of keeping the army,
they fear that Your Majesty may have no means to keep it up, and that, the King of England slackening, he may tire or
come to terms, and that the French may press on to make themselves masters of things here.

His Holiness tells me that he would like one of two things. If Your Majesty had the means, what he most desires is that
the King of France be destroyed; that for this Your Majesty should not rely on his money, for he says that [?for a long
time] it can hardly be kept up, given the debts and how ruined the Church is; but I believe that if he saw a way for
Your Majesty to be able to follow this, he would help with money, and likewise publicly. This being impossible, he wishes
that a settlement of truce or peace be made before Your Majesty's affairs came to necessity, so that it might be made on
the best terms and with more reputation.

This is the Pope's aim. On this, let Your Majesty see what best suits his service, and let Your Majesty order that more
