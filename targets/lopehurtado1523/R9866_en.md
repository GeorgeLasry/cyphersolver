# R9866 — Lope Hurtado to the Grand Chancellor Gattinara, Rome, 28 February 1524 — English

Translation of `R9866_reading.md`, with the same holes. Passages in cipher in the original are in *italics*.

Most Illustrious Lord,

The coming of Monsieur de Beaurain has been very timely. I have briefed him on matters here as best I understand them,
as Your Lordship will see by his letters; and since the third party in everything is negotiating here, I shall be brief.

The French want, by craft or by force, to stay on this side of the mountains, given the *credit that [?they have]
[?levelled], by not having been harmed* in those parts by *the armies of Your Majesty,* and that it is *lost* by being
unable to *do anything.* If *His Majesty can, in [?no other] way does it suit him [to grant], for few or […] days, peace
or truce, if the French stay here with […] a foot of [?ground],* for it will be, fearing the *[?Swiss] and the
[?reputation] they have gained, [?to be], when they [?wish], lords of Italy;* and that His Majesty should not be [lord]
of the world, with them leaving it.

Since so much is spent over there on the war, *and it profits as much as His Majesty […] to avoid the cost over there
and [?to set] with the army that he has here,* for it is the best that any prince ever had, in captains and men by land
and sea; and if His Majesty is to rely on *this, let him not [?think] that money from the [?contributions] [?will be] of
[?help], […] that no […] army;* it is needed *to get it from them, the allies, so as to resist the enemies,* until the
time comes that *the French do not hold […] Italy.*

And in the opinion of all His Majesty's servants, although *[…] the […], and [?] the best that His Majesty has in his
[…], he must not fail [to] […] and [?drive] the French from here; for if they are not beaten, they remain the victors,
and all here is lost. Let His Majesty provide money with […],* and if in Naples *[…] is to be sold,* let him give a
commission to a private person so that *he sells and the army is paid;* for it seems that, if the *viceroy had done it,*
and everyone knew that *he would not [?lack] money, the French would [?all] go;* and some who now *say they have none
would have [?looked] and would [?look] for it, to fulfil what has been agreed.* But as they are *afraid that on His
Majesty's part […], needs; and peace seems so profitable to them, [?as] [?I have] written, […] they would not be very
harmful to His Majesty,* as Monsieur de Beaurain will bring.

So I do not say it here, except to beg Your Lordship to look at a chapter he wrote to Soria, and to do me the favour
of helping me so that His Majesty does not allow me to suffer so much harm and shame by not ordering that what is owed
me be paid. May Our Lord keep and increase Your Lordship's most illustrious person and estate. From Rome, 28 February.

Lope Hurtado
