# R9869 — Lope Hurtado to Charles V, Rome, 28 March 1524 — English

S[acred] C[atholic] C[aesarean] M[ajes]ty

*[…] of the money that the Pope offered to Monsieur de [?Beaurain], he has [?sent] […]; the [?four] of these took […].*
His Holiness has told me that *he will fulfil the rest; […] but he speaks of his need.*

From Florence no *resolution has been taken* so far; they still *[?make] excuses.* They say that next week Cardinal
Cortona will leave for there with Ippolito, His Holiness's nephew, and that once [there] *[…] [?good].*

The news that they had from these parts and from England had *changed everyone's purpose.* Now, holding it for certain
that the French have lost Fuenterrabía, having the news by reports from Monsieur de Bourbon and from Lyon, although they
differ: some say by assault and others by terms. The French acknowledge that it is lost, but say it was by treason. It
has greatly favoured Your Majesty's affairs.

And if the archbishop, having reached Your Majesty, finds *Your Majesty's army in […] to [?be able] [to go] into France, or
Your Majesty with the means to sustain the war here,* his *report* being *[?good], I do not [?hold] for […] that here* his deeds
will be in keeping with what the *Pope sent to offer Your Majesty when he was elected.*

Your Majesty should have it seen to there that the *archbishop* is *taken in such a way that his […] [?profit] […];*
and if he came, and *[?settlement]* of what was said would be much better, because of whom here *[…] all […] has the
confidence that of him.*

And given the *need of money* here, and how bad *they are […] [?to raise],* any *help is good, […] the more so the
archbishop's,* for in the judgement here Your Majesty has not *[…] [?taker].*

The goodwill and words of the *Pope* could not seem better *[…].* God grant that *the deeds* be such.

This Easter two pieces of news have come to the Pope: one from Germany, saying that the Hungarians were coming to terms
with the Turk, giving him tribute in a certain [?sum]; and the other by way of Venice, that a vassal had risen against
the Turk with Cairo and the greater part of Syria. So far neither of the two is held as confirmed.

The Legate reached Nuremberg on the 14th of this month; he writes on the 19th that he was very well received and treated
by the Lord Infante, and that he had already made his proposition, but that they did not think anything could be done
at that diet. Salamanca writes the same to someone who looks after the Lord Infante's affairs here.

It does great harm to affairs here, as everyone says and as it seems, that so few letters come from Your Majesty. Since
it is now summer, when sailing will be easier, I believe it would be very useful for Your Majesty to have letters
written more often. Nothing else offers at present to report to Your Majesty, whose most royal person and estate may
Our Lord keep and increase. From Rome, 28 March.

Lope Hurtado
