# R9868 — Lope Hurtado to the Grand Chancellor Gattinara, Rome, 20 March 1524 — English

Translation of `R9868_reading.md`, with the same holes. Passages in cipher in the original are in *italics*.

Most Illustrious Lord,

By the letter that goes with this, which is a copy of the one I wrote to His Majesty on the 11th of this month, Your
Lordship will have learnt of the departure of the lord archbishop of Capua. With his absence all business has come to
hang on the Datary, and he stands as far forward with the Pope as can be. I have written to His Majesty in other letters
of the need there is *to [?reward] him,* because *he was ill-content with the past,* given the place *he holds.* I
hold it *to be inexpedient,* and every day it seems to me more *necessary to [?satisfy] him.* Let it not be in *the
ten moneys that the Pope* held on the *[…] of […],* because he would *not take them from them,* for they have been shared
out among the Cardinals like the other benefices; and he thinks His Majesty will approve, as the King of France has done
with the benefices he held in France. Since the payment *of this one* is necessary, His Majesty should *consent to it*
where he may be content.

In my opinion the absence of the *[?archbishop]* from here will not help *Your Majesty's* affairs, because I am more
*[?confident] of him than of those who are here,* and it is surer and better served. Since Your Lordship is there,
have His Majesty *treat him* in such a way *that he comes back more [?obliged];* and the sooner *he returns,* the
better he will think it.

Bernaldino de la Barba has likewise gone there. It would be well to do him some favour, to encourage His Holiness's
servants who stayed here, who *are […] very mistrustful.* Some are already speaking to the lord Duke about petitioning
for naturalisations; if it could be done amicably in that kingdom I think it would be useful, at *least for two and
three* of those who are *closest to His [?Beatitude].*

I likewise wrote to His Majesty some days ago how many Cardinals wished to be protectors for His Majesty, and that as I
understood it [?it would be] better with *Colonna.* In that or in anything else His Majesty should remember him,
because he is the most valiant person *[?there is] in the College,* and in his house. Vespasiano *obeys him as he did
Prospero his father when he was […].* He has many sons and nephews, who all *[…];* none of these is engaged in the war or
in anything else, not one with His Majesty's pay. *The cardinal,* in the matter of the *[…],* Your Lordship will have
seen what he did, and how he gave up the *[…] of [?Catania]* freely. If His Majesty did not remember *him, it seems he
[?could] complain with cause;* and such a servant, *it is very useful [?to keep him] here, if they understand that His
Majesty has them taken as […];* and in this time, goods; *[?avoid] all things they could complain of.*

I believe the lord Duke has written at greater length on all these matters; so I say no more here. May Our Lord keep
and increase Your Lordship's most illustrious person and estate. From Rome, 20 March.

Your Lordship's most sure servant, who kisses your hands, Lope Hurtado
