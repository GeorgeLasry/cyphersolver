Sacred Catholic Caesarean Majesty,

By the Duke's letters and the despatch he sends, Your Majesty will understand the state of things here and the good
will His Beatitude has towards Your Majesty's business. I hope the deeds will be such, and so it has been seen in what
has come up since the arrest of Volterra, so much so that everyone at this court already knows it; and things here
stand as befits Your Majesty's service.

The Pope's servants show the good will they owe, *of [?Theodoric], that only he […] is more […]; […] well […] for […]
with him and with the others […]. […] has given […] in […]; the [?Duke]* is well liked *by His Holiness, […] towards his
[?service].* It would help if Your Majesty ordered the matter of the benefices of the chamberlain and of Francisco to be
put right, because it has [?grieved] His Holiness greatly that Your Majesty has taken them from them, and they and the
others feel wronged.

Likewise it would be very well for Your Majesty to order that the Pope be paid what is owed him there, for by contenting
His Beatitude in this it could help *so that the [?crusade] […] of the […],* and for other things that Your Majesty
wants.

And because I have written at length to Your Majesty in other letters, I say no more here. May Our Lord keep Your
Majesty's most royal person with increase of many kingdoms and lordships. In Rome, [?10] June 1523.

Lope Hurtado
