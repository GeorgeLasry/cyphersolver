# R297 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 25 August 1629, "A 67": English translation

Translation of `R297_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Two stretches follow the Roman
decipherment printed by Kiewning (1897), Nr. 164; the rest is read from the figures. The sheet breaks off in mid-word.

---

The latest letters from the Empire of the thirteenth of this month report that the Duke of Mecklenburg [Wallenstein] was
still staying at Halberstadt, without its being known when he would leave, but that he was waiting for news about the
affairs of Italy in order to set out in that direction; but I have word that he is holding back, resolved not to march
into that province with the bulk of the army, and much less in person, unless he has a provision of money, victuals and
munitions of war; and that Collalto had sent a commissary post-haste from Nuremberg to [?Milan] to find out clearly from
that [446] what he is going to give and can actually give for the imperial troops, and to make him understand that,
unless there is a sure and effective assignment, the troops will not march, since he does not want to send them to be
consumed and […] by hunger and hardship.

On the Spanish side I likewise have fresh indications, from what the Marquis of Aytona has said to a confidant of his,
that they do not like to see the Duke of Friedland or any other great commander enter Italy with a large army; and that
these ministers too find themselves in no small perplexity, considering the difficulties mentioned and the greater ones
that are bound to arise in pursuing the war in Italy; and with the news that Gabor is either dead or cannot live, and
that […] is to put in his place others no better than he in those states, and also in the counties that after his death
are to return to the Emperor; and that the King of France, still intent on disturbing the affairs of [?Germany], keeps
three gentlemen here as if incognito to stir up the minds of the malcontents in the Empire, and, united with them and
especially with the free [?cities], intends to enter in person with a very powerful army, making Strasbourg his place
of arms, on the pretext of procuring the restitution of the Palatine and the preservation of the liberty of Germany and
of the Empire.

I hear from a good source that they thought, with the preparations, with the seizing of the [?passes] and with the
report of the general's march with a large army, to bring it about that Nevers would throw himself into the Emperor's
arms, the [?Venetians] would not dare declare themselves, and the French too would withdraw, both so as not to be left
alone and supposing that they would be busy with [665] [973]; and that in this way the Empire would succeed in carrying
out easily its other great designs in Italy against the [?Venetians] and others. And although so far they find their
assumptions frustrated, nevertheless, finding themselves committed, and perhaps not entirely laying aside their hopes
under the pretext and goading of the Spaniards, who would still like to regain some reputation and free themselves from
the fear and jealousy of Casale; of Savoy, which aims to secure what it has gained in Monferrato and still to fish in
troubled water; and of Guastalla, which seems to place in the rupture the increase and establishment of a great fortune,
they do not cease to hurry their troops towards Italy.

And I have it from a [?good] source that the general has been [?ordered] to come to terms, even at little advantage, and
to disengage himself from Magdeburg and the other cities that might hold him back; so that the most sensible people
think that only seeing […] resolved and ready to assist the allies in Italy powerfully can bring about peace, making the
Spaniards persuaded that the [?league] could do them greater harm than leaving Nevers in the states of […] and
Monferrato.

Some days ago the [?secretary] of the Marquis of Aytona returned, who had been sent from here with money towards
Nuremberg to pay the [?soldiery] that was afterwards made to pass into the Grisons, and military provisions for that
enterprise; now the same Marquis is sending him to Spain. As far as the Count of Castro has told me, it is thought that
he is to represent there the reasons why it is not fitting that a formed army of the Emperor should pass into Italy, and
that in any case without sure and large assignments of money they will not march, and if they did march they would
perhaps be more [939] [207] of harm; but so far I have not been able to find out with certainty what he is going for.
The Marquis of Aytona has received orders to stay here until the affairs of Italy are concluded and [?settled]; the
Count of Castro also said, until this business of [?England] is settled; which, since it can only be understood of the
point of the restitution of the Palatinate, raised on the occasion of the peace with Spain, by which it is greatly
desired, gives reason to believe that they have some intention of procuring it, in accordance with what I mentioned with
my last letters had been written by the ambassador of [963]. In which, on this [652], it is believed that the order
given to Aytona to stay has come about because it was written from here to Spain that the Count of Castro is not equal
to bearing the weight of these very grave […]; and generally here they seem to like and esteem the Marquis of Aytona more
than him; and I [do] not […] content.

About the affairs of [?Flanders] marvels of success for us against the [?Dutch] are published every day; and lately they
said that Count Henry had fought them for three days on end in the Veluwe, after which some six hundred of ours were
found dead and over five thousand of the enemy; but from what I hear from the said ambassadors and their confidants,
they think that Count Henry will be forced to withdraw for lack of victuals, and Bo[is-le-Duc?] […], impossible to
relieve because of the impregnable [?fortifications] of the besiegers, will be forced to surrender to the enemy.

A certain [336] Francesco S[…]inich, chief [336] of the Uskoks, left this court a few days ago, as I hear from a person
who says he knows it, resolved to gather a great number of boats to harass the [?Venetians], and with despatches to the
chamber of Graz that [?they] give him timber and [929] other help and assistance for that purpose.

As to the matter of a plenipotentiary on the Emperor's side, in answer to what Your Most Illustrious Lordship writes, I
have nothing to add to the [233] […] with other [390]. I thank Your Most Illustrious Lordship for your kindness in
informing me of what the courier sent to Monsignor [?Nuncio] [553] has brought back, about which [320] has written to me
only for my information; and since it is to be told to the [?Prince Savelli] so that he may then communicate it here to
His Majesty, I shall make no move either with him or with his ministers. […] on the matter of [349][920] I have [?not]
been able to speak, because His Majesty has been away [726] this week, as the others have, and is not due to [?return]
until this evening; if I learn anything more on this matter I shall inform Your Most Illustrious Lordship, from whom
meanwhile I shall await the instruction you will be pleased to send me.

I did not, after all, go out to serve […] at the hunt, [?as] I had resolved and wrote to Your Most Illustrious Lordship,
and therefore I have [?not] been able to deal with His Majesty about the restitution of the church property [?to be
put] into the hands of the [590] in the Empire, the revenues of the church property of the Palatinate and the other
matters I was to deal with His Majesty; and the reason why I did not go is that His Majesty had invited the Lord Duke of
Neuburg, to whom His Majesty has shown extraordinary honours, greater than he had shown in the past, having him dine
with him and giving him a place above the [?archdukes] and [?archduchesses]; and in the palace chapel he had him placed
in a separate and [?secret] place, but without moving our place or putting him above us. But on the day of Our Lady,
when there was chapel in the church of the Fathers [?Jesuits], he had him placed in the window where the ambassadors
and I usually are, and for this the velvet was [?placed] in another place, now lower, where the [?Count] of Castro
attends, to my no small displeasure, although I, celebrating pontifically that day and sitting in my chair, was not
prejudice[d …]
