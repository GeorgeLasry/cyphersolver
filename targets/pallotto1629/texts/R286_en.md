# R286 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 4 August 1629 ("A"): English translation

Translation (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5) of `targets/pallotto1629/crib153.txt`, the hand-corrected Italian text of
Kiewning, *Nuntiaturberichte* IV/2 (1897), Nr. 153, printed from the Roman decipherment of this despatch (Arch. Vat.,
Nunz. di Germania 119 fol. 11-19). That text is R286's reading. The despatch continues on R287 (from "...acquired it
through a commissioner of his", in the last sentence of the fourth paragraph below), so the crib's last two sentences
overlap the start of R287 (see `R287_reading.md` and `R287_en.md`).

---

I have received the answer given me in writing to the request I made by the note, a copy of which I sent with the last
post. Not only do they persist in their first views, but they show that they have entirely forgotten what His Majesty,
Prince Eggenberg and other leading ministers heard from me again and again at the very time when His Majesty's letter
was delivered to Our Lord: the reasons concerning the public peace, but more precisely the interests of the crown of
Spain and of the House of Austria, for which His Beatitude judged it well not to make the representations asked for in
that letter; and finally the effort His Beatitude chose to make to hold back the coming of the King of France into Italy
by sending an extraordinary nuncio, and the reason that delayed it, which was the representations and declarations of
the Count of Monterrey.

I send Your Most Illustrious Lordship the enclosed copy of that answer; but because I mean to have it revised, both on the
point mentioned and in some words that also have a sting in them, I mean to say that I have not sent to Our Lord and to
Your Most Illustrious Lordship the answer given me, as indeed I do not send it. I most reverently beg you to see that
nothing whatever is known of this answer, so that they do not infer from it a want of sincerity.

Yesterday Father Magni told me of a conversation he had had with Prince Eggenberg about the settlement of Italian
affairs, from which he had gathered that they would draw back from the rigour of their answers and that His Majesty
would be content to give way on some points, while making it appear that he did so not at the instance of the King for
finding himself under arms, so as not to seem to do it out of fear, but rather at the instance of Our Lord as common
father.

When Father Magni came to me I was actually with Monsieur de Sabran, and as the Father had sent word that he wished to
speak to me before Sabran left, about current affairs, I took the liberty of having Sabran wait in another room and heard
Father Magni. After that, having called Sabran back into his presence and then also sent for Monsignor the Bishop of
Mantua, so that the Father might hear their views directly, and so that neither he nor anyone else could suspect that
they spoke on my instruction, but rather so that he could report on the representations I made in conformity with the
views of this court, I had the Father himself explain what had passed with the Prince and the points to which the
business seemed to be reduced, which are noted in the attached sheet. Monsieur de Sabran answered that he had no power
to accept any course other than the one proposed in his King's name. That notwithstanding this, out of the desire for
peace which he knows to be in the King himself, he would have ventured to send him a courier and wait for an answer, if
the proposals had come closer to what was possible; but that those being made were not such, and he gave many and
strong reasons. Nevertheless, after a long discussion, it was drawn from what he said that he would sound out the King's
mind in the manner indicated, provided that the civil and unarmed possession of the states were declared to mean that the
Emperor would acquire it through a commissioner of his who was not distrusted by the Duke of Mantua, and that no change
or alteration whatever would be made in the civil, political and military government of the two states, nor in the
treatment, pre-eminence and prerogatives of the person of the Duke and his family. Provided that justice were declared
to be done by the ordinary way and in conformity with the imperial laws and constitutions, and that the succession to
the said states were judged according to the rights belonging to each of the parties at the time of the death of the
last Duke Vincenzo. And finally, as to the Most Christian King's withdrawing his arms from Susa, he would propose to the
King one of three courses, namely: that if the Emperor remained in the Rhaetia (the Grisons), the King would remain at
Susa for the same time; or else that, if the King withdrew from the one, the Emperor would at the same time withdraw from
the other pass; or else that, if the Emperor remained in the said passes for the dignity of the administration of
justice and the execution of his sentence, the King would withdraw from Susa to the nearest places of his own state until
the case was finished and the Emperor was in a position to withdraw from the Grisons.
