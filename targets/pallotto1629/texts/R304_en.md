# R304 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, [28] August 1629, despatch "B": English translation

Translation of `R304_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Read from the figures; Kiewning
(1897) Nr. 168 gives only a three-line German regest, which confirms the first paragraph.

---

Notwithstanding the arrangement made with Father Magni not to give the letter and writing mentioned in my letters of
Saturday about the Carolina, he saw fit the following day, without telling me anything more, to send the letter to
His Majesty and to give the writing to Count [?Slavata], one of the deputies; having learnt it from him, and spoken of
it with the Father, he says […] sent a copy to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, [and] that he judged it necessary to give
them because the business was asleep, […] and that he has grounds to think that an order is expected from there
excluding him from this business, and that before such an order comes he wants to have given […] and perhaps to the
Congregation […] writing, so that it cannot […] back […], and the Sacred Congregation stays committed in the name of
the [667] and of Our Lord. He handles this [549] with letters of credence from the same Sacred Congregation; […] that,
once the said writings are given, and if […] at once the effect he intends, he does not want to leave before the [566]
[…] comes from there, because […] and not to suffer a check nor be affronted by the business being put in other hands
while he [690] has it […].

I have not thought fit to go to any other means with the Father than persuasion; not because I fear his force and […]
[650] more than is proper, but because this business has been committed to the said Father and entrusted to his zeal
and prudence without any subordination; and [966] in [?these] days I have seen the privileges granted to him there, by
virtue of which he, with three companions, is exempted from every other superior of his and made subject immediately
to the power, jurisdiction and direction of Our Lord or of the Sacred Congregation, solely in the business committed to
them by them, to whom all his actions and negotiations can very easily be referred. So that […] it remains for me to
wish that the writing now given by the Father, with the others given […], may be [?found] there to conform to the
commissions and ideas communicated to him by Our Lord and the Sacred Congregation, and may produce good effects with
the Emperor and [other] ministers, as the zeal of the Father himself […].

Had I known of his exemptions, I [?would] not have told the Emperor that the said Father [?would] in that be
subordinate to me, and that I could have made provision when it was necessary; and I should be sorry if His Majesty
took occasion to attribute to lack of sincerity what I said in good faith […] principle [326], because […] that
exemptions from nuncios and ministers of the Apostolic See are never granted to any kind of person; and […] on the
surer ground of what Your Most Illustrious Lordship was pleased to write to me […], and particularly in yours of 26 May
and 2 June. And the truth is that in the patents granted to Father Magni for Upper Germany and for the kingdom of
Bohemia he is not made subordinate to [?any] superior, but only that he proceed "requisito consensu" […], in the first
[…] of the Nuncio of Cologne and in the second of the Archbishop of Prague, without any [?mention] of the [552], although
the countries spoken of are subject to this [559].

But since this is a point that depends on the will of our masters, it is not my part to do other than […]; I cannot
allow it to be felt that in my time the dignity and reputation of this [550] should suffer so great a prejudice, which,
as I know it was not [?given me] for my claiming or seeking it, knowing very well, indeed weighing, my small ability and
merit [?before] I came here, so I should not wish to see it prejudiced by reason of my person, […] by the sole excess of
kindness of Our Lord and [679] Your Most Illustrious Lordship [967] the Sacred Congregation […] has rested and still
rests here […].
