*On the occasion of visiting the Lord Duke of Neuburg.*

On the occasion of visiting the Lord Duke of Neuburg I took the chance to suggest to him how much it matters to the [?new]
house and to the Catholic religion that His Highness [?should take a wife] again, so as not to [?depend] on a single breath,
that of the Lord Prince his son; and that therefore, for the one reason as for the other, [?I knew] […] to be desirous of
it. The Duke showed [?very great] pleasure on hearing this and said at once: Is it possible that His Holiness should be
pleased to turn his thoughts so lovingly to the interests of my house? And when I affirmed to him again that it was so,
and assured him that [he] would receive it as [?very great] news when he heard that His Highness had taken this
resolution, he rose to his feet, took off his hat and added: [?Truly] I acknowledge myself most obliged to the fatherly
love of [?Our Lord]; and he disclosed that he had had this thought [750] [750], and that […] princesses had been
p[roposed] to him, and that he would willingly have inclined to one [405], and he hinted at Savoy, [?if] the bad state in
which his [?states] find themselves did not make [?inconvenient] the expense of the [?journey] to bring her and then of
the ordinary household to keep her in the splendour to which [796] are accustomed to see their courts in Italy; but that
knowing Our Lord's mind and his exhortation [?would] make him apply himself to it all the more, and I would see the
effect.

The [?next] day he came to return my visit, and we passed through various subjects, particularly the affairs of [Italy];
he showed himself desirous of peace and of exerting himself for it, and [?let it be] […] that he would have liked it if
they had chosen his person to send […] at the instances of [?peace]. Four days later he came back to me, with the preface
that he would speak with me [?in] utmost confidence and that I might communicate it only to Our Lord and to Your Most
Illustrious Lordship; he [?told] me that I would see the fruit of the conversation of [750] the days before, since he was
resolved in any case to marry, and [?to do it] as soon as possible; but he showed himself averse to doing it in Italy,
both for the expense mentioned and for the custom of not speaking and dealing with the person before having her as wife,
as is the usage in Germany, since one cannot therefore [?divine] whether their tempers agree and found the hope of mutual
satisfaction. He would incline to a daughter of the Duke of Zweibrücken, of his own […] house and related to him in […] or
the third degree of consanguinity, [?still] a heretic but disposed to [?become a Catholic], and His Highness [?would] not
take her unless she had first done so; and if this did not take effect, he would think of a daughter of Duke Albert of
[?Bavaria], brother of his late wife, and so related to him in the second degree of affinity; but he wished for Our Lord's
dispensation […] for the one and for the other, because on the return journey he will make towards home he intends to
pass through those places, and if he were satisfied with the person he could at once celebrate the marriage, hinting that
he wished it so as not to find himself in danger of offending [697] in that […].

I told His Highness that dispensations for two marriages could not be obtained [?at one and the same time], as that
implied a contradiction; [762] His Highness has now limited himself to the Zweibrücken one, and would wish to be granted
that any priest, secular or regular, whom he had in [?his] company [?might] marry them, because in the house and state of
the said duke, all being heretics, it could not be done otherwise. I said that I had written according to His Highness's
wish, assuring him that so far as possible he would be gratified by Our Lord. His rank as a prince, as much by [573] birth
as by having come from [?heresy] to the [?Catholic] faith; his merits with the Church for having brought […] his states
[to the] Catholic [faith]; his piety, which is not ordinary; the interest this marriage carries with it of securing […]
states [so that] they do not pass to the duke's nearest kin, all h[eretics]; and the gain of this princess for the same
faith: I do not doubt that these will dispose Your Most Illustrious Lordship and Our Lord not only to grant what the duke
asks, but to do it with pleasure, and also to accompany it with some particular expression of the [?con]solation,
[?af]fection and goodwill that [557] towards His Highness, and with some demonstration [when] the business [?has] effect
towards the princess whom he is to marry, [690] as a new daughter born to Our Lord.

The duke has not been able to tell me the young lady's name; but so that this may not hinder or [?delay] the [?licence],
you can, if Your Most Illustrious Lordship is so pleased and judges it proper, have the name left blank in the brief, to
be [?learned] meanwhile and added here, and have her described as a daughter of the Duke of Zweibrücken; and have it
[?made] […] a condition in the dispensation that before the marriage is contracted the said lady must have abjured
[?heresy] and professed the [?Catholic] faith; and it will matter greatly for the Lord Duke's satisfaction that the
dispensation [?come] as soon as possible.

The same Lord Duke told me further that Paul [279], [?before] he became or declared himself a [?Catholic], granted him by
word of mouth (vivae vocis oraculo), communicated to a Father [440], then his confessor, and to another [966], and the
other […], and the second is now in Rome, the favour that he might […] at his pleasure the ecclesiastical revenues of his
states, though in ecclesiastical and pious uses; and that a few weeks ago he had petitioned Our Lord for the confirmation
of the said favour in writing, in case the deaths of those named should occur, and he has asked me also to make
representations for it [762]. I have not […] to give Your Most Illustrious Lordship this hint, supposing any other
representation unnecessary since it concerns a thing done, which could not be retracted or made difficult [?without] the
gravest scandal and offence not only to the duke but to all the other […] [?Catholic] […], as [469] […]
