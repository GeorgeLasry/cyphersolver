# R298 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 11 August 1629, "D 62": English translation

Translation of `R298_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. The last paragraph from "because
to give this title" follows the Roman decipherment printed by Kiewning (1897), Nr. 158; the rest is read from the clear
words and the figures on the sheet.

---

To Signor Baron von Thun, majordomo of the King of Hungary.

I have given Signor Baron von Thun, majordomo of the King of Hungary, the brief that Your Most Illustrious Lordship
obtained in his favour from Our Lord for the priory of Hungary in case of vacancy; he showed the greatest pleasure at
it, and said he remained most obliged to Our Lord and to Your Most Illustrious Lordship. But at the same time he
declared that his wish was, with the brief of faculty to the Grand Master, [?to be released] immediately, for two
purposes: one, to secure himself against the difficulties that will be raised against him in case of vacancy; the other,
to be able to begin recovering the occupied property of the said priory; and that he would then have liked to be
provided now, ex nunc, with the title of coadjutor, which however he would want directly from the Apostolic See. He
showed himself much offended that the Grand [?Master] has paid little attention to the recommendations of the King of
Hungary in his favour, and that now, at a simple request of the King of France, he has given the grand cross to a French
knight who is in no way to be compared with him, nor in services and merits to the Order.

I put several difficulties to him, and especially that coadjutorships are not granted, or at least not unless the
titular is old or infirm, which is not the case with the Prior of Hungary, and that he does not consent to it; which
would cause difficulties. And the Baron told me that he would not accept it on these conditions, because he would not
seek the Prior's consent nor want to remain obliged to him; but that I would nevertheless satisfy his wish by writing of
it to Your Most Illustrious Lordship. I chose to forestall him with these difficulties, so that either a refusal would
seem less strange to him, or he would value the favour all the more when he obtains it.

He has then spoken of it with Father Magno, as his friend and as the man who is believed to be able to do as much as he
likes with Our Lord and Your Most Illustrious Lordship, and to have obtained there things never granted and refused to
others, in more [590], among which are counted that of the [?capes] of Olmütz, of the abbey of Ber[…], the faculties,
commissions and exemptions granted to him and his companions, the correspondence [620] and [226] he has with Your Most
Illustrious Lordship, and other such prerogatives and extraordinary confidences. From what I understand, the Father has
given hope of a good outcome of the business, for which he has offered to write to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, to
Paolucci and to others. Besides the motives mentioned above, I think I am not mistaken in believing that the Baron would
like to be able to wear the grand cross. Your Most Illustrious Lordship will consider whether it is fitting to console
him with a brief of [?coadjutorship], as he asks, or [?else] another one in which, supposing the brief already sent, it
is granted that meanwhile, and until the case of vacancy comes, […] [?to wear] [the …] of the cross as prior, and
to procure the recovery […] of the priory, and of what is recovered by him to have the administration, and to be able to
spend the revenues at his discretion, provided it is for the benefit and service of the same Order.

Father Valeriano has told me that he informed Your Most Illustrious Lordship of the conversation he had with Signor von
Thun about the title of "Apostolic" for the King of Hungary, and that Your Most Illustrious Lordship answered him that he
should deal with me about it. I answered that it was a grave matter, to be considered and well informed upon in order to
reply to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, as I had done, and I wished His Paternity would do the same, so that we could
reply in concert; but that at first sight it seemed to me untimely to raise this point, because to give the said title
as due to the kings of Hungary did not befit the Apostolic See: for even if the said kings had had a right to the said
title by apostolic concession, it had been prescribed against over the course of hundreds of years, and the Apostolic
See would gain no merit or obligation from the King, because he would think it due, and for the same reason he would not
much esteem it, as a thing not granted in regard of his person and merits; and that giving it in regard of the merit of
his person was prevented by the fact that today not only eight parts out of ten of the kingdoms of Hungary are in the
hands of heretics and infidels, but even the small part that is in our hands is for the most part infected with heresy,
indeed an asylum and sink of the filth of Bohemia, Austria and Moravia, whose preachers and heretics, all driven out,
take refuge in Hungary, where it is said that there are today a very great number of all the sects of heresy, and
particularly of Arians; and that therefore the title of "Apostolic" could be reserved to the present King, after the
example of Saint Stephen, for when God shall have granted him the grace to recover his states from the hands of infidels
and heretics and to plant and establish there the purity of the Catholic religion. Then the Apostolic See would rightly
honour him with so sublime a title, and His Majesty would enjoy it and rejoice in it with as much reason, as due and
proper to his virtue and merit.
