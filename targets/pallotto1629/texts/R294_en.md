‹... he answered that he wishes to please› His Majesty, but that he also wishes to know how he means to treat the
cardinals. What is certain is that the Lord Cardinal, like the others who are subjects, will not depart from the
Emperor's views; and we must pray God that the Emperor does not take a fancy to treat them even worse, for I fear that
they will put up with that too.

I have persuaded the Lord […] that, without declaring that he claims the baldachin, so as not to put himself to this test
and not to irritate His Majesty and his ministers, he should avoid occasions of singing Mass under various pretexts,
which he will not lack; and so far he has excused himself twice, one of them on the occasion of laying the first stone of
the church of the Camaldolese fathers, which he had already promised, and [557] I stood in for His Most Illustrious
Lordship.

I also await the resolution Your Most Illustrious Lordship will have taken about the Augustinian fathers […], who had me
speak [to] His Majesty a few days after I wrote to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, exactly in the way I had been told;
and afterwards the Emperor himself spoke to me about it with [553] insistence, and so far I have put it off on the
pretext of gathering some news and information so as to [?examine] the business well, waiting to hear the will of Our
Lord and of Your Most Illustrious Lordship, and according to it to try to guide His Majesty's inclination, so that they
take no offence. For the last fifteen days these fathers have had occasion to assemble [327] for the election of a
provincial, and I keep putting it off for the purpose stated above. Certainly, the more I think of it, the more fitting
