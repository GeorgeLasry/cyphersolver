# R307 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 15 September 1629, end of the despatch "A 93": English translation

‹Of Father Lamormaini I know that on the morning of the Nativity of Our Lady he› spoke most freely and most effectively
with His Majesty on this matter, and when His Majesty unburdened himself on his councillors, he answered plainly that His
Majesty ought not to defer to one man alone, but to such and such, naming four or five others; and though these
particulars are not commonly known, it is known everywhere that the said father opposes this war; but that they have
reassured the Emperor's conscience with the opinion of others, and have impressed on him that he must rather again leave
all the rest at risk than leave his dignity and authority despised in Italy.

For all that, if the French show themselves ready in time with sufficient forces and application, it does not seem
credible that the Spaniards will give up using their usual prudence, since in Italy they have so much to lose, in
Flanders they are suffering such great ruin, and the Emperor's forces depend on so many and such grave accidents, which
do not seem remote; and for many other reasons that they will consider well, and that will be much more present to Your
Most Illustrious Lordship's singular prudence.

At this moment I am told that it has been learned from a privy councillor that in a council held this morning at
Ebersdorf, with Stralendorf present, on the affairs of Italy, lasting from seven to eleven, that is to one hour before
noon, the settlement of them was vigorously discussed, because of the most effective representations made by Father
Lamormaini to His Majesty and to the councillors; and that the said councillor added that he hopes it will follow, His
Majesty being seen to be most inclined to it. May God grant that the effect be seen.

While the above-named Hungarians were here, letters came from Transylvania with news that those lands were on the point
of declaring themselves united with the Turks against His Majesty within two weeks, because György Rákóczi, a leading
Transylvanian and a heretic, claiming to succeed Bethlen Gabor both in the principality and in the seven counties, is
intriguing everywhere with the barons and country people, and the Haiduks and great men, indeed most of the
country, had already declared for him; and that in a diet that had been held a few Catholics had barely opposed the
general resolution to rebel, and had been pushed out by force; and Rákóczi, having met the pasha of Eger, was ready.
This movement may however be checked, because yesterday the Chancellor of Hungary told me that Gabor was somewhat better
in his illness.

The Palatine of Hungary confirmed to me what I wrote at other times about the conversion of the Princess of Transylvania
to the Catholic faith, and that he had three letters written to him by her in her own hand; and that, it having been
discovered by her husband and the leading men of the country, who are mostly heretics, they had demanded that she declare
herself, and when she answered that she was not obliged to do so they had forced her to give an undertaking that she will
do it when the case of the succession to the principality arises, of which she has the investiture from the Turk, and not
long ago the sceptre and other insignia of that principality, obtained for her by Gabor her husband. But it is believed
that if she declares herself Catholic it will be easy for Rákóczi to exclude her with the support of the country people,
unless she has great assistance from His Majesty.

I give most humble thanks to Your Most Illustrious Lordship for what you warn and command me on the occasion of the note
written to His Majesty about the appointment of a plenipotentiary, and as far as my small capacity allows I shall try to
carry it out punctually in future. Not omitting, however, to tell you that one of the arguments for having the answer
given the first time reworded here, which I used with most energy, was to tell them that they should refute my proposal
if it offended them in anything, and if it did not, there was no reason either that the answer should sting.

On the proposals for an agreement with Father Magni, I wrote in my subsequent letters, and you will have seen that Prince
Eggenberg does not hold to what was arranged. From this these interested parties took all the more occasion to suspect
the father, whose very dealings with the Tuscan ambassador here, without his coming out to say what he is negotiating,
are not liked either, since it is known that the Grand Duke is entirely united with Spain.

In the business of the Carolina no decision has yet been taken.

I have made a great effort with some of the deputies that, supposing the papers given by Father Valeriano in specifying
certain particulars against the Jesuit fathers show some passion, from which the Sacred Congregation is far removed, and
it will therefore not like them, one must nevertheless look to the substance, which concerns the universal authority and
jurisdiction of the Apostolic See, and particularly over the said university. I have also informed them of this at the
right moment, although as of a business that does not concern me, and suggested that the confirmation of the transaction
will never be granted, and pointed out that for every reason and motive touching His Majesty, the kingdom, the city and
the lords both secular and ecclesiastical of that kingdom, it is fitting that the said university be truly universal, like
those of Coimbra, Salamanca and the like, whose statutes and ways of governing themselves I have urged them to send for;
and I believe I have not spoken altogether fruitlessly. For the rest touching the person of the father I shall follow
Your Most Illustrious Lordship's command; the father himself will write to you what passed in the last audience His
Majesty gave him, a very gracious one, as he says.
