# R313 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 29 September 1629 (despatch "B"): English translation

Translation of `R313_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Read from the figures and the
clear words on the sheet; the last sentence agrees with Kiewning (1897), Nr. 185.

---

On the matter of the apostolic visitor for [254: Aquileia] I spoke with the Lord Prince of Eggenberg on Wednesday. I found
His Excellency very hard, [760: ?although] I tried to show him the difference [of] principle between the one who exercises
[964] ordinary [jurisdiction] and the one who exercises it directly from the Apostolic See, so that in the latter there
is no need to admit the tie of their being of one nation or another. His Excellency gave me to understand that it seemed
more [404: ?expedient] to him for His Majesty to [209] a native [?suff]ragan than a foreign visitor. To this I answered
that the middle course of a visitor had been suggested by me on my own idea, to avoid the difficulty that was being made
over admitting the suffragan de consensu [599: ?patriarchae]; that whatever form was judged more advantageous for His
Majesty, it was in his hands to accept it; [and] I had no word that there was any change in the disposition to grant it in
this form.

I spoke of it the next day with His Majesty, and put before him as effectively as I could how it was no prejudice at all
to His Majesty that someone [665] [...] by Our Lord should be deputed, provided he was not distrusted by him, but on the
contrary very devoted [932] His Majesty's service; and on the other hand how prejudicial it would be to the Apostolic
See, not only in this region but in all the rest of Christendom, if the door were opened to the contrary; that it
nonetheless suits His Majesty's interests, for the principal business, in [?which] [552] to be taken, the grace of
meeting His Majesty's wish so as to win [her] and bind more firmly the [...] towards His Majesty's satisfaction.

I spoke of it with some other councillors [...]; by one of whom it was said to a servant of mine, whom I sent to find out
[...], that when [...] was about to propose [?there] a cipher from Prince [?Savelli] [...] on this matter; but [...] to
follow [...]; which I do not believe, because I have no indication that it has come, or else that they had [...] to say
[...]; which I do not think more probable, and that therefore it was not dealt with. By the next post I expect a
resolution will have been taken, which I shall report to Your Most Illustrious Lordship.

The same [203: councillor] suggested that by admitting a foreign visitor His Majesty would be entering into new
difficulties, confirming what I indicated under the fifteenth about paying the visitor's expenses; which would cease if
[?Our Lord] deputed one of the bishops of the country, whichever pleased him most; and if not [...]. And the Lord Prince
of Eggenberg, in the conversation mentioned, said: if we cannot settle an interim in this business, much less will the
principal business be settled; and so, if war is not made over the disputes of Mantua and Monferrato, it will have to be
made over the patriarchate of Aquileia.
