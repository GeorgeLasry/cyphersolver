# R300 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 25 August 1629, despatch "B": English translation

Translation of `R300_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Read from the figures; Kiewning
(1897) Nr. 165 gives a German regest of this despatch, which confirms the sense. Page 2 is read in part.

---

After writing the [?cipher] I have learnt [that] these Spanish ambassadors are sending Bruneau to congratulate the new
Elector of Mainz, but in fact so that under this pretext he may negotiate again with the [?said Elector] and with the
other Electors, and particularly with that of [?Bavaria], to obtain the troops of the [?League] to relieve Bois-le-Duc,
for which the Spaniards are greatly afraid. [?The one] whom the Marquis of Aytona is sending to Spain is not after all his
secretary, but his equerry, [whom] he sent on other occasions, as I wrote, in the company of the
secretary with the imperial troops to the Grisons.

The said Bruneau told a person in his confidence that he thinks the Duke of Friedland will not go to Italy, because he
has little wish to and goes there unwillingly, and that they too have little liking for his going, and that the same
reasons would hold for anyone else; and that in fact they would be glad for the affairs of Italy to be settled, and
that if something were proposed by way of compromise they themselves would help to make it take effect. And when this
person asked him what compromise there could be, he let it be understood that if something were given to Guastalla and
the French withdrew, matters would be settled, since they too would disarm and would see to it that the Emperor also
withdrew from the Grisons (Rhaetia) and gave Nevers the [?investiture].

I have told this person to obtain leave to treat of it with me, and, to be sure that nothing has been said [or]
understood wrongly, to bring it to me in writing, once he has first had it seen by Bruneau and by the Marquis of
Aytona, with whom, this same person told me, the whole of this business rests. I do not doubt that it will meet with
[546]-est reluctance in Nevers to give anything whatever to Guastalla; but I also hold it for certain that His Highness,
and much less the Venetian lords, will not think it expedient, for an interest to the sum of a hundred thousand scudi,
to [?expose] themselves to a most dangerous and very long war, which in a single month, in one week, costs it much
[…]. Nor am I at all sure, nor do I promise myself, that in the end the Spaniards or the Emperor will want it, above all
if affairs in Italy or in Flanders go […] their [?dis]advantage.

But without claiming that those who must be most watchful should [?remain] asleep on that account, and to prepare
[?themselves] […] to promote [676] business, for […] and I […] within the terms prescribed [?to me] by [976], whom I beg
to let me know, in case the treaty went [?forward], [?the] treaty, [?where] […] to govern myself on the point of the
promotion of Don Cesare […] [?came] […] moved […].
