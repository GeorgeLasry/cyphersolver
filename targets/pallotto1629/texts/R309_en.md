# R309 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 15 September 1629: English translation

Translation of `R309_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. The clear text and the cipher runs
are read on the sheet; the middle of paragraph 3 agrees with the Roman decipherment printed by Kiewning (1897), Nr. 178.

---

By the last ordinary post I understand that His Majesty wrote to Our Lord and to Signor [596: Prince Savelli] about the
church of Verden in [...] of Count Adam Schwarzenberg; and from what I know of having hinted at in other letters of mine
on this matter, this request will not have reached Our Lord as news. To one [207: privy councillor] I gave a hint, as of
something that had leaked out here, that it had [?already] been promised to Tilly for a relative of his. [?Werdenberg]
showed much feeling, since His Majesty has much committed himself in this business, and moreover [said] that Tilly's
relative is unfit for this church, being [425: ?a Fleming], and on other counts.

I have managed to obtain copies of the letters written by His Majesty in this matter, which I send enclosed; and although
I am willing to believe that by the arrival of this one Our Lord will have received the one addressed to His Holiness,
nevertheless the contrary is not impossible, and in any case he ought not to be displeased to see the one addressed to the
Prince, especially since two quite important points are found in it: the one, His Majesty's nomination, and the other,
that of the possession to be given before the apostolic provision, or rather confirmation, as they call it.

I point out to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, however, that it would be prejudicial to the person who got me the copies,
and consequently to Your Most Illustrious Lordship's service, if it leaked out there that I have them, and especially if
they were shown [?outside] of [...] of them. If it is possible to grant what is asked, it will be most expedient, both for
the [?feeling] mentioned and for the fruit that may be hoped for through Count Adam, as I have indicated in other letters.
In the manner [of granting it] some caution will be needed, so that no prejudice is suffered on the point of the
nomination, which will become greater because they will probably state him to be provided on His Majesty's nomination in
the despatches they issue here for the grant of the temporal regalia and investitures by right of the principality; and
therefore it will perhaps be necessary not only to state the reasons why it belongs to the free provision of the Apostolic
See as devolved, but to see to it that the imperial despatches do not say the contrary, and likewise that no act of
possession is done before it is done by virtue of the apostolic bulls; and perhaps both points will be provided for if,
before it is known that the grace has been made, I can tell the Count that it will be done for him on the said conditions:
not to meddle in the spiritual and temporal until he has the apostolic bulls, and not to accept an imperial despatch that
does not conform to the same bulls; and to take from him a declaration and obligation in writing.

It is true, on the other hand, that [?Tilly] has deserved so well of the Catholic religion and of the Emperor that it
seems very fitting that the Apostolic See should show him this gratitude, and that the Emperor should not hinder it for
him, especially since he himself [46] by his valour and industry recovered the said city and bishopric of Verden
individually, and his garrison is actually inside it. I have not wished to go into these or other similar ideas
[?without orders] and without knowing the mind of Our Lord and of Your Most Illustrious Lordship.
