# R303 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, [28 August 1629], end of despatch "A 79": English translation

Translation of `R303_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Read from the figures and the
clear words on the sheet; Kiewning (1897) Nr. 167 gives a German regest that confirms the sense.

… and if […] he has not had it, on [?Friday] I shall send again, [?this] time with every care.

What I wrote in other letters of the haste with which Wallenstein was being pressed to cross into Italy I heard from
the very mouths of the chief ministers of this court and of the Prince of Eggenberg, indeed from the [?Emperor]
himself; but at the same time I believe I indicated […] difficult, chiefly because it was thought that the Spaniards
could not consider it expedient to their interests.

I shall make use, when the occasion offers, of the information Your Most Illustrious Lordship is pleased to give me
about the indult that it has been resolved to grant to the [690] Leopold William; but […] [930] I shall make no move,
since Your Most Illustrious Lordship gives me no hint of it, just as I have not done regarding what you wrote to me in
your last letters about the letter written to you by Her/His Majesty [564], which I am glad to believe Your Most
Illustrious Lordship will not have failed to answer, and will have sent the letter by another hand. I have received one
by this ordinary addressed to the Emperor, and not knowing what it contains, I shall simply deliver it, without
accompanying it with any other representation. I await with longing the decision on the matter of the visitor or vicar
apostolic for the diocese of [?Aquileia], because the delay in this business, besides the grave spiritual harm, can
bring in present circumstances other, not slight, temporal and political harm.

As to the representations made by the imperial ambassador there on the matter of a promotion, I am sure Your Most
Illustrious Lordship does not doubt that I had the least part in them; and I can further affirm to you with most sincere
truth that I had not even the least knowledge of them, just as I can equally affirm that I have no such pretension nor
such aim, but only to serve well […] Our Lord and that […]; and when God grants me the grace to do so, and Our Lord and
Your Most Illustrious Lordship are persuaded that I have done my part as well as I can, if not as I ought, […] to have
obtained [the] most sublime reward and recompense I could hope for; and with this, everywhere and in the hovel of my
house, I shall acknowledge and profess myself obliged and grateful to the person and house of Your Most Illustrious
Lordship equally with anyone else. And from what I indicated in my [326] of […] about Klesl's baldachin, you will have
been able to see some proof of how detached I am from such a pretension, above all where the interests of that [920]
are concerned […].
