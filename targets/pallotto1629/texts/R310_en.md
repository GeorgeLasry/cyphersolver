# R310 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 22 September 1629 ("A 109"): English translation

Translation of `R310_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. The stretches printed by Kiewning
(1897), Nr. 182 follow the Roman decipherment; the rest is read from the figures.

---

This week I received from Fortiguerra, vicar general of the imperial army and very dear to the General because he writes
his histories, and much more because he holds him to be a great astrologer, the letter of which I send the enclosed copy.
And since pressing the proposal being made for settling the affairs of Italy is not in my hands, and indeed grave
difficulties could be met over the point of the deposit, while on the other hand excluding it seemed to me most
unsuitable, I took it as [404: ?the expedient] to answer him in the form you will see from the copy, which I likewise send
with this. I have other indications that the Duke is utterly averse to going into Italy, chiefly because he believes,
according to astrology, to which he is much addicted, that it would turn out unhappily for him; and therefore [649] that he
will not go unless forced, and like one who goes to execution, and with similar exaggerated terms. This feeling grows now
all the more from the fear that the Swede will come into the Empire, now that the [347] with Poland is supposed concluded,
and that before anything else he will attempt to recover the state [of] Mecklenburg for its old masters. In that
enterprise no one doubts that the inclination of those subjects will join the Swede's own forces, not only for the
interest of religion but for the very ancient possession of that house, which, without example in Europe, counts over a
thousand years of continuous legitimate male succession in those states; and as Gustavus, for his valour, vigilance and
every other gift of a great captain, is an enemy to be reckoned with, the General will reckon with him all the more, on
principles of astrology as well.

These ministers have continued to speak of peace and to say that it will be made without fail, without specifying [...].
I have an indication that a c[ourier] from Spain has brought this week ideas of peace and perhaps some proposal; but I have
not been able to learn the resolution taken on it here, since [203: the councillors] were away with His Majesty, and I,
under other pretexts, had omitted to go there, except for the particular ceremony of [...] of the ring, to which they
[?invited] me, so as to avoid the occasion of meeting the Duke of Neuburg in third places, having made sure on Sunday
week [?last] that he means to take precedence over me. But just as by keeping my own place I tried [?not] to prejudice
myself, so [...] on occasions that are not necessary I think it fitting not to put myself to the test, but rather to let
the [232: Spanish ambassadors] act, to whom I have adroitly tried to suggest in this matter what is fitting, and they
have [?said] they will make some efforts, and I shall stand and watch; for if [900] settle it for themselves, it is all
the more settled for the nuncio.

But to return to the point: I have no slight conjectures that with these rumours of peace, and perhaps with some proposal
to France, they aim to lull the French in particular and, staying astride the ditch, to see whether they can make some
considerable gain, and not to omit trying it, and when they [...] the enemy strong, then to close on terms [...]; and that
therefore nothing will be concluded here about any proposal until it is heard from [603: ?Spinola] and Collalto in what
state things are or where they think they can [?put] them. From the Marquis of Aitona I have heard that peace can be made
now, since his king and the Emperor are in Italy with such forces that accepting means of peace cannot be put down to
anything but a wish for public tranquillity; from the Count of Castro I then drew that they still persist in the claim to
the exchange, or something else that would secure them from the jealousy of Casale and restore their reputation in this
respect, and likewise that satisfaction be given to Guastalla. And it may be feared that, if France lets itself be lulled
to sleep, its [...] will have [266] dreams.

At the end of last week I spoke to His Majesty about the particulars set out in the copy of the note given to His Majesty,
who asked for it and showed the best disposition, but I have not yet had an answer; I shall press for it with all the more
urgency because I have learnt from a very good source that it may be feared with much foundation that the Palatinate will
be restored to the Palatine, the Spaniards inclining to it so as to conclude peace with the King of England, and that
they have already had it suggested to Bavaria by [?Bruneau], who the other day, as I wrote, on the occasion or pretext of
congratulating the new Elector of Mainz, went to His Highness for this purpose; so that it would go with this as with the
church property of the kingdom and states of the King of Denmark. Of the Duke of Bavaria I know that he answered that on
this particular it is necessary to negotiate and come to terms with the Emperor, since His Highness holds that part of the
Palatinate because it was given him by His Majesty in payment of his credits in place of Upper Austria, given him first in
pledge; whenever His Majesty either pays him what he owes him or gives him back Upper Austria, His Highness will be ready
to give up the said part of the Palatinate. His Majesty, then, not only does not incline to this, but claims that the
Spaniards should put into his hands the Lower Palatinate, which they hold, and a gentleman will go for this business,
though perhaps under another pretext, all these things running so far in great secrecy. Nevertheless the views and
interests of Spain prevail so much in this court that one may believe that whatever is effectively wanted there and suits
their interests will always be done.

From Signor D. [955] I have never had an answer to the [479] written to him towards the end of July, [nor] has anything
been written to me by him, or at least none has reached me so far, since the arrival of the courier sent him from [200]
under the name of Signor [353] his father; for at the beginning of this month I had word that, having passed through the
[?imperial] camp, [...] the General at Halberstadt, thinking that he [?was] there, [649] was sent towards Hamburg, where
he is understood still to be convalescing from his illness. In the matter of [...] offices for St Ursula I shall do
[?as] you [?command]; but I beg [...] to forgive my [...] devotion, and not to put it down either to importunity or to
excessive boldness, if I make bold to [...] you that these are precisely the matters where, supposing their wish to be
pious, they are worthy rather of thanks than of refusal, and, by citing examples of offices [...] granted for less
eminent saints and at [...] lower than that of one [...], the [...] who is not well disposed [could] take occasion to
exaggerate to her the usual ideas of little [?good] will.
