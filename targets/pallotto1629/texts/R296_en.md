# R296 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 18 August 1629, "C 57": English translation

Translation of `R296_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. The first and last paragraphs are
printed by Kiewning (1897), Nr. 162; the middle is read from the figures and the clear words on the sheet. "Father
Magno" is the Capuchin Valeriano Magni.

---

What Your Most Illustrious Lordship was pleased to hint to me about the person of Father Magno has been no small relief
to me in bearing his proceedings, of which until now I have written to Your Most Illustrious Lordship only what I judged
I could not keep silent without failing in the precise obligation of your servant and of a good minister of that holy
See; and Your Most Illustrious Lordship may be certain that, as I have borne it until now, so I shall do in future,
however reluctantly, in order to second the wishes of Our Lord and of Your Most Illustrious Lordship.

Some time ago I [?assured] Your Most Illustrious Lordship that, the business of the church property of Bohemia, or at
least the division of the salt revenue given in compensation, having been entrusted to Signor [275], to Father Valeriano
and to me, those two being entirely of one mind, my intervention would be superfluous. Now I see clearly that it is not
only superfluous, for the reasons mentioned, but dangerous, because of the Father's aim that the Emperor and everyone
else should know that this business, in Rome as well as here, has been and must be guided and carried out under his
direction. I therefore asked Your Most Illustrious Lordship as a great favour, if you will be pleased, that I may
withdraw from this business and let Father Magno and the Lord Cardinal freely do what their great zeal dictates to them,
without anyone else sharing in the glory and honour of this business, for which I am certainly without any ambition.

The first of the two [957] he had [?agreed] without any difficulty with His Majesty's [?ministers]; but Father Magno
intends to make three of them, and […] gave me his word, so he holds firm; and every time it has been discussed, although
the Lord Cardinal shows himself inclined that there should be only two for now, because […] in his will, [?supposing]
that the Lord Cardinal's will will not be at variance with it. But in spite of this I promise myself to see that it is
settled in accordance with Your Most Illustrious Lordship's view before the answer of […] arrives, without your being in
the least committed or named.

I could say much about this good Father, and especially about a handful of impressions with which he had in these days
not a little upset the Lord Cardinal's mind; but I leave it at that, so as not to weary you, and also so that Your Most
Illustrious Lordship should not think I make more [593] of it than is proper. Indeed in this same […] I beg Your Most
Illustrious Lordship to [?keep] to yourself what I have hinted to you, and not to move on that account to the least
provision or positive remedy, which is not at all […]; for as far as Signor [275] is concerned everything is settled,
because [?His Most Illustrious Lordship] has satisfied himself that I need no bonds or [?syllogistic] precepts to serve
[?His Most Illustrious Lordship], doing so solemnly of my own will, and he has seen and touched with his hands what is
going on, and has been completely reassured; just as Father Magno too has been convinced, and has done so as well, and
has confessed himself obliged, that I have declared to him my nature and will to be just as disposed and inclined to love
him, […] and to serve him in everything that he sincerely represents to me as to his taste and service, as far as
offices and serv[…], […] when he sees himself drawn by industry, artifice and force of wit.

But I must not omit to point out to you that the paper given by the said Father to His Majesty and his ministers about
the property of Bohemia, before the draft of the instruction from there arrived, was given without my participation; and
when I saw it I could not bring myself to [?approve it], because, besides some word of which one could […], its
substance being more favourable to His Majesty than the draft that came from there, it gives these people occasion to
[?insist], or to say that there has been a retreat over there, […] that, Father Magno having advanced His Majesty's side,
it has been lowered by Our Lord or by the Sacred Congregation. I do not send a copy of the paper, supposing that the
Father has sent it to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, as with the papers given by the same Father about the Carolina, of
which the Father tells me he has [?given] a copy to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, and which were likewise given without
my participation. And I cannot […] approve that he commits the judgement and will of Our Lord so freely, and
going so far into particulars, against a religious order such as that of the [?Jesuits], without necessity and, as I
am persuaded, without a precise order of Our Lord or of the Sacred Congregation; and to some weighty ministers who have
spoken to me of it with astonishment I have said that I am not informed of this business, but have no doubt that what
others might take offence at is the effect of the Father's zeal and not an instruction of Our Lord or of the Sacred
Congregation.

I omitted to write to Your Most Illustrious Lordship with my last letters that, when His Majesty spoke to me of Father
Magno, showing surprise that so much account had been made of him in Rome, I told him that the chief ground and motive
of the marks of favour shown to Father Magno had been on His Majesty's account, because of the strong letters he wrote in
his recommendation and because of the indications they had from his speech of the affection His Majesty bore him and of
the confidence with which he dealt with the said Father; and that it was therefore right that His Majesty should keep a
grateful recognition of all this, which I was telling him, and that His Majesty had believed that he would serve him well
with his good offices, but that he had done the contrary.
