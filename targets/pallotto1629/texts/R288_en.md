# R288 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 4 August 1629 ("B 24"): English translation

Translation of `R288_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. Two passages follow the Roman
decipherment printed by Kiewning (1897), Nr. 154; the rest is read from the figures and the clear words on the sheet.

---

On the matter of the new bishoprics to be erected in Bohemia, I see what Your Most Illustrious Lordship commands me, and
in accordance with it I shall seek to bring it about in due time, not without hope of achieving it.

I informed His Majesty that the brief and other papers had arrived for carrying out the business of the church property
in Bohemia, taking the occasion to express to him forcefully the fatherly affection that His Holiness [?bears] him, the
esteem in which he holds [?His] Majesty's piety and religious zeal, and the pleasure with which he meets the occasions of
giving him signs of it. His Majesty showed that he welcomed what I told him on this point, and came out with asking me
whether Father Fra Valeriano would have a part in this business, and showed himself much displeased with him, saying
that he had [?had] some writings [?given] him concerning the Carolina, as impertinent as those of the Frenchman; that
before leaving here he had promised and sworn to him that he would not say a word in this business, and then had done
the opposite. He assured me that he had long had no good opinion of him, that he had always gone about with a thousand
inventions of mines, of alchemy and of secrets for prolonging life and the like, and that all had come to nothing; that
he is a restless brain and an open enemy of the Society [of Jesus], and that he therefore thought not to allow him in
any way in his states, and was so resolved; but that he told me this in confidence, and I should [?keep] it to myself
without telling anyone.

I said, as to the point of the Carolina, that I had not seen the writings His Majesty spoke of (as indeed I had not seen
them, although Father Magni had told me he would show them to me before handing them in), but begged her [Your Majesty]
nevertheless to have them read attentively, and if they said anything of weight, they should not [?lose] […] by the
Father. As to the rest, I assured His Majesty that, [?if] he [?overstepped] the bounds of what is proper, His Majesty
should not trouble himself, because I, as his superior, would see to it as is fitting; and if His Majesty resolved to
have him out of his states, I myself would find the way; but I begged His Majesty to suspend this resolution [?until]
the business of the church property of Bohemia was settled, for which [332] [993] is much [697] and necessary;
afterwards he would have [249] to decide what he [?had] judged most suitable. With this His Majesty showed himself
satisfied.

Your Most Illustrious Lordship sees […] already the second time that His Majesty raises this matter, and I should not
like it if, when His Majesty makes a show of saying this in confidence and with orders to keep it secret, he meant to be
understood, and as it were to make a tacit excuse for doing it himself, should others whom it may concern not do it. I
wished to tell Your Most Illustrious Lordship all this so that you may be pleased to instruct me how I am to conduct
myself if the Emperor comes out with this matter again, or if he resolved to put his intention into effect.
