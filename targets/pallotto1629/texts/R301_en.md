# R301 — Pallotto to Barberini, Vienna, 25 August 1629, despatch "C": English translation

Translation of `R301_reading.md` (22 Oct 2026, Claude Opus 5.5), with the same holes. One passage follows Kiewning's

The day before yesterday Father Magni told me that he had made another writing on the business of the Carolina, to
give to His Majesty as soon as possible, but that he did not want to show it to me, so that he could say truthfully
that I had not seen it, so as not to involve me in it, it being incomparably more [?against] the Jesuit Fathers than
the first; declaring to me, among other motives, [?to give it] […] to forestall any order that might [?be issued] from
there to take the business out of his hands. I induced the Father to tell me the substance of the said writing, he
saying that I would not on that account be prevented from [?affirming] that I had not seen [it]; and, seeing clearly
that it would give grave offence, to the prejudice not only of his person but of the business itself and of the
others who carry its weight with him, I tried to persuade him [to hold it back] at least eight or ten days, in which
time I was sure no answer or order could come from there about anything written; and, after he had given the first
writing to His Majesty, to see in the meantime what the said writing and his own spoken word could achieve with His
Majesty and with those deputed [to] this business by His Majesty.

This morning, when he was with me together with the Lord Cardinal, he told me he had put the last hand to the said
writing, had added to it a most beautiful letter to His Majesty in the form of a [?fraternal] correction, that […], and
that he was resolved to give [it] to His Majesty tomorrow, and then to go off at once to Nikolsburg; and if he did not
hear from there that it had had the effect intended, he would go to Bohemia, to attend there to the business of the
propagation of the faith […], and would never again see, let alone speak to, the Emperor, holding it a very great
honour, indeed a glory, if in this way and for this cause he should leave not only the court but His Majesty's states
as well; setting out other ideas which Your Most Illustrious Lordship can very well infer from the Father's vehemence.

The Lord Cardinal had advised him against coming at present to this [662], and to defer it at least until the business
of the church property was settled; but the Father would not bend. [762] seeing the very grave drawbacks that could be
feared from such an action, not only on the part of [the] Emperor but for Father Valeriano too, if the force and skill
of his mind, tongue and pen committed themselves so far and made this particular cause the universal cause of all the
religious orders, of the Jesuits and of the Apostolic See, I showing myself to agree […], and without blaming or
rejecting the Father's motives and reasons, […], he showed himself persuaded [532] give the said writing, but rather to
let me see it and to [?amend it] according to my opinion. […] chiefly with the thought of being able to [?send] a copy
this evening to Your Most Illustrious Lordship; but he had said that tomorrow […] he would come here to bring it to
me; and so as not to disquiet him, make him suspicious or anger him, I have put no further pressure on him.

In truth I know that the reasons put to him so far have made […] impression; they would have made a much greater one
if he had refrained from touching […] on particular matters the Jesuit Fathers, who without doubt are the apple of
the Emperor's eye. So I have wished to report all this to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, that you may know what is
happening in so grave a business, [in which] that [920], the Sacred Congregation and the very person of […] are so
much engaged; and that you may be forewarned in case some effort should be made there, on the part of the Jesuit
Fathers or of His Majesty himself, to discredit the representation made by Father Magni; being sure that the singular
prudence and […] of Your Most Illustrious Lordship, so that, even if it should please you to attribute some
particularity or excess [?of pen] to the Father's vehemence, […] of the business remain confirmed in the ideas and
sense of the Sacred Congregation and of […] in this matter; in which it will be of much […] if the occasion is taken
away from whoever [it may be] to make himself there the interpreter of the will of Our Lord and of Your Most
