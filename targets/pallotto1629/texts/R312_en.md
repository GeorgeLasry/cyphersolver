with His Majesty, the Empress, talking with me, opened herself with some feeling about this towards the Prince, saying
that they try to make her suspect in this business and to see that she should not even speak of it; and therefore, not
staying to speak in secret all that she would have wished, so as not to give rise to suspicion that these affairs were
being discussed, she told me that she would take the occasion to speak of it at greater length, and that she would say
she had then spoken with me about the business of this convent of St Augustine and of the office of St Ursula. These
particulars I wished to indicate to Your Most Illustrious Lordship, so that with your singular prudence you may infer
what can be hoped for in this business; especially if a person's report were well founded, that the Marquis Spinola, so
as not to return to Flanders, where he is supposed to have orders to go once the affairs of Italy are settled, seeing
the affairs of that province in such ruin, is rather inclined to stoke the war in Italy, or at least to drag out the
settlement.

Of the proposal made by the King of France in Spain and passed on by the lord nuncios, by whom it was sent to me, I find
no trace here, either with the Spanish ambassadors or with the Emperor's ministers; and whether they have not
communicated it from there, or want to keep it secret here, either is a sign that it is not meant to be accepted, but
that they want to give fodder to keep the resolutions of France and the other interested parties asleep and in suspense
until they manage to strike some blow in that province; and it seems that for no other purpose than this they have for
some time now been stirring up negotiations and hopes of a settlement, as I have reported from time to time; and this
week they had again set the Bishop of Mantua going, so that it seemed they wanted to settle the business with a simple
letter of compliment from the King of France; but when it came to the point, he found the usual ways of negotiating.

Yesterday the Count of Castro, speaking with me, opened himself at length, urging that the Republic of Venice could give
peace to Italy if it remained neutral, as Our Lord does, and that it would be given security of suffering no prejudice of
any kind on the word of the Emperor and the Catholic King; indeed, that His Beatitude would be made to give it too, and he
pressed me hard on whether His Holiness would give it, being assured by the Emperor and the King. Although I could have
answered sufficiently by saying I did not know, nevertheless, since I could suspect that this was not entirely his own
view, and since it seemed good to me that such negotiations be cut short as far as possible, besides saying that I did
not and could not know Our Lord's mind on such a matter, I added that in my opinion His Holiness might find very grave
difficulties, but if all the others ceased, that of putting at risk the supreme respect owed to him by that Republic
would suffice; of which one could more than probably believe that, in a matter in which it is so engaged and where such
grave interests of state are at stake, it would not defer to or rely on the word of His Beatitude.

If the slowness of France gives the imperial and Catholic arms no room to make some considerable gain, it may be hoped,
not without foundation, that the will of the Spanish ministers and of those here will be brought to yield by the
misfortunes of Flanders, from where, besides the fall of 's-Hertogenbosch, news comes that all the rest is tottering,
both for lack of money, men and leaders and for the alienation of the inhabitants from the Spaniards, besides the more
particular one of the heretics; and that therefore in the principal strongholds new plots and dangers were discovered
every day; and likewise by what may be feared here from the King of Sweden after the truce concluded with the Pole, it
being written that the said King is moving with a powerful army towards the duchy of Mecklenburg, with the help of men and
money from France, England, Denmark, the Venetians and the Dutch, and that therefore General Wallenstein was returning
towards the said duchy to defend it; which is also confirmed to me by letters of the 17th of this month from Halberstadt,
where the said General was still staying.

I send a copy of the letter written by the Swiss to His Majesty, obtained in confidence from a good source, and I have
confirmation of these requests from the Lord Prince of Eggenberg, who told me further that he had been written to by a
leading personage of that nation that the French and Venice were trying to commit them against the Emperor and to divert
the imperial arms from Italy onto them, but that they would not succeed.

I likewise send a copy of a paper containing the statement made by Monsieur de Sabran in Genoa in the name of the Most
Christian King.

At this moment I am told by a confidential person, in keeping with what can be drawn from Questenberg's negotiation, that
he heard from a councillor that, if Our Lord exerts himself for the settlement of these disputes and proposes some plan
or compromise, he will have every satisfaction from it and will come out of it with honour. I told this same person, to
whom I can suspect this opening was made so that he would report it to me, to ask this gentleman to be pleased to
indicate one, so that it could be suggested to me and I could pass it on to Our Lord; and I made the same request myself
to Questenberg, that he apply his mind to a compromise this business could admit and communicate it to me in confidence,
since Our Lord can do no more than represent to each party the claim of the other and beg on every side to come down as
far as possible to agree on terms; but the qualifying or tempering of the claims he leaves to the parties themselves, so
as not to make himself suspect and disqualify himself from being mediator for the peace.

Vienna, 29 September 1629.
