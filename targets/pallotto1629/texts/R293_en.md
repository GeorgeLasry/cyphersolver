‹... he wondered that it had been neglected to conform for the› disposition of the […], and in this too he has promised me
his assistance. I did not think it right to speak of it with His Majesty before hearing the Prince; meanwhile His Majesty
has gone out again to the hunt, where he will stay some weeks. Next week I shall go out to wait on His Majesty and shall
speak to him [609] this business.

As to the provisions of benefices and churches to be recovered, it is true that the Lord Prince declared to me that His
Majesty did not claim, nor would let himself be drawn into claiming, what I was told, as I wrote on 7 April, namely the
ius nominandi pro prima vice, so that he would not even be satisfied if Our Lord provided the persons His Majesty
desired, unless the provisions were made as of persons nominated and presented by His Majesty; and this I think I wrote,
or at least believed I wrote, in the plain letter of 9 June; but not that the Lord Prince and His Majesty give up the
claim that, for His Majesty's merit, expense and zeal, for the service of God and the good provision of churches and
bishoprics, it is not owed by the kindness and gratitude of the Apostolic See that the said churches be provided with
suitable and deserving persons proposed by His Majesty and for whom he will intercede. I am persuaded nevertheless that
they will come to see the fitness of the Apostolic See rewarding persons who have deserved well of the Catholic religion
and of the Emperor himself, such as those Your Most Illustrious Lordship mentions in your letter, and I shall not fail to
suggest it at suitable moments.

As to the point Your Most Illustrious Lordship touched on in your letter of 28 April, I would not know what to say,
except that - given the said claim, which is and will be fostered by those who hope to be provided through the Emperor;
given the idea, which they have had and which cannot be uprooted, that Our Lord is not well disposed to His Majesty;
given the affection and credit the Germans give to the Emperor, above all when set against the Pope and Rome, and all
the more to the present Emperor, set against whose views and actions those of the Pope are condemned merely by being
believed contrary to the Emperor's - I would be of the opinion that on this occasion the Emperor should not be displeased
nor given cause for complaint; but that the claimants should be told that, to further their claims, they should obtain
at least a general recommendation from His Majesty, either by letters, or through the ambassador resident there, or
through the nuncio resident here, which anyone will find very easy to obtain and they will not think difficult; or, if
this were found inconvenient, I would think the signing of the petitions could be […] long enough for word to be sent
here and to hear whether His Majesty had any particular view about the person or about the provision requested. This I
would try to do so that the name of the masters were not committed, for I would speak as if some friend here had asked me
to recommend that person there, so as to have information on his qualities and on the benefice or dignity he seeks; and
if I found them well disposed, I would then say that he had been provided on my information that the person was
acceptable to His Majesty; and if I found opposition and difficulty, on my reporting it there, if it seemed best not to
provide him, I could take occasion to represent here the regard Our Lord has for His Majesty's pleasure; and if, despite
the opposition, it seemed best to provide him, I could say that, to the great displeasure of His Beatitude and of Your
Most Illustrious Lordship, my report came later than the diligence of that man, who so many days before had obtained the
provision by another way and had received the grace. As to the manner or title of the provisions, to state that the
benefices are provided as vacant propter haeresim, although it could be upheld even under the terms of the compacts of
the religious peace, one might nevertheless run into the dangers and contingencies Your Most Illustrious Lordship
mentions, without need; for I would think it enough, without specifying the title of the vacancy, to say that such a
benefice, dignity etc. is at present vacant and that under the provisions of the sacred canons and of the concordats with
the German nation its collation and provision belongs at present to the Apostolic See.

The [269] of Dohna, to whom I made known the order I had to recommend his person and house to His Majesty, has let me know
that he would wish, in [?regard] of [his] great services in the past troubles, that [His Majesty] would grant him the
favour of redeeming two estates [264] […] which the city of Breslau holds in pledge from His Majesty, and of giving him
the title of prince over them. Before speaking of it to His Majesty I [?thought it best] […] to speak of it in confidence
to the Lord Prince, as if for advice, saying that I would ask for it if he believed His Majesty could […] [?gratify] and
oblige Our Lord and […] with that [702] token of His Majesty's love and goodwill towards Our Lord, and of seeing his
intercession honoured; otherwise I would excuse myself […] with the Lord […] of Dohna, so as not to give occasion for
cooling the new correspondence […] of affection which [337] we have tried to […] between His Majesty and [?Our Lord]. The
Lord answered me that for a long time the […] of Dohna had been making this request, that Father La[…] had interceded
very warmly for him, but fruitlessly, and that […] declared he would not make any more princes; but that notwithstanding
[…] I could make the representation, since [690] Dohna was a knight who had deserved very well of His Majesty and of the
Catholic religion, and His Most Illustrious Lordship's intercession would always carry […] great […], though he did not know what His
Majesty would do; and that he for his part would serve His Most Illustrious Lordship and help the Lord of [330] […]. So with this opening
I shall resolve to speak of it […] at the first occasion.

As to the matter of the baldachin, I shall await the orders Your Most Illustrious Lordship sees fit to give me; from here
I have nothing to add to what I wrote in other letters, except that two days ago [540] told me [that] he has been written
to from there, he does not know whether directly by the Sacred Congregation or by others, as [642] [992], of what great
glory would […] to him if […] the satisfaction that is fitting in this with him. His Most Illustrious Lordship added: This glory I am content
that the Congregation and Our Lord should gain, because I am in no position to break with the Emperor, and it is a good
deal that he had the Prince of Eggenberg tell me that, although His Majesty does not wish cardinals to use the baldachin
in his presence, since he knows that neither Spain nor France permits it, which are kings and not emperors, nevertheless
it was neither his order nor his will that this be done in the way it was done; indeed he had been much displeased by it.
That His Most Illustrious Lordship could not remedy it, but Our Lord could do so with two words, if, when the Emperor had
a request made to him on the occasion of a promotion of cardinals, he answered that he wishes to please [His Majesty …]
