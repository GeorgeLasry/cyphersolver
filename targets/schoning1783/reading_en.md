# Schoning's cipher passages, 1783-84: English translation

Translation of `reading.md`. Code words in [brackets]; "..." marks clear text not repeated or signs unread.

**No. 1, 28 July 1783.** I have the honour to inform you that I arrived yesterday on Prussian territory without the
slightest unfortunate accident, except that I am a little unwell ... This town is full of Dutchmen, among whom there
are some false patriots ... [I] told him to give well-deserved praise to the reigning burgomasters of Amsterdam. I
arrived in time to stop him doing the contrary, for the duke's party had [tried to undo] the good they have just
done. He is furiously worked up, that is, the ill humour he speaks of against Monsieur Thulemeier; he gives him no
pardon; but I told him to keep calm on this subject and to be very cautious for some time, for fear that he might
lack well-intentioned agents ... He suspected Monsieur your brother the general of belonging to the party of the
[...]; [...] to undeceive him ... before one is quite sure of success ... [Her Royal Highness] ... Serene, and the
unhappy [Republic]. This idea ... with zeal ... the treasury of [...].

**No. 2, 3 Aug 1783.** (I hope to be able) to present myself in person to the King; for the correspondence everything
is arranged. (On the 7th I shall be early) at Potsdam, and I believe [the Prince of Prussia] will have made the
arrangements needed to be told the moment I arrive and to have his orders sent to me.

**No. 3, Potsdam 8 Aug 1783.** [...] speak [to the King]. [Thulemeier] is a scoundrel. [Hertzberg] will be sent away.
[The King] hates and heartily despises [the Prince], but on the other hand he loves [the Princess] tenderly. Shortly
an express courier, and then more fully. Time is short, adieu. Potsdam, the eighth of August 1783.

**24 Dec 1783.** [My] mission ... [to ask the whole] privy council to be well on their guard, so that [His Serene
Highness] does not thwart Berenger over the money negotiation for ..., nor the intrigues he will play to form a
party against the Emperor on the orders of [His Majesty].

**Jan 1784, the plan for an alliance.** I believe [His Serene Highness] would do very well to try to persuade his
friends that they should [show] de Verac, the future French ambassador at [The Hague], by their advice in the
[States], that they are anything but Anglomaniacs; that on the contrary they are perfectly convinced that the
friendship of [His] Most Christian [Majesty] is dear to them and absolutely necessary for ... the harmony and
tranquillity of the [Republic]. They should show themselves on every occasion opposed to an alliance with England,
Austria and Russia. On the contrary it seems to me that an alliance for some years with [France], Spain and Prussia
would be not only useful but even necessary to [the Republic]. This alliance should be defensive and offensive, to
prevent [England] and the [Emperor] from profiting by the weakness of [the Republic]. In that way it would have time,
once minds are calmed, ... to improve and fortify the ports and frontiers, to redress the true abuses in the
constitution, and to reform ... the ministers. Without such an alliance the Republic runs the risk that [England]
and the [Emperor], arms in hand, ... dictate to it what they please: [England] will first seize the Dutch possessions
... and renew the treaty, as harmful as it is dishonourable ...; [...] will open ... navigation ..., and seize all
the possessions of [the Republic] ... [France] alone ... because of the disorder of their finances ... Spain has almost
no interest ... But if these three powers unite in a defensive and offensive alliance for some years with [the
Republic], then the circumstances of the time make all four powers find their interest in it, and, allied together,
they will prudently give themselves the forces needed to stop the ambitious views, harmful to all Europe, of the
[Emperor] and of [Russia]. Write to me on this subject.

**Jan 1784 (s173-s174).** ... what the politicians of your country think of it; and if I am left to act, I shall turn
the whole to the good of [the Republic] and of the House of Orange. The change of ministry in [England] will not
change the plan formed between the [Emperor] and the King of [England] ... for the needs of my mission ... the purse of
[His Serene Highness] in a secret correspondence ... known to [His Serene Highness] ... that [His Majesty] be interested
in my mission, and that his interest and the fate of the House of Orange, which alone are dear to me, with regard to
[Her Royal Highness], absolutely required it, I would never have revealed myself ... out of love for [Her Royal
Highness] ... to watch over the mind of [His Majesty] and make him conceive and accept all that tends to the good of
[the Republic] and of the House of Orange ... But if by chance it succeeded, these are the points on which I could
make my reckoning: ... to remove Monsieur Larrey's prejudice that I am only a simple carrier of letters ... that is,
my true secret, the necessity of my mission, the influence I have over the mind of [His Majesty] ... in political
affairs ... with all the King's men of business ... in matters of negotiation ...

**26-27 Jan and Feb 1784.** P.S. [The King] is much better today, and at bottom his illness is not dangerous; it is
only a cramp in the arms. If the news that reached Holland was not written directly by Madame la Marquise de
Chatelet or by Count G. von Goertz, it still comes from that source ... will have told it to Monsieur [...] and
Schula will have written it to [Heiden]. As regards the correspondence, the channel is not discovered and never will
be ... of [the Prince]; here there must also be a regular exchange of letters ... there is no one of [the Cabal] who
knows me ... the confidence of [His Serene Highness] ... informed of much of what passes at foreign courts ... for the
interests of the House of Orange and of the Republic ... nothing can hold me but love for my very dear Princess, for I
have long loathed my secret post ... underlined letters of [His Serene Highness], [Her Royal Highness], [Van der
Hoop], [His Majesty] and [Schoning] ... that he be at [Berlin] ... to the new [...] of [France] ...

**10 July 1784.** The revolt the Gazette placed at Petersburg was really at Constantinople, stirred up by "our"
Gaffron; [His Majesty] recalled him, and he will be put in Spandau ([the Cabal], [Prussia], [the Stadholder], [the
King]).
