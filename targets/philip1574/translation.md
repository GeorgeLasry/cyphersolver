# Translation of the cipher passages (from the clear version, corrected against the cipher)

The ensign Diego de Esquivia has come here and told us how he was taken captive by the Moors in 1558 on the
expedition the Count of Alcaudete made into Barbary, being ensign of the company of Captain Juan de Arcayda; of
the time he spent in Constantinople as a slave of Hasan Pasha; how he gained his freedom in [15]73; and how he came
to Naples, where you then were, to warn you and inform you of some things he thought fitting to my service and the
good of our affairs. He gave me a very detailed account of it, saying he had given you another like it. Having seen
that account and the good report he gives of everything in those parts, it has seemed that, for the practice and
experience he has of these matters, it will be well for you to make use of him near your person, of which I have
wished to inform you; and for this we have assigned him 15 escudos a month of allowance near your person. I have
wished to tell you this so that you know it, and to ask and charge you earnestly, as I do, that since you know him
and have knowledge of him, you employ him and make use of him in whatever, according to his quality and
experience, you see he can do.

Because the said Diego de Esquivia brought us a letter from Oram Bey [Hürrem Bey], who they say is the Turk's
chief interpreter, and has advised that it would be very fitting to answer it, encouraging him to continue in our
service and offering him that, if for this reason he were forced to come to our kingdoms, he would be received and
maintained in them, he is written to in this same sense by the letter that the said Diego de Esquivia will show you
in the original; it has seemed best to hand it to him so that he uses it by your order and as you think fit, and in
no other way; so it will be well for you to order him what he is to do in this.

Likewise another letter is written to Mostafa, who they say is a Milanese engineer, a renegade, who was at La
Goleta and now goes with Uluç Ali in the Turk's fleet, in the sense the said Diego de Esquivia has advised; he also
carries it and will show it to you, so that it is used as you think fit. Madrid, 28 August 1574.
