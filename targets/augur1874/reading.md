# Augur's route-cipher telegrams, January 1874: reading

Decipherment by `route.py` from `ct_22.txt`, `ct_29.txt`, `ct_30.txt` and `ct_45.txt`. The word order below is the
order the route gives; nothing is reordered by hand. Punctuation and capitals are added. Square brackets mark
code words (arbitraries) left as written, with a conjecture after `=?` where the context suggests one; `[sic]` marks
the letter-book clerk's copying slips. Fillers at the end of the last column are listed separately.

## Telegram 22, to Lt. Gen. Sheridan, Chicago, 9 January 1874 (indicator "Baltic", 9 columns x 10 rows)

The Supreme Court of the State has decided the law under which the late election was held here unconstitutional.
Governor Davis anticipates, however, that the legislature and governor then elected will meet in Austin on Tuesday
next and attempt by force to get possession of the State government. The [sic: He?] asks me to prevent this by use
of United States troops. I decline to act without orders from the President [written "precedent"], who by law is
made judge as to when United States troops are to interfere in the troubles of a State.

In clear after the cipher: Am I correct, please reply.

## Telegram 30, to Maj. G. B. Russell, Austin, 12 January 1874 (no indicator, 7 columns x 5 rows)

I [written "In"] am informed through [written "though"] General Sheridan that the President has no instructions to
give on the subject, but that if any change occurs I will have immediate notice. I have sent the Governor's
dispatch.

Filler: Brownsville.

## Telegram 45, to Maj. G. B. Russell, Austin, 17 January 1874 (no indicator, 7 columns x 10 rows)

It seems to me if Governor Davis yields to force without armed collision it is all that can be expected of him.
Under the circumstances it is evident neither the President nor the party desire trouble. You need not specially
recognize either governor. You can return to-morrow unless your further stay there is desirable. Instruct Sellers
not to interfere in any way without orders from me.

Fillers: Waco Denison Houston.

## Context

Edmund J. Davis, the Republican governor, lost the December 1873 election to Richard Coke. The Texas Supreme Court
(the "semicolon court", Ex parte Rodriguez, 5 Jan 1874) held the election law unconstitutional; the new legislature
met in Austin on Tuesday 13 January and Coke was inaugurated on 15 January while Davis held the ground floor of the
Capitol. Grant refused Davis's requests for federal troops and Davis left office on 19 January. Augur commanded the
Department of Texas at San Antonio; Russell was his officer in Austin.
