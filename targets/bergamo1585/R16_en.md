# R16 — the nuncio in Paris to Rome, 7 March 1586: English

Translation of `R16_reading.md` (same holes).

## Translation

7 March 1586. From France.

Today the Duke of Guise sent word to me through the ambassador of the Duke of Ferrara that he now holds it for certain
that the Most Christian King must make peace with the heretics as soon as he has collected what is to be sold under the
bull, but that the Duke of Guise will never consent to it; and that he has also written what he means to answer the
Most Christian King if he raises it with him, and he showed it to the ambassador, who added that what was written said
in substance that the Duke of Guise neither could nor would consent to that peace. That the Most Christian King really
has this in mind I gather is now believed by others too, and above all by the prelates of the congregation. I am now
told by a person of great authority that he heard from a gentleman that, when this gentleman lately talked with the
Duke of Guise about this war, he came to say to him that he might be content for the peace to be made if he were given
Metz and a good sum of money, and the Duke of Guise answered: would to God it were done so. I also hear that some of
the Duke of Guise's closest people have hinted the same quite clearly in certain talks with persons of standing,
adding that with the money they would now receive they could then prepare for a new enterprise if need be. There are
those who believe, indeed it is almost the common opinion, that although the Most Christian King shows the Duke of
Guise every sign of confidence and affection, His Majesty's heart does not match the outside; and it seems that the
Duke himself is of this opinion, and for this reason chiefly he [?is] about to leave here very soon, and without any
settled plan for resisting German troops [?should] the need arise. Not only the members of the congregation of the
clergy but the other churchmen of the city too, I see, remain very discontented with the grant of the bull for [?the]
alienation as it stands, and show that they would not complain had it been [?made] for the first sale only. And I
hear again that the congregation is inclined to appeal to a [?future] council if it sees it cannot obtain what it now
attempts; nor [?do I see] that the congregation has much hope of being heard by Our Lord when it has recourse to His
Holiness, though I do not fail to encourage them in this, and I offer to send their letters, put in my cipher too. […]
