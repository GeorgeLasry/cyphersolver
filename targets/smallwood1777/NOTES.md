# Smallwood to Washington, 19 Sept 1777: erased words marked with symbols

Status: explained (not a cipher: the marks stand for erased words and carry no recoverable text), 26 Oct 2026.
Catalogue item 486 (class C, from the Oct 2026 Founders Online sweep, `research/founders_emlo/`).

**Document.** Brig. Gen. William Smallwood to George Washington, "James Mechlans Tavern" [West Caln Township,
Chester County, Pa.], 19 September 1777. ALS owned (1994) by Joseph Rubinfine, West Palm Beach, with the franked
cover; Sprague transcript in the George Washington Papers, Library of Congress (DLC:GW). Printed in *Papers of George
Washington*, Revolutionary War Series 11 (2001), p. 272; online at Founders Online 03-11-02-0272. The editors: important
parts of the text on the ALS "were erased and replaced with typographical symbols", apparently against interception,
and "no key for such a code has been identified".

**What is on the page.** The whole letter is clear English (`transcription.txt`, from the edition). Five words or
phrases are replaced by four distinct marks: `*`, `§`, `§§`, `†`, `§`. That is all the "ciphertext" there is.

**Why it is not a cipher.**
- The marks are the printer's reference marks (*, †, §, the series used for footnotes), not invented cipher signs.
- `§` stands in two places for things of different kinds: a number ("our united Force amounts to §") and a route,
  place or carrier ("my Baggage I shall send by §"). One sign, two unrelated fillers, so it is not a substitution
  for a word, letter or number.
- `§§` (doubled) fills a verb phrase ("exerted to §§ as expeditiously as possible"), `*` a number of troops, `†` a
  verb ("I have—† this County Lieut."). The marks look like a sequence of blanks, not values.
- Five tokens with four types give no statistics and no key; a code of five groups cannot be rebuilt from itself.
- The editors say the words were *erased* on the ALS and the marks put in their place. The information was removed,
  not encoded. Whatever the marks pointed to (a verbal message by the express rider, or a separate slip) is not with
  the letter.

**Context (not a reading).** Washington to Smallwood, 17 Sept 1777 (Founders 03-11-02-0255), asks him to "form a
junction with the Main Army as soon, as you possibly can" and names Warwick Furnace on French Creek; so `§§` almost
certainly concealed the junction ("join you" or the like). Smallwood's 15 Sept letter (03-11-02-0234) says he was
"afraid the Express has fallen into the Enemys hands", which explains the precaution on the 19th. Gist was marching
to join him with Maryland militia (editors' note 1, Md. Archives 16:378-79). These are contextual inferences about
what was erased; nothing in the marks confirms them, and the page does not fill the blanks.

**Not seen.** The ALS (private hands) and the Sprague transcript (LoC, behind a bot check when tried). Neither is
needed for the conclusion, but the LoC image would show whether the marks are Smallwood's own or Sprague's
renderings of blank erasures. The Rubinfine catalogue (1994) was not found online.

**Prior art.** None beyond the editors' note (Founders, checked 26 Oct 2026; one web search). No decipherment exists
because there is nothing to decipher.
