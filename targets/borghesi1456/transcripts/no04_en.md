# no. 4 (DECODE R4793): Galgano Borghesi, Naples, to Leonardo Benvoglienti, 12 May 1456 — English (partial)

Translation of `no04_reading.md`, same holes. The letter is not read to the bar (second paragraph fragmentary).

## Translation

Worshipful and magnificent sir, honoured as a father, after commendation etc. Since you left us here we have had no news from you, which is not without some surprise. We do not think there is in you […] any negligence, which is not your way, but rather some difficulty that we think [?has arisen]. Which, besides surprising us, also displeases and troubles us, and in [?frequent] […] that we sometimes use your term, and we say that someone, and by the road too, […] does not get here. So we ask you to let [?this] be advised quickly, and whether it is a thing to be said […] and to my instruction well etc. If there is anything else, if need be use the cipher and say we do it, and we here will keep this [?order] […].

Next. His Majesty the King had his adversaries in a tight corner and […] at my instigation wished to include some of the […] And in the end this was […] that we said that when Lucca was besieged by the Florentines, the […] promises to the captain […]. And with this […] we stayed […] and being sent for [?the Pope?] the writings, he said they had been left at Milan and that we could not show them. Then the King made light of it […], and so they were left almost mocked; all of it good, though, to bring water to our mill. But let us know about […] troops.

Antonio d'Arezzo here was saying that the Duke was well disposed both towards Lucca and towards peace. And that he had satisfied [the] Pope that, for the peace with Iacopino [Piccinino], he should take some of the […] ready […] of the clergy of the Florentines […] who had not yet allowed either the Pope or the cardinals to take any […] one day in another way […]. […] and then, as needed, against the Turk […]. This same, if it is true, you must have likewise […]. […] and so [?he said?] all of it, on behalf of the Duke, to [?report] to the King […].

At Siena we think the same, by the letter left with this one […] that we do not know whether it is true, to inform them of everything, and that we have news of us […] when it happens there is need of another expense to send it […] to Siena […] could do more, that we have been kept without a penny […] so that we have news of you […] Nothing else […] greet. Given at Naples, 12 May.
