# R790, ff. 230-232: St Germain, [22] September [1645], in modern English

St Germain, [22] September.

I have written at length to the King on what I touched on to you last week. Porter has arrived. In his letter you ask
for an answer on the point concerning the [234]. The Queen bids me tell you that, this being a matter of church and
bishops, she will give no opinion on it; and besides, you do not say clearly [486] whether you want an absolute promise
made of [469] all [353]ment, or rather the hopes of it, swallowed by them rather than given to them. In short, she
leaves this whole business to the King without putting in any opinion of [?her] own, and [525] she bids me give you this
as her answer.

This week I received a long dispatch from Sir Kenelm Digby. The substance is that there is no hope of money from there
except on new terms proposed by [the] [?Pope] to Sir K. D.: the total removal of all the penal laws against Catholics
and a toleration of religion settled in England, and that the peace of [?Ireland] be concluded to the satisfaction of
the Catholics on their terms. [On those conditions] the [?See] of Rome will take up the King's quarrel and spend on it
to the last penny they can command; and truly it will be little enough for a toleration.

I have answered Sir [350] D. that I have written to the King for orders for him in this business, which he must wait for
before the Queen can give him any; that in the meantime [545] the removal of the penal laws, which he might promise, and
which is something on which he might, with good enough grounds, ask the [?Pope] for those small sums that would satisfy
us. Now I have no more [541] to [486], but remain, in great [?haste], yours.
