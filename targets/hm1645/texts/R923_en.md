# R923, ff. 233-234: St Germains, [29] September [1645], in modern English

St Germains, September [29].

[529] my dispatch of last week to the King and to [120], there is more to add now. I write only for the sake of
writing, and to tell you that the King will hear more from the Queen by this [?post]; she had a migraine today, which
is a very fair excuse. Our design for the [424] is going well, and the Duke of Lorraine again gives us some small,
distant hopes, but nothing should be built on that yet. I do believe some foreign [?help] is so likely to be had that I
already fear our forces coming together for the relief of [85], lest that bring on a battle, which I think ought to be
avoided as much as the relief of [85] ought to be attempted. Relieving the place will give us what alone we ought now
to hope for after so many disasters: the advantage of the winter without any further ill effects of them; and the
battle will take that from us if we lost it. I am leaving for Fontainebleau this very moment and have no time to tell
you why until next week. I am entirely yours.

here, and his father too, that it was intended by them to be done. If there was no [489] order and no cause for it, as
I suppose there could be none, it will be necessary to free him from his father's suspicions on this occasion; [...]
