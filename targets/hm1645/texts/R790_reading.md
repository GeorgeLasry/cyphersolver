# R790, ff. 230-232: from St Germain, [22] Sept [1645]

Header: one letter, unsigned, with a passage in the left margin; Lasry dates it to 1645 (the penal laws, Digby at
Rome).

## Reading

St Germain, [22] Sept.

I have written at larg to the King that I touched last week to you. Porter is arrived. In his lettre you desire an
answear to the point {concern}ing the [234]. The Queen bids me tell you, it being a {business} of church and bishhps,
that [she] will interpose no opinion in it; and besides, you do not clearly [486] whether you will have an absolute
promis made of [469] all [353]ment, or the hopes thereof rather swallowed by {them} then given {them}. In fine,
{she} leaves this {who}le {business} without any interposition of [?her] opinion to the King, and [525] {she} bids me
give you in answear.

I have received this week a large {dispatch} from {Sir} K Digby. The substance is that [?there] is no hope of money
from thence but upon new termes proposed by [the] [?Pope] to {Sir} K D: a total takeing away of al the penall lawes
against papists and toleration of {religion} settlud in {England}; that the peace of [?Ireland] be concluded to the
contentment of the Catholiks upon their termes. The [?See] of {Rome} will wed the King['s] quarrel and spend in it to
the last penny they can be {master}s of; and truly it will be little enough for a toleraton.

I have mame answear to {Sir} [350]d that I have written from orders for him in this {business} to the King, which he
must stay for byfore the Queen can give him any; that in the meanspame [545] takeing away of the pen[all] lawes, which
he might assure, and which is a thing upon which with ground anough ha might demand of the [?Pope] {those} small
summs that will content us. Now I have no mor [541] to [486], but rest in great [?haste] yours.

## Notes

- "byfore": the figures give 292 (by) + 341 (for) + e; Lasry's "because fore".
- "toleration of {religion}": 475, Lasry's own fill; it is also the French "alliance d'estat non pas de 475" in the
  22 Sept letter of ff. 218-221 (same key).
- "the [?Pope]" (221): a name number (the 2xx block holds names: 208 Prince of Orange, 229 Rome); the Pope is the
  reading the two contexts need ("new termes proposed by 221 to Sir K. D.", "demand of the 221 those small summs"),
  Digby being at Rome on the Queen's business in 1645.
