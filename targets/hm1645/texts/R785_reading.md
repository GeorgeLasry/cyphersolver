# R785, ff. 213-215: from St Germain, 1 Sept [1645]

Header: the figures begin with a separate line, "The Queen is perfectly recovered". Image 1 (f. 213) and its left
margin, then image 2, are one letter signed "A Cowley"; image 3 (f. 215) is a second letter in the same key and
hand, beginning in mid-sentence and signed "much entirely yours" (its first leaf is not on the record).

## Reading

The Queen is pertectly recovered.

St Germain, [1] Sept.

I have very little to add to my last more than to tell you the {business} of the {ship}s [?doth] advance excellently,
half the money being already givenn, and the [?remainder] will be got. A lettre also from the Duke of Orleans, writ
as wee could have wished, is in the Queen's hands to return tomorrow; so that on our partys all is performed that was
demanded. I hope they will not fail in [?what] [is] promised on [522]s.

Ned Prodgers is arrived, by [541] I have receiveds of yours; I have also intertained him at large. I {do} not
{who}ly excuse you, though you seem to have some reason for your discharge in the {business} of [228], for you ought
not to be lyable to provocation, so necessary is both the effect and example of your agreeing.

I hear [148] is angry with me for a lettre I wroto to you [541] hee was concerned. I shall wonder both how hee could
be so if he saw it, and how he could see it; but I hear this from otheres, and from him [488] no[ne] but the
exprsssions, the [k]indnes that in truth hee owes me. So that I beleive this is rather the practise of {those} that
will give him occation to be displeased than the verity of the {business}.

The Duke of Boullion, being at {Rome}, hath made a proposition to Sr K Digby of raising and transporting into
{England} for the King his [500] 1500 horsemen and [500] dragooners at his own charge, without expectation of
reimbursment until the end of the war; and hath desired a gentleman might be sent from the King to mget him at Cullen,
with intention to treaty and deal with him. Ue hath dewanded many things to be donn for him hear by the Queen, which
are too hong and not materialll you should know. The Queen hath resolved to return D Wyat to prepare his opinion
{concern}ing this {business}. Hee intends to give your {lord}{ship} a larger acount by the next. I remain for
[?ever] your {lord}{ship} much obedient servant, A Cowley.

[Image 3, f. 215:] ... him to deal with him. I am of opinihn this money layd out upon {foot} well armed, and {foot}

From the Queen I must tell you {she} is not yet of opinion the treaty y of the marriage with [208, Prince of Orange?]
shall be made [?public]. Anothere thing this day is grown if [of] expectation more than formerly, that is a speedy
[507] with [237], a thing that ought to awaken us in {England}. It looks severall ways upon us. It is true that [543]
wee [348] falling if [into] the {rebel}s hands, more is so like as that to deliver us; and in that regard, in the

There is a patent to be prepared for a baronet for {master} Wright; you have the {particular}s from W Crofts, {who}

{particular}. Pray {dispatch} it and it return it by the [344] [334]. I thank you heartily for your care of Campsfeild
and desire your to chntinue it; he deserves it of you by his just acknowlsdgments. If your sent the lettre from [212]

## Notes

- Lasry's plaintext reads 292 as "because" and 435 as "of"; every occurrence needs "by" (here by the Queen, by the
  next, by degrees, by unbusying, by his just acknowledgments) and "next" (by the next ocation; until next week in
  R923). Both changes are in the key decode above.
- "if [into]": the figures give 384 (if); 383 is into. Not checked on the image. "anothere thing ... is grown if
  expectation": the same group, where "of" is meant.
- "sone, sone way or other": soon, some way or other.
- "your" for "you" twice at the end (desire your, if your sent): as the figures give it.
