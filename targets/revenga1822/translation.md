## Fragment 1 (frame 0057, right; heading "Second duplicate. To the Minister of State and Foreign Relations of Colombia")

«The two great parties, the republican and the constitutional royalist, go on as be[...]», but the first is working to carry out «[?its] revolution», which, if it happens, will be «bloody and disastrous». They are acting in concert «in Cadiz, Barcelona and the [?northern] coast», and nothing has been spared to act with more effect.

«The banishment or [?confinement to quarters] of Riego» was made necessary on the part of «the ministers», to keep the plan from going to extremes at that time; and although «Riego» was later given «a distinguished post, the republicans» in revenge brought about «the [?removal] of the ministers». «The Cortes», to quiet them, are passing one after another «democratic measures, such as» the abolition of entails, the suppression of the bodyguards and others, and the abolition of tithes and of the seigneurial rights now under debate.

But these same measures, which are far from satisfying, stir up and embitter other enemies, «not only among the republicans but among the constitutional royalists». «Today» the Cortes were told «in secret session» of an «anti-constitutional revolution being plotted in [?Andalusia]». «The republicans», who do not like the «present ministers» either, at once raised their voices, «accusing them» of not having foreseen and prevented even the intention of such a plan.

The ministers have tried to shield themselves against such attacks by «joining» beforehand «the [?association]» to which the others belong; but these have formed one or two «schisms» [...]

## Fragment 2 (frame 0058, left)

[...] «[...]rados» [...] of which decided the fate [...] the degree of «poverty» is beyond belief: last week no more could be raised «in the General Treasury than seven million reales de vellon», and as «St Ferdinand's day» approached, [...] «the King», without any consideration and without regard to the growing number of the disaffected, the aggrieved and the unfortunate.

A revenue plan is being debated that is rather a collection of invented and possible taxes and duties; and this plan, besides being so faulty, will not begin to yield anything noticeable before next January.

It has been suggested «privately to the ministers that they propose a loan; but» none dares, both because the last one, «of ten million duros», ruined the reputation of «Canga Arguelles, and because», having been spent «on none of the public purposes» for which it was raised, «that remedy» has become hateful to the people.

Every sign indeed shows that the country is moving «[?fast] towards civil dissolution», and «national bankruptcy» is already spoken of almost openly.

With feelings like these, it is all the harder «to bring about agreement», since «each party» sees an obstacle in backing the full emancipation of America, and not, as it should, an added force that would «secure victory for whoever declares first».

And in this struggle of opposed feelings, aims and interests, our friends think they can find a way of conciliation by playing up, more or less, the state «of bankruptcy in which» the nation finds itself.

Our first task is to bring into agreement «the American deputation», which is badly divided, and then, «with its [?weight in numbers]», to negotiate with each of «the European parties».

## Fragment 3 (frame 0056, left)

[...] «the state of utter nullity in which it finds itself»; the fear that, if we were not «recognised by Spain, we would not be» recognised by anyone else; «and also the opinion of several French politicians», and above all that of «Madame [?Orloff], that haste was needed, because the Emperors of Russia and Austria ("Alemania") and the King of Prussia had divided America among themselves», led, or seemed to us to lead, «[...] to think that it would be wise to accept this proposal».

We, on the contrary, have seen that «the very utter nullity of this government» increased, if anything, «our right»; that the fear of «those three powers» can only be a fancy; and that to count our recognition «by the others» as out of reach «otherwise» is to leave out of account the true interests «of each».

We decided to pay him «a [...], in which [...]» some of the reasons that must guide us. Both his opinion and his help are of great use to us, because there are friendly relations «between him and the ministry, and we expect that [?he will act]» in full agreement.

## Fragment 4 (frame 0056, right)

[...] it will be arranged that, «before they dissolve, the present [Cortes] authorise the Executive to treat» definitively with us.

In any case it will matter to us that the news from America should not contradict itself. It is said in public that Apodaca writes that there is nothing to fear; but in a private letter «to the minister, received last night», he tells him, as we have been assured, that he will probably be «the last viceroy; and his wife tells [...] the Minister of Marine, whose aunt she is, that it is better to resolve to lose it» single than to be forced to lose it double.
