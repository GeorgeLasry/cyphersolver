# R1887: Ferdinand, King of Hungary, to the Cardinal-Infante, Stuttgart, 28 October 1634: English translation

Since the common concern of establishing the welfare of our most august House requires above all ... one may easily
gather that he will not leave off any [attempts] that he believes will serve to restore those princes who have thrown
themselves on his faith and protection, above all the Württemberger and the Durlacher. And the [outcome] (crisdus) from
it will bring no less profit or harm to Your Dilection's affairs than to my [side?]. Therefore, since for my part I shall
not cease to strive to overturn these attempts of the enemies of our common House, I have thought it most necessary to
rouse the same zeal in Your Dilection, asking you most kindly that, by means of someone who, their minds being drawn off
in different directions, more easily for me to carry through this and their (... 16 tokens ...) business by the movement
and diversion of your arms, you will not disdain to promote the common advantage of our aforesaid most august House,
which we both have in view — no less [than that] glory which, from the battle of Nördlingen, by your very great merit,
is owed to you with the Most Serene ... posterity.
