# mssEC 19 pp. 1-7 (War Department Ciphers Sent, 27 Jan - 5 Feb 1864): telegrams T1-T11 as sentences

Code words are resolved with Cipher Book No. 9 (cipher9_key_used.tsv) and written in plain words; the line-by-line
decode is mssEC19_p1-7_decryption.txt. The telegrams are in English, so no translation is given. The check words at
the end of a telegram ("very warm here", "raining", "what a disagreeable day") are left out. The ledger's odd
spellings (temper = rarely, Harp hers Very, Commune = nick = eight, Capt your), probably meant to disguise the words
on the wire, are written as the words they stand for. Each heading gives the operator or office the ledger names and
the date (all in Washington, 1864); dates and time words are written in words, as the ledger writes its dates.

## Reading

T1. To Sheldon, Fort Monroe, twenty-seventh January. Washington, January twenty-seventh, half past three p.m. For B.
F. Butler: Baker's cavalry have left here. They should reach Yorktown some time tonight. D. H. Rucker, Brigadier
General.

T2. To Smith, St Louis, twenty-eighth January. Washington, January twenty-eighth. For U. S. Grant: Major General
Sedgwick telegraphs that two brigades of Ewell's corps have left for Johnston or Longstreet, one on the 20th and one
on the twenty-fifth. Adjutant General Anderson telegraphs from Lexington of an expected raid by Morgan through Stone
or Sounding Gap into Kentucky. Halleck, half past one p.m.

T3. To George D. Sheldon, thirtieth January. Washington, January thirtieth, half past ten a.m. For Butler: On the
absence of the Major General commanding I have the honor to inform you that those men of Parker's regiment of
cavalry now in Washington will leave this city tomorrow morning by steamer. They will not number more than fifty
enlisted men. There are said to be some two hundred recruits for the regiment in Maine who should be sent to Fort
Monroe direct, instead of through Washington. Signed J. H. Taylor, Chief of Staff, Assistant Adjutant General.

T4. To Captain J. P. Willard, thirtieth January. Washington, January thirtieth, half past eleven a.m. For Major
General Thomas: Probably Johnston's detachment is made to counteract Sherman's movements, as I presume no immediate
attack on Mobile is expected. Major K. Garrard, late chief of Cavalry Bureau, has been ordered to report to you for
duty. What is it about the evacuation of Corinth? Signed Halleck.

T5. To Baldwin, first February. For Brigadier General Lockwood: Please send immediately to Harper's Ferry such
troops as you can spare for a few days for the protection of the railroad against raids. Reply how many can you
send. Halleck, ten a.m.

T6. To Lieutenant W. B. Kelly (Lawrence), first February. For Brigadier General Kelley, Cumberland, and Brigadier
General Sullivan, Harper's Ferry: Brigadier General Lockwood has been directed to send you all available troops in
his department; also one battery, one regiment of cavalry and one regiment of infantry have been ordered from this
place. As these will soon reach Harper's Ferry, reinforcements can be drawn from that place. Signed Halleck.

T7. To George Baldwin, Baltimore, first February. For Lieutenant Colonel Cheesebrough: All troops that can be
temporarily spared must be sent forward immediately to Harper's Ferry. Signed Halleck, one p.m.

T8. To George W. Baldwin, Baltimore, first February. For Mr. Garrett, President Baltimore and Ohio Railroad: Major
General Lockwood is ordered to send troops to Major General Kelley. Two regiments and a battery are ordered from
here. Signed Halleck, half past one p.m.

T9. To George Baldwin, Baltimore, second February. For Brigadier General Lockwood: As you have forwarded one battery
and one goes from here, I think they will be sufficient unless others should be asked for. Signed Halleck, half past
ten a.m.

T10. To Lawrence, Harper's Ferry, third February. For Sullivan: Would it not be better to make your proposed cavalry
raid on the rear of Early's forces, to cut off or embarrass their retreat by destroying bridges and supplies and
felling trees in the roads? By this measure you may enable Major General Kelley, if he drives them back, to overtake
and capture them. The First New York Veteran Cavalry left here yesterday morning to report to you. Signed Halleck,
half past three p.m.

T11. To G. D. Sheldon, Fort Monroe, fifth February. For Butler: Your letter of the third just received. You will use
your forces in Virginia as you may deem best for the relief of North Carolina. All available troops from this place
and Maryland have been sent to West Virginia. Major General Sedgwick has been asked to give you such cooperation as
he can and to communicate directly with you by telegraph. Signed Halleck, noon.

## Notes

- Ranks are given as the code words give them. Where the Official Records print the same telegram (or_clear_texts.txt)
  they differ in three places: T4 Village = Major, where OR has "Brig. Gen. K. Garrard"; T8 Vernon Lockwood and
  Vermin Kelly, and T10 Vernon Kelly (= Major General), where OR has only "General" (both men were brigadier
  generals). The clerk seems to have used Vernon/Vermin loosely for "General".
- T2 Antwerp = "Adjutant [General]" in the book; OR has "Assistant Adjutant-General Anderson".
- T4: OR has "the evacuation and burning of Corinth"; the ledger has only the evacuation.
- T1 and T3 were not found in OR vols. 32 pt. 2 and 33. D. H. Rucker's rank is as coded (Vermont = Brigadier
  General).
- Time words agree with the printed times in 6 of the 8 printed telegrams that have one; T2 has Martha (1.30 p.m.)
  for OR 1.24 p.m., and T7 Harriet (1 p.m.) for OR 1.15 p.m.
