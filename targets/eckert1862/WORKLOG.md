- 2026-10-27 access: Huntington Digital Library (CONTENTdm, collection p16003coll11) lists 67 Eckert items: 35 ledgers, cipher books #1 (6 printed copies + 2 handwritten), #2, #5 (16 copies), #9, a Generals-and-Places book, Stager's memoranda on key holders. - 2026-10-27 transcription: harvest.py written; pulls every page's transcription to data/items/<pointer>.jsonl. mssEC 15 (War Dept Ciphers Sent, Feb-Jul 1862): 172 pages, 162 transcribed, 23,516 words.
- 2026-10-27 hypothesis: the ledgers do not hold route-transposed cipher. Sent and Received ledgers alike record the telegrams with the route already undone, in reading order, with the code words still in place and the padding words at the end ("For Andes ... Alden light snow falling"). So the open problem is the code-word layer, not the transposition. Result: confirmed on mssEC 15 and mssEC 01 (War Dept Ciphers Received Feb-Jul 1862, pointer 6796) pages 9, 14, 19.
- 2026-10-27 key from source: Cipher Book #1 printed copies (mssEC 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46) are printed word lists (two code words per line) with the meanings written in by hand. Copies 41, 43, 44, 45 agree on p. 9 (Adam = Maine ... Alden = Indiana, Andes = Fort Donelson); mssEC 46 has 1864 fillings (Lt Gen Grant, Atlanta campaign places). The printed words match the 1862 ledger (Irving, Indus, Juno, Jargon, Humboldt, Shylock, Saffron, Rapture, Lark, Myrtle, Merlin all occur in mssEC 15). Result: failed for 1862 - the meanings differ: in Feb 1862 "Andes" is McClellan (Lander's "For Andes", "Andes plans as to Twinkle"), "Alden" signs Halleck's St Louis telegrams. Book #1 as filled in these copies is a later issue reusing the 1862 word list.
- 2026-10-27 key from source: mssEC 40 and mssEC 37 ("handwritten cipher book", Eckert's) are post-1866 codes (entry "Act of Congress of July 1866"); mssEC 38 (Fairchild, Duncan's Union Book Store New Orleans) is Banks's Department of the Gulf issue, 1863 (Port Hudson, Brashear, Opelousas). Result: failed for 1862 - none of the surviving Eckert cipher books carries the 1861-62 meanings.
