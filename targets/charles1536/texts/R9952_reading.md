# R9952 — Charles V to Prince Philip, Cremona, 19 June 1543 (AGS Estado leg. 59, ff. 10-14), the cipher passages

Rests on: the contemporary decipherment written out in full on ff. 13-14 ("del Emperador a Su Al[teza]", DECODE images
P12-P18, P20; transcribed line by line in `R9952_decifrado_ff13-14.txt`), checked against the letter's figures
(ff. 10-12, images P3-P11): every clear word or phrase that the cipherer left inside the cipher runs ("las quales son",
"tener continuament para la conservacion", "venir adelante de seguirse algun gran inconviniente por", "la qual para las
cosas que tenemos en las manos podeys bien juzgar si nos seria necessaria y la reputacion", "dignidad imperial nos trae",
"difficultad dexandolo a las espaldas podrian", "con mas facilidad y mejor se podria hazer", "respondemos al dicho
principe", "le remitimos que haga tomar en Seçilia y Napoles o donde", "siendo menester se ha de buscar y proveer en
qualquier manera que se pueda hazer", "Assimismo que provea de algunas naves ... de otra manera") falls in the same
order and place in the decipherment, and Tomokiyo's code words (go para, pi enemigo, sa este, xu dinero) stand where
the decipherment has those words. No key of this cipher is in the repository, so the runs between the clear words are
not checked sign by sign. Where the figures' clear words differ from the decipherer, the figures are followed
("dexandolo", decipherer "dexando").
The decipherment gives only the ciphered passages; where the letter goes on in clear the decipherer wrote "/ lo otro
claro /" or "/ claro /" (here a full stop and a new paragraph). Spelling is the decipherer's; certain abbreviations are
written out (nro = nuestro, Su San.d = Su Santidad, S.r = Señor, duc.s = ducados, Fran.co = Francisco); struck words are
left out and interlined corrections taken in, as the decipherer meant.

## Notes

- The matter is the offer made at Genoa (the Busseto meeting with Paul III, June 1543) through the Marqués del Vasto
  ("marques del Gasto"): two million in gold and a yearly census from the Pope for the investiture of Milan to Ottavio
  Farnese, duke of Camerino, Charles's son-in-law (husband of Margaret, "la duquesa nuestra hija"); "duque de Castro" is
  Pier Luigi Farnese. The arguments for and against follow, then a loan of 150-200,000 ducats from the duke of Florence
  (Cosimo de' Medici) and as much from the barons of Naples; the last part (f. 14) answers "el dicho principe" (Andrea
  Doria) on the Turkish fleet: the galleys to go towards Sicily and Naples, La Goleta and Bugia, the marqués de Aguilar,
  the hundred light horse and the two thousand Spanish infantry.
- "aduocacion" is the decipherer's word for the "abocación", the meeting with the Pope.
- "[?viendo]" (f. 13r): an interlined word over "y", partly legible. "[?vee a] nosotros" (f. 13 bis r): the decipherer
  wrote "no se be a nosotros". "[?el]" (f. 13 bis r): a blot before "rompido". "[?movimiento]" (f. 13 bis v): the word is
  written cramped. "[de]" before "vender" is supplied. "paresce […]" at the end of f. 13 bis v: a short word before
  "claro" not read. The last line (f. 14 bis r) ends in a short word or flourish after "scripto", not read.
