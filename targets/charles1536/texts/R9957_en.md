### ff. 273v-274r: the last cipher passage, as deciphered on the leaf below the date and signature (stamps 0017-0018)

Having, besides the Germans who are already in my camp with Antonio, and the others we have provided to come down to
the full number of twenty-five or thirty thousand men, as is said in the [?other] letter, to do on this side of Italy,
with these and with the Spaniards and with the other troops and provisions being gathered, all that can be done against
the enemies; a greater number of men being made ready in Germany, and it being necessary to keep them in pay and make
use of them so as not to give them occasion, being ready as they are, if we do not use them, to go and serve the King of
France; and that to bring a greater number than thirty thousand Germans down into Italy would be confusion; and that
nothing can be more profitable than to divert the forces of the enemy and give him trouble in many places; and having
for this [?purpose] […] up to a hundred thousand ducats in Germany and as many in Flanders, which we could not well use
for affairs here: we have agreed that the said Germans be raised and gathered, whom, besides those who are to come down
into Italy, we have ready, as has been said; and that with these, and with what we have provided for this purpose to be
made ready in Flanders, for which, our high steward having come from there, we have sent him back to press and put in
order what will be needed; and also so that some help in men may be sought from Germany, as has been offered to us at
other times; and if it can be done in time to help in this, let it join the rest, and if not, with the said Germans
[and] the rest from Flanders, the Count of Nassau and our said high steward are to enter France by the side of
Luxembourg towards Mouzon and do the greatest damage they can; for this, at the right time, together with what will be
done on this side of Italy and by sea, will be of no small profit to press and harm the enemy and put him in greater
need, etc.
