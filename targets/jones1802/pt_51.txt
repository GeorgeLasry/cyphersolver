I had this Morning a very long and confidential Conversation with the Bacha, in which, after making me
acquainted with the Contents of several Dispatches he had received from Constantinople on the 15th Instant,
he began, in terms that astonished me, on account of their openness, to regret the Alliance the Porte had
been forced into with their natural Enemies (as he called them) the Russians; he even went so far as to say,
that the Porte had put herself completely in the Power of her Enemies; and added this remarkable Expression,
some of the wisest and best Heads at Constantinople were and are against this measure - He then put a very
unexpected Question to me - whether I thought England would, if she should find an opportunity, to do so,
make a separate Peace? - This Question, and a train of Political Reasoning the Bacha afterwards entered
into, cause me to think, that he was repeating to me Reasoning that had been made use of by one Party in the
Divan at Constantinople, and of which he had received an account by his last Tartar. -
The Bacha is regarded in the Empire as a Man of consummate Wisdom, Prudence and Political Knowledge - his
Opinion has great Weight at Constantinople - his Opinion on the Russian Alliance, he has not scrupled to
declare to me, is in direct opposition to it; and this Opinion I am satisfied he now transmits, in strong
terms, to Constantinople. The English and the Turks have sufficient Force, he said, to defend Turkey, and can
prevent the French passing through Turkey to India - Government (said he emphatically) are embarrassed with
this Russian Alliance, and the People dislike it. -
Perhaps these particulars may fortunately be acceptable to you, and His Majesty's other Ministers, at least
I hold the transmission of them will not be displeasing to you. -
