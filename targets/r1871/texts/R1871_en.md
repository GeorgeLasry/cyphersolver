# R1871, Sebastiano Foscarini to the Doge, Paris, 11 September 1680, despatch no. 117, P.S. sola: English translation

Most Serene Prince,

After the despatch was signed, a report was sent to me. Although I cannot give it full faith, having no time to look
for confirmation, the weight it carries means I must not fail to pass it on to Your Serenity.

On the reports that keep coming from Italy that the Spaniards are considerably increasing their troops in the
Milanese, I am told that the King, who has a great number of soldiers under arms, has resolved to reinforce the
garrison of Pinerolo up to six thousand men of the old regiments, and to distribute sixteen thousand fighting men in
Dauphiné and Provence, so as to be ready, should the occasion arise, stronger than the Spaniards themselves.

It is not unlikely that the news written to me by a confidant of mine at court is true. These precautions may have
been advised by the illness of the Duke of Mantua, by the suspicion that the abbé Morel keeps alive of some sudden
accident to his life, and by the rumour that during his last illness the Spaniards had meant to push five or six
thousand men into Casale.

I am also given to understand that munitions continue to be carried to Pinerolo from [the French? 269?], but these
will be the metals I already mentioned in my letters of [114?], which were being brought from the sea by way of
Savoy, and for which passports had also been requested.

I shall watch so grave a matter as closely as it needs, and shall try to sift the reports so as to give a better
account of them to Your Serenity in the next post, assuring you that I spare no diligence in doing my duty.
202.106.100

Paris, 11 September 1680. Sebastiano Foscarini, Ambassador
