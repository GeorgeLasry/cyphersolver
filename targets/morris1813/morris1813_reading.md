## What the page says

The page is a fragment of a longer despatch: the frame is headed "4", and the code runs on other frames (see
NOTES.md). As far as it reads, it deals with Spain's Floridas. Someone (the subject in L1-L2 is lost) would be induced
to send "a respectable force" there to counteract the British. The British had offered "last year" to send [troops?]
"under the [pretext?] of preserving the territory to Spain, but probably with the intention of holding it". A change
in the Regency, once the Cortes are organized, would help "their views". The page then turns to the democratic
government of the United States "under the administration of the Frenchified Señor Jefferson [and] Madison",
before and during the European war, to the separation of Spain's colonies, and to the insurrection of [West?]
Florida and Baton Rouge in the summer of a year "1 [?] 1 [?]". The territory is said to belong to Spain, to have
been an integral part of the Floridas, and something was "conceded" under certain conditions concerning "both
Floridas". The page ends with the "treason" of a governor and the "ambassador of the revolutionary government" of a
place. The tone ("Frenchified") suggests that Morris is reporting Spanish charges against the United States, but
whose words they are is not clear from this page alone.
