# Hugo de Moncada to Charles V, Monaco, 6 October 1524 (BNE MSS/20213/12, DECODE R1191): English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `reading.txt`: the clear letter with the cipher runs (in {braces}, as
there), read after CODOIN XXIV (1854) except where marked [here], which the print left out. Same marks: `<…>` code
groups left as signs (each occurs once; no key), `<99>` two signs at a line end, probably nulls. Spanish.

## Translation

Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty.

After having written that of the 5th of this month, and the weather not having served for the brigantine to leave,
Your Majesty's army has arrived at Nice today, which is the sixth of the said month, from where they write to me that
it passed the mountain by the Riviera of Genoa the following day without having had any hindrance. The artillery they
were bringing they broke into pieces and are carrying on the backs of mules. The King of France is said to have been
at Saint-Maximin, but it is not known for certain.

The brigantine which was coming in company with the one taken by the French, in which the Duke of Bourbon's [man] was
coming, who could not be found, put in at Toulon thinking it was held for Your Majesty, so that both of them have
fallen into the hands of the French; and so it has been reported to me.

Monsieur de La Motte came to me tonight, at three hours of the night, with a letter of credence, which will be with
this, from the <=D.> {Duke of Bourbon}; the credence is that <P#D> {he begged me to carry him to Spain [here], <99>
giving me [here] many reasons for it. I have answered him that, once he puts Your Majesty's army in safety, and that
these galleys have no bread} for two days, as I have written to Your Majesty, and it is necessary to go to Genoa to
victual them, and that after they are victualled it must be seen {whether the Duke of Genoa will be willing to let the
galleys go, and the Viceroy of Naples those of that kingdom, for only those of Spain remain, in bad order; and that,
in case his person had to go, I would want it to be strong}; which being at Genoa, {I shall be with him, and there}
it will be dealt with more {at length. I shall hold back this journey because I do not know} in what Your Majesty will
be best served. Let Your Majesty send orders with speed of what is to be done {for him. Turning to this business, he
is coming very ill content}, as the said {Monsieur de La Motte} has given me to understand <=P#D.f>.

The weather being good for the brigantine to leave, I have not waited for letters from the Duke of Bourbon or the
Marquis of Pescara, since their men who stay here have not been willing to go with him. Sacred Caesarean Catholic
Majesty, may God our Lord keep and exalt Your Majesty's royal person and estate for very long times, as Your Majesty
desires. From Monaco, 6 October 1524.

Of Your Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty the slave and servant who kisses your royal feet and hands,
Don Hugo de Moncada
