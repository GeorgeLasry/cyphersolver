## Translation

Most magnificent Lord. On the 14th of this month I received your letter with a papal brief and two other letters of
the Venetian ambassador with one of his own, urging my going against the Turk; since this agrees with the will and
command I have from Their Highnesses, in the strength of Jesus Christ I prepare myself for it with all the speed and
haste the case requires.

But it seems to me that so good a work as this, with so much difference and labour of this fleet, should not pass as
a mere duty without great reward. [?And] [xob] that this, [how much] it would be, if an enterprise suited to Their
Highnesses, [?…]; I do not speak of it, [zeb] that the season does not allow, according to the need there is of them;
because [?to speak] at such a time, in such regard, would seem to have more respect to [what] seems to benefit
Christendom. And so the going, which is my part, I set about at once, [zer] that [?the sea matters]; I refer myself to
[…] because we have better disposition and time, the business being in such danger and so far from where […] can
negotiate what I in this case, [rix], and I believe Their Highnesses would not take it ill.

That […] of the [?Turk] which are in the Venetians' power should pass into ours, but because, given the nature of the
Venetians and of the opinion, not [quanta] how it would be received […] that [quanta] should ask […] regard […] not that
in this way: that this fleet goes into such strange kingdoms, and its people many and of strange condition, to mix
with many other people, that it will be well that they give us […] ports of Italy where this fleet can shelter when
the season requires, so as not to have to go back so far, to be of use at the needful time, and also to put our sick
and wounded, and clothing and victuals, and these ships, if they […] to repair something. So that we may be at […]
ours, without the inconveniences of being in other men's houses; [?further] to grow; and the opinion is not [zog] […]
is [?asked], because when he [the Pope] called me to the siege of Ostia I asked him the same through Garcilaso, and he
was content and [?granted] me […] the city […] [?Terracina] willingly; and because the castellans were Spaniards and
servants of the King our lord, I was content with the [?agreement] […], so that in conformity with this way it seems
to me, Sir, you should speak of this under colour of our rest and refitting.

And because in that part […] they have not [vog] that so much would satisfy from here, they would make [it so] that
they have those [bog] in Apulia, which the Venetians [hold], Brindisi and Otranto; they ought to satisfy us [?…], for
now and according to their need I do not think any of this impossible for them, and I believe that for want of
[?…] industry they [?let it be] lost; for if the season here allowed it, the danger not being so near, I do not
doubt that much could be gained with this fleet, as [xie] the ambassador of Venice, not the one at Naples, has
offered; and the need, which is known on all sides, that is […] [bog]. I strip myself and discharge myself, and am
content that we have this business in our hands.
