# Fiesque to Catherine de' Medici, Vienna, 14 August 1568 (cipher passages)

[f094] ⟨… in which you write to me⟩ that I should try to learn in detail what the couriers from Spain have brought,
which I have done as diligently as I could; of which, however, I have been able to learn nothing other than what I wrote
to you in my previous letters. You will have learnt from that of the [?sixth] of this month how the Emperor kept telling
me that he would speak to me. Since then he has told me that what he wanted to tell me was that he is sending his
brother Archduke Charles to Spain for two reasons: one, the affair of the Prince of Spain, and the other, the affairs of
Flanders, which seem to be in a state of great need of good provision, [?for] the good of the country, the peace of
Germany and of all Christendom; and that, holding the office he holds, he is bound to make every effort in it, which he
could make with no greater effect than by sending his brother himself to […], to make known to him his advice and what
all the princes of Germany judge necessary for the good of his country of Flanders. He will be very glad if the embassy
is found good and bears fruit, and if it does not profit, at the least he will be discharged before God and all

[f094v] the world of having done his duty and what belongs to a good brother. He also told me that on his said
brother's return he will be resolved on the matter of the marriage, for he will certainly come to the conclusion of
the said [?…], and all, not wishing on any account to wait longer; and although he has sent a courier ahead, he expects
no answer on this matter, because he says that up to now it has been handled by letters, which speak only once; but that
to be better satisfied and avoid delay and so many words, he has thought it better that his brother himself should
bring about the conclusion, which he hopes to have within four months, the length of his brother's journey; he will
leave on the fourth of this September to go, and will embark at Genoa. And that on his return he [?would like] very much to pass through France; to which I told him I would be very glad of it, for I was sure that [ϖ] (the King) and you would
take great pleasure in it, and in having occasion to welcome him and show the goodwill you have always borne and still
bear him, and all who depend on his majesty; for which reason I begged him earnestly to do me the favour of letting me
know, so that I could tell you. He told me he was not yet sure of the road he will take on his return, but that he
will not fail to

[f095] let me know. I think it would be well for you to write to him that you have heard from me that his brother was
going to Spain, and that if on his return he wished to pass through France you offer him the country and passage. You
will please forgive me if I go too far in giving you advice, for I do it thinking that this will greatly help to keep
[?your] good friendship; and also because I believe that [mm] touched on this, being sure that I would let you know it.
Someone has tried to tell me that the eldest son of [the Emperor] will return with Archduke Charles, about which [ϖ] (the Emperor) has told me nothing; I shall not fail to inquire into the truth of it and let you know. The second son of the Duke of Bavaria will also go
to Spain with the said Archduke Charles.
