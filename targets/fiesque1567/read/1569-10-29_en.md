# 29 Oct 1569, Scipion de Fiesque to the King, Vienna

[f158r] ⟨…⟩ with the [?copy] of the answer you made to [the Emperor], whom I told how you were ready to leave to join
your camp, to pursue the fruit of the victory that God has given you, [?where] all the principal lords [will be] and captains of your
kingdom, with whom to take some good decision; and also about the [?offer] he made you to be willing to employ himself to
negotiate some good pacification of the present troubles; and [that] you commanded me to thank him very warmly for the
demonstration he gives of always holding in singular regard the good, profit and advantage of your kingdom, and that
[you] would answer with like affection in everything that concerns him. He told me that he was moved to put forward this
course only by a sincere affection and great desire to see [you] delivered from this civil war, and especially now that
he will have this close bond of your marriage with his daughter, offering to do everything in his power; being of the
same opinion he has always been, that where you could remedy things with gentleness you should not use rigour, being
[?by] nature [your …] always [?so well] loved by these subjects.
