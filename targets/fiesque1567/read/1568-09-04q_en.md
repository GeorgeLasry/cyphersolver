# 4 Sep 1568, Scipion de Fiesque to the Queen Mother, Vienna

[v109r] Since I wrote you this first letter, [the Emperor], who had promised to tell me something of his will before the
ordinary courier of Flanders left, told me that, [?] having the wish to be the mediator of this marriage as he had told
me, he thinks fit to satisfy him, and that he believes that you [?too] will find it good, for it will [?be] the better
for it; that he has resolved to send Lord Ernest […] to Spain, and for this purpose he has sent for him in Bohemia; and
that he hopes that [the King] of Spain […] the marriage, for he will write in such a way that he will see that he wishes
this marriage to be concluded as soon as it [?can]. I assured him again that I believed you would be very glad that he
[?should mediate] this marriage, and that you had no less wish than he to come to the conclusion. He told me that there
is no longer any obstacle, but [?] that one must still wait this little time; and that even if the prince of Spain had
not died, his intention was always […] that he should [have] an alliance with [the King] through one of his daughters;
and that if, on the […] occasions, some difficulties had been raised, he did not consider them such as [?ought] to
hinder this good work; and that the Bishop of Retes himself, who had stayed a long time

[v110l] with him, has […] very well been able to see the good affection he always bore you. Then I took the occasion to tell him
that, the Bishop of Retes being assured of the good friendship he bore the King, he judged not to [?carry] the writing, that he knows [ϖ] (the King) gives no occasion to alter their good friendship, persuading himself that some people had put
forward this obstacle to separate their union; but that in the end this friendship would be confirmed by a good
alliance. He told me that he loves his eldest daughter dearly, more than all his children, and that he hopes that you
and [the King] will be content with her; and he said it in such a way that one could see in his heart the joy he had in
seeing her Queen of France. I assured him that [the King] and you would behave towards her in such a way that every day
he will be more content with this marriage. He told me again that he will write to his commissioners that they take
care that no false reports are made [?to the] princes, and that no troops are raised in their lands against [the King];
and that I should write to you that he advises and begs you very strongly to maintain […] in your kingdom. I told him
that [the King]

[v110r] is doing all in his power, but that there are some who are not content with reason, […] that [ϖ] (the King) [?more] assures [?them] every day that he will maintain his edicts. […] Lord Ernest […] of [?…] and knight of the Golden Fleece has arrived, I will try to find out whether he will pass through France; [ϖ] will let you know, so that [?…] welcome him as his rank deserves.
