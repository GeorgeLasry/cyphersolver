# Fiesque to Catherine de' Medici, Vienna, 15 November 1567 (cipher passage)

[v022r] Madame, since the departure of Monsieur de Lignerolles, which was on the fifth of this month, and who went by
way of the Duke of Saxony, nothing else has happened, except that the rumour ran in this city that many foot and horse
were being raised in Germany to go to France in favour of those who have taken up arms against the King. I spoke to the
Emperor, who told me that he had been warned that men were being gathered, but that he hoped that once his mandates had
arrived no one would dare disobey, and that he would soon have news of it; that it is quite true he has been written to
that some soldiers [are going] to France by stealth from over there, but not in complete companies. And when I told him
that these levies were being made in the lands of the Duke of Württemberg and of the Count Palatine, he told me that he
has written to those two in particular in good black ink, and that he hopes they will not go against their faith nor
against what they have sworn; and that I should rest assured of what he has told me so many times, that he wants you to
know by experience the real wish he has to keep up good friendship with the King, and that he goes about it in good
faith; for which I thanked him, and begged him to believe and be assured that the King and you would have no less
affection towards him. He keeps asking me how the affairs of France are going, but I do not know what to tell him, and
the news we have over here says most of it [?through] Chantonnay. The Emperor is holding today a diet

[v023l] of his country of Austria, which is for nothing other than to get money; and after it is over he goes on his
own to Prague. […] you there to this [?court]. [?The] people of Flanders […] as he […] what [?Chanton]nay [?in] answer
from the one you have sent to Switzerland. Which will be the end, after praying God to give you, Madame, in perfect
health a very long and happy life, with the full accomplishment of your wishes. From Vienna this 15th of November 1567.
Your most humble and most obedient subject and servant, Scipion Fiesque.
