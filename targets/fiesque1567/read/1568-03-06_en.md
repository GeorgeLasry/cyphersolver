# Fiesque to Catherine de' Medici, Vienna, 6 March 1568 (cipher passages)

⟨Madam, my last letter was of the last day but one of last month, since which⟩ [f039r] I have heard that Duke Johann Wilhelm of Saxony, who has gone to France in your service, has brought with him six preachers of a sect called the Flacians, and has told them that with them and with arms he [?hopes] to chastise the papists and Huguenots; which I did not make much of, as it seemed to me he had not forces enough for such an exploit. But [...] told me one must make [?more] of it than I think, for it is only too true that he strives only to spread and increase that sect, of which his brother Duke Johann Friedrich, whom he holds prisoner, was long a member; and since then he drove them out, and the said Duke Johann Wilhelm took them in; that it is to be feared [?the more], since several of his captains are relatives and friends of those of [?Duke] Casimir and almost of the same sect, that you may not get such service from him as your affairs will require; not that he doubts the said Duke Johann Wilhelm will fail you or do anything against his honour.

And many at this court find it very strange that you have given him so great a command, being son-in-law of the Count [⊸] who has been the cause, as you think, of the length of this war, and that you do not perceive that he and Casimir [...] [...]. For this reason I believe it would be greatly to the King's profit, as I have written to you several times, that these people [...] the kingdom, and your French forces [...]. And indeed [...] who [?held] [...] a long discourse with me on this matter, is of the opinion that it would be much better that [...] a single one to severity towards those who [f039v] have offended him, provided they do not want any strongholds in their hands, and [?to yield] to some of their demands in order [?to be rid of] these troubles, because he sees that these wars last [?too long] and can bring only great ruin and damage to this kingdom. And according to the answer he will have [...] that he has sent you, he will exert himself to see you out of these civil wars. And as far as I can see, as I wrote to you before, he is waiting to learn the King's will on this, so as afterwards to [...] a similar dispatch to the King of Spain to beg him to be willing to restore those of Flanders to his good grace.

He has sent a courier to Spain, who was for nothing else than to [...] and to negotiate some reconciliation between him and his son. A courier from Spain has since arrived, but up to this hour I have not been able to learn anything of it. It is quite true that [?people] know the cause of the arrest of the prince, which he keeps secret.

⟨Madam, seeing that at present I can do you no service ... From Vienna this 6th of March 1568.⟩
