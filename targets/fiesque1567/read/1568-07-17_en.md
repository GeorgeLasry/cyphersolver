# Fiesque to Catherine de' Medici, Vienna, 17 July 1568 (cipher passages)

[f079] […] that the lord […], the Emperor's grand [?equerry], was to go to Spain; he has not yet left, nor has any
courier, and I have not been able to learn what this long delay hangs on. Archduke Charles has come here to speak to the
Emperor, and some have tried to tell me that it is [?because of] the long delay in deciding to send [?…] there, to
[?…] earnestly [?…] the release of the prince; and that there has been some talk that Archduke Charles himself would
undertake this journey, the Emperor and the Empress having a very great wish for it, and being resolved to work with all
their power for the release of the said prince. However, I cannot vouch for it, for the dealings are so secret that up
to now I have been able to discover nothing of them, of which I have [?…], and also because I have not dared raise it at
all with the Emperor, because [?…] at our last conversation together, that after he had sent his despatch to Spain he
would tell me how long he expected to wait for an answer; and since no one has yet left, it seemed to me that if I
[?tried] to learn something of it,

[f079v] it would have been out of place, and that [?…] in such a way [?…], I shall not fail to inform you of what I
[?…] diligently from day to [?day]; but I fear this affair will be very long. The rumour also runs here that Archduke
Charles's coming here and [?…] that he [?…], and [?…] that he should go to reside in Flanders, and [?…] [?the country]
is so discontented at seeing itself governed thus by the Spaniards; but for my part I see no likelihood of it.

[f080, postscript] I have [?…], and I have heard that he has sent a despatch to Spain, and that a courier will leave
tonight, which is a sign that the said lord [?…] will leave as soon as the said courier has gone. I shall try to learn
here discreetly from [the Emperor] [?…], and what I have written to you above.
