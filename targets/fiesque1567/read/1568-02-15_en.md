# Fiesque to Charles IX, Vienna, 15 February 1568 (cipher passages)

[v037r] [I found him] somewhat vexed that he had had no news of you since the departure of Monsieur de Lignerolles.

[v038l] But in talking with him it seemed to me that he would be very glad if you were out of these troubles and affairs with honour, by some good [?settlement]. And indeed you will have seen, from the dispatch of [?the man] whom he sent you last Sunday, that the resolution of the diet of Fulda is to learn whether it will be your good pleasure that the Empire should mediate to reconcile the Prince of Condé and those of his following, and restore them to [your] good grace. And I am told that once they have [?learned] your will, they intend to send the same dispatch to the King of Spain to persuade him to restore those of Flanders as well and to pacify the country, which is discontented with the proceedings the Duke of Alba is carrying out there, which are not thought good here, but too harsh; and it seems to many that [?they] will not be able to come to a good end. And among others Duke Augustus of Saxony [?presses] this matter hard; besides the alliance and great friendship he has with the Prince of Orange, he fears, as [v038r] several others also do, that these troubles, both of France and of Flanders, will in the end bring great ruin upon Germany. And the said Duke of Alba shows that he suspects some rebellion in Flanders, because he is spending heavily in Germany, for he has already had reiters raised, and besides this he secretly keeps several captains of horse and foot to be ready when he sends for them.
