# 2 Dec 1568, Scipion de Fiesque to the Queen Mother, Speyer

[v129r] [The Emperor] told me that he has been written to that Monsieur d'Aumale was going to besiege a town of Duke
Wolfgang of Bavaria, and that if this was true, he feared that all the circles of the Empire would resent it and would
stir up something that might well turn to the King's harm. I [told] him that I thought that if Monsieur d'Aumale had
gone to the lands of Duke Wolfgang, what he will have done [was] to divert him, so that he should not lead into France
against [the King] the [?force] which rumour said he was assembling. He told me that he thought it very good that any
forces should be prevented from going against [the King], that good order and diligence should be used in this; but that
as for besieging lands belonging to the Empire, he fears it may give occasion to

[v130l] those who stay quiet to [make up their minds]; which he likewise gave Duke [?of Alba] to understand, as far as I can
make out. [The Emperor], who has done every good office he could with the princes of Germany so that they should give no
help to another […] did not want them to take occasion to assemble under colour of defending the lands of the Empire.
I wrote to you how he had given the legate Commendone to understand that nothing would be innovated touching religion,
and that he had dismissed the deputies who were to assemble at Martinmas; and that as for the marriage, nothing will be
said of it until he has had news from Archduke Charles. I believe you will have been able to find out on the side of
Spain what the intention of [=ii] is, and that it will please you to advise me how I am to conduct myself, for I see
well that there is a change. Nevertheless, as far as I can judge, the intention of [the Emperor] is that if [pp] (the King of Spain) marries
his eldest daughter, [the King] should marry the second. I think that on the Archduke's return all will be settled.
