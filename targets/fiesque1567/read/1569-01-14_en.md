# 14 Jan 1569, Scipion de Fiesque to the Queen Mother, Vienna

[v140r] On Wednesday a courier arrived whom Archduke Charles sent from Spain, who left Madrid on the tenth of last
month. I tactfully tried to learn from [the Emperor] whether any decision had yet been taken touching the marriage. He
told me that this courier brought only news of his brother's arrival, and that […] at the beginning of the business;
but that he soon expected another, by whom he hoped to have some light; and that this courier had met the Cardinal of
Guise between Barcelona and Zaragoza. However, although I sounded him, saying that I wished the marriage to be
accomplished soon, and that I hoped, now that the Cardinal of Guise is in Spain, that everything could be concluded
without further delay, I could not draw a word more out of him on this matter. The rumour runs strongly here that [the
King of Spain] will marry the eldest daughter of [the Emperor]; and even someone who often knows secret things told me
that this marriage of [the King of Spain] will certainly be made, but that [he] is working so that [the King] should
marry the second daughter of [the Emperor], and that Madame Marguerite, [?your] daughter, should marry the King of
Portugal; which

[v141l] you will know better than I could write it. As I have written to you several times, that you [?could] expect the decision [?very soon] from the side of Spain, since on this side it seems to me that everything depends on the will of [the
King of Spain]. [The Emperor] told me that Duke Wolfgang has about three thousand [?men] together, and that the rumour
runs that the Queen of England was raising three thousand, but that so far he has nothing certain. It seems to me from
his talk that he fears that a great war may break out this spring. The ambassadors of the [?Landgrave] of Hesse are here,
but I have not been able to learn what they are negotiating. The eldest son of Count [?] has a son by the sister of the
[=ii]; and the man who had told me that he had no children is a person of great authority, who told me so, wondering
that the Elector of Saxony should give his daughter in marriage to Casimir.
