# The Indus script without sound values: what 650 registered tests established

Daniel Bourdeau, 26 October 2026. A summary for specialists (Andreas Fuls, K. Rajan), written for critique. Every claim
below cites a registered set in `targets/indus/PREDICTIONS.md`, where the hypothesis and threshold were committed before
the test ran; scripts and results are in the same folder. Nothing here is a reading.

## 1. Data and method

- **Corpora.** A database derived from the Interactive Corpus of Indus Texts (ICIT; Wells and Fuls), as exported by the
  indus-website project: 5,680 records, of which 3,681 clean texts with find spot, object type, motif, size and material.
  Mahadevan 1977 (M77, 2,906 texts) aligned to it sign by sign through the 991 lines they share (96% of signs on one
  counterpart). The 1,664 M77 texts absent from ICIT are the replication sample ('B') throughout; the open digitisation
  of CISI vol. 1 was a third check (92% of sign positions agree with ICIT).
- **Rule.** Hypotheses with thresholds committed first, then run, recorded as held or failed; counts over distinct texts
  (mould copies and sealings once); replication on B or on a second city; two readable scripts as controls, Linear B
  (DAMOS) and the Ur III seal legends (ORACC). About 650 sets, 3,430 hypotheses, from September to October 2026.
- **What was given up.** Published readings (Parpola 1994-2010, Mahadevan 2014, Fairservis 1992, Kak 1988, Yajnadevam
  2024, Wells 2015) were tested as predictions about use and none holds beyond the positional facts it was built on; a
  character-model scorer that recovers Ventris's Linear B key against 100 shuffles finds no published key reading its
  language better than its own shuffles (sets 205-207, 354, 641). Free fitting of values picks the wrong language on
  Linear B (Sanskrit over Greek), so a key that 'fits' is no evidence.

## 2. The grammar a reading must fit (sets 176-280, replicated on M77)

1. Four kinds of text on one vocabulary: a name with its ending, a line ending in a closer, a count, a bare sequence;
   3 of 2,722 distinct lines fit none.
2. The ending slot (740, 520, a dozen closers, then 400 or 90) is chosen by the name's last sign: 95% predictable on
   M77; 30 of 30 signs that prefer one ending on ICIT keep it on M77. Names are head-final; numerals precede what they
   count; no number agreement, no chains of suffixes.
3. Scribes never split a bound pair or a name from its ending across a line (0 of 31 against 69 of 383 other gaps), and
   almost never split an opener from its marker 1 / 2 / 60 (1 of 33, 2 of 85); they do not break lines at Wells's
   initial-cluster boundary (set 651).
4. Graphic marks are morphology: the cage of four strokes turns a fish into a closer; strokes inside the jar or the U
   change the ending into a name element. Other drawn marks keep the sign's job (set 351). Ligatures are not
   abbreviations: the short-stroke sequences they would stand for are never written, and the long-stroke ones share no
   contexts with them (set 656).
5. One system served the whole Indus world: the grammar learned at Mohenjo-daro parses Harappa nearly as well as its
   own lines, seals parse tablets, late levels parse early ones (sets 227-229). Within square seals no sign differs by
   region (set 371).

## 3. What the texts are

- **Seal texts are individual compositions.** Capture-recapture across the two cities puts the population of names at
  3,000-5,500, 92% on one seal, as Linear B personal names behave at matched length; on Ur III seals only the whole
  legend is that individual. A name is not tied to its animal.
- **The kind of object shapes the text, the region does not.** Models that know the object kind (seal, tablet, pottery;
  copper, incised, moulded tablets) predict unseen lines better (4.635 to 4.559 bits per sign, also on sign tokens
  alone, replicated within ICIT); period, material, shape, size and workmanship add nothing beyond it (sets 357-376).
- **The size of a number decides what is counted.** Numbers 1-3 count the fish signs and 803 / 806; numbers 4 and up
  count 390, 405, 407, 575, 585 and 900, which stand at the line end in every length band (sets 552-564; 12 of 14 skews
  replicate on M77; holds within one numeral series; Wells 2015 pp. 69-70 noted part of it). The same split in Linear B
  is bookkeeping: at Knossos sheep, grain, wool and olives take large counts, chariots, women, horses and months small
  ones, 9 of 12 skews repeating at Pylos (set 643). The units do not go with the picture on the object (set 642), and
  both groups also take endings; the large-number units stand bare at the line end (set 652).
- **The measure sign 700** takes long-stroke values 2, 3 or 4 almost exclusively (1 once in 172, 5 never), mode 3-4
  even with plain 700 counted as one; measure-like in lacking 1, as Linear B T and Z do, but mid-peaked unlike any
  Linear B shape (set 655). Wells's volume of about 40 litres per stroke rests on three measured jars and cannot be
  extended: no inscribed pot in ICIT has dimensions.
- **Substitution partners are a property of the sign.** Minimal pairs (texts identical except at one position) sort
  Linear B tokens into numerals, measures, ideograms and words (positive control) and ICIT signs into numerals, endings,
  markers and the rest, though not M77; a sign's partner set on ICIT recurs on M77 (p = 0.001) (sets 653-654;
  `results/substitution_classes.tsv`). A lexicon of 369 recurring units with their sites, object kinds, endings, counts
  and pictures is in `results/unit_lexicon.tsv`.

## 4. The Tamil Nadu graffiti (sets 204, 635-640, 646-647, 657; data from tngraffiti.in, used with its own parallels)

- Composite marks do not combine signs the Indus way: of 193 adjacent component pairs, 62 are Indus neighbours, no more
  than with the parallels shuffled (p = 0.16) or the signs re-paired (p = 0.14); order does not follow Indus order.
- The parallels are about what chance gives: matched blind under one strict standard against 90 random signs from each
  of four scripts, the 42 base signs find 5-6 exact Indus parallels, 2-11 in Cypro-Minoan, Linear B and Linear A; at
  the loose level ('about 60%' in the book) Indus reaches 57% and Cypro-Minoan 64%.
- The Indus-like graffiti are not the oldest: within 12 sites they lie no deeper than the rest; at Keeladi 74% of the
  graffiti below 300 cm (5th-6th c. BCE) have a parallel against 82% above 200 cm; Kodumanal 77% against 79%.
- The book's positional remarks do not hold on either side: the jar is the Indus line's ending (leftmost only because
  the scripts run opposite ways); the jar and the ladder are not mostly initial in graffiti composites; the wavy line
  stands alone 73 times in the corpus. The composition habits do not match either (rho -0.05).

## 5. Against a decipherment, and what would move it

Meaning checked against outside evidence stands at the copper-tablet equations (749 goat, 341 rhinoceros, 753 hare,
777 goat / archer, the lens sign bull) plus local text-picture labels on tablets that do not travel between tablet
kinds. No published key covers those anchors. The frozen models (set 353) await inscriptions published after the 2022
corpus, which would be the first true held-out test; none with sign numbers or usable photographs has been found online.

Requests to the reader: (1) corrections to any count above, which can be rerun from the folder; (2) objects added to
ICIT since 2022, or photographs of the four cited Harappa objects absent from it (H-396, H-568, H-598, H-602); (3) for
the graffiti, the trench and layer of each sherd, which would replace depth bands with dated layers.
