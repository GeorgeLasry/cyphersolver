## Translation

Sire, ⟨since [?] the dangers which the French who pass through Germany meet with, and your ser{o r h e}⟩. The
hindrances, and the greatest causes there have been for two {?years} {...} all Thuringia, Meissen and the Mark of
Brandenburg, delayed me so much that I arrived in Poland at Mezeritz only on the twenty-fifth of February. And
without the favour I received from some of Your Majesty's servants and pensioners, principally the Sieurs Alexander
von Miltitz and George [?Luelz], who accompanied me there with a good and strong troop of their friends and assisted
me with their means, I would doubtless ⟨at the least have been detained or prevented from passing further. But⟩,
thanks be to God, I joined Monsieur de Valence a long time ago now, who taught me the state of the affairs which he
has well and worthily begun and continued until now, and of which I have made a summary together with what I have been
able to judge, which I send to Your Majesty. And knowing nothing at present worth writing beyond the contents of the
said summary, except that I have received the letter which it pleased Your Majesty to write me of the [?23rd] of
February, in obedience to which I shall [?endeavour] to conduct myself so that I hope the good of your service and the
affairs to be promoted here will remain advanced by it, I shall pray the Creator to give you, Sire, in very perfect
health, a very contented and long life. From Warsaw, the 26th of April 1573.
