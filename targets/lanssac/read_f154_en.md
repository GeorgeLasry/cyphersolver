# ff. 154r-v: Lanssac to the duc d'Anjou, Warsaw, 24 April 1573 (BnF fr. 4735 no. 60): English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of the reading in `read_f154.md` (section "Reading"): the cipher blocks,
with the clear words round them in italics or [clear: …]. Same holes: `{…}` and `{n signs}` unread, `[x]` letters
supplied, `(?)` doubtful.

## Translation

Clear: *… Since the conclusion of which* — cipher:

> {*et su-e i nt*} I have learned two things, one which gives me my case, the other which troubles me much. The first
> is that I went to meet the Lithuanians, whose chiefs have promised me to give their vote to no one else but you,
> [clear: *which will be of such importance that*] with the good party we have in the kingdom there is no doubt one
> will put this great crown on your head, unless the Grand Seigneur's recommendation, which he has made of you, as I
> think with guile, through the voivode who(?) of Wallachia, who is(?) {5 signs}, brings some unexpected change; which
> is the second point, which gives me a setback, from which, with the help {4 signs} of God, we nevertheless hope to
> come out.

f. 154v, first block (clear before: *… could not do without money*):

> For I have not fifty é[c]us, although I have borrowed more than fifteen hundred of them. [clear: *Also I had set
> out*] a very beggarly party to come so far and stay so long and stand up against him who [with] many great gifts
> will not be able to overcome our[s], so inferior {2 signs, gutter}.

Second block (clear before: *… more than three hundred écus apiece*):

> And I hope that within a few days all those, with many others, will be your subjects.
