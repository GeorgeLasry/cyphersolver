# ff. 160, 164, 174: Lanssac's letters of 1 and 9 May 1573 (BnF fr. 4735 nos. 63, 65, 70): English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of the readings in `read_f160_f174.md`: the cipher passages (plain) with
the clear words round them in [brackets]. Same holes: `{c}` a stray sign, `{Mc}` a word sign with no key entry (the
party that spent 30,000 écus), `l[e]` a letter in doubt.

## f. 160, to Charles IX, Warsaw, 1 May

> [… according as the occasions of affairs first arise.] For I see that this nation is as venal and as liable to let
> itself be won by money as are the Germans, their neighbours. [Today they are putting to deliberation whether they
> ought to take for King a Piast or a foreign prince …]

## f. 164, to Anjou, Warsaw, 1 May

> [… for there will be the means to employ it] in all sorts of ways {c} to come to your desire. [In which we shall not
> fail … to point out the obstacles of our opponents, who spare nothing there to hinder us,] for if up to now {Mc} has
> spent thirty thousand écus in this negotiation, the Emperor has spent more than three hundred thousand in it, and yet
> does nothing worth anything there for himself; but only he troubles your design, doing the worst he can against you.
> [Again I will not fail, Monseigneur, to remind you …]

## f. 174, to Charles IX, Płock, 9 May

> [… in an affair of such importance, and which has succeeded so happily] against the will and the intrigues of the
> Grand Seigneur, of the Emperor, of the princes of the Empire, of the King of Spain, of the Muscovite and of the King
> of Sweden, who were all banded against Your Majesty. [Yet I shall have still more occasion to praise God …]
