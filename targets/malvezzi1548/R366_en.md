# DECODE R366: Ferdinand I to Johann Malvezzi, Augsburg, 23 January 1548, the ciphered passages: English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `R366_reading.md`, the cipher passages only (key R367/R386); the clear
text between them is not transcribed in the repository. Same marks: `{word}` a word read after correcting a writer's
slip, `[?Semaslia]` and `[?]` unread signs, `[word]` a word supplied. Code words are translated as plain words. Latin.

## Translation

*p. 1.* … the talks Rüstem Pasha held with you, namely that, on account of the treaty of peace begun with us, he has
drawn great hatred on himself, as the author of the making of that peace and now the promoter and defender of its
preservation; and moreover what you answered to whatever objections, questions and enquiries of the same Rüstem Pasha
…

*p. 4.* … that [they] will observe [it], the French trying so manifoldly and deceitfully to overturn this peace; who
indeed do this for no other reason than that, since the King of France and his like do not dare to provoke with their
own forces the sacred Imperial and Catholic royal Majesty, our dearest brother and lord, they may {avenge} themselves at
the expense of the Emperor of the {Turks} against His Imperial Majesty, who suffers nothing to be taken from him by
them beyond what is fair and honourable; but His Greatness and also Rüstem Pasha himself to doubt …

… As for your allowance, we informed you by our last letters that we had decided that one thousand five hundred
ducats a year should be paid to you for your {ordinary} allowance and for your other extraordinary expenses, which we
have been shown can be of little moment, apart from the expenses which will have to be incurred in

*p. 5.* sending your own couriers, for which we shall see to your payment separately; and we shall order Justus de
Argento, our secretary, who will set out shortly with the money, to take your {account} of receipts and expenditure.
By the same Justus we shall also send you that sum of two thousand eight hundred ducats which you wrote to us you are
owed for what you laid out there, not only for your maintenance and support but also in other business of ours, as
well as one thousand five hundred ducats for your allowance for one year to come, to be reckoned from the first day
of the month of March now coming; so that you will receive from Justus himself the sum of four thousand three hundred
ducats at once. Moreover, we are writing to Guglielmo [?Semaslia], as also to Argento, as you will see from the copy
of such letters, that in any case of pressing need, of which you could not warn us in good time, he should not
refuse to lend you, at your request, on one or more occasions, up to three thousand ducats in our name, at some
honest and fitting interest, on the amount of which you and Justus will have to agree with him,

*p. 6.* and that he may be secure that, on presentation of your note of hand [?] in our name, he will without doubt
have and receive payment at Venice. But we wish and require, by your fidelity towards us, that you spend and lay out
this money also, as we do not doubt you have done entirely until now and will do hereafter, only on necessary uses,
or if something useful should occur in which, before you could warn us because of the distance, you needed money for
some useful and timely matter. We shall see to it that a master clockmaker is found, so that our secretary, who is to
come there with the money, may bring him with him. If perhaps Rüstem should question you about our troops on the
borders, you will be able to answer that we [have] no other troops on those borders than are necessary for their
protection, both to restrain [?] curb robbers and violators of the peace, and also to preserve and {maintain} the
obedience due to us from

*p. 7.* our subjects, [?] for no other reason. Furthermore, concerning the captives whom you write were freed at the
command of the Emperor of the Turks, you will answer, if necessary, that this was most welcome for us to hear, and that
we in turn will also gladly see to it that, wherever we learn that subjects of His Greatness have been captured, and
complaint is made of it to us, we {have} them freed and released, and indeed that we omit nothing that pertains to the
mutual sincerity of keeping the peace.
