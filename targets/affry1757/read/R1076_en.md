# R1076, d'Affry, The Hague, 7 January 1757 (no. 124): English translation

I continue to believe that the arming of the eight ships will take place without the augmentation of the land forces,
and we shall know it in a few days, for the resolution on this must have been taken the day before yesterday in
Guelders. Once resolved, this operation will be pushed with all the more vigour as people are beginning to see that the
English will respect Dutch navigation in proportion to the protection it has, and that it is therefore necessary to
escort strongly all the convoys that have to cross the Channel. In the first days after my return here, I was told
from Amsterdam that we or our contractors had in the Texel nearly sixty ships loaded for the King's navy; that the
captains and the contractors would not risk the voyage on the mere summons of a few captains of English warships to go
to England; and that the owners of the ships chartered for us would not let them spend the winter loaded in the Texel. I
had a conversation on this subject with Mr Borel, clerk of the Admiralty of Amsterdam, in which I spoke to him with all
the force of which I am capable, and I ended by telling him that, once the States were assembled, I would go to the
lodging of Amsterdam to repeat what I had just told him, and declare there that since the navy's contractors had dealt
in good faith and in confidence in the safety of the flag, the States, and above all the city of Amsterdam, would be
guilty through their weakness of the failure to supply our navy. Mr Borel was dismayed and begged me to take no step.
He assured me that he would take charge of everything. Indeed, from that moment he never stopped working, and he brought
about the convoy, which would have had four escort ships but for the storm that put two of them out of action. I cannot
describe to you the anxiety they have felt here over this convoy. We learn at last that it has arrived, and people here
speak highly of the help given at Belle-Île to one of the two warships, which had been very badly handled by a gale. A
great part of the Republicans keenly desire some favour for the herring. If the resolution on the arming passes without
an augmentation of the land forces, it is certain that this will be one more means of estrangement between England and
Holland. I hope, Sir, that you will see once more how much I seek to follow my instructions. I repeat to you in truth
that I owe them my conduct, and the successes that you have been pleased to approve until now.
