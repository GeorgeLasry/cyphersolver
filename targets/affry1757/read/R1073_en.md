# R1073, d'Affry, The Hague, 25 January 1757 (no. 133): English translation

The rumour had been spread that I had given a memorandum to Their High Mightinesses in which I declared to them that
His Majesty would regard an augmentation of the Republic's land forces as an infringement of neutrality. This rumour
was destroyed at once; but I thought I should take this occasion to see the Grand Pensionary. I declared to him that
the King did not intend, and had never intended, to meddle in the affairs of Their High Mightinesses; but that out of
his affection for them, His Majesty would see with sorrow that they engaged in heavy expenses, whose consequences the
Republic ought to fear and whose cost it ought to wish to be useless. I repeated to him what I had told him on this
some time ago, and with the same coolness, because he answered me: "I am sure that this operation will meet many
difficulties, and I firmly believe that if the Republicans cannot prevent it, they will at least be strong enough to
have it preceded by a declaration to our court, to that of Vienna and to that of London, by which the Republic will
confirm its resolution to remain strictly neutral; and none of this will be in question until about the twentieth of
next month." It seems that the Abbé Senski, as you will see, no longer supplies very interesting news from London. We
should see whether this does not improve.
