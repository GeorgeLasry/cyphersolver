# R1054, d'Affry to Bernis, The Hague, 19 July 1757: English translation

The purpose of Madame the Gouvernante's journey is still not being given out. Some believe it is to work for an
augmentation of the land forces. It is certain that all the partisans are grumbling about the state of weakness in
which the Republic is being left. The ministers are even beginning to make use of the danger to religion. Others say
that Her Royal Highness, pressed by the repeated complaints of the merchants, means to propose a naval armament. It is
to be wished that this last object is what brings her here. If on the contrary she were to work at getting an
augmentation of the land forces passed, it seems to me that it would be essential to the King's interests to send me
orders to ask at once for a confirmation of everything the States-General have promised His Majesty, and at the same
time a declaration on their part that they will not have any corps encamped on their frontiers. I feel the harshness
of this proposal, but it seems to me that it becomes natural if one adds that His Majesty, from the just confidence he
had in the word of Their High Mightinesses, has not ordered Marshal d'Estrées to take precautions for the security of
his communication with the kingdom, but that precautions would become necessary if, in spite of the word given, the
government were dragged by the partisans of England, and forced by the insults of the populace, into dispositions and
steps that made one fear that Their High Mightinesses were departing, against their will, from the wisdom of their
rules. An attack of gout keeps me from seeing as many people as [...]

(The cipher page ends here. The register continues: "... as I would like. I hope nevertheless to be in the know, and
to be able to report to you towards the end of the week or a week from today. I enclose the account of the Delfzijl
affair, which was handed to me late yesterday; you will also find in it the news from England of the 12th. I received
today a letter from M. de Moras of the 11th, which confirms me in what I had the honour to tell you about East
Friesland. The Count of Wassenaer, to whom I spoke of it yesterday, thinks as I do. He is to send me today the summary
of the articles of the convention between the King of England and M. de Bentheim, for the Countess of that name. P.S.
The Count of Wassenaer has not yet handed me the summary I had the honour to mention to you. I shall have that of
sending it to you as soon as it reaches me.")
