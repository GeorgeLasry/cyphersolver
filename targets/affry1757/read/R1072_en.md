# R1072, d'Affry to Rouillé, The Hague, 6 January 1757 (no. 123): English translation

I had the Angelo come to my house. He told me that he expected to be rewarded already for the service he claims to
have rendered through the reports he gave on England's projects against our possessions in the two Indies, and
namely through the report of a man named Maham, or Mahom, commandant or sub-commandant of the fort at Crown Point.
After much debate we agreed that he would stay here at a ducat a day until you had given me new orders. He now asks for
a gratuity of a hundred louis. At that price he will leave at once for England, from where he proposes to inform us of
the state of that power's land and sea forces; of the true state of the kingdoms of England, Scotland and Ireland, of
the troops maintained in those kingdoms, and a detail of the quarters they occupy; of the present state of English
America, of the troops there, of the state of their forts, of the state of the inhabitants and their inclination, of
the projects of the two proprietors of Pennsylvania, Mr Penn and Lord Baltimore, of the attacks that will be made
there, and of the instructions that will be sent to Lord Loudoun. He assures me that he will communicate everything
secret that is dealt with in the London Cabinet, all the projects proposed to the Ministers, all its intrigues in
foreign courts, all the plans of campaign for this year, and the correspondence the English have in France. He assured
me that he knew of very interesting letters written from Madrid by Mr Wall to England, and that if one is willing to
pay the price, he would undertake to carry off some of them, but that in that case he would have to be assured a
pension that would enable him to live in France or in England. You see, Sir, what this man proposes. What he asks is
considerable if he is an impostor, but if he fulfils his engagements, it seems that the money given him could not be
better employed. I shall await your orders on this with impatience. If you accept his services, his return to London
cannot be too prompt.

I have agreed with Mr Hensi that I would give him tomorrow twenty-five guineas for a quarter's pay of his
correspondent. We shall see what he does in future.
