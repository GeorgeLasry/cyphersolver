# R1070, d'Affry to Bernis, The Hague, 22 July 1757 (no. 210): English translation

It is said today that Her Royal Highness is to go to the States of Holland to harangue them in favour of her father, to
represent forcefully the necessity of helping his Electoral States, and to set out the extreme danger to the Protestant
religion. I do not believe she will bring herself to it; but in case she does, I am almost sure that if she proposes an
augmentation of the land forces as the first means, they will on the contrary propose to her an augmentation of the
navy, as an absolute remedy against the vexations and the robberies of the English. Their partisans are spreading the
most odious rumours about the occupation of Ostend and Nieuport by our troops, but sensible people understand well that
the port of Ostend is closed to the English. Amsterdam and Rotterdam will profit from a trade that England carried on
with the Low Countries and Germany worth more than six hundred thousand pieces a year. There are some towns where some
Dutch ministers have preached on the danger to religion, but the people do not seem roused by it. The event of Ostend
will bring no change to the resolutions of the Republic, unless Her Royal Highness makes it serve as a pretext for steps
dictated by her heart but surely disowned by reason. In that case I shall not hesitate a moment to inform you, and if
it were pressing I would think I should, without waiting for His Majesty's orders and yours, ask this princess for a
conference in which I would try to prove to her how essential it is for her, and for the interests of the Stadholder
her son, to deserve His Majesty's goodwill. That will not be difficult for me. You will find enclosed, Sir, a letter,
or a fragment of one, of the 15th from London, and a statement of the English navy.
