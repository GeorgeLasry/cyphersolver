# R1074, d'Affry to "B", The Hague, 17 January 1758: the Astier extract and d'Affry's news: English translation

Extract of Mr Astier's letter. On what I had heard of a negotiation to be made in England for a loan of twenty
millions, I wished to know whether it had really been in question, and whether it would have been possible for us to
find such a resource in that country. All the information I could wish for has been gathered on this subject, and I
have been assured from a good source that it would not have been extremely difficult to find this sum before the
proclamation, but that the business was so badly conducted, and such clumsy persons were employed in it, that it was
inevitable that the affair should come to the knowledge of the British Ministry, which gave rise to the new law.
Nevertheless there still remain means of evading its execution, and if we wished, it rests only with us to borrow
today four millions at four, or four and a half per cent at most. As to the funds we should assign to the payment of
this interest, the way of making the payments, and of bringing the principal into France without danger to the
lenders, it is a very delicate matter, which requires profound secrecy, a perfect knowledge of the country, and
intelligence in the people who would be employed in it; and I believe it should remain between the Abbé, you and me. I
shall await his orders and yours.

We are short of wheat in Canada. I have just been told that a fleet of twenty-six sail was ready to leave Portsmouth.
Mr Macdonald arrived from France a few days ago and has left here for England. I have some reason to believe that he
has been charged with some commission on behalf of our enemies. I believe this man served in Bulkeley's regiment. Some
attention should be paid to the Scots and Irish who are in our troops; some are spies, but it would perhaps be
imprudent and dangerous to show distrust towards the others. I shall see whether there is a way of being informed of
Mr Macdonald's movements and of his correspondence.
