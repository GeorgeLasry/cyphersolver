# R2067, d'Affry to "B", The Hague, 17 January 1758 (no. 283): English translation

I have the honour to send you today a ciphered copy of what Mr Astier wrote to me. It seemed to me useless to attempt
any money operation in England itself, which, if it came to be discovered, would make our needs appear greater than
they are and would cause the precautions to be increased. Despite these precautions the English will still invest with
us when the interest can tempt them, and they still have at present, with bankers of this country and at Brussels,
funds that could not find a place in our life annuities. I know that there are here two or three young Englishmen, one
of whom is called Macdonald. If he is really leaving France, and it is of interest to know where he is going and what
he is doing, I shall try to put myself in a position to give you an account of it. Two Spaniards have arrived here
from Paris, one of whom is Mr de Lastiri, Secretary of the Embassy at Paris. They are to go over to London any day now.
The letters of the 10th from London have brought us nothing new.

Yesterday I again had the Jew Boas. He told me that Mr de Beckerschee of the Pels counting-house was at The Hague about
the money seized at Osnabrück, in which that house has an interest. He asked me whether he had come to see me; I told
him no. He answered that he was surprised at it. I replied that I was not, since, as the Messrs Pels produce nothing to
justify the use of the money, it was apparent that it concerned our enemies. Whereupon Boas told me that he did not
believe it was intended directly for the payment of their troops, but that he believed it was intended for and
ad[dre]ssed to subjects of His Prussian Majesty; and I do indeed believe that all the chests that are not [?specified]
in the petition will end by being declared good prize.
