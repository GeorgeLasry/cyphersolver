# R1064, d'Affry to Rouillé, The Hague, 1 February 1757 (no. 136): English translation

The States of Holland have separated and are to reassemble on the 20th of this month to deal with the matter of the
augmentation. I hope it will be without success for those who want it. The funds for the war establishment this year
are no larger than last year's, and the means that are proposed for finding them will appear so burdensome that it is
likely this proposal alone will stop everything: they are to consist of an increase of one sixth on the house tax, a
general increase of the excises, and a reduction of the interest on the public funds to 2 per cent. It is to be
presumed that the States will not consent to an operation that would crush the people, revolt the rich and wholly
discredit the public funds abroad. I shall have the honour to report to you on the state of this affair during the
assembly itself, if I can. I shall see the Italian today. I shall carry out what you order me, and the arrangements I
make with him will be the subject of my first despatch. There is no doubt that M. d'Urvoy, who was writing from London
to the Seneschal of Quintin, who travelled from Antwerp to Breda with the merchant from Lyon, and about whom M. de
Hatzel had written to me, is the same man. I am sure that if he comes to Holland he will be arrested, and if that
happens I shall not lose a moment in informing you and in asking for His Majesty's orders in the matter. I am waiting
for a reply from M. de Hatzel about what concerns him.

From the knowledge I have tried to acquire, Sir, about our trade, I have seen how important it was to reduce the
profit of the Dutch herring fishery and to encourage our own; but this encouragement did not seem to me essential at a
time when we have only too much occasion to employ our sailors. I thought it would not be a great prejudice to His
Majesty's subjects to see an important fishery made easier, to which they cannot contribute as long as the war lasts.
Besides, this favour is not a treaty, and it is only temporary. It would also have been the price of good conduct, and
could have ceased at the moment the King thought it fitting for the good of his subjects, or at the instant His
Majesty ceased to be satisfied with the Republic. Of all those the King can grant to this country, this favour is the
most desired, because it concerns nearly all the inhabitants of the province of Holland; and as this fishery is the
sharpest point of jealousy between England and the Republic, this favour would set them further apart. I would
nevertheless ask you for it, Sir, only on conditions, and putting myself in a position to communicate it if Holland
resists the augmentation and behaves well, or to keep it quiet if she let herself be carried along.

I beg you to be so good as to tell Marshal de Belle-Isle that M. de Nispen, a captain in Löwendal's regiment, has come
here, and that I expect to send you on Thursday or Friday a ciphered account of his observations. The Italian told me
today that one La Combe, who must be a clerk with a farmer-general, left England two years ago, that on leaving he had
borrowed money from Pichon, and that he was borrowing more of it to keep up a correspondence with him. This Lacombe is
the same who translated the Philosophical and Political Letters of the Earl of Orrery. He also told me that one M.
Lanzi of Liège, about 50 years old, who speaks Italian, French, English and Latin very well, left England three weeks
ago for France, that he is charged with reporting on the present state of our navy and with getting himself in with
some junior clerk of our offices.

You will see, Sir, at the end of the supplement of today's Amsterdam Gazette, an article from Paris of the 24th. It is
true that the two captains it speaks of passed through here. Here is the extract from my register of passports of 18
January, of that day: "Messrs Joseph du Perieux, captain of the ship Comte de Maurepas of Bordeaux, native of
Libourne; Bernard Clement Omeri, surgeon, native of Coutras (written Coutrai); and Perot Imbert, captain of the ship
la Subtile of Bordeaux, native of that town; going to Paris, and from there to Bordeaux, for one month." You will
notice that the passport concerned the two captains and the surgeon, and that the Gazette speaks only of the first two.

I enclose two letters, one for M. de Montaran and the other for M. de Crémille. You will find in this packet, Sir,
three copies of a work to which M. Rouillé d'Orfeuil gave the form and the wording. I hope you will be pleased with it.
[Notes in the register:] The letter of N. to his uncle van Oller was enclosed in an envelope addressed to M. de
Montaran. [The work:] La Vérité des Faits.
