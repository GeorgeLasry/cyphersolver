# R1063, d'Affry to Rouillé, The Hague, 27 January 1757 (no. 134): English translation of the contemporary decipherment

No. [134], d'A[ffry] to R[ouillé], 27 January 1757.

I am writing today to Mr de Hatzel as you order me; but I believe he will have nothing but vague things to reveal to
us. I am persuaded that the man he speaks of to us is none other than the Comte d'Urvoy. Nothing has yet been heard of
him in this country. The rumours of the augmentation of the land forces have been gaining ground strongly since the
evening before yesterday; to believe the public, it is about to be decided. I have reason (to believe) that it will not
be so soon. As Mr von Reischach has his court's permission to press the request for help more or less, I thought I
should advise him to see the Ministers and ask them how matters stood. This produced what I hoped for, that is,
embarrassment; and every embarrassment is a means for them to delay, and sometimes even to destroy, the projects that
might hamper us. The court of Vienna has in hand the means of holding them back, through the questions it can raise
about the Barrier towns, and above all about the occupation of Namur. Be pleased to recall what I had the honour of
telling you about it; but as this means is very delicate, I believe that to use it in concert with Vienna we must wait
until the delicacy of the moment leads us to it; for the most singular thing might happen, namely that the Empress
would have, against her will, a garrison in one of the strongest of those towns, which these foreigners supply amply
with everything. As I am informed of nothing, I can only put before you an idea of what (may) happen. I cannot believe
that this country is so lacking in reason as to wish to commit itself and increase the troubles that agitate Europe;
but if it is so, a prompt explanation about Namur would be more essential than I can express.
