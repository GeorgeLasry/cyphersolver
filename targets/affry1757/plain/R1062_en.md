# R1062, d'Affry, The Hague, 1757: English translation of the contemporary decipherment

The Assembly of Holland has begun. The question of the augmentation is to be debated there, and if the debates are
lively I shall be warned in time to speak, without however departing from what you prescribe to me. I know for certain
that the combined corps which is to assemble to cover Hanover, and if possible Guelders, was to be commanded by Mr de
Cumberland, as I had the honour to tell you; but the King of Prussia will not have it. The command was offered to Duke
Louis of Wolfenbüttel, Field Marshal of the Republic, who refused it. I am sure of this, and it is a great secret. It
is easy to judge that they hoped by this means to draw the Republic in more easily. Baron von Reischach has just told
me that he knew positively that the King of Prussia would have subsidies from England, and that if the English
Ministry could not have them granted to him by the Nation at present, he would have them tacitly.

(I believe that in Mr de Hattel's letter it is said that this light horseman is a Breton. Chance has brought me a
merchant from Lyon who says he left Antwerp for Breda with a man who told him on the way that he was a former
musketeer; that he was entering the service of the King of Prussia; that he was abandoning a young wife, four children
and a great deal of property, because he could no longer return to France; and that there would shortly be great
changes in the kingdom; that he was from Saint-Brieuc in Brittany; that he had wanted to make it easier for the English
to land in that country; and that there had been secret dealings. This man gave the merchant addresses in his own
hand, which you will find in this packet, and an itinerary for the merchant in case he wished to go to Brittany,
which the merchant gave to me. He added that this man was at Cologne and might perhaps come to Amsterdam. Their
conversation took place on the twenty-eighth of ____ last, on the road and at Breda. This man called himself Mr Delf at
Brussels, and is said to have taken back his name of Comte d'Urvoy. In the distress I am in, I did not think to have a
copy made of Baron von Hatzel's letter; but as you have it, you will be able to see whether, despite the difference of
condition, this man is the same, or whether they are two monsters. The merchant from Lyon, named Santerre, came back to
