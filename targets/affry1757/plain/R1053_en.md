# R1053, d'Affry, The Hague, 1757: English translation of the contemporary decipherment

A courier arrived here yesterday from Mr de Haren, the Republic's Minister at Brussels, by which this Minister gives
notice of the march of the King's army, made up of ninety thousand men. This news at first caused alarm, and some
Republicans came to ask me whether I knew of it. I assured them that I did not; and I told them that you would not
fail to put me in a position to notify the Ministers of the Republic of the march of this army, and at the same time
to assure Their High Mightinesses of the uprightness of His Majesty's intentions towards them. I added that if the
courier I was expecting brought me the news, I would communicate it at once, and that if you did not tell me of it,
they could count on this news being nothing but a trap of the English party to undermine our mutual confidence; but I
hoped no one would be its dupe. You can judge, Sir, how important it is that I be informed early of everything His
Majesty sees fit to have communicated here. Mr Burdin, who brought me a letter of recommendation from you, has made
enquiries from Antwerp to here, and along the various routes he has travelled in Holland, about a Swiss officer in the
service of the States General named Ficher. This officer left a few days ago for Paris, accompanied by a Frenchman
arriving from England, who is said to have borrowed a great deal of money in Amsterdam on England's behalf in order to
ply the trade of spy in Paris and in the kingdom. This Frenchman is called Meunier; but he has translated his name
into English, and is said to go by that of Muller or Miller. It seems likely that Ficher will try to see Mr Burdin,
through whom one might discover the truth of what I have had the honour to tell you.
