# R1065, d'Affry to Rouillé, The Hague, 3 February 1757 (no. 137): English translation of the contemporary decipherment

P1: I had the honour to tell you the day before yesterday that the States of Holland would reassemble on the 8th of
this month. This seemed so odd to me that I wished to clear it up, and I went straight to the Grand Pensionary's
yesterday evening. He told me that the Assembly might indeed resume at that time,

P2: and that he nevertheless expected to be able to delay it until the fast day. I repeated to him, regarding the
augmentation, what I had told him before. He does not seem to me far from making an issue of it. In truth this
sentiment comes from his real fear of losing his post; but as all the

P3: Republicans want him to keep it, I would not be surprised to see him work feebly for the augmentation, certain of
seeing it thwarted by them. He also told me that if they decided on it, it would not be without taking the precaution
of declaring to the other neighbouring powers what the purpose of

P4: this step is. I answered him: you must surely judge, Sir, that the King my master would not learn the news of a step
that might be contrary to the neutrality you have promised without ordering me to put very positive questions to you
about the Republic's intentions. I hope to know more before the end of this month. I shall have the honour to

P5: report to you on it. Mr de Nispen, who came here, left this morning to carry out what Mr de Crémille ordered him.
He will be at Maaseik in 6 or 7 days, and I am certain that he will carry out well everything prescribed to him; but I
beg you to be so good as to represent to Marshal de Belle-

P6: Isle how inconvenient it is, in entering enemy country, to be reduced to roads so out of the way that one can only
advance in divisions of two or four battalions. I agree that Guelders is so bare of troops that one need not fear to
find any in the field or on one's

P7: way; but this manner of arriving piecemeal has the drawback of announcing an army from very far off; by which
means the provisions that this country could furnish us will be partly shut up in Wesel and in Guelders, and the rest
will be saved into Dutch Guelders, all the more easily since the

P8: Rhine and the Meuse lead there. I would therefore think it very useful to send ahead of the army's advance guard a
considerable corps of light troops, and to give this corps a few of the lightest field pieces: this corps could then
move briskly towards Cleves and onto the Rhine

P9: and would thereby prevent the export of a great part of the provisions. Mr de Nispen has undertaken to explain to
Mr de Crémille the usefulness and even the necessity of this arrangement, so that I shall not make a separate memorandum
of it. Mr von Reischach's courier took advantage of a moment when the wind was favourable on the 26th of last month,
and although the

P10: wind has varied, I hope he will have arrived in England and that you will soon have news of him. I have given the
Italian the hundred louis according to His Majesty's orders. This man left this morning for Helvoet and from there for
England. He assures me that he would manage to bring Pichon to Liège. He will use the pretext of watching our moves and
of reporting

P11: to Hanover everything we do. As it often happens that one cannot cross here with east and north-east winds, and
these winds do not prevent arriving at Ostend, he will write to me directly by that route. I shall inform Mr d'Aubigni
[the cipher adds: at Liège] at the same time as I have the honour to pass on to you the news of

P12: the Italian, if you approve. The day before yesterday I sent you a note on a certain Lacombe. I think no action
should be taken concerning him before we have Pichon, because the latter might take fright if he learned that Lacombe
had disappeared, and it is to be presumed that Pichon has more than one correspondent. The Spanish ambassador
despatched

P13: a courier to Paris yesterday. I do not know on what business.
