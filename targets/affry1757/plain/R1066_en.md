# R1066, d'Affry, The Hague, 1757: English translation of the contemporary decipherment

I saw Mr van Slingelandt at my house this morning. I told him what you prescribe to me in your despatch of the 11th
regarding the negotiation with England. He answered that he thought he should communicate to me the substance of
Lord Holderness's letter to Mr Yorke. Here it is as he dictated it to me. His Britannic Majesty has avoided war as far
as prudence and the interests on his side allowed. His Majesty sincerely desires peace, provided that its conditions
are equitable enough to make it lasting, that it includes all the respective allies, and that it preserves the rights
and possessions of the subjects of Great Britain. In that case the King of England will be pleased to see this
negotiation opened between Mr Yorke and Mr d'Affry, without the mediation of a third party. You see, Sir, that this
language has nothing excessive in it; but neither has it anything acceptable, since it is right that England should
take the first steps. Besides, the reservation of all its allies, and the vague condition of rights and possessions,
do not offer easy terms.
