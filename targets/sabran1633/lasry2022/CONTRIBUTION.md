## The key

A homophonic substitution of 22 letters with 51 signs (up to four for S). Some letter signs are digits: 7 = A, 8 = E, 9 = I, 10 = O, 11 = V. The nomenclator has:
- 41 two-digit codes for words and syllables, in the order of the first letter of their meaning (17 *bon*, 24 *ce*, 26 *de*, 35 *Genes*, 47 *monsieur*, 59 *que*, 68 *vous*, 95 *ou*);
- ten name signs;
- three signs that double the preceding letter.

![Key snapshot from CTTS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GeorgeLasry/cyphersolver/writeup-sabran-lasry/targets/sabran1633/lasry2022/cryptiana/images/sabran_key.png)

The clerks' hands differ from leaf to leaf, so one sign can look different from page to page. One example: the "6" shape is H and the straight "b" is F in the 1631 key and the 215/232 hands, but the clerk of 57b/57v and 242v–245v writes his H with a straight stem and his F as ℒ.

## Reading conventions

- **Bold:** written in cipher in the manuscript; regular text was written in clear.
- **Code signs:** given by their reading in brackets, e.g. **[France?]**. A ? marks a tentative identification; [Name1?] is an unidentified person.
- **Editorial marks:** [?] = unreadable; word[?] = doubtful reading; letters in [ ] are supplied (abbreviations, clerks' slips).
- **Omitted:** struck-out text is left out.
- **Spelling:** the manuscript's (v for u, y for i).

**English translation**

(f. 57) [...] I believed I was completely out of the affair of which you have had news from here. But **Garbarin** came to see me on the 23rd in the evening, as the first time dressed as a peasant, with a letter which he says is for me **from the munitioner of Savona**, who is in his plot, written in my favour as you know, and in the same style as the others that **[Savoy?]** has him write, by which he confirms all that the above-named had told me before, and asks me to believe him.

Then he told me that during his first journey to me he had sent a man **to Sassello**, where **his family** lives, and had left a letter there, to which he replied on his return; since then he had received two letters which **[Savoy?]** had had written to him by a man named **Captain Velute**.

I would set before you here the copies of those he has written to me, and the explanation he gave me of them. **[Savoy?]** wants the above-named to meet with him, together with a gentleman who was **commissary of the castle, and with** the **munitioner**, who would both take part in the design; and as soon as the above-named came back with the assurances needed for the execution, he would send him back with money and fix the day when everything would be ready. But the **commissary** is no longer there; he now lives in **Genoa** and does not want to be involved any more, though he wishes the affair to go ahead without him. On his departure from here he would let **[Savoy?]** know the time and place at which he could send the above-named to meet with him and **the munitioner**, and the place where **the ladders** would be, and the one where **a thousand men** were to arrive, **by sea** only, named **Le Fornace**, **one mile** from (f. 57v) **Savona**, two musket shots from **the castle**; the guards[?] that could be sent **from Nice** would stay **on** the water nine or ten miles from **Savona** the day before, and land at the said place at four or **five o'clock at night**; and he will leave **one of his sons** in the hands of **[Savoy?]**, without getting anything from him but what he had written in advance **to reassure his companions**. ## Historical context

**Melchior de Sabran.**
- In 1629 Sabran was sent as gentilhomme ordinaire de la chambre to the Emperor (instruction of 15 June 1629, Avenel III, 343). The mission failed.
- From the end of 1629 he was the King's resident at Genoa, re-established after a gap. On his way there he landed at Monaco.
- He stayed until about 1638 and was by then styled ambassador. His later despatches of 1636–1637 are printed in the *Correspondance de Sourdis*.
- In 1644–1645 he was French resident in London.

**His correspondent.** The letters are copies in his own letter-books, addressed "A Monsieur Bouthillier".
- Claude Bouthillier countersigned the King's letter of 1631; he was secretary of state for foreign affairs from 1629 to March 1632.
- From March 1632 the office passed to his son Léon, comte de Chavigny. In 1634 Richelieu notes that "le sieur Bouthillier (Chavigny) écrira à M. de Sabran".
- Yet the letter of 18 October 1635 speaks of "monsieur vostre filz", which points to the father. Which of the two received each letter stays open.

**Monaco (1631, 1635).**
- In 1630–1631 Honoré II of Monaco negotiated with France through his procureur fiscal Orazio Rossi of Menton, the "sieur Rossy" of the King's letter. The talks were carried on through Sabran.
- After the peace of Cherasco Richelieu broke them off so as not to offend Savoy (Saige, *Monaco*, pp. 195–197). This matches the King's letter of 28 March 1631.
- In May 1635 Sabran reports that Rossi is dead and proposes either to resume the talks or to surprise the Spanish garrison.
- Monaco came under French protection only with the Treaty of Péronne (1641).

**The Savona plot (1633).** Genoa and Savoy had been enemies since the war of 1625 and the Vachero conspiracy of 1628; the Republic set up its State Inquisitors after that conspiracy.
- In 1633, before the Genoa–Savoy peace of 5 July 1633, one Giambattista Garbarino of Sassello was caught dealing with Victor Amadeus I of Savoy to hand over the castle of Savona. He was arrested and executed (Donaver, *Storia della repubblica di Genova*, II, p. 280; the OCR reads 1638).
- This is Sabran's "Garbarin". The deciphered *a sacel* (letter 6) is Sassello.
- The fr. 4134 letters show the affair from the French side: Garbarino offered the castles to France or Savoy. Sabran passed the offer in cipher to the French agent in Savoy and kept out of it, suspecting a trap.
- By September the munitioner and Garbarino were prisoners in Genoa.

**The campaign of 1635.**
- By the Treaty of Rivoli (July 1635) France, Savoy, Parma and Mantua joined against Spain. Créquy and Odoardo Farnese besieged Valenza from September to 28 October 1635 and failed.
- On 13 September 1635 a Spanish fleet under Santa Cruz took the Lérins islands off Provence; they were retaken only in 1637.
- In the Valtelline Rohan won at Livigno, Mazzo and Morbegno.
- The governor of Milan was Cardinal Gil de Albornoz until the marquis de Leganés took over in November 1635.
- Genoa, Spain's banker and the link between Spain and Milan by sea, stayed formally neutral. The fr. 4135 letters show Sabran arguing that the Republic's help to the Spanish fleet justified seizing Novi, after Tortona and Serravalle; watching the Genoese families (Sauli, Centurione, Spinola, Fieschi, Doria); and finding his own servants in the pay of the State Inquisitors.

## How the key was obtained

In 2022 Lasry transcribed the images in CTTS and recovered the homophones with its built-in cryptanalysis, starting from the King's letter of 1631 (all in cipher after the first words). The resulting key reads the cipher passages of fr. 4134 and fr. 4135 as well.

In October 2026, with Claude Opus 5.5 and OpenAI Codex:
- every run of clear text was boxed, and the clear text was transcribed, reviewed against the images and corrected;
- 144 cipher signs that had been missed in the transcription were added;
- boxes that held two signs were split; for example the supposed codes "98", "72" and "82" are 9 + 8, 7 + R and 8 + R;
- every symbol type was compared instance by instance with its look-alikes, and about 50 signs were relabelled;
- the code numbers were read from context, using their first-letter order. For example: 13 *[armée?]*, 22 *[cardinal?]*, 33 *[guerre?]*, 34 *[gens?]*, 95 *ou*, and 16 as a second *de*;
- the name signs were read from context.

## Open points

- **Tentative name signs.** They are read from context only: o-- [France?], o- [Espagne?], qp [Republique?], h3 [Savoie?], 12 [Agent?], o+ [Plaisance?], psi [Senat?], # [Parme?], i= [Mantoue?], qp2 [Name1?]. On ff. 215v–216 several o- signs need France rather than Spain, so o- and o-- may be confused in that hand.
- **Unknown or weak readings.**
  - Unknown: 46 ("d'une [46] de l'armée navale").
  - Weak: h4, hgm, pi2, 25, 60, 15.
- **Cipher runs that do not read.**
  - 50v: "veraivre", "fevitga" (probably "ledit Garbarin"), "tmineli…".
  - 215b: "parico".
  - 228v: "duc de s / vop".
  - Also "Vbr[?]" (54b, 57b).
- **Recipient.** Claude or Léon Bouthillier, letter by letter.
- **Dates.** Several are inferred from the catalogue order; most images show only part of a letter.
- **Not covered.** Leaves on Tomokiyo's list but not among these images: fr. 4134 f. 51; fr. 4135 ff. 217 (to Créquy), 226v, 227v, 231v, 239v, 243. Also Baluze 156 f. 157 (1636).
- **Originals.** The letters as received should be in the Archives diplomatiques, Correspondance politique, Gênes. They are not digitised.
