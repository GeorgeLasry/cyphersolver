# R8572 = BL Cotton Vesp. C IV ff. 214r-218r: "el Gilino" to Francesco II Sforza, Paredes, 15 Sept 1527: English translation of George Lasry's edition

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of George Lasry's Italian working reading, `lasry/plaintext_reading.txt`
(7 Oct 2026, contributed 14 Oct 2026): the whole letter, clear passages and deciphered passages, in its restored

## Translation

### f. 214r

*Clear.* Most Illustrious and Most Excellent, my only Lord, my ever most honoured Lord. I kiss the hands of Your
Excellency.

After visiting these places around Valladolid I came to Paredes on the 9th of this month, where I have in part been
given knowledge of what I write to you below, of which Your Excellency will be able to judge with the prudence that is
natural to you.

The Lord Grand Chancellor, by his letters of the 27th of last month, arrived in Barcelona with Messer Giovanni
Bartolomeo di Gattinara, Martino Centurio[?ne], [?general in the making] of Monseigneur de Bourbon, and a few of his
household. Most stayed in Genoa for lack of ships. His Lordship had a very contrary voyage, both from storms at sea
and from having been followed by some galleys of the French fleet. He is expected at the Imperial court at the end of
this month.

*Cipher.* I am assured by many that His Lordship stands in little favour with the [?Emperor] for one single [?thing]
that is imputed to him: namely that, by two letters of his intercepted to the Viceroy, [he asked] that he send the
galleys of Naples to the relief of Genoa, and order Lope de Soria to use the rest of the [?hundred] thousand ducats of
the exchange for the benefit of that city, both for providing infantry and victuals.

### f. 214v

*Cipher.* Otherwise the whole would be lost. His detractors, who are openly the confessor and the German secretary,
will have it that Lautrec, on such information, sent [?part] of [?his] troops to the siege of Genoa, which had been
destined for another place, and that for this reason the said city was no longer defended.

I, for the great need that I see His Majesty? has of him, cannot think but that he will keep himself in the Emperor?'s
good favour; and for this reason I shall not cease to urge Your Excellency to order the Bilia by your letters to keep
on good terms with him. Now we cannot hope for anything but good offices from His Lordship?, above all with the death
of Bourbon.

I have resolved to go to meet him as far as three days' journey, and I know that for this I shall not be held
suspect by the ambassadors of the confederates.

### f. 215r

*Clear.* Monseigneur de Tarbes has given me a copy of the aforesaid intercepted letters, which I would have sent to
Your Excellency if the present bearer had not shown himself so difficult.

*Cipher.* The ambassador of the King of Portugal, newly come in the Pope's name, is not sent principally by His
Holiness. So [uncertain passage] the Datary writes to the Lord Nuncio. But as he was about to go on to his king, he
begged Our Lord that he would [?do] him the honour of a brief, in his person, to the [?Emperor], with authority to
negotiate his settlement, promising to have him released at once and restored to his former friendship with [?His
Majesty] and to the ancient state of the Church. Which has not come about for him, and he had promised, [as] said, not
only in [?Italy] but also in Spain.

Here he has been known for a light man, and with this good opinion of himself he leaves the Imperial court tomorrow
for Portugal. This ambassador has brought

### f. 215v

*Cipher.* a letter in the Pope's [?own] hand to the Nuncio, as credence for Saporito, chamberlain of His Holiness, who
died in Genoa, which [was]

*Clear.* written so wisely and prudently that hardly anything more could be wished,

*Cipher.* exhorting him not to fail in his due service. Likewise two briefs to the [?Emperor], full of many fine
sentences, and with fine reasons it persuades [?His Majesty] to take up the patronage of the Church and not to oppress
it, above all sub fide, which [?unlearnedly: uncertain passage] written.

The Abbatino returns despatched and desires […], and the [?Emperor] has granted him all he knew how to desire and ask.
It is true that on many heads, since [?His Majesty] does not know of what moment they are, he writes to the Viceroy
of Naples, and better informed of the affairs of Italy, that he should satisfy the [?aforesaid] Marquis as far as he
can, for such is his mind. [to to qu gu: unresolved sequence.]

*Clear.* [?No] particular have I been able to learn beyond what is already written; on learning of any [… a word in
the right margin not read with certainty], at the first opportunity I shall advise Your Excellency of it. Hold this
for certain:

*Cipher.* that the settlement has followed with this Majesty; that he had come to terms with France, and here we have
news that he was nego-

### f. 216r

*Cipher.* -tiating it through the Cavalier Casale.

The Emperor? has given his mind to this settlement through the reminders of the Duke of Ferrara and the work of the
Nuncio. I believe, however, that the Marquis, being obliged to the Florentines, will not show himself wholly in favour
of the Emperor?, but that he will see to it that Pagolo Luzasco, with three hundred light horse, passes into the
service of His Majesty?.

And yesterday the aforesaid Abbatino was to leave for your parts in all haste, and by him I have written to Your
Excellency under cover [?of] the Lady Marchioness of Mantua. [Su: unresolved final fragment.]

*Clear.* The Magnificent Lords the French ambassadors hold letters from the Most Christian King of the 29th of August,
by which they are informed of the [?defeat] at Portofino, the surrender of Genoa, and the hope of soon obtaining
Alessandria. Together they tell me they have a commission from their King that the negotiation of the peace be
conducted on the content of the articles made at [?Madrid], a thing to which up to now the aforesaid Most Christian
[King] had not been willing to assent, and that for this reason they have almost certain hope that an agreement [?will
come to] follow [an interlinear correction not wholly resolved]. They, with the Lords the English ambassadors, have
been at Palencia [?and are staying there] to discuss what their King can or cannot swear of the above-written
articles.

### f. 216v

*Cipher.* Both have sworn to me that the Most Christian [King] lately writes to them that he absolutely wants Your
Excellency to remain in the state of Milan in the settlement, which does not agree with what he wrote to them lately.
Nonetheless I leave it to the truth.

The Lord Venetian ambassador and I alone, the day before yesterday, were with the Lord Apostolic Nuncio at Usiglios,
and begged him to be willing to go to Palencia and stay there while the French ambassadors are there, and to use
diligence to learn the truth of what they will negotiate, and above all concerning that matter. He promised us to do
it willingly, and so we understand that His Lordship has gone there.

I am almost certain that he will serve us, both out of respect for Your Excellency, for what he owes to all Italy, and
for the friendship he holds with the said

### f. 217r

*Cipher.* Venetian ambassador. I understand that the [?Emperor], as soon as he heard of the surrender of Genoa, sent a
new despatch to Monseigneur de Miliao, who [?until] that time had not embarked for Rome. And it is hinted to me that
[?His Majesty] is revoking the liberation of the Pope because of the new events in Lombardy.

What leads [?me] to believe this are the bad offices that the Ferrarese ambassadors and the agents of the Colonna
publicly do against His [?Holiness] with the [?Emperor]; [?of] whom, [?as often as] they speak of him, they cannot name
him except as traitor, false pope, bastard, maker of poisons, and a thousand other [?insults] which it would be long
to write. [Three signs left in the cipher block, o29 vp b+, literally v-v-u, function unresolved.]

*Clear.* Today the Magnificent Messer Ludovico Catto, doctor of laws, the old ambassador of the Most Illustrious Lord
Duke of Ferrara, has gone to Valladolid to take leave of His Imperial Majesty [a word struck out] to return to his
country. I have always found him much inclined to the benefit of Your Excellency, and therefore you [?are] under an
obligation to him.

*Cipher.* With him, I am told, they will despatch Troilo Negrono; [and] that [?His Majesty] now [?says] that he wants to
grant many favours to the Lords Colonna. If this is so, the present difficulty of the affairs of Lombardy has caused
it; they fear that after the one ruin the other, of the kingdom of Naples, may follow.

There are here letters of Messer Andrea Borgo

### f. 217v

*Cipher.* [?to] the [?Emperor] and to Valdesio, formerly his servant, full of counsels pro opprimenda Italia [for
crushing Italy] and long discourses for keeping the state of Milan in the devotion of [?His Majesty]. At the end they
conclude that the Infante is the one who can hold it and defend it from any violence that might descend to occupy it.

Since the taking of Genoa it is said that the [?Emperor] has sent word to have the Infante come down and to give all
Italy as a prey to the Germans; that [?His Majesty] shows greater heart than ever, and that it has come out from the
confessor that the loss of Genoa has been a strong spur to the [?Emperor], [that] he should show himself wholly bent
on [?war], and [?how] powerful their [?forces] are. [The final groups LU-V-I follow, unresolved.]

*Clear.* It is also said that His Imperial Majesty has sold the state of the Count of Caiazzo in the Kingdom for
twenty-eight [thousand] ducats to the son of the Count of Monteleone, Viceroy of Sicily.

In the Lord Marco Pio I see every day his goodwill towards Your Excellency growing, and his desire to do you service;
which has caused in me a certain uncommon regard for His Lordship, which obliges me to be with him many times and to
honour him greatly.

### f. 218r

*Cipher.* Written thus far, I have been advised by several safe ways that the [?Emperor] has sent by three couriers
the investiture of the state of Milan to the Infante [abbassi: word not yet resolved in context] to the defence of
that state, and that at present he is providing him with a hundred thousand ducats.

The loss of Genoa has been confirmed by way of the Lord of Monaco, whom [?His Majesty] is providing with money so that
he may keep himself in his devotion. [Final sign overwritten or corrected, not resolved.]

*Clear.* I would not know what more to write to you for now, except that I commend myself to your good grace as
humbly as I can, praying Our Lord God to give you that happiness which you desire for yourself. From Paredes, the 15th
of September 1527.

Of Your Most Illustrious and Most Excellent Lordship the most humble and perpetual servant, el Gilino
