# R9961, Muscetula to Charles V, Rome, 24 Oct 1531: English translation of the contemporary decipherment

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `texts/R9961_decryption.md`: the contemporary decipherment bound with the
letter as doc. 91 bis (sections A, b, C (III), as the decipherer lettered them) and the two marginal decipherments.
Same marks: `[x]` abbreviations expanded or a letter supplied, `[?]` and `...` uncertain or unread in the secretary's
hand, `[d:]` a struck letter (left out). Spanish.

## The decipherment

A: and the same nuncio of His Serenity, that although it was said publicly that because of this death the meeting had
not taken place, nevertheless, from what he believed, the King of France, having heard Your Majesty's answer which you
gave to the Most Serene Queen's man, and the conditions that were set for the meeting, took this pretext of his
mother's death, since it did not seem to him that he could get out of this meeting what he hoped for; and of this same
opinion is His Holiness and all men of judgement.

b: the Pope. The truth of it is what Your Majesty has written, and for this reason His Holiness says that Your Majesty
has little need to make anyone believe it, since it is well believed; and, talking about this with me, His Holiness
told me that he believed that Your Majesty, with your authority and accustomed prudence, would govern yourself in this
which the King of France says, and would let that King of France say what he pleases with the others, since he has
not sent to say or answer to Your Majesty except in accordance with what he has written to Your Majesty; besides
which, it is well believed and well known how truthful Your Majesty is, and what the others are wont to say and talk.
His Holiness said this holding it for certain that Your Majesty would do so, and that His Holiness himself would do
the same in this case if he found himself in the same terms.

C (III): I will not fail to tell Your Majesty that, His Holiness talking with me of these affairs of France and
England, he has told me that England is pressing the King of France to be willing to break into war against Your
Majesty, and they tell him that by way of the sea he could do Your Majesty a great deal of harm in those parts; and
up to now the King of France has put this off, and in the end he said that they should leave off doing this a while
longer, because he would find Your Majesty in greater need. He also told me that he thought he understood well that
[the King] would want Your Majesty to stay in the parts of Flanders, so that, if some disturbance happened in Germany
because of the Lutherans, Your Majesty would be in a place from which it would not be so at Your Majesty's will to go
and return to Spain, or to do what seemed good to Your Majesty with your person; since he would think that by sea you
would not return to Spain, and that to pass then through all Germany, it is not known how it could be done well. And,
His Holiness talking about this very much at leisure with me, and showing that he felt much the ill will of these men,
both for what touched Your Majesty and because His Holiness has to run one and the same fortune with Your Majesty, and
without your favour the matter of the faith could only go worse, His Holiness told me that if Your Majesty could do
something good and quickly, the diet of Speyer too would be a very good thing, so that afterwards you could come into
Italy and return to Spain by places safer and more convenient for Your Majesty. But if this matter of the diet of
Speyer were drawn out, he would commend that Your Majesty prorogue the diet to some place that was nearer to Italy and
to the states of Austria and the county of Tyrol; and that it would be more fitting that the place where Your Majesty
would have to stay should be one where you had Italy and your own particular states at your back, being able to have
from Italy and from your states what might be needed, and to go by whichever road you were best pleased, rather than
to find yourself so far from this road that you could have trouble if a disturbance of the Lutherans occurred. His
Holiness told me this speaking as of his own accord, not knowing well how things stand there, as is sure, and because
of the great care he has for Your Majesty's affairs, which in all respects will hit upon the best; and it has seemed
right to me to write it to Your Majesty just as he told it to me, because it is always good that the things said in
such grave matters should be known, and afterwards Your Majesty with your imperial prudence can recognise the best.

## Marginal decipherments

On scan 009 (f. 91-1₃): "and moreover it is seen that the King of France helps the eight, and to make all the
dealings with them, and does not leave off deal[ing?] of ... to oblige them ... secon[?]bierta of the eight."

On scan 017 (f. 91-1₅): "these Frenchmen also tell the Pope that they have news that the King of England and other
princes among the great ones of Germany are beginning to favour the Lutherans, for His Holiness does not leave it, and
they foretell news [?] to draw out themselves, as they are wont."
