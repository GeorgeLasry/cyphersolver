## Translation

… and to follow up the victory.

⟨Monseigneur de Saint-Pol has commanded me to se[ek] his leave from this Signoria, which it has not been willing to
consent to, but has protested that, if he departed, it would be the total ruin of the [en]terprises. And it seems to
me that there is no reason for it, considering the enterprise of Milan, the coming of the Spaniards with money to
relieve it, and the spring approaching. I believe he will not depart if the thing [...]⟩ without ⟨the consent of all
the confederates; and so it would bring [...] an irreparable prejudice.⟩ I have [?told and] written to him ⟨as best
I could⟩ to ⟨turn him from this opinion, which is all too dangerous.⟩

Monseigneur, may it please you to keep me in your good grace and remembrance, to which I very humbly commend myself.
Written [at Venice] the 14th of January.
