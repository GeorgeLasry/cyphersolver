1528
Status. Solved about 85 %: the opening words of two points and one phrase remain uncertain. 15–17

analysis, isomorphs, cribs).
References
• Archivio di Stato di Siena, Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2 (Lettere in cifra), no. 17

Ciphertext
282 cipher signs in 8 lines (CTTS lines 5 to 12 of P1), set in a clear frame of heading, salutation, connecting
words ('In prima', 'Secundo', 'et', 'quando', 'Terzo'), closing, signature and endorsement.
Key
41 distinct signs. Homophonic substitution without word division: up to five signs for each vowel,
homophones for the frequent consonants, every double letter written with two different signs, and the
ordinary “che” abbreviation used as a cipher sign.
Signs as cut from the manuscript, with their number of occurrences; homophones stand under their letter.

Letter
1528. Most magnificent and generous Knight etc. Since Your Lordship is absent from the magistracy of the
Balìa, we communicate to you some important matters of the government, so that you may understand the
things that will occur and have the inside knowledge. First: here, among many and almost publicly, [it is said]
that the Mustetto(?) has(?) certain promises of I know not what bishoprics for the nephews; I do not know
whether it is true. Second: the Cardinal of Naples has(?) given and gives [his hand] in the affairs of
Pitigliano, because he has kinship there; and it has also been a matter of the Countess, when they were
young: it seems that he made love with her. Third: the Otto(?), wishing to have it, [in order to] constrain(?)
[it] ..., let no victuals go there, nor let [anyone] be allowed to deal with our contado.
I speak briefly and in substance because the cipher wants it so. Messer Bartolomeo Lamilucci(?) [Landucci?],
who was secretary of the Pratica, [is there] and will be able to read it easily. From Sarteano(?), on the 25th(?)
of August 1528. Most devoted Bartolomeo Tantucci. [Endorsement:] This cipher is that of the Balìa.

Contents
From: Bartolomeo Tantucci
To: messer Antonio del Vescovo, rector of the cathedral, member of the Balìa
Place: Sarteano(?)
Date: 25 (or 20) August 1528 — dated in the clear text
Tantucci writes to messer Antonio del Vescovo, rector of the cathedral, who is away from the Balìa, to give
him 'the inside knowledge' of three matters of government, each put into cipher between clear connecting
words. First, it is said here by many and almost publicly that the 'Mustetto'(?) has(?) certain promises of I
know not what bishoprics for 'the nephews'; he does not know whether it is true. Second, the Cardinal of
Naples has intervened and intervenes in the affairs of Pitigliano, because he has kinship there, and also
because of the Countess, with whom, when they were young, he seems to have had a love affair. Third, the
'Otto'(?), wanting to have Pitigliano, mean to constrain(?) it: no victuals are to go there, and no one is to be
allowed to deal with 'our contado'. He speaks briefly and in substance 'because the cipher wants it so', and
names messer Bartolomeo Lamilucci(?) [Landucci?], formerly secretary of the Pratica, as the man who will be
able to read it easily. The letter is dated from Sarteano(?) on 25(?) August 1528, signed 'Deditissimo
Bartolomeo Tantucci', and endorsed 'questa cifara è quella di Balìa'.
Historical context and dating
In August 1528 Siena was governed by the Balìa of the popular (Libertini) regime that had expelled the
Noveschi in 1524–25 and repulsed the papal–Florentine attack at Porta Camollia on 25 July 1526; the city


[report page 27]

leaned on the Emperor, while the restored Florentine republic (1527–30) was at war with Charles V and the
Pope [Hook, Siena: A City and its History, 1979; Roth, The Last Florentine Republic, 1925; Malavolti,
Dell'historia di Siena, 1599]. Sarteano, in the Val di Chiana on the Florentine border, was a Sienese subject
town. Pitigliano was an Orsini county (Count Ludovico, son of the condottiere Niccolò, then Gian Francesco)
under Sienese suzerainty, a standing object of Florentine and Roman interest [Bruscalupi, Monografia
storica della contea di Pitigliano, 1906]. The 'Cardinal of Naples' of 1528 should be Vincenzo Carafa,
archbishop of Naples from 1505, created cardinal on 21 November 1527 [Eubel, Hierarchia Catholica, III];
this identification, his kinship with the Orsini and the affair with 'the Countess' are not verified here. The
'Otto' are unidentified: Florence's Otto di Pratica had given way to the Dieci di Libertà e Pace in 1527, so a
Sienese body is possible. The 'Pratica' of the former secretary is likewise unidentified.
Open points. Unidentified: “el Mustetto”, the Cardinal of Naples (Vincenzo Carafa?), the Countess (of
Pitigliano?) and the “Otto”.
