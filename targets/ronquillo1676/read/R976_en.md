# R976, Ronquillo to Fuenmayor, Nijmegen, 10 Apr 1677

⟨... though not my spirits, because every day⟩ these negotiations get worse, even faster than our misfortunes in
Flanders follow one another, whose griefs I do not want to repeat to Your Lordship. And I see that these Dutch now want to
come to the [?truces], for yesterday they sent the mediators the new full powers, although all the allies [...]. One
might have waited until Wednesday, when the sixty days of the term run out, and presented them together with those of
the Emperor, Denmark and Brandenburg, which have arrived; and the excuse they gave me today, that it was of no great
substance, is not very courteous.

And these rumours going round that we are negotiating directly in England and in Holland worry the allies, and those
of new marriages of the King our master leave the Imperial ministers little satisfied. I foresee that the surrender of
Saint-Omer, after that of Cambrai, will be the essential article and the first of the settlement of the
[?satisfaction] or of the establishment of an armistice.

The mutual guarantee of the House of Brunswick for its conquests, winter quarters and the defence of the towns of
Hamburg and Lübeck is already under way. And there is no lack of news that Hanover is going straight to the courts of
Sweden and Denmark to mediate a separate treaty, and Brandenburg may follow, though so far the ministers of these
princes have not been willing to reveal the new [...] their masters have made; if Your Lordship has it, I beg you to
send me a copy.
