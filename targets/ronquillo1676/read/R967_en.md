# R967, Salinas to Fuenmayor, London, 8 Dec 1676

⟨... that although it may be difficult, so exhausted as that crown is left,⟩ nothing is impossible today for the
fortune France enjoys, and none greater than in having this court so bewitched that, whatever extortions they commit,
it does not take offence; ⟨for on the complaints of these people about the piracies of the French privateers ... to
publish placards of another's,⟩ at all this the King and the court resented nothing.

For they are already at the beginning of the turmoil, so they await the Parliament, where great things will be seen;
though I foresee that, if it comes in furious and the King sees no light of obtaining anything, he will prorogue it
before they have room to go further. So it is doubtful whether it suits us to trust this tribunal or to negotiate with
the King; and in the doubt [...] in both, to see whether on some side we have fortune.

⟨... and the honours I have always owed him,⟩ but when my paper was seen in the Council, they turned the King's mind
and persuaded him to cut short with a harsh answer the course of papers being given at this juncture, since they were
motives to unsettle the minds of the members of Parliament; and with [?this] they resolved to give me the absurd
answer, of which affair I had light, and I went to the King and his ministers and put them in a good way, so that they
came to terms, giving me satisfactions in words, and that both memorials be suppressed. To which I have replied that I
had already given an account of mine to His Majesty, so another form is being sought, in the meantime of which I
suspend sending [the] answer, though I will do it one way or another so that Your Lordship may see it, towards one or
towards all. And because I already knew this way of acting, and that they tried the same with the lord Count of
Molina, it has seemed best to me to hold firm with a little spirit; for if we let ourselves fall everywhere, these will
despise us more than anyone, because they are prouder than all. Of what results I will inform Your Lordship, as I do
now that amid these comings and goings I see some favourable paths for our business, which I try to follow in case we
find some of the relief that is needed today, of which I will keep Your Lordship [informed]. ⟨And nothing more
particular offering ...⟩
