# R973, Salinas to Fuenmayor, London, 2 Mar 1677

⟨... of whose news I send Your Lordship an account, which I shall continue with what happens every day,⟩ these being
not such bad beginnings, since they make them in a business that matters so much to us as withdrawing the troops from
France, in which I expect they will persist; a negotiation not to be despised when it is achieved at as little cost as
the one I am making them, though with some [money] greater things could be achieved, in which the provision of many
months has not been enough, so that everything goes headlong and on charity. The talk about our negotiations has
calmed down for now.

⟨... but the King of France's march worries me; they say he left on Sunday,⟩ for, if that is true, he has already made
the march from Paris.
