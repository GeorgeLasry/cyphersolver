# R966, Salinas to Fuenmayor, London, 21 Aug 1676

⟨... of the much that can be done.⟩ Although, in a long conference I had with the King, he told me that [the] entry
into France would rather harm the peace than advance it, so that, though we must [...] believe the opposite, we
should suppose it for the best; and when I told him that the other siege could not be avoided, he said that with these
two fortresses being taken the campaign would end; whose malign counsels I fear may influence the Nephew.

The King is in a bad humour these days, and it is believed that the good fortune of the Germans has a part in it; for,
though there is no other talk than that of these sieges, counting down to the last musketeer lost in it, he has kept
quiet the favourable news, which Courtin has not done with more gallantry.

On the business of the protest there has been no further talk; and on that of the passports I spoke to the King, who
told me those that had already been exchanged, and that he had written to France about the claim of those other
princes, and in particular of the [Duke] of Lorraine, doing the offices of a good mediator; but he argued with me that
the footing of the congress of Münster ought to be followed, and that if they wished to introduce novelties, these were
to the prejudice of the kings, so that neither the Emperor nor our [King] should support it. To which I
replied what seemed to me favourable to the [Duke] of Lorraine, for whom we are pleading; on which we shall see what is
resolved; and so the rest of the summer will go by, which will cause no novelty to this King, for he himself told me
that no talk of any treaty could be entered into until the last stages of the campaign were seen. ⟨Nothing else
offering to add from here ...⟩
