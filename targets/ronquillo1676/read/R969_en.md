# R969, Salinas to Fuenmayor, London, 8 Sep 1676

⟨... with the inconsolable fatality of the event at Maastricht, which Your Lordship will have in more detail than I,
and on which I will not dwell, for even for this the spirit is lacking.⟩ On this occasion the talk of peace grows
stronger, which is what this King continually holds with me in public and secret conferences, to which the
inclination of Van Beuningen, who has always erred on this side, contributes not a little; but I never saw it more
necessary than today, in my opinion and with the little knowledge I have of the bad footing on which our affairs stand
everywhere, and worst of all that of Madrid, which Your Lordship experiences like everyone.

This matter could be pressed hard at this court, but Your Lordship is [not] unaware either of how limited the orders
reach us for venturing on any negotiation. Notwithstanding this King's efforts to put the congress on its track, new
difficulties arise every day; and on top of the one that still stands today over the limits, there are those Your
Lordship knows of over the passports, in which I recognise that, as regards the Duke of Lorraine, it will be obtained
that his minister be treated as ambassador, because I see this King inclined to it, and the ministers convinced by the
precedents that Serinchamps produced; but as for the other princes, no one will face it, and there must be great
difficulties if His Imperial Majesty does not take the lead, for his example will decide this point, as one who looks
more to the prerogative these princes solicit; on which the answer from France is awaited. ⟨Yesterday the Duchess of
York gave this kingdom another little duchess ...⟩
