# R974, Ronquillo to Fuenmayor, The Hague, 21 Sept 1676

⟨The taking of Wabur is not confirmed ... I doubt this one will take it.⟩ It would be all the same if Stralsund fell,
for we would be finished with the Swedes in Germany, and we would be litigating over a thing already done, as the Lord
Emperor will do from Philippsburg. And seeing how far the season is advanced and how little disposition there is for
the troops of Münster and Celle to act on the Moselle, it would be much better for them all to go to Pomerania; but in
this, as in everything else, one must follow destiny.

⟨That a mother-in-law with one daughter should make a hundred sons-in-law, we see everywhere, ...⟩ but that in Spain
they should want, without paying subsidies, which is the same as having a share in the conquests of the North without
[?the marriage], is very much in keeping with the great skill of our Council of State. What is certain is that it
depends on the commitment the King of France makes for the Swedes, for otherwise he will not know with whom they are
trading the imaginary share that may fall to us. And it seems to me that the King of France has no doubt spent half a
sheet of paper on the declaration of war against Denmark in order to tell the Swedes that he has done all he could,
but that they have not helped themselves; with this threat he arms a negotiation with Denmark and Brandenburg. Your
Lordship has acted with great consideration towards the minister of the Duke of Celle; and although the agreement this
prince has made with the Bishop of Münster is called provisional, I hold it among them to be very decisive, and in any
case it will always be well that at the assembly of Bremen this be referred to the congress, and that Your Lordship
take the trouble to keep me informed of the maxims of those potentates; for in Spain they say that the share should be
drawn according to what the royal authority of His Majesty has counted for, and his having armed it at his own expense,
for the conquests they have made.

⟨The Prince of Orange went off in great haste to Brabant ... Don Manuel de Lira will inform Your Lordship of all this,
and I shall content myself with telling you⟩ that these whims of a young man will cost him dear; and both this and the
whole recovery of the monarchy depend on there being in Antwerp, by 10 November, ready and effective remittances to
sweeten the allies, and on the governor of Flanders having an army of 20,000 men freed from the garrisons of the
fortresses and provided with everything needed to take the field; for otherwise the allies will not continue in the
costs of the war, and we shall make a peace such as Master Louis pleases to cook for us.
