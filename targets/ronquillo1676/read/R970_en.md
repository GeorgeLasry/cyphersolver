# R970, Salinas to Fuenmayor, London, 15 Sep 1676

⟨... I shall let Your Lordship know, so that you know where your orders are to find me, and meanwhile I beg you not
to deny me here any that may serve you.⟩ Your Lordship will already have heard of the fatal event at Maastricht. So
that no resource should be left to our hopes, fortune chose to shut this door on us, leaving us as our only relief the
disillusion of how little can be expected of the Prince of Orange and of the armies he may command. This knowledge,
added to pressing necessity, will carry us at a great pace to peace, which is what these people want and the end to
which they direct these moves. So today the talk of it is renewed, and every day they shoot arrows at me, which I must
parry until clear orders arrive to guide us to the right course.

The progress on your side moves these minds no less, for, as Your Lordship knows, they neither wish for it nor is it
in their interest. I advise them to press hard, because the soup cannot last them, as I see our affairs and the mood
of the Dutch, whose minister at this court has obtained by this event what he wished, both from his ill will to the
Prince and from his great longing for peace. I keep him well in view, and so as not to make him distrustful, so that
he does not slip away from me, I humour him, and I hold him back so that he does not fall into some rashness. The
minister of that King fears him greatly, and Bergeyck no less, because he knew him here; so his coming has alarmed
him, since it is certain that the two together will do something rash. So he tells me that he has written to His
Danish Majesty to remedy this blow and to make representations so that I stay here, which I did not know until he
told it to Manuel de Fonseca. And at this moment he has just told me that last night Van Beuningen had a long
conference with the King, so I am going to find out what is happening.
