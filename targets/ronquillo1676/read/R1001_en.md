# R1001, Ronquillo to Fuenmayor, Nijmegen, 6 Feb 1677

⟨... and believing that no other objections will be raised, we shall very soon be done with this preliminary,⟩ to the
settlement of which it has been necessary to contribute on our side because of the haste of the Dutch and the clamour
they have raised to this end, going so far as to reveal their mind publicly, and Beverningk in particular. But it is
also true that when I speak to him alone I find him calmer; and all of them give me great assurances and promises that
they will not abandon us, drawing as proof of this the gallantry with which the Prince of Orange has sent to Flanders
the troops that were asked of him. But this does not quiet my concern that in one way or another they may play some
trick on us, and so I do not let go of them, notwithstanding some news I have had (of the kind I can obtain in the
absence of Don Manuel de Lira, whose absence from The Hague I cannot sufficiently convey to Your Lordship, how much harm
and inconvenience it does us) that the States are attending to our interests and know that it matters to themselves.
But as Don Manuel de Lira is staying in Brussels, and he is the one who could find out and discern more openly the mind
of the Dutch, nothing can be believed; and so I say again to Your Lordship that my greatest concern is to watch the
actions of these Dutch ambassadors and to walk together with them.

So far there is strong talk of the separate peace with Sweden, as I related distinctly to Your Lordship in my last, and
I told you the reasons the Dutch have for getting out of the war, directing their policy in this to our benefit. But I
try to make Beverningk, who is the one who talks most about it, understand that it can by no means be so unless this
settlement comes within a general peace; and Your Lordship will have seen the reasons I put forward. How it will end
[is not known], nor has it so far been spoken of formally in the congress.

I repeat my thanks to Your Lordship for the news you give me of the steps being taken in that assembly and of the
difficulties Your Lordship sees in settling those matters. And as to what Your Lordship tells me, that an opening has
been made for getting out of the difficulties by way of an interim, to which the Danes seem inclined, I must tell Your
Lordship that, according to my information, it seems to me that the Dutch will agree to nothing that is a settlement;
and that the minister of Holland has come to The Hague for this purpose, and I believe it will go up in smoke, because
the States want to keep up the difficulties that arise so that the conclusion is afterwards left to their discretion.
Your Lordship, who is on the spot, will know better than anyone what there is in this, and it falls to me only to
praise the judgement and prudence with which Your Lordship reasons, and to beg you to be pleased to continue sending me
your news.
