# R977, Ronquillo to Fuenmayor, Nijmegen, 17 Apr 1677

⟨... and so much desires.⟩ Very indiscreet has been the conduct of Count Windischgrätz, who revealed to the minister
of Brandenburg the secret that Your Lordship had entrusted to him, which has been the origin of our [?message]. But so
that Your Lordship may know that this has not lessened the confidence with which I treat you, as proof see the
jealousy that the progress of the allies in the North arouses at the court of Vienna: I will tell you that the
moderation of the [?peace terms] which the Englishman sent to be made to the Swedes, which I have managed to see,
plainly shows his wish that he would rather have Sweden as a neighbour than the eminence of the princes. But having
reflected on how harmful they could be to us if they came to be proposed in this form, not only for what would
irritate those princes, but because with them the hope we may still have of some compensation for the losses of the
Low Countries would vanish, I have obtained that before it is presented a reply be made to the Lord Emperor, whose
answer we are awaiting.

From this Your Lordship may consider the cares that pile up on us every day, when to the public misfortunes and
disasters are added [?fostered] the particular fears of the inconstancy of our allies, whose mind is plainly seen
from the paper Your Lordship sends me of the declaration they have made at Celle: that if it were of a sincere mind,
the [...] thousand escudos might well be given. Your Lordship be pleased not to forget the papers you offer, and to
continue to send me the news of the assembly, and also to keep reserved what I impart to you. ⟨All the positive news
I can tell Your Lordship by this post ...⟩
