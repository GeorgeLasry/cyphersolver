# R968, Salinas to Fuenmayor, London, 6 Oct 1676

⟨... amid so many troubles as the state of the times brings us, and above all that of our affairs.⟩ Your Lordship
will already know the resolution taken at The Hague to fix a day for the congress, and will not doubt how much it will
have been [...] from here, because they wish to advance the peace before the hour comes for Parliament to meet, where
our party could have more strength; but the great deal that has to be overcome in that assembly, and the need to call
it, this promises me that we shall yet come to know their good will.

Your Lordship will also have heard the opinions held on whether the Prince of Orange will come to this court; and
what I can assure you is that this King has said he expects him this winter. But if to this evidence were added what
they add, that he brings powers to treat, it will be as bad for the Dutch as for us, and the talk of this prince's
marriage to the Duke of York's daughter not at all good; and although everything is still a threat, there are great
appearances of truth, and in my opinion the greatest is the way this gentleman has behaved this campaign, with which
he will crown his works and leave the incredulous less in doubt. A large part in their opinions is played by the
jealousy the misfortune of the Swedes gives them, and the thought that in the longer term they will be left out of
Germany, and the Empire more its own master; with which they will also blind the Dutch so as to drag them over the
precipice. ⟨Which is all that offers to tell Your Lordship today ...⟩
