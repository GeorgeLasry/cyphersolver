# R1212, Pál Bornemisza to Ferdinand I, Transylvania [c. 1556]: English translation

### Page 1

⟨To the Lord … Thur. Prim.⟩ Of all these disturbances.

⟨I have hitherto always commended to Your Most Sacred Majesty a man in all good things, of which all […]; of signal⟩
perfidy I must deservedly accuse him. ⟨For no other⟩ is the cause of this uprising but Francis Kendi, three times
a traitor; he himself ⟨to this end⟩ induced Antal Kendi and Menyhért Balassa ⟨and⟩ a few others of the nobles, ⟨and⟩
[…]. But the treacherous man committed this treason ⟨out of⟩ his own cowardice and excessive despair. ⟨May the gods⟩
overwhelm this cursed traitor, who has lost this ⟨kingdom to Your Majesty⟩, no one else. {24} {15} ⟨and⟩ {10}
⟨beyond the gate⟩ are being solicited to come into this kingdom. ⟨I have written this to Your Most Sacred Majesty
briefly but truly; there is no doubt in the matter.⟩ {41} {70} {55} {78} {18} ⟨[?have been killed]; still, to Your Sacred
Majesty,⟩ they await help ⟨from⟩ the Sacred Majesty [?vestras], but not for long. If more troops were
here now with a good captain, ⟨the wound would therefore still⟩ be curable.

### Page 2

I will say again that Francis Kendi alone has lost the kingdom to Your Majesty, no one else. This is as true as the
Gospel of Christ. Without his counsel nothing is done in this kingdom; therefore he himself inflamed the others to
stir up these disturbances; otherwise ⟨the manifest ruin⟩ would not have come about. Although there have been
horrible complaints everywhere against the insolence of the voivodes' soldiers, about the deflowering of tender
virgins, and this in the presence of the public assembly of the three nations, many violations of married women;
besides many other plunderings, ⟨alas⟩, which no man could enumerate.

### Page 3 (Practica)

Of all these disturbances and pernicious factions the sole and most certain author is Francis Kendi […], despairing
of the defence of Your Sacred Majesty after they understood that the castles of Babolchia and others had been lost;
who now hopes to have from {27} the [?banner] for the principality of {34}[?nie] [Transylvania]. And under a certain
pretext he [?en]ticed me with his blandishments, saying that I ought to remain in this bishopric, but that I should
cede Alba as a residence to the son of {52}, and I should [?reside] in the castle of Gialu, and sometimes at Alba in the
Provost's house. These are the words of Francis Kendi to me at Vásárhely during the diet, in a private room, the two
of us ⟨alone⟩ being present. If Francis
