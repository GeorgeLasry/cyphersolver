# HM 64 (catalogue 835): astrological and medical compilation with cipher words

Huntington Library mssHM 64, English, c. 1485-1499, 196 paper leaves, Latin and English, secretary script. The catalogue
(Dutschke; repeated on the [HDL record](https://hdl.huntington.org/digital/collection/p16003coll14/id/1503)) says
"occasional words written in cipher (e.g. ff. 50, 102v, 111, 143, 163)". The Huntington's own commentary
(Verso, "forbidden texts") says vowels were swapped for numbers in a childbirth charm on f. 163r. No reading was
found in the catalogue, DS record DS9605 or the web search of 28 Oct 2026 (the Verso page itself returned HTTP 429
and was not read; open item).

## What was looked at (28 Oct 2026)

Images: HDL IIIF manifest (`https://hdl.huntington.org/iiif/p16003coll14:1503/manifest.json`), 401 canvases,
2000 px wide. Downloaded locally (not committed): ff. 50r, 50v, 111r, 111v, 143r, 143v, 163r, 163v.
f. 102v was not fetched. Only ff. 163r and 111r were read at crop level; 50, 143 and 163v were not searched.

## Findings so far

- **f. 163r** (childbirth charms): the many red cross-hatched boxes are rubricated crosses/ornaments at the
  signs of the cross in the Latin charms ("In nomine patris [x] et filii [x] et spiritus sancti"). They are not a
  cipher; they never replace a letter inside a word. The one cipher candidate is the bold English heading word in
  the right column, line 1 of "for a woman that ... the childz": `T R y 6 z l e t h` as drawn (first letters
  T R, then three odd signs, then plain l e t h). Context requires a verb like "travaileth" (labours); on that reading
  the three odd signs would stand for a, v, a/e. One word is not enough to fix a key. Not claimed.
- **f. 111r** (epilepsy and a "restoratife for man or woman that hath lost her kynde by sykenys or by ..."): the
  last word of the heading, line 3, is drawn `S C G e G N (T)` in the bold display hand. It is probably the cipher word
  the catalogue means. Plaintext unknown; "sorcery" and "sickness" do not fit the shape. Not read.
- The signs are not digits in either place; the "numbers for vowels" description from the Huntington does not match
  what these two words look like. The visible alphabets are mixed (plain letters beside odd ones), so a vowel-only
  digit substitution is not confirmed.

## Second pass (28 Oct 2026)

- **f. 50r:** viewed whole page and cropped the odd-looking word "of the Mazzs" (col. 2, line 1): ordinary secretary hand, not cipher. No cipher word found.
- **f. 143r:** the "Salus vite" book title and "noli me tangere" are plain; red marginal numerals are experiment numbers. No cipher word found at page level (a single word could still be missed at 1600 px).
- **f. 163v:** red hatched boxes again are crosses in Latin charms (Agios, Lazarus veni foras, ...); no cipher word.
- **f. 102v** was not fetched (download stalled); ff. 50v, 111v, 143v downloaded but not viewed.
- Pooled set is still the two words from 163r and 111r (no repeated letters or words to test), so pooling gives nothing.

## Remaining gaps

f. 102v and the versos 50v, 111v, 143v are unread; 50r, 143r and 163v showed no cipher word. The volume's other cipher words are not located. The
words are single items of 7-9 signs, so a ciphertext-only solver has too little text; a key would have to come from
pooling all cipher words in the volume and from glossed or repeated words.
