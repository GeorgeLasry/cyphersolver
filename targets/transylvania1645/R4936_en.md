# R4936 in English (translation of R4936_reading.md; same holes)

# Numbered points sent to Ferenc Bethlen during his Warsaw mission of 1645, in the prince's "we" (probably György I
# Rákóczi). Words in italics are in clear in the letter. [?word] partly carried, [...] unread.

1. The King would have to be persuaded, considering the lifelong disgrace [it would be to him], and [that] he would give way in his purpose.

2. The Respublica too should not disgrace themselves: if they do not *want* to make war, they should *also* show firmness even alongside kings, strengthen their arms, and not throw the alliances [and] the neighbouring countries into danger; they should not let themselves be despised with the Turk, *for if* they send in an envoy, [it is] to their undying disgrace; here such things would make their good name *vain*, and their honour; they would show only fair words, [...] they will surely fall upon them. *There are enough* motives *about this in the* instruction, [?sound], weighty reasons.

*Even so*, it would only come to what Your Grace understood at Gorlice: *that* all honour, future advantage, the dignity of kings, good repute and good name they want to count for nothing. In such a case, [?as] *we leave it so to Your Grace*, it is in the instruction to be carried out [...] about this; *Your Grace* would have to wish for it. *He indeed* [is] to bring it into effect with the King, *since* we too, as a country, are suspected for [?His] Majesty's sake; he should stand by us.

*If only* this winter, if our greater need does not require it, His Majesty would set aside two thousand musketeers at his own cost, and *for us too*, for our pay, three thousand good musketeers and a thousand dragoons, not [...] from his troops, *so that* by that occasion we too could strengthen the peace of our country to the utmost. If he did not want to keep them, he should release those [?three] thousand musketeers *too*, [and] the thousand dragoons, to our pay; *and this* should also be solicited and obtained through the Prince; I believe, if needed, [they are] to be released directly to His Majesty; His Majesty's action in this will serve to strengthen our goodwill towards him. *At* the very least, if only two thousand musketeers and a thousand dragoons can be had.

*Our money: at Danzig there are 4,000 florins, with Jacab 2,000 fl., with the Vim captain 1,000 fl.; we will give the Danzig money to whoever gives the money here in Warsaw.*

*Your Grace must turn all* your reflection, ability and industry to this: since we stand with such great [?sincerity] towards the King and the Respublica, that we should not fall into danger on their account, *as if* they would leave us in it; and ever a Christian prince too, whenever they are needed, yet does not serve them. *[clear, unread: buevett igen]* Your Grace should keep away from and avoid [it]; let *Your Grace* hand in nothing in writing with which they could [?slander] us anywhere.

*Those whom* they could hire must be brought out straight, some to Munkács, some to Makovica; *the* György Rákóczi promised two hundred [?dragoons]; *the* Prince too may perhaps give two hundred musketeers. If you meet Bregy face to face, *Your Grace should* inform [us] as soon as possible *of what* you can learn from him about Croissy and other matters [?about him]. They should give assurance also that, if more substantial help were needed, we would not be left short of it.

*Your Grace* can simply say, where it is fitting: if they desert us and we have to be enemies at some time, they will have themselves to blame.
