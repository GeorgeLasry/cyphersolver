# Letter of 1646 to "dear Hofmeister" (Ferenc Bethlen), sender not named on the page. Words in italics are in clear.

According to the Emperor's diploma he wants to effect nothing at all *cum satisfactione Evangelicorum*; faithlessly *they want* [?to break it] [...] [?is] *the affairs and* their state [?pretending], they give us to understand; *neither* to the constancy of *this* peace nor to the satisfaction [promised] in the diploma *should* we trust, *all* their *intentions, will, the sending away of their people* [are] all falsehood, *and* they want to trample the worship of our God [and] our freedom underfoot. The clergy has prevailed over the Emperor; *[clear:] mm, to act against us*. In view of which, dear *Hofmeister*, keeping this only secret, [?even if] the King *indeed* began to make war against the Turk [...] *let us act*; *Your Grace*, let us not be so obliged to the King *that we would have to be drawn away* from the service of our God and our country *too*. *This must* be kept secret from the King, *why* we must do it, *if facing* [them] His Majesty should move against the Turk, and we *against such* [?false] believers would have to go too; so we could serve His Majesty. God is our witness, we have been embittered by *these* falsehoods; if they begin to send the armies, we will compel them to their loyalty towards us. *Your Grace should not be slow in this; let him with all his forces* hire four thousand good foot with all their officers, [and] dragoons, *or if that* cannot be, two thousand good armed men, even if he made war on the King; *and if only* a thousand foot [and] five hundred dragoons, *or* let him bring armed men. [?...] the Prince's goodwill *and* help *Your Grace* should ask; I believe that so we serve them in the future too, *with them too*.
