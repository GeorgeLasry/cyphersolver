## Translation

High Noble Honourable Sir! I respectfully refer to my humble last letters to Your Honour of the 26th ultimo and the
1st of this month, in which were enclosed the answers of the courts of France and Spain to the proposal which the two
warring courts had sent them following the known preliminary proposal, the courier whom the Austrian minister
received from his court on Sunday, and of which, in mine to the Heer [590] [797] [?made], it is supposed that [29^^]
brought the despatch.

Concerning both the known answers and that of England on the same subject, [228^_] just as little as at first both leaves
any means by which the aforesaid warring courts could give their [?mediation] any further effect with [?hope] of
success. The court of England still persisting in some way [...] the American [?colonies] [...] assured that they are
still [?flirting] with the Roman [Emperor] [...]. It is even said that for a considerable time both have been very
[?busy] exchanging and sending over plans and projects.

Whether the Emperor is dealing honestly with her in this, [?or] whether he only seeks to amuse her and keep her on his
line, as many suppose with [738], cannot well be discovered for certain; but it is certain that she is so far very
[592] disposed towards the Emperor, and that for the [?furtherance] of this favourite project of hers she counts and
relies much on His Majesty. And as proof that she has by no means [?abandoned] the project: besides having for a
considerable time placed the young Grand Duke Constantine in the hands of [a] Greek woman, as [I] have already had the
honour to report to Your Honour in one of my previous letters, and having him [?taught] the Greek language, further a
Greek [?education] [553], she has also lately sent orders to [644^_] Rumanzow to procure twelve Greek boys, and
to make the [...].

People here have been satisfied with this answer, and accordingly the Russian ministers at [?Stockholm] and
Copenhagen have been instructed to support and second these dealings of [?Prussia] there in every possible way.
Besides this, the Vice-Chancellor, on the Empress's orders, also [?spoke] about it [?last] Wednesday with the
ministers of Sweden and Denmark residing here, to the [?end] that [?they] should commend the matter [485] to their
courts, representing Her Imperial Majesty's [?wish] and [239^o]: that, in order to make the principle of the maritime act
[718] and so to fix it for the future as a firm and unchangeable law and [?rule] for the rights of the neutrals, the
accession of His Prussian Majesty, on the footing he himself proposed and in which the Empress found not the least
objection, be accepted by both courts and [?concluded] with [85^_] His Majesty. The success of this must now be
awaited.

Although things have for some [time] been entirely quiet about the known Greek project, and the Empress has kept
[77^o] deep [?silence] about it towards her [?confidants], one cannot [?conclude] from this that she has wholly
dropped it; rather, her reserve on this subject seems to have been caused by the untimely [792] which about it here as
elsewhere [583444] begun to [477^_], and which they mean thus to want to [...].

Purely and simply [...] the act concluded by it with this court, without adding the secret articles, Her Imperial
Majesty would not be [?averse] but ready to use her offices with those courts, to the end of [?disposing] and
[?engaging] them to it. The Prussian minister having reported this answer to his court, the King was satisfied with
it, and accordingly sent both ministers [614^"] of the answer that he would give to the known answer of Sweden and
Denmark, so that they could communicate it to this court and ask its support for it. In this answer the King proposes
and [361^_]s the aforesaid two courts once more to [271^"] the act concluded with this court purely and simply,
adding that, as to the difficulty raised by Sweden and Denmark about the closer definition of the aid detailed in the
[?fourth] article of their act, His Imperial Majesty was of the opinion that the [723] [...] that such aid was
required, the fifth and [?sixth] articles of their act with this court determined it sufficiently, without [...] of
May of last [?year] [...] this court had concluded for the maintenance of the freedom of commerce of the neutrals.

And the satisfaction His Majesty felt at that answer, the Vice-Chancellor made known a few days [?later] to the
Prussian minister in the Empress's name in reply: that she was very [?sensible] [874] of the trust the King his [497^_]
placed in her; that although she [414^_] regard to the work of the known accession with Sweden and Denmark felt the
same satisfaction as His Prussian Majesty, she [?nevertheless] could or would not in this [950] her high allies, and

known accession between His Imperial Majesty and Sweden and Denmark were effected and arranged in one way or another;
but that, as [583] to Sweden and Denmark, just as little as to His Royal Highness [...] Majesty she could or would
prescribe [?anything] about it, that nevertheless [...] His Prussian Majesty [?however] the accession with the
aforesaid courts of Denmark and [...] in the [718] peace negotiation to admit, or to treat with them otherwise than

In this state of affairs, since the courts of France and Spain are also [374] and unchangeably [963^"] not to enter

England, back and forth, on the known answers received, with some exhortations and proposals, perhaps by way of

and obedient servant J. I. de Swart. St Petersburg, 8 March 1782.
