# R1011, Carpio to Fuenmayor, Rome, 18 Sept 1677

The Marqués de los Balbases writes to me that the effects of the siege of Charleroi are already felt at Nijmegen. But
that is no wonder, when at this court they make themselves felt with less reason, though to our greater surprise. As
soon as the news arrived, those ill-disposed to Spain spread the rumour that we were raising men here by force for
Sicily, and having [thereby] got the populace stirred up against our nation, they have insulted various Spaniards in
the streets these days. And although I have proved these rumours false, and the Pope and his ministers are not
unaware of it, I have not been able to obtain justice or satisfaction: not only is there [?no move] to punish the
seditious, there is [none] to find out the truth either. Such is my forbearance that for more than fifteen days I have
been putting up with their stoning the King's subjects, so as to put myself in the right and because I see that
their only aim has been to provoke me into a rash step. And they have brought in some cavalry of the militia of this
district, and mounted more guns on the Castel Sant'Angelo than there were before. This happens in plain view of the
irregularity and inequality with which they treat the French: the last time their galleys were at [?Civitavecchia] they
raised more than a hundred men and put them aboard, picking them by force, without sparing the reapers on their way
to work in the fields. This is proved, and it was tolerated, the Pope and everyone knowing it better than I. So Your
Lordship can infer the just displeasure I am left in, not knowing where these disorders will end, when the use of
all the gentlest means is not enough, and I must see to my own safety, to the dignity of my post and to that of the
King's subjects.
