# Magnus Stenbock (prisoner in Copenhagen) and Peter Malmberg, cipher correspondence 1713-1717 (catalogue 455)

Status: in progress (unblocked 27 Oct 2026: Erlund's 1732 manuscript with the key and decipherments is online, Riksarkivet R0000422, per Brice Dudout)

Attempted 26 Oct 2026. No ciphertext could be reached online, so nothing has been transcribed or read. This session
located the letters, which the catalogue entry had not done. It also found that the cipher was broken at the time by
the Danish post office. The Danish decipherments are the likeliest source of an existing reading.

## The documents and where they are

- **Key.** Riksarkivet, Peter Malmbergs (Palmcreutz) arkiv, SE/RA/720725/~/E 4698 (record rpJEfXEhiqkAx4ZNqQe7c0).
  The note reads "chiffernycket mellan Magnus Stenbock och Malmberg för brevväxling under Stenbocks fångenskap (brev från
  denna brevväxling finns i Ericsbergsarkivets autografsamling)". The open-data API shows a physical instantiation only,
  with no IIIF manifest, so the volume is **not digitised**. The sok.riksarkivet.se page sits behind an ALTCHA captcha.
  The metadata came from data.riksarkivet.se.
- **Letters.** The Ericsberg autograph volume for Stenbock M-W (SE/RA/720266/01/Vol. 1-245/205, IIIF manifest
  `https://lbiiif.riksarkivet.se/arkis!A0040286/manifest`, 477 images) is digitised. It contains no cipher letters.
  Its image 68 is a cross-reference sheet ("Hänvisningsblad"): *Magnus Stenbocks brevväxling med Peter Malmberg under
  fångenskapen m.fl. handlingar, se: Vol. 52 "Magnus Stenbocks papper"*. That is Ericsbergsarkivet, Familjen
  Stenbocks papper, Magnus Stenbock d.ä.:s arkiv, **SE/RA/720266/02/02/005/52** ("Handlingar och brev, personalia
  rörande Magnus Stenbock d.ä."). Its neighbour, **005/50**, is "Handlingar rörande fångenskapstiden". Neither volume
  is digitised.
  - All 477 thumbnails of vol. 205 were scanned by eye on contact sheets. They hold letters of 1690-1712 and family
    letters of the 1760s. Images 72-75 are a child's letter dated 1768. None of the letters is in cipher.
- **Riksarkivet in Lund.** SE/LLA/30139/-/1, "Inkomna skrivelser till Peter Malmberg från Magnus Stenbock", is the
  Scanian governorship correspondence of about 1708-1710. It is not digitised. The 1712-1713 letters to Malmberg are
  printed in Naumann's edition (see below). They predate the captivity.

## The cipher was broken at the time, in Copenhagen

Christian Christoffer Erlund of the Danish post office was set to watch Stenbock's mail in 1714, while Stenbock was held
in Kastellet. Erlund broke the cipher of the letters to Malmberg and so learned of an escape plan. According to
enigma.dk ("Postspionen", Post & Tele Museum), the letters were written "i karakterer (chifre)". The system was a simple
substitution, with symbols standing for plaintext letters. Erlund was not trained in decipherment. He guessed one
correspondent's name, Aron Goldziher, in an enciphered signature and worked out the rest of the alphabet from it. He
also forged Stenbock's hand and added text of his own before sending letters on. Frederick IV's propagandists printed
extracts of Stenbock's captivity letters in German and French pamphlets (Populär Historia 1/2009). The cover name
"Abraham Budde" was used for Stenbock in the intercepted Hamburg mail (enigma.dk, "Brudte segl 1").

So the expected system is a simple symbol substitution, and the Danish copies or decipherments probably still exist.
That makes this likely an "existing decipherment" case once the letters are in hand.

## Ruled out on the Danish side

Rigsarkivet (Copenhagen), Krigskancelliet, Kommissionen vedr. Magnus Stenbock, "Ekstrakt A" (Arkivalieronline
epid 21658773, 207 images; image API `https://api.rigsarkivet.dk/ao/v1/images/75496591`-`75496797`). The title reads
*Extract A über des Grafen Stenbocks intercipirte Brieffe und Schrifften so im Futter gewesen*. Its table of contents
(images 2-3) shows that it summarises Stenbock's field archive, seized at Tönning in 1713. The archive is in 45
packets, including no. 27 "Correspondence mit dem General Kriegs Commissaire Mallemberg" and no. 45 Malmberg letters
received at Flensburg. These are 1712-13 campaign papers in German summary, not the captivity cipher letters.

## Prior art

- E. Naumann, "Magnus Stenbocks brev 1712-1713 till generalkrigskommissarien Peter Malmberg", *Karolinska förbundets
  årsbok* 1923, pp. 243-268 (21 letters). These are before the captivity and probably in clear. Not checked for cipher.
- O. Malmström, *Magnus Stenbock i fångenskapen* (1901). This is the standard study of the captivity and the likeliest
  place for a printed or quoted decipherment. Not seen online.
- S. Loenbom, *Magni Stenbocks lefwerne* 1-4 (1757-65), and *Bidrag til den store nordiske krigs historie* 2-5
  (Generalstaben, 1903-15). Not checked.
- Erlund's own memorial of 1719 and his papers at Rigsarkivet (Copenhagen). Not located at series level.
- DECODE R4101 (KB, Stenbock autograph 1713, decrypted) is a different item, as the catalogue says. It was not re-opened.

## Brice Dudout's report (email of 27 Oct 2026)

Not blocked after all, he reports. **Erlund's 1732 manuscript is in the Riksarkivet: SE/RA/760004/42, IIIF
R0000422, public domain.** It holds the key (Lit. F) and interlinear decipherments of the intercepted Stenbock-Malmberg
letters, the escape contract among them. Stenbock's own enciphered petition to Charles XII (pp. 697-707) is in a
different code that Erlund only partly glossed. Dudout has rebuilt it and read about forty passages Erlund left as dots,
but only about a third of the values are certain so far. Not yet checked here. Next: fetch R0000422 through the
Riksarkivet IIIF, transcribe Lit. F and one glossed letter, and compare with Dudout once he publishes.
