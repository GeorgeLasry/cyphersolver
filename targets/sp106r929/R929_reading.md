# R929, SP 106/10 f. 247: Paris, 26 January [1646], to "Sir"

Credit: George Lasry (decryption on DECODE, used with his permission, 24 Oct 2026)

Header: one letter, eighteen lines written sideways across the lower part of the leaf and three lines in the margin; a
seal on the outer fold.

## Reading

Paris, Ian 26. Sr, the [?Queen] has this weke received {your}s of the 26 of Decemb and of the 4 of Ianuary, and
[?give]s {you} thankes in the continuation of {your} care in [?giv]ing {her} wekly aduertisements of [566] the estate of
the {King}s affaires. How bad so euer it be, I hope it is not so desperate as not to be able to subsist withovt
farether losses till our endeauours {her}e for his [?Majesty]s releif may be brovght to effect. For which, as likewise
concerning the embassage and mareriage of [?Port]ugall, I refer {you} to the {King} his [?letter]s, and remain, Sr,
{your} most [554] [620] hvmble seruant, [316] [440] [236].

## Notes

- Code values (none is in Lasry's key; all read here): 425 {your} (three contexts: "received yours", "your care",
  "Sr your most ... servant"); 429 {you} (two: "gives you thanks", "I refer you"); e3 {her} (two: "giving her weekly
  advertisements", "our endeavours her-e"); 241 {King} (two: "the King's affairs", "the King his letters"). From one
  context or sense only: 334 [?Queen] (the writer's principal, at Paris, who receives "Sir"'s letters), 199 [?give]
  (two contexts, "199s you thanks" and "in 199-ing her weekly advertisements"; "send" fits both equally), 208
  [?Majesty] ("his 208s relief"), 319 [?Port] (319 + u-g-a-l-l; the Portuguese match for Prince Charles discussed in
  1645-46), 259 [?letter].
- Open: 566 (between "advertisements of" and "the estate"), 554 620 (between "most" and "humble"), the signature
  316 440 236.
- "for his [?Majesty]s releif may be": the image has c5 (for) and g3 (may), where the transcription had e5 (in) and
  993; "for which" is c5 p6 on the image (transcription e5 106). See R929_corrections.tsv.
- "the [?Queen] has": the writer speaks of her in the third person and signs for himself; DECODE's sender
  "Assistant of Henrietta-Maria" fits.
