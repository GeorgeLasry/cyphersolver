# Rudolf von Ehingen, Jakob Ramminger and the envoys at Ulm to the Austrian government in Württemberg, letters "in Chiffren", 21 Apr - 1 May 1525 (Unsolved Catalogue no. 560)

Status: no write-up. Blocked on access: the three letters are not digitised (HStA Stuttgart H 54); a candidate key from the same government, printed by Baumann 1877, is ready to test once images are ordered.

Nothing below rests on a sight of the three letters. They can't be counted, transcribed or tested from here.

## The documents

Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, Hauptstaatsarchiv Stuttgart, H 54 Bauernkrieg, section 1, "Österreichische Regierung
in Württemberg". The finding-aid entries (Archivportal-D, and the LABW online finding aid search, both read 28 Oct 2026):

| Signature | Date | Finding aid (abridged) |
|---|---|---|
| H 54 Bü 51, 5 | 21 Apr 1525 | Jakob Ramminger at Ulm assures Rudolf von Ehingen in Stuttgart that Truchsess Jörg is hurrying toward Württemberg, though he would "aus übel ärger machen" by leaving "oben" before the rising there is put down. **"In Chiffren gibt er seine Meinung an"** on what to do in the worst case if the peasants advance. |
| H 54 Bü 13, 13 | 25 Apr 1525 | Rudolf von Ehingen gives the government news, **"zum Teil in geheimen Chiffren"**, on the state of things and where Jörg Truchsess means to turn. |
| H 54 Bü 13, 18 | 1 May 1525 | Item 20) of the file: a letter from Rudolf von Ehingen and the other envoys to the government **"in Chiffren"**. |

Bü 13 is "Unterschiedliche Schreiben an die Regierung, v.a. Berichte von der Bundesversammlung zu Ulm" (6 Feb - 5 Jun
1525); Bü 51 is "Vermischte Schriften zur Zeit des Bauernkriegs, Teil III" (21 Apr - 25 May 1525, 1827 Nachtragsakten).
The writers are the Württemberg government's men at the Swabian League's assembly in Ulm: Rudolf von Ehingen (later
named a war and League councillor) and the registrar Jakob Ramminger, whose hand Baumann identifies on Stuttgart copies.
The government they wrote to is the Habsburg Statthalter and Regenten in Stuttgart, at Tübingen by 9 May.

Language: German (expected). System, length and extent of the cipher: unknown.

## Key material

**Candidate key, in print: the Württemberg government's cipher with Truchsess Georg, May 1525.** Baumann,
*Akten zur Geschichte des deutschen Bauernkrieges aus Oberschwaben* (Freiburg 1877), no. 284, pp. 275-276: the
Statthalter and Regenten at Tübingen to Truchsess Georg, 11 May 1525 (Wolfegg, original). The passages printed in
spaced type are enciphered in the original, and Baumann gives the signs ("Dieser Chiffres wegen" he reprinted a letter
Walchner and Bodent had already published, *Biographie des Truchsessen Georg III. von Waldburg*, 1832, pp. 297-298).
The key is in `key_baumann1877.tsv`. It is a simple substitution of 20 signs for single letters, one sign for both
u and v, one for c and k, and one sign for the digraphs ei and ie. There are no homophones, nulls or code words in what Baumann prints. Page image:
archive.org aktenzurgeschich00baum, leaf n293 (https://archive.org/details/aktenzurgeschich00baum/page/n293).

Why it is the first thing to try: it is the cipher of the same government, ten days after the 1 May letter. Ehingen
and Ramminger were that government's envoys and registrar, and the regiment probably issued one alphabet to its men in the field.
Two things argue against it. The 11 May letter went to the League's commander, not to the government's own envoys, and
the government may have had a separate alphabet for its own men. If it fits, the method would be "read after matching
with key from external sources".

Other leads:
- HStA Stuttgart A 2 Bü 9, 10, 11 (Regierungsakten der habsburgischen Verwaltung, Jan-Apr 1534): **digitised**, with
  several letters "in Geheimschrift, mit Transkription". Same government nine years later. If its 1534 signs match
  Baumann's 1525 table, the key was in long use, and the case for the Ehingen letters is stronger. Not yet compared.
- HStA Stuttgart J 2 Nr. 10: Jacob Wille's collection, "Schlüssel von Chiffren aus dem Münchener und Marburger Archiv
  von 1530-1534" (11 leaves). Later and other courts. A second lead.
- Wolfegg (Waldburg-Zeil archive) holds the Truchsess's side of the 1525 correspondence. Ehingen's reports from Ulm
  may have copies or replies there. Baumann's no. 280 (10 May, Ramminger to the government) is a Wolfegg copy.

## Prior art

- Baumann 1877 (full OCR, archive.org aktenzurgeschich00baum) has no regest of the 21 Apr, 25 Apr or 1 May letters.
  He did take some Stuttgart pieces in short regests (preface p. X). His Ramminger items are no. 280 (10 May) and 334
  (28 May), both in clear.
- Web search, 28 Oct 2026 ("Rudolf von Ehingen" 1525 Bauernkrieg Chiffre; "Jakob Ramminger" Ulm 1525): biography and
  archive pages only, no edition or decipherment.
- Not searched: Walchner and Bodent 1832; Vogt's edition of the Ulrich Artzt correspondence (ZHVSN 6-10, 1879-1883),
  which prints League papers from Ulm in April-May 1525; Wilhelm Ohr; recent Bauernkrieg 1525 anniversary editions
  (LABW 2025). Any of them may print the Ehingen reports.

## Escalation

- Siblings: the three letters are siblings of one another (same office, ten days). The 11 May 1525 government letter
  (Baumann no. 284) is the nearest known cipher of the office, with its key printed.
- Clear pages: Bü 13, 13 is only "zum Teil" in cipher, and Bü 51, 5 is mostly clear, so the clear parts are a crib
  for the ciphered sentences.
- Known keys: Baumann 1877 no. 284 (`key_baumann1877.tsv`); the 1534 A 2 Bü 9-11 letters with their transcriptions,
  online at LABW.
- Print: check Vogt (Artzt correspondence), Walchner and Bodent 1832, and the 2025 anniversary source editions for
  printed versions of these reports.
- Next step: order reproductions of H 54 Bü 13 Nr. 13, 18 and Bü 51 Nr. 5 from the Hauptstaatsarchiv Stuttgart.
  Transcribe them, then apply `key_baumann1877.tsv`. If that fails, compare with the A 2 1534 signs, and only then
  try a ciphertext-only attack (simple substitution, German model from `lang/`, a same-length control).
