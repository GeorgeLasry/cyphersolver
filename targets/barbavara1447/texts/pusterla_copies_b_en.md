## f. 66r-v = clear copy of f. 67 (Ferrara, 6 Mar 1447)

1447, 6 March.

I have seen what Your Lordship writes to me in yours dated the fourth of this month, and the explanations you give me about the enemies' offer to this Duke of Ferrara ꝺ. I have done everything to induce him to help the Most Illustrious our Lord in this need of his, but I suspect he will do nothing at all, although he speaks general words, and at other times has spoken to me of particular ones. I know well that he has taken suspicion of me, and certainly wrongly, but the simplicity of Giovanni del Maino was the cause of it, having had him told of the suspicion Your Lordship had, and of me for the warning I had given to [gap] ꝺ. Still I shall solicit and importune as much as I know and can to induce him to it, though I have little hope of it. Further, I am much astonished that, having informed Your Lordship from Reggio on the twenty-fourth of last month that I was coming here, begging you to send one of your men soon, to whom I would pay out twenty thousand ducats, and that the rest was still being got ready, I have never had a reply; and I also begged Your Lordship to write to me about the way and manner you thought I should take in coming to you, to tell you what the Duke has charged me with, and I have never had an answer. For which reason I have decided to write you this one, fearing that the other has gone astray, and so I pray that you will inform me at once, so that I may satisfy the commands of my Lord. I send enclosed to Your Lordship certain briefs, which my said Lord has sent after me, which he addresses to you [?].

Given at Ferrara, the 6th of March.

C. S. ꝺ. V.

Humble servant, Pietro da Pusterla.

[Address:] To the Most Illustrious Lord Francesco Sforza, etc.

## f. 70r-v = clear copy of f. 71 (Ferrara, 7 Mar 1447)

1447, 7 March.

[I have?] seen the Duke of Ferrara. Your Excellency writes to me in yours dated the third of this month, pressing me to make every effort to induce this Lord Duke of Ferrara to pay the expense of the Magnificent Lord Guidemo ꝺ. I have used every art and ingenuity possible, using even great liberality, that of the enemies, which, however, it has not been possible for me to make him do what I wished, because today he answered me that he had wanted to see his revenues, and has recognised that he cannot meet that expense; nevertheless he says he wants to be a good son of the Duke, and also to come into this enterprise against the Venetians, on condition that he is referred back to a proposal made at other times with His Lordship, in which he offered to give him forty thousand ducats a year, and besides, to be in that enterprise, so many and such things that with half of them, without him, the world would be won entirely. I have informed my Lord of it, and I shall also let Your Excellency hear everything by word of mouth. I am greatly astonished not to have had an answer from you to the letters I wrote on my arrival here, and about the money I had for you; I sent those letters four days before the one I wrote to the Lord of Faenza, and according to what Belino answered me, he wrote that he had sent them to you by a messenger of his own. I beg Your Excellency most urgently, if you have not answered me, to be pleased to answer me and to send for the said money, so that I may soon come to you to do what the Lord has charged me with; he, I suspect, will not send {"remeta", with "era" interlined to give "remetera"} more money here until he knows from me that I have counted out this, so as not to put so large a sum here at one stroke. If it were not for the money I would have set out towards Your Excellency, as the Lord commands me; for the proposal to which the Duke of Ferrara refers is a thing, first, not to be greatly esteemed, even if it was negotiated by others than me, who will have to pursue it most promptly [?]. I urge and pray Your Lordship to press warmly the negotiation begun with the Bolognese, because it will infallibly succeed for you, and the Lord is greatly pleased with it, and I have such knowledge of it that if you press it well it will be accomplished for you: by means of which there will also be occasion to do our will on this man, besides the other benefits that will follow from it ꝺ. I do not write in too much detail, because I still hope from hour to hour to set out towards Your Excellency, to whom I always commend myself.

Given at Ferrara, the seventh day of March.

E. S. ꝺ. V.

Humble servant, Pietro da Pusterla.

I send you enclosed three briefs which the Lord sent me today.

[Address:] To the Most Illustrious Lord Francesco Sforza, etc.

## f. 76r-v = clear copy of f. 77 (Ferrara, 8 Mar 1447)

8 March [1447].

Today it is eleven days since I arrived here, and before and since I have written several times to Your Lordship to send one of your men here, to whom I would give twenty thousand ducats, and I have never had an answer, at which I am in very great distress. I suspect the messengers have gone astray. I have made this one in duplicate, and sent it by way of the [gap], the other by way of Epelino, so that one of them reaches Your Lordship, whom I inform, from what I hear from this Lord Duke of Ferrara, that on Sunday the [gap] came with the whole army of the Venetians to Cremona crying "Long live Saint Mark", and that at once the Ghibellines and the Maltraversi were together with Your Lordship's men-at-arms and showed such spirit and loyalty towards you that none of the Guelphs dared to stir {corrected from: to stay}, and the [gap], seeing that he was given no {corrected from: by} further hearing, has turned back. From what the Duke of Ferrara has, they have returned {corrected from: they have set out} towards Bordolano, and Giovanni Filippo seems to have gone to them. What I hear further I shall report to Your Lordship, whom I pray, if you have not sent to collect the money that I have here, to send for it, and also to inform me of the manner I am to take in coming to you, so that I may come to carry out what my Lord has charged me with. Further, as a true servant of Your Lordship, I urge you to make sure of and come to a clear understanding with the Count of Urbino; and I say this because I have it from a worthy person that he is in close negotiation with the Venetians, and disposed not to come into Lombardy with Your Lordship. Otherwise, I have written to you {corrected from: avio}, begging you warmly to press and pursue the negotiation of Bologna, because it will be accomplished for you, and so I repeat it to you anew; and at Lodi [?] such an egg is cooking for you, which comes to Your Lordship; keeping him well in hand [?], I am certain he will do what you wish.

Given at Ferrara, the eighth day of March.

E. M. E. V. [C. M. E. V.?]

Humble servant, Pietro da Pusterla.

[Address:] To the Most Illustrious Lord Francesco Sforza, etc.

## f. 80 = clear copy of f. 81 (Ferrara, 10 Mar 1447)

1447, 10 March.

The day before yesterday I wrote to Your Lordship about the deed which they had done [gap] {6tr} at Cremona; since then I have word that they have gone to Soncino. The Duke writes you four briefs, enclosed, all of the same content, from which you will better understand the matter. Now I no longer know what to say to myself about so many letters that I have written to Your Lordship without ever having had an answer: it grieves me more that Giovanni Matteo writes to me that he has recovered [?] another good part of your dispatch, and that once these are paid out, he will inform you at once; which I am certain he says for no other reason than to send them here as soon as these are counted out; so for God's sake write me something. Further, I repeat to you the matter of the Count of Urbino, because I have fresh news that he is in close negotiation, still 6 [?] [gap], when this was written. The Duke of Ferrara has become somewhat bold, having heard that Master Tommaso has been made Pope, as one who has firm hope that he must be our friend. Messer Andrea Dandolo, on his way to [gap], happened to say to a trusted man of his that the hope the Signoria had of taking Cremona was given them by a squadron captain of the [gap], who is his kinsman; so I wished to inform Your Lordship of it, to whom I always commend myself.

Given at Ferrara, the 10th day of March 1447.

Pietro da Pusterla
