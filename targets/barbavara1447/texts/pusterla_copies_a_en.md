# Pusterla to Francesco Sforza, 1447: English translation of the clear copies (set a)

Translation of `pusterla_copies_a.md`. [gap] = blank left by the copyist (margin signs given in the transcription).
Uncertain readings carry over with "?".

## f. 14 = clear copy of f. 13 (Gualdo, 21 Jan 1447)
Most illustrious Prince and excellent Lord, humble and devout recommendation. To carry out what Your Lordship charged me with at my departure, I inform you that today I arrived here, and tomorrow I shall set out towards Montefiascone [?], and there I shall wait until Wednesday morning to be informed by Your Lordship of your whereabouts, and where I am to come; not having anything else by Wednesday morning, I shall set out towards Monte Guiduzo, and from there I shall not leave until I am advised by Your Lordship of what I am to do; and I earnestly beg you to be pleased to send me a guide who is to guide me in your way. Further, I inform Your Lordship that Antonio da Trezzo is in company with me, so may it please you now to trust us [?], if you wish, that we come together or separately, because we shall all keep the order that will be given by Your Lordship, to whom I always humbly commend myself.

Given at Gualdo, 21 January 1447.

Of Your Excellent Lordship the humble servant P. de Pusterla

To the illustrious Lord Francesco Sforza, etc.

## f. 27 = clear copy (Ferrara, 6 Feb 1447; cipher original not located)
Illustrious and excellent Lord, humble and devout recommendation. This morning I arrived here, and at once I was with [gap], whom I found not without suspicion, for not having heard for days past from anywhere at what stage our affairs stand, etc. I used with him all the good words that were possible for me to remove his suspicion, without however revealing to him anything done, but I assured him of your good disposition, and urged him to be willing to come to the conclusion with [gap] soon; by means of which conclusion, whenever it should take place, I was certain Your Lordship would agree to everything that would please my lord, and what would be useful to the same [gap]; and so he suspected, indeed he was certain, that if His Lordship did not provide for the said [gap], the matter would come to no good end, and to the danger that Your Lordship might become vexed with the negotiation, etc. He answered me plenty of good words, which however are not of such effect that one can build great foundations on them; still he has charged me to carry some messages to the Duke on his behalf, showing himself well disposed. I do not know now what effect will follow, but I shall inform Your Lordship of everything. Marcolino Barbavara is going to His Holiness the Pope, and also to His Majesty the King, for the money business, etc., and because [?] what he is to do there about this will be laid on him by Your Lordship, and he not knowing how to write to you, having no cipher, I have given him the copy of mine, so that he can write, and so that no scandal may arise. Tomorrow I shall set out towards Milan, and I shall strive with all diligence to carry out what Your Lordship has charged me with, to whom I always commend myself.

Given at Ferrara, 6 February 1447.

Humble servant Pietro de Pusterla

On the back: To the illustrious and excellent lord, lord Count Francesco Sforza Visconti, lord of Cremona etc., his most singular lord, etc.

## f. 33 = clear copy of f. 32 (Ferrara, 10 Feb 1447, per the margin note)
Lord Count. Since I have been here I have heard nothing from Your Lordship, nor learned either at what stage things stand. I am here, and wherever I may be, I want Your Lordship to be certain that I am the usual Piero, and more a servant and more willing to do something that pleases you than for all the rest of the world; and so, if it seems to you that I should come here [there?] by one route rather than another, let me know, for I shall do everything that Your Lordship commands me. I beg you to be pleased to attend to your affairs while the time allows us. And may it please you sometimes to write me something, and to indicate to me what it seems to you I should do and say.

## f. 41r-v = clear copy of f. 42 (Milan, 14 Feb 1447; Mazzatinti 16 Feb)
Illustrious Lord. I have arrived here, and by the [gap] our most illustrious Lord I was seen exceedingly willingly, and I reported to His Lordship in full what you charged me with, and so assured him of your true good disposition, at which he has remained so glad and content that I do not believe His Lordship ever knew what contentment was until now; and so I assure Your Lordship of it, that there is no man in the world better disposed, nor more inclined to live according to the will of another, than my said Lord towards Your Lordship, as I shall make you understand more fully on my arrival, which will be without any delay; and I would already have left, but it did not seem right to my said Lord that I should come without a good part of the money; and from Ferrara I shall inform Your Lordship of the day on which you are to send to Ferrara for the said money. Further, because the Lord wants me to make every attempt to induce the Duke of Ferrara to pay the expense of the Lord of Faenza, and about this he gives me the fullest commission, but because I am informed that the Duke of Ferrara, to pay the said expense, will want a promise of having something of what the enemies hold, and the Lord does not want any promise to be made to him of anything that the said enemies hold, except as far as shall seem good to Your Lordship, [gap] I beg you to be pleased to inform me straight away at Ferrara of your opinion about this, to gain more time, reminding Your Lordship well that the [?] said Duke of Ferrara has many lodgings [?] at Verona — [gap] and by offering him that, I believe he would make every great leap; but the Lord does not intend that anything be promised him, except as and when it pleases Your Lordship, to whom I always commend myself.

Given at Milan, 14 February 1447.

Of Your Excellent Lordship the humble servant Pietro de Pusterla

To the illustrious Lord Francesco Sforza, etc.
