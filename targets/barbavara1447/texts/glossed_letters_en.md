## f. 135 (undated, late April 1447?)

Illustrious lord. From what your Lordship writes that you have from Milan {about the affairs of Roberto da
Montalboddo}, and also from some other words I have heard at the court of His Majesty the {King}, I have come to
think that there is no further need {for me to stay here for the affairs of the [said?] [?] and this}; rather,
one should attend {to taking the other towns than [to?] leaving this}. And so, if it is as I think, and your
Lordship has any news of it, I pray you to be pleased to let me know at once, so that I do not waste time here
without bearing any fruit. Given as in the brief.

The same servant, Marcolinus.

## f. 143 (Rome, 4 May 1447)

Illustrious and most excellent lord, most honoured lord. We have received two letters from your Lordship, dated
the one the 27th and the other the 28th of last month. Replying to them, we say first, as to the matter of {the
Lords Malatesta}, that on our side everything possible has been done and will continually be done so that they
are with {His Majesty the King}; [and?] the bishop of Romagna[?] will be told at once, who with full [?] and
{mandate [?] to conclude with them}. And if the agreement is made, as we believe it will be, given what your
Excellency writes about the good disposition {of these Lords Malatesta}, you need not doubt that {His said
Majesty} {will provide} {the money} so that {[they too will be able?]} {to make ready}. And so that it is done,
we will not fail in care and diligence with {the said Majesty}, and wherever it is needed.

As for the matter of it[?], we say that of that letter by which your Excellency says you were urged {to [?]}
{deposit it}, I, Messer Pedro de Monferera, as he has told us, nor we either, have learnt anything; and your
Lordship knows well that I, Marcolino, was on the road in those days coming to you, which is why I cannot have
had news of that letter. But as to the parts by which your Lordship charges your Messer Pedro and [?] to press
again with {the Pope}, so that you may have {the bulls} {for the vicariates} {?} {an outcome}, as with the
others [?], we pray your Lordship to be certain and clear on this: that with {the Pope} {and the cardinals}
everything that can be done about it has been done; and if it comes to nothing but words, let your Lordship
take it as certain that we will change every opinion [?], for the more we look into this matter, the more {we
find the road harder, not only on account of the Pope, but also on account of the King}, as I have written to
you at length another time and have now said again, I, Marcolino.

And your Lordship will hear from Messer Alessandro that, although {it is agreed to pay four thousand ducats,
and the ambassadors of the Count of Urbino} {?} {are agreed to pay me [12?]} {?}, they have not been able to
obtain {their first-fruits[?]}, {the Pope} saying that he first means the matter of {the outcome} to be
settled, and that this is the way and the door to settling the other things; nor could anything else be
obtained from him about it, although everything needful has been said to him. Also, since I, Marcolino, came
back from your Lordship, by which [?] dukedom your Lordship can well understand how {?} {the Pope} should
agree to grant {the vicariates} {are} {when we} {[?] the will to do it} {with the money}, unless the matter of
{the outcome} is settled first, we could well offer[?] that, if your Lordship carries through the matter of
{Roberto da Montalboddo}, on which you have been fully answered by letter, Messer Alessandro[?] should have
sent.

And seeing {the Pope with the [?] [?] and perhaps} because {fearing worse} {would become} {[dangerous?]}, but
from the words we can say to him, we are sure he will hold firm in his opinion. And so, for the enclosed
letters, we are answering the lord your father, who wrote to us, that we pressed again with {the Pope} for the
matter {of [?]} {and for the other agreements}, praying your Excellency to be pleased to send them to his
Lordship by the first courier you happen to have. And because you write that you have from the lord Count that
he has sent and written on these matters {to the Pope and to His Majesty the King}, we tell you that we have
nothing else from his Lordship, as said. It may well be that Messer Pietro has had something on account of His
Majesty the King, and if so, we doubt he will tell your Excellency {to the bishop of Novara [for?] goodness}.
{In this respect, your letters have not been shown}; so we pray your Excellency to make no show of it at all
{?}. To whom we commend ourselves. Given at Rome, 4 May 1447.

Your Excellency's devoted servants, Marcolinus de Barbavara and Symon Arrigonus.
