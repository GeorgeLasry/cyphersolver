# R9237: Coligny to Elizabeth I, Camp at Meur, 2 January 1563: English translation

To the Queen of England.

Madam, I have previously informed Your Majesty of the state of affairs here, and of the outcome of the battle the
Prince of Condé gave. In it, although our infantry was defeated without fighting, our cavalry, which alone did the
execution (as Your Majesty will have heard), is intact, and resolved to see again soon the enemies of God and of this
poor kingdom; who have more reason henceforth to fear us than to attack us or to wait for us: so that we stay here only
to rest our reiters for a short time.

And because, Madam, we have our principal hope in your aid and succour, after God — who, as you know too well, has set
you in this high rank, given you knowledge of Him, and put the sword in your hand to help those unjustly oppressed, to
defend religion, and to oppose those who wish to abolish His true and pure service, as is the duty of all the princes and
potentates of the earth, among whom you hold so great a place — this whole company asks for Your Majesty's aid; and
begs you most humbly, Madam, in keeping with the zeal you have always shown for the advancement of the glory of God, and
also for the preservation and liberty of the king and of this poor kingdom, to be pleased to employ your power for the
defence of the cause of God, and of so just a quarrel, and on so good an occasion, as to prevent His church from being
ruined in this kingdom; for which we shall moreover all remain for ever indebted and obliged to you. Informing you, as to
our needs, Madam, that at present we have few foot soldiers: so that we would need Your Majesty to be pleased to send
over as large a number as you can, and with the greatest speed you can; and also to help us with money, according to the
offer you were pleased to make to the Prince of Condé and to us, to be used for the pay of our reiters; to whom we have
answered for one month, besides what is owed them for the past, until they have news of the said Prince of Condé, who
is held captive, which we shall have the means of letting them know between now and then, to give them better heart.

For this reason we all further beseech Your Majesty most humbly, Madam, to be pleased to let us know your intention on
this at once, and the succour you will be pleased to give us, and the time by which we can be sure of it; so that we may
thereby resolve what we are to do, whether to go and join your people or to take some other plan: whereupon praying the
infinite goodness of God to keep Your Majesty, Madam, in most perfect health and prosperity, and to bless and guide all
your actions to His glory. From the camp at Meur, this second of January.
