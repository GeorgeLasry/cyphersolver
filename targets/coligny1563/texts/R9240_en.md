# R9240: Coligny to Elizabeth I, Orléans, 29 January 1563: English translation

To the Queen of England.

Madam, I did not wish to fail to inform Your Majesty at once by this bearer how today I have taken a resolution with the
[...] [Nor]mandy; having given them assurance that they will receive their pay there by means of Your Majesty's good aid:
on which this company has relied entirely, on your goodness and on the promises and offers that Your Majesty was pleased
to make so liberally to the Prince of Condé and to us; having always held this succour to be beyond doubt, and all the
more certain since, ever since God put the sceptre in your hand, everyone has seen that you have embraced this cause of
God with so Christian a fervour, and with conduct so memorable, that we could never think that any change or human
artifice could have moved you from this good will and holy intention. Informing Your Majesty besides, Madam, that I have
made the reiters consent to leave all their baggage and encumbrances in this town (a thing never heard of before): so
that by the tenth or twelfth of this coming month of February at the latest, with God's help, we shall be quite near Le
Havre de Grâce; well determined and resolved to employ ourselves and them, by your advice and under the trust of our good
God, in whatever shall be found fitting, after they have received the pay owed them for three months, amounting each
month to six score thousand livres, as I have previously informed Your Majesty. Whom I shall only beseech most humbly on
this, Madam, to be pleased to consider how much this would matter, not only to me, to this whole company, and generally
to all the faithful of this kingdom, but also of what consequence what would follow would be for the whole Christian
church, and for the recovery of the liberty of the Prince of Condé, if, having brought them that far, their said pay
were lacking and our expectation frustrated: a thing which we are sure God and Your Majesty will not allow.

It remains for me therefore to inform you, Madam, of the state of our affairs, which is this: the Prince of Condé
continues to bear himself in his captivity constantly and virtuously, as we know from all the news we have of him. Our
enemies are spreading the rumour that they are coming to besiege this town; where, if they come at it, they will find
people so determined to receive them that, with God's help, they will gain nothing there but shame and confusion: and I
have left Monsieur d'Andelot my brother there to guard it. We have just been informed from Lyon by Monsieur de Soubise
that the Baron des Adrets, having been won over by Monsieur de Nemours, had plotted to bring some men-at-arms and foot
soldiers of Monsieur de Nemours into Romans, a town of Dauphiné: from which he was prevented by the Sieur de Mouvans and
by the nobility of the country, who seized his person and took him prisoner to Valence, to send him into Languedoc to my
brother, formerly Cardinal de Châtillon, and to Monsieur de Crussol (who have almost delivered the whole said country of
Languedoc from the tyranny of the enemies of God and of the king), so as to have him punished, and to serve as an
example to the other deserters from God, from their duty and from their country. Whereupon the said Monsieur de Nemours,
seeing his enterprise failed, and also that many soldiers had left Lyon to bring provisions into it, tried to surprise
the said Lyon by escalade; but he was sharply repulsed, with slaughter of his men, and the said town is provisioned for
more than three months: so that the Lyonnais and Dauphiné are today preserved from the great danger in which they were
through the machinations of our enemies.

That is all I shall tell Your Majesty for the present, so as not to weary you with a long letter: beseeching you most
humbly to keep so good a remembrance of the Prince of Condé and of this whole company that we may feel the succour and
favour of your goodness and greatness as much as the occasion, the present necessity and the justice of this cause
require; whereupon praying God to keep Your Majesty, Madam, in most perfect health and prosperity, and to bless all your
actions. I will not omit to tell you either, Madam, that Monsieur de Guise has had a letter written, signed by the king,
the queen mother and some princes, addressed to the Princes of the Empire, and another to the Marshal of Hesse and the
Rittmeisters, which the said Sieur de Guise has likewise forced even the little princes who are under age to sign, to
declare that this whole company is rebellious and seditious. Your most humble and most obedient servant. From Orléans,
this 29th of January 1562 [old style, i.e. 1563].

It will please you, Madam, to believe Messieurs the Vidame, Briquemault and de la Haye, together with this gentleman, the
present bearer, in what they will tell Your Majesty on my behalf.
