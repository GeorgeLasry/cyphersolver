# R9238: Coligny to Elizabeth I, Camp at Villefranche, 12 January 1563: English translation

Madam, I have previously informed Your Majesty of the battle fought on the nineteenth of December, and of the resolution
I took after it to rest our reiters for a few days, as they had asked me: so there is no need to trouble Your Majesty
with a new account of it. I shall therefore only tell you, Madam, that the author of these troubles that afflict this
poor kingdom, seeing himself alone and thinking the road of his ambition now open enough and well prepared, instead of
being touched with some grief and compassion for so many evils and desolations as are seen everywhere, and seeking the
means of putting a good end to them by some sure agreement, is rallying his followers from all sides and making greater
efforts than ever to attain the aim of his pernicious designs: which are to uproot the Gospel entirely from this
kingdom, where it had already made such great growth, and to ruin entirely all the faithful and good servants of this
crown, and generally all those who will oppose his enterprises and violences. But on the contrary this good God by His
grace so strengthens us and arms us with virtue, strength and constancy, that we have an even firmer resolution than
before to employ our goods and lives, to the last breath, for the service of God and the liberty of the King and of this
kingdom.

And because, Madam, Your Majesty has always exerted herself to maintain the Gospel with as great zeal and holy affection
as any prince or princess in memory, and in particular you have shown that you recognise and favour with good affection
the justice of our cause and wish to help us, we have recourse to Your Majesty as to her whom we know God has chosen and
reserved in this time for the advancement of His glory and doctrine: beseeching you most humbly, to that end, to be
pleased to help us with the great means in men and money that God has put in your hands, as far as you are able and as
occasion and necessity require. And further, Madam, since our reiters are already in the third month of their service
without receiving their pay — which has always rested on the succour we have expected from Your Majesty, and which of
your grace you were pleased to offer to the Prince of Condé — may it please Your Majesty, in accordance with the said
offer, so to favour, honour and help us as to inform the Marshal of Hesse and his captains that the money and loan you
have granted us is intended to pay them; and that, since it is very inconvenient to have their pay delivered to them
because of the difficulty of the roads, they should decide on a safe place where they will receive it, and Your Majesty
will give orders for it to be delivered there; adding, if you please, an affectionate request that they continue in the
goodwill they have shown so well in so just, holy and praiseworthy an enterprise; so that by your means and theirs France
may be delivered from the tyranny and oppression to which it is reduced, and the Prince of Condé from the captivity in
which he is unjustly held by those who show plainly enough that they wish to usurp the place that rightly belongs to
him.

In so doing, Madam, you will do a thing you know to be pleasing to God, it being the true charge and duty of great kings
and princes to help the oppressed and maintain the true religion, which the enemies of God wish to abolish in this
kingdom. Moreover the Prince of Condé, this whole company and the nobility of France will be bound and obliged to you for
ever, for so great a benefit and one so timely. Whereupon I shall pray the infinite goodness of God to give you, Madam,
in most perfect health and prosperity, a very long and very happy life. From the camp at Villefranche, this twelfth of
January. Your most humble and most obedient servant.
