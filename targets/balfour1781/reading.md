## A. Balfour to Cornwallis, Charleston, early March 1781, Nos. 1-3 (pp. 717-725)

**No. 1.** The principal occurrences since you left us are the arrival of the Chatham with convoy from
Europe, Governor Bull and Sir Egerton Leigh passengers, both of which are now at the Board of Police. Fifty
thousand pounds came in the Chatham; she arrived 29 January. A party of Marion and Lee surprised the
sergeant's party at Monk's Corner on the 2 February, killed two and made some prisoners, which, with burning
three waggons and carrying off some horses, was all they effected. Colonel Small with the 84 and fifty
Hessians marched immediately there; the church was fortified and the inhabitants' horses ordered in to mount
the infantry there, as well as at Neilson's, where fifty men are placed. Two troops under Colonel Fenwick and
Lieutenant Campbell, 71 regiment, raising and in forwardness. The light company of the 84 sent to Pocotaligo
to cover that country from plunderers. The 82 with convalescents of the army took possession of Wilmington
the 28 January without opposition; the enemy were driven from their post at Heron's Bridge by Major Craig;
the marines and seamen landed and a post close to the town taken, which is now in a state of defence not to
be forced but by a large corps [with] cannon. Craig is informed of your movements so far as we know them.
Tho' not afraid of the militia now against him, wishes much for a reinforcement; this is not in my power to
send. He has all the articles wanted by the army and is perfectly safe. His last letter, 28 February,
mentions the enemy being about six hundred and in their post near Heron's

**No. 2.** Bridge. If you mean to communicate by Cross Creek the banks of the river must be cleared, as the
enemy have posts on it. Georgetown was attacked and Colonel Campbell with some few men taken prisoners as
they slept in a house out of the redoubt. Some small parties of men have since been at that post, from the
bad conduct of the officers. Fanning's is now moved from thence and joined Watson's corps; a detachment of
one hundred men are now in Georgetown under Captain Gray of Fanning's. A fleet from Ireland with provisions
and stores under convoy of the Assurance arrived 23 ult., and being in want of water put into this and
Beaufort before they proceed to York. A packet from England with duplicates of the dispatches by Ross 20 ult.
On the last of February a packet sailed for England with accounts of your being at Salem and the state of
affairs here. An express from General Arnold in the Chesapeak 23 ultimo, informing of a French 64 and two
frigates having blocked up that place, and desiring assistance. This letter was shewn to Captain Barclay,
who altho' possessed of the Chatham, Carysfort, Assurance, Galatea and Blonde, would not proceed in quest of
the enemy. The Chatham with her convoy sailed 26 for New York. Sumpter, having raised at least three hundred
men, attacked Congarees nineteenth ult., but by the conduct of Maxwell was beat off; at Thompson's he had the
same fate and was forced to cross the Santee. Lord Rawdon moved with great speed after him to Congarees,
Camden and Sumpter's [?], detaching 64 and York Volunteers in such directions as gave reason to expect the
success his movements merited, by misconduct in

**No. 3.** some of the detachments and other unforeseen events he only got at him as he crossed the river,
by Watson's corps, which acted with [...] and drove him with the loss of 18 men and 40 horses; we lost none.
Watson is now in pursuit of him, and Marion with the [Hightown?] and Fanning's. Lord Rawdon is also towards
[Pedee?]

Notes. "Sumpter's [?]" is "sumpters" followed by a setting change; a word may be lost there. In No. 3 the
cipher has the numeral 18 for the men lost; the decipherer wrote "fifteen" (and "4 fifteen" in the margin).
"[Hightown?]" is "hitown"; "[Pedee?]" is "s?edee" at the foot of the sheet, where the letter breaks off.

## B. Balfour to Cornwallis, Charleston, about March 1781 (pp. 729-732)

In Gen. Arnold's letter is the following paragraph: "If the cooperation will be of service, I intend to send
some boats with four or five hundred men through Currituck Inlet to sweep the Albemarle Sound as high as
Edenton, and to go to Newbern and destroy their shipping, stores &c., while a few armed vessels take post at
Ocracock Bar and a frigate cruises without." The General's intentions to prosecute this plan is corroborated
by Capt. Eyons of the Charlestown, who arrived yesterday from Gardner's Bay; left Arnold at Portsmouth on the
tenth of Feb[ruary]. By the progress of Arnold, his operations are mentioned in a newspaper.

By the bearer [?]: the removal of the French and intelligence of your movements are both necessary before
Arnold proceeds to execute his plan. I have sent him by Evans the cypher, in case he can communicate with
you. Evans left this today, being sent to collect the force here, which is now dispersed, to attack the
French in the Chesapeak, and proceeds to try to get to Arnold. Having reason to believe the enemy are gone
from thence [Savannah?] is well, but seriously alarmed for want of provisions; the river Savannah must
shortly be our dependance for supplying that post. The enemy make such serious inroads [and] raise such force
that I fear, without an addition soon to our corps on the frontier, we shall be awkwardly situated. A
fortunate stroke at Sumpter and Marian will do much. What is to be done with the troops from England, if they
come, as is expected, here? What is to become of the post at Wilmington if your movements leave that place
uncovered by your army? The bearer, I believe, if he delivers this, he must be well rewarded; he is to
proceed to Philadelphia and will be useful there if you wish it. Scott's letter is a blind. Sir George Rodney
has taken possession of St Eustatia, has engaged a Dutch line of battle ship, taken her and a large fleet of
merchantmen under convoy for Holland, so that I think there is no kind of doubt a war with the Dutch must
take place.

Notes. "By the bearer" is the end of a sentence in the cipher ("...in a newspaper by the bearer"); its
place is uncertain. The "&" before "Marian" and in "French & proceeds" is a cross sign, not a number
(the decipherer wrote "&" over it on p. 731).

convenient.
