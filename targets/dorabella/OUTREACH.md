# Dorabella: getting the Courage cards and Elgar's cipher sheets

Status: 28 Oct 2026, Padgett contact form sent (elgarsenigmasexposed.wordpress.com/contact/), awaiting reply. Others not sent yet.
Contact details from public listings, checked 28 Oct 2026; confirm before sending.

## Where the material is likely to be

Since 2018 the Elgar Birthplace Museum's holdings are split:
- **The Firs** (National Trust), Lower Broadheath: the Birthplace collection of objects, albums, scrapbooks and memorabilia.
- **British Library**, Music Collections: the manuscripts and some letters, catalogued as **MS Mus. 1843** ("Elgar Birthplace Museum Collection").

The cipher material (Courage cards, the two cipher-notebook pages, the annotated Pall Mall articles) could be in either.
Robert Padgett photographed the notebook pages in 2021, so he knows where they are.

## 2. The Firs / Elgar Birthplace Museum

**To:** birthplace@elgarmuseum.org (listed address; phone 01905 333 224). If it bounces, use the contact form on the National Trust's page for The Firs (nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/worcestershire-herefordshire/the-firs).
**Subject:** Research request: photographs of Elgar's cipher material (Courage cards, cipher notebook)

> Dear Collections team,
>
> I am researching Elgar's Dorabella cipher of 1897, the note to Dora Penny, and am writing to ask whether the following items are in the Birthplace collection, and if so whether photographs could be supplied for non-commercial research:
>
> 1. Elgar's set of nine index cards (the "Courage" cards) on which he solved John Holt Schooling's 1896 Pall Mall Magazine cipher, and the wooden box on which he painted the solution. The first card is reported to carry about ten signs in the same semicircle system as the Dorabella note.
> 2. The two facing pages of Elgar's notebook with his cipher key table and practice lines ("Marco Elgar", "A very old cypher", "Do you go to London?").
> 3. His annotated copies of the four "Secrets in Cipher" articles in the Pall Mall Magazine (January to April 1896).
> 4. Any other sheet with the same semicircle signs, and any photograph of the Dorabella note itself (the original is lost).
>
> Ordinary photographs are fine; a ruler in the frame helps. I would credit the collection in any publication and follow your terms. If these items went to the British Library with the manuscripts in 2018, a pointer to their references would be just as helpful.
>
> The project's write-up is at https://dbourdeau.github.io/cyphersolver/dorabella.html. In fifty-odd calibrated tests no reading of the note has emerged, and a second text in the same system is now the most useful thing for the question.
>
> With thanks,
> Daniel Bourdeau
> dnbourdeau@gmail.com
