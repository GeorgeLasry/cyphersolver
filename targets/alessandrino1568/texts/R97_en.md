# R97 — English (Cardinal Alessandrino to the nuncio, cipher, Rome 1568)

Same holes as R97_reading.md. Modern spelling; codes as names.

It is known for certain that the Duke of Ferrara, out of the dissatisfaction he has perhaps felt with His Holiness in
a matter of the exiles of the county of Bologna, dealt with here in his name by the Cavaliere Gualengo, intends to
make some complaint of it to the King; and if Count Ercole Tassone comes to your court he will probably have a
particular commission to speak to him on this matter. That office ought not to be done by this ambassador,
considering the fatherly affection His Holiness bears him and the little respect shown on his side to this Holy See,
of which he is a feudatary, not observing the terms of his investiture, and in particular one that obliges him to
hand over the exiles of this state whenever they are asked of him. […] I wanted Your Lordship to know, so that if you
hear it spoken of you can tell the King to be pleased to keep an ear for His Holiness, who will justify himself in
such a way that he does not doubt he can satisfy all the princes, if it comes to hearing their reasons; but if you do
not hear it spoken of, there is no need to take any step, and you will be […] watchful while the said man stays at
court, and keep me informed of what happens from time to time.

It would be well to see, through His Holiness, that that bad ambassador who is there in the name of the King of France
be removed, who does so many ill offices for Christendom that if he is tolerated he puts it in great danger, as he
offers to unite the Huguenots with the Protestants in a league with the Turks to attack […] and besiege poor
Christendom from every side; and to establish that league he affirms that ambassadors of the Prince of Condé and of the
Protestants will soon be in Constantinople, promising that after everything has been ruined and destroyed they will
all unite and be all of one faith with the Turks. This talk, which at another time would have been laughable, is now
much to be feared for afflicted Christendom; so that by removing so bad an instrument from there, besides the King of
France showing that these offices are not done with his consent, no small benefit would be done to the affairs of
Christendom.

And by another route it is likewise understood that in Adrianople the ambassador [of] the King of France has done many
ill offices with Piali Pasha, general of the sea, against His Holiness, against the Emperor and against Italy, urging
him to procure from the Sultan to come with a very great fleet to seize Rome and His Holiness; and he has done this
not only as ambassador [of] France but also on commission from the Protestant princes, promising that ambassadors on
their part will soon be there […], and offering that in Italy countless Huguenots would easily and quickly be sent to
corrupt the minds of the Italians.
