# Battier to Fagel, Madrid, 5 December 1686 (cipher passage): English translation

The court intrigues still stand on the same terms; yet it seems that the Count of Oropesa will not yet become first
minister, partly because his confessor advises him against it, and partly because this King is not much inclined to
it either; meanwhile all public business remains undone, because there is no one who looks after more than his own
interests. He may well become Master of the Horse, but then he would have to give up the presidency of the Realm of
Castile, and if Cardinal Salazar became president and chief, the Count of Oropesa would not much like it; how they will
still turn it, I shall make known to Your Honour in future.

From all sides it is written that France and England are still deliberating in what way they can most conveniently make
war on the State; the English ministers themselves, in Brussels and here, speak of nothing else with their confidants,
and add that the successor of Mr Skelton will be the harbinger of war, just as Downing was in 1671; but at this court it
is believed that these are only rumours spread by France, and that England may well put on a show, but will not for all
that come to war so easily.

⟨I remain ...⟩
