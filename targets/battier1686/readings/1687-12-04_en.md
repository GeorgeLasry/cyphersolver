# Battier to Fagel, Madrid, 4 December 1687

## Translation

### 91R

It is now absolutely intended that the government of the Netherlands shall be deliberated upon; that the Count of Melgar
and the Marquis of Leganés would be the nearest to it, and that, if Leganés prevails, the Marquis of Gastañaga would then
receive the viceroyalty of Catalonia; but all without the least certainty. On the contrary, Monsieur Novelli [is]
sounding out [the post of] Hofmeister for the son of the Palatinate, and [?expects] further work [from the] Elector
Palatine [...] [?armed].

The Spaniards have so far not wished to show their hand much, so as not to be obliged to improve the conditions, and the
Palatinate for its part will form no claim, for fear that no advantage would then be obtained; and meanwhile there comes
the news that in Vienna they are working to make the Grand Master president of the council of war and to send the
Margrave of Baden to Regensburg, or, if that does not come off, to appoint the Grand Master as principal Imperial
commissioner. Here they will not press [11:466] much, not only because there is still time enough, but also because they
want to give the Palatinate time

### 92L

to think it over well. Meanwhile no remittances are sent to the Netherlands, and the Marquis of Gastañaga complains
greatly that he is being left to perish; and yet His Excellency sent last post 50,000 and by this post another 80,000
guilders, the proceeds of offices sold, in bills of exchange on a Netherlands merchant. The Spanish gentlemen also still
have the old speculations about the Netherlands, without one being able to make out whether they are in earnest or not:
namely, when peace with the Turks is concluded, and consequently the Emperor and the Elector of Bavaria no longer need
their troops in Hungary, then to hand over the army in the Spanish Netherlands to the Elector of Bavaria, or to enter
into some arrangement with the Emperor and the Roman Empire. But because I know for certain that [889] the [?...] son of
the Palatinate [...] suspected [...] to govern, that the old "Sex" (the key's word; perhaps the Queen Mother) pushes the Palatine intercession, that
everything is kept [?certainly] [?secret] so that the French notice it the less, and that the Count of Oropesa does not
oppose it, it is probable that they will not hurry. There is also talk of two other marriages in the Palatine house, with
Tuscany and Parma. The factions in Vienna also fear the Count Palatine and the Grand Master, since His Electoral Highness
would cede and give up the electorate to the Electoral Prince, not to govern as viceroy in Hungary, but to stay in Vienna
and direct affairs there, especially if the Grand Master should obtain the presidency of the council of war. Catalonia is
still full of disorder; the towns complain that

### 92R

Leganés wants service and lodging money for 14,000 men, whereas by their account there are not 4,000 men actually in
service. The Prince of Parma has arrived with a train of 140 persons; and although His Excellency is well paid 1,000
pistoles a month by the reigning Duke his brother, and also has 40,000 pieces of eight assigned on the Cruzada to receive
from his office of prince de la mer, it is not believed that this train, winter though it is, will stay unmelted. His
Majesty has given the husband of his mistress María de la O a post in Granada and charged him to take his wife with him,
so that she had already left before the Prince arrived. Señor Valenzuela now enjoys more freedom and better treatment,
so some think His Excellency will come back into His Majesty's favour. They work continually at setting the monarchy to
rights, without making much progress, partly because it is very hard to heal an old wound all at once, and partly because
it is better fishing in troubled water than in clear, and there are more fishermen than healers at this court. I remain,
Right Honourable Sir, your most humble and obedient servant, Battier. Madrid, 4 December 1687.
