# Battier to Fagel, Madrid, [19] December 1686 (scans 31R-32L): English translation

⟨Right Honourable Sir, My Lord. For the Netherlands nothing has yet been resolved, nor can it be until a good sum of
money is found ... Nor is it known whether one of the said three will obtain the governorship. The Count of Melgar had
good hopes, but since then the Admiral has written to Don Manuel de Lira in such terms about his son's disgrace that the
King is very ill pleased with it. Now there is also talk of the Marquis of Leganés, and some speak⟩ of the Count of
Mansfeld; but since this last once failed, his person is little esteemed and his credit here at court very slight, and
therefore there is little likelihood for him either.

⟨The Count of Oropesa remains in great credit; the King's confessor, who was not his friend, has asked for and obtained
his dismissal, and another has already been named;⟩ but for all that public business is not done, and everything goes
on dragging.

⟨Mr Cantelmo, envoy of the Marquis of Gastañaga, obtains little; he demands 800 thousand patacoons,⟩ but if half of it
could be found, another governor would soon undertake the journey to Flanders.

⟨Of Vander Haghen's project there is no more talk. The Marquis of Gastañaga also proposes to make a new alliance with
Brandenburg, Brunswick-Lüneburg and Hesse-Kassel, but without money it cannot be done; and besides, the professed
goodwill (as they put it here) of the King of France, the removal again of the posts near Namur, together with the hope
of the amicable settlement at Dinant⟩ make so much impression that people think no further of defence; and what is
more, however much one may try to disabuse them, they are so persuaded that England and the State will have to defend
the Spanish Netherlands for reasons of their own interest that they think little of any other means.

⟨The Duc d'Arré intends to leave here in a few days ...⟩
