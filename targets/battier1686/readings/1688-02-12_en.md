# Battier to Fagel, Madrid, 12 February 1688

## Translation

### 99R

Sir. Since my most humble last letter I have read the letter that the Count Palatine wrote to Monsieur Novelli on behalf
of the Grand Master, to this effect: His Electoral Highness orders him to sound out on what footing and conditions His
Majesty would hand over the government of Flanders to his son, and His Electoral Highness hoped that the last [431] [374]
[137] [512] would have brought his son into [604] reputation and credit, so that His Majesty would also take it into
account. But because His Highness in this letter also offers Novelli the post of Resident at this court, which for many
years has been, and still is, held by Monsieur de Rougemont, and the said Rougemont opened Novelli's letter by mistake and
now seeks friends everywhere to forestall this move (although, as a sick man who cannot otherwise subsist, he is much
protected by the Old Queen), he has also made no difficulty about letting the Countess of Soissons, who through one

### 100L

of her servants had offered him her credit, know the contents of the said letter. And since this business has so far
[...] the Queen [...], it is easy to infer that these two will throw something in the way. As soon as I learn more of it
I shall not fail to advise Your Honour in detail. The other gentleman is still having work done under hand, and I hope
in a fortnight to be able to tell Your Honour something more about it. In the matter of Monsieur Robiano nothing further
has happened. The French ambassador presented a memorial about a fortnight ago and, in his King's name, reclaimed the
French refugees who had been held prisoner in Bilbao, so that they could be brought to the Catholic religion. His
Excellency was then shown the attestation the Corregidor sent, that they had been delivered onto an English ship, and,
as His Excellency presses for it, this answer will also be given in writing. A rumour has run that the said French
ambassador had presented a memorial, or at least put forward orally, that his King wished to have the succession of
this monarchy secured to the Duke of Burgundy, and to that end a royal declaration, and for its execution Pamplona and
Barcelona, as well as the title

### 100R

of Prince of Asturias and a certain allowance as presumptive heir; but without the least foundation; on the contrary,
it seems that Mansfeld had all this put about in order to have the Imperial prince come to Madrid [11:719], [which]
[?...] nevertheless cannot be done.

I must also tell Your Honour that the Grand Master, by the last letters, found himself [suspected] [11:759], [and is] to
consult his lord father how best [?...] this matter could be put forward by the Emperor to the Council of the
Netherlands; so it seems that it is now in earnest, and that the Imperial letters are expected with the first post.
I remain with respect, Right Honourable Sir, Your Honour's most humble and obedient servant, Battier. Madrid,
12 February 1688.
