# Battier to Fagel, Madrid, 24 April 1687 (scans 58R, 59L): English translation

⟨Right Honourable Sir, My Lord. I have nothing else by this post than that the Duke of Arco has gone back to Brussels;
Mr vander Hagen has also gone with him ... Before his departure he informed the Count of Melgar about it at length; and
some therefore wish to judge that there would now again be great likelihood of making the said Count succeed the Marquis
of Gastañaga,⟩ but I can assure Your Honour that, if he gives Castañaga wholly new cause for complaint, since Her Majesty
will remove him before the end of the three years, or they might wish to employ him in Peru or Catalonia.

The Emperor's ambassador still does everything he can to have the same profit as his predecessor had, and moves heaven
and earth to come back into the Queen's good gra[ce]; but there is very little, or better said, absolutely not the
least likelihood of it, as with the King; he employs a nun close to the Queen; he tries to win the confessor and the
ministers with everything that can help; they have made a new friendship again with the Countess of Soissons, only in
the hope of enjoying good offices through her, who stands very well with the Queen; but it seems indeed that Her Majesty
will never forget the harm the Emperor's ambassador has done her.

⟨I remain with respect, Right Honourable Sir, Your Honour's most humble and obedient servant P. Battier. Madrid, 24 April
1687⟩
