# R4736, Kurz von Senftenau to Trauttmansdorff, Glückstadt, 14 Apr 1639

Most high-born Lord Count,

They would not gladly let me go from here; ill satisfied and satisfied is against all their principles. What counsel
[...] of arms to [...] the maxim His Electoral Highness wants [...] to hold for ever: that the war in Austria is held
to be peace in Lower Saxony, [even] though the Swedes stay in Mecklenburg and Pomerania. With the best right we
could ask our most gracious lordship not to let Pomerania be ruined for us [...]. What will happen when we have done
everything? The return of our soldiers to Austria, or a new war in the armed [...] of the Empire, and those who then
join the [?Swedes]? For such bulwarks we give our sweat and blood, stake everything in compromise [...] we have. After
the Swedes have thoroughly exhausted us, [...] everything, [they] will not be content, nor do they even now hold the Swedes
[?back], or could drive them out of the Empire.

Hildesheim and Glückstadt are one: the one shoots the bolt, the other catches it. Penz is the cleverest at
dissembling; to the others [...] my thoughts. One thing makes thoughts here, namely the Latin trash: the King sees the
ingratitude, [that it] now forces us to enter into terms of peace, while even with prejudice [...]. I have never let
anything further out about it; they [say] nothing about the point of the conjunction. So everything here rests on
whether, with the King [as] mediator, [...] when all other ways are blocked and we have it in mind to settle the
business and the interested parties together. Then from that [it has] meanwhile become [...]; one should give the Emperor no satisfaction
at all, have no occasion with another's good offices, apparently the Roman Empire a single hope of good service following from the King; so both might well [...] the [...] of affairs on the Weser and the Elbe, and the authority meanwhile go
to pieces. The business [...] worthy; all the councillors of the realm are heard on it, I too, in the hope of a peace
that will not come.

Brandenburg is to be brought to something; to be sent on to Schwarzenberg; [they] have been sent, all the more
because Arnim may perhaps have the Latin [paper] at Königsberg, who on the way [...] the King. I hope they will
get through. Outward respect for His Imperial Majesty is nowhere greater than at [?Glückstadt]; [...] to be judged
by deeds. How the good Lord von Stralendorf of blessed memory deceived himself! I think they wished I [?had] never been in these parts; [...] always it should profit His Imperial Majesty. Arnim has not received the pass from His Imperial Majesty
through me; to bind him to sit still is impossible: he can go by water and land without a passport wherever he wants.

Glückstadt, 14 April 1639. [Signature.]
