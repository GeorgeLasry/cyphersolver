# R3815, Kurz von Senftenau to Ferdinand III, Hamburg, 6 Feb 1639: English

Most gracious Emperor and Lord. Your Imperial Majesty has no doubt lately most graciously received my letter sent off
[...]. Herewith comes the answer, and what has been further replied to it; I have not thought it Your Majesty's service
to answer everything it contains so [?exactly]. My few reports, and also what Sergeant-Major-General Count Puchheim and
Mandelslo put and brought before Banér, will explain more than enough [how] the whole imperial army [is] left to perish
while waiting for the Circle's [?resolution]. It could well have been done, for over the frozen Elbe the enemy's
crossing could hardly have been prevented without particular resistance. But whether an equal force would have been
[?needed], since the enemy [came] over here little by little by ship, that I doubt. But it is now a thing that has
happened; and if His Princely Grace, with the participation of Count Hatzfeldt, had still been willing to resolve
on something, they would have had to deal with one of eight to nine thousand men and moreover with an exhausted and
starved enemy. [...] is the voice of the people here, and they bring [...] with them, what was sent out to
reconnoitre.

I most humbly leave it to Your Imperial Majesty whether you will have General of Ordnance Salis, or another by command,
provided particularly with [...] imperial letters to Hildesheim, not about what has already happened but to prevent
what is to come. I would most gladly have gone there myself, but I must await the final answer of Salvius here,
[...] of the foundation of the future Danish [...], or the resolution that will follow before long, which [they] will
attend at the Circle diet no less; they are also provided [with it], since the state of affairs might change so that
I could come to Duke Georg; otherwise, as far as the pass and the time allow, [I shall] set out there after the Circle
diet has closed. Salis will in any case attend His Princely Grace on this, and I mention the above merely to accredit
him further. Your Imperial Majesty has also [given] Field Marshal General Count Hatzfeldt timely reminder, and whether

[...] not [...] let be lacking. The King of Denmark, as far as I notice, is quite perplexed, [...] obligation to strive
for it, [...] into safety, and [?pressing] [him to declare] that they have no intention of peace, as is fitting, lets
[it be seen] whether he might temporise with Denmark, the sooner to escape the conjunction. [...] the Council of the
Realm and the estates [...] Your Imperial Majesty's arms [...], and of the accident [...] irresolute and faint-hearted,
and hardly [?anything] substantial to be hoped for, until one most graciously again [...] into the field.

Also on my letter about the Holstein complaint, again to Lüneburg, I have received no answer yet, and Count Franz's
[...] reply has not arrived either. What His Royal Dignity of Denmark has resolved on this towards the English envoy
here, the enclosure shows; and I [...] most humbly commend myself to Your Imperial Majesty's continued imperial grace.
