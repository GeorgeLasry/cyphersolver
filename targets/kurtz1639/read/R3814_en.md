# R3814, Kurz von Senftenau to Trauttmansdorff, undated, 1639: English

Most noble lord and gracious lord. After [...] three posts of Mithoff and the letter of Salvius at Leipzig had been
brought here without my [...], and opened, he handed them to me: the letters are mostly in cipher, at discretion.
Whether I have got fairly far with [?Mithoff], round about in laments, that one [...] to work off: they lay the blame
on me, they ask Salvius and hope for better from him; and he promised him [...] would do nothing until they came
again. It is added that Sweden will in this way let Denmark prescribe no laws to it, and force the safe-conducts as he wishes.
Sent over [the news] that Denmark had worked honestly in this. But that for the rest there is no hope, nor of naming
the day.

[...] they would have it by feudal right [...], whether not to bring it on the heirs of [?Gustavus] and the present
Queen alone; for the crown is immortal. 5. Whether it could not be kept, in this case, by a simultaneous investiture of
both, as with Pomerania and Brandenburg; and this concerns the satisfaction of the crown. The passage about
contenting the soldiery must be left out, it is in vain [...]; [...] whoever comes over gets money; the amnesty rests
with His Imperial Majesty in its own time. The greatest [question] is whether Sweden could restore anything to us,
[...] Alsace and those which it occupies after [...] the treaty, in the said letters of Salvius he sends. NB Breisach
will be the Swedes'; it is directly against it. To their advantage, however, [...] of the Kingdom of Hungary [...] we
restore to them [...] before [...] have; besides which, places to be restored could not be [...] if they are to be
specified thus, and everything into the Emperor's hands. NB to consult what interest Denmark has in Riga; and in sum,
in highest secrecy, to [...] a project, as one thought to conclude it with these people. I have never in my life seen
anything falser; to weigh every word [...], whether he [...] with too much prolixity, the way of [?treating] now
without most general safe-conducts, also Dutch ones and others that may still occur to him.

For the rest it appears that Salvius fools Mithoff, alleging for example that one calls Duke Bernhard [...] the
Landgravine Elisabeth like a servant and a maid; Duke Bernhard will after all already [...] the Danish letter, the
crown remit Burgundy, Alsace, Lorraine, [?Savoy], Mantua entirely, [which] are in the hands of the French and so in
their power, because [they are] to be restored. He adds at last that one can hope for peace from universal treaties.
One wanted to make him an offer about Pomerania. Then I find from Balbey's letter that Salvius is playing in this
business, and that there is nothing so absurd Mithoff might ask that he does not promise himself from the imperial
court. After that nothing came of the work from [...], [it] did not do much harm, but no good either; but by the
way, what Count of Schwarzenberg reminded [about] Hungary. Herewith [...] the paper communicated by me to the Elector:
let the point of satisfaction be examined a little. First, whether it is to be brought onto a piece of land by right of
pledge, naming the sum. Second, if it is to be half of Pomerania, whether [...] or to leave behind Pomerania, [...]
should continue behind Pomerania; whether it is not to be brought to [...]. So one learns more counsels in one day than
otherwise in years. It is asked whether it should happen. Rákóczi is said to have received money from France with the
Turk's consent; and so for information.

[...] I wrote; but it is in the same [way] the matter to be drafted with me too with N.N., and then the form in words,
so that nothing would remain but to baptise the child. I would long since not have disliked this particular way, [...]
to treat secrets with Mithoff, who not only does not keep silent but boasts of the palm snatched [from] Denmark; before
one even begins to treat, it is and was impossible. If these lords would only follow me, I would have brought them
well; it is impossible for them, and then [the] servant, this quite harmless; this grain time [...]; on it impossible
to conclude with Sweden. The preparatory work, if it stays secret, can do no harm, even with my universality kept
distinct. If I come, hard pressed, with the other [...] both, worth nothing for peace, rather for a war until the Last
Day. Then, at a safer opportunity, the letters will follow [...], as far as possible. Separate correspondence with
Salvius goes from the hereditary lands here via Nuremberg with [?the] same post, best sent down; those of
Wolfenbüttel do this, quite unnoticed; afterwards one lets his bailiff find the bill of exchange, who collects a great
treasure besides and sends it on.
