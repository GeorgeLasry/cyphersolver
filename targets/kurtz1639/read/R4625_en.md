# R4625, Kurz von Senftenau to Trauttmansdorff, Hamburg, 10 Feb 1639: English

[...] to raise. If one would rather bind oneself to the Circle than to the Emperor and the Empire, since this is and
always has been the only remedy, to keep the Circle disunited and thereby [...] the House of Saxony, so as to meet
[danger] to the whole Roman Empire: Halberstadt, Magdeburg, Hildesheim and Wolfenbüttel must keep us in the Circle and
keep the Empire its authority, if we ourselves do not want to abandon it. To do it ourselves, to exclude [others] from
it with little, what other estates do: it can be done, how easily two and three thousand men can be kept in these
places, if they are fed head for head, and the rest is received from them in [...] or Silesia. Politically, then, I am
not at all of the opinion that one should withdraw the troops, but rather add more [...], than six regiments; but those
that are there [should] receive their necessities in the [?designated] places, be given plentifully in the hereditary
lands, the artillery be provided as it should be.

We have hopes in the Halberstadt country of becoming military; there one will know whether the troops, with any
further [?irruption] of the enemy and wherever it retreats [...], should then go to Hatzfeldt, or where [...], out of
the Empire. It is now the finest [?pretext] in the world to bring troops into the Circle, since the enemy is in it; so
they say then that Halberstadt too wants to be neutral. In sum, I know no other counsel than to keep a foothold in the
Circle in the name of either the Emperor or the bishop, and the stronger the better. Two hundred easily do for a
thousand, eat without the rabble; so we shall soon be as strong as Duke Georg, and at the last [?the] bishopric goes to
ruin over it, which will not happen, or less in a year and a day, with [...] His Imperial Majesty's and the Holy Roman
Empire's [...]; when a thousand eat for a thousand [...].

[...] fear and respect. The King of Denmark, as Duke of Holstein, always keeps in Holstein for need some 8,000 men; the
estates have contributed to His Royal Majesty for it, and the Dithmarschen have given and will still do so, which is
[?even] against their privileges. Holstein has security and respect from foreigners by reason of the Danish crown;
Halberstadt is every day the enemy's prey. Pomerania has 1,000 musketeers extraordinary and 200 horse, which the
estates are bound to keep. If we had wished to use such counsels, what would the archbishopric of Bremen have
profited? Brunswick keeps a number of troops, has given quarters for a year besides, [?offers] to do it still, keeps
the troops summer and winter: why can Halberstadt not do the same, and what does the [?country] not give the army,
which it tacitly received from His Imperial Majesty, thereby serve religion and the common weal, and be as it were a bulwark with
the [...] conjunction, other imperial and Electoral Saxon troops, His Imperial Majesty's hereditary lands, keep respect
for both the other estates, draw the enemy into jealousy, so that he can never be sure of England? To hold the
undecided against the Swedes, to encourage the wavering for the Emperor, and with ill timing lose themselves and the
bishopric, dispense bad counsels. If they once get it into a Circle deliberation, by way of a Circle resolution, then
His Highness is never bishop, but the estates and the chapter. I say: when quarters cannot be obtained for the
Emperor, it is wiser that it be done for this time and the other [...] be saved for the next year. We have hoped to
have Halberstadt, to wait on the [...], the cathedral canons, the [...] court, but [to keep] a foot in the Circle.

[...] attack the Mark and do in time what I [...], as I wrote lately; these are things of which some have already
happened, some are to be met. But neutrality not, with [them] asking exemption [...], and under this word Salvius [...]
neutrality [...], and perhaps those asking themselves. We shall see to find a handle for the axe, if it came to that.
There are three grades in the Circle war about the Electoral Mark. First: that the Circle ask for the defence of the
Mark without distinction whether it be in the Circle or the territories; what is this defence? If they cannot defend
[?Lauenburg], [...] Lüneburg, this falls by the very weakness of those asking. Second: the promise to the Circle under
the constitutions of the Empire: there is no Circle constitution, so that each one [?sits] at home and guards his fence; they are without a head, they want no king, [...] not [...] others, so much the better for us; between two quarrellers the third
rejoices. Third: let them ask for neutrality; certainly it must be asked of them, who is enemy before, who friend; they
will not resolve at the same [...] to have the Emperor [...]. The imperial army is as good as gone from the world,
Hatzfeldt's too; and if, even with the neutrality standing, which is none, until the Emperor consents, the rest are
armed, can we not also be armed in the Halberstadt country, in the three towns in Hohnstein, and indeed much
sooner than if such a thousand horse [are] effectively in [?the] Halberstadt country, while the staff [is] in Bohemia.
We have cause [for] retreat, we shall be [?formidable] to others, and so much less does one eat in Bohemia; and in
God's name, let them eat from it, and in Halberstadt, what then? They scoff at living from it. If in the Emperor's
name we are excluded from quarters, we can be in the Circle in the name of the bishop of Halberstadt, and [?stronger]
than anyone, since the staffs stay at home and the country is so much less burdened; without the reputation of good
discipline we shall have in it the service of religion and of the most praiseworthy Archducal House, and nothing
happens that the subjects of other princes do not feel coming to them summer and winter. We shall not see the enemy
out of Pomerania so soon, and every year let an army starve to death, and the [...] consume little by little, but keep
the enemy blockaded from afar, even if it should last many years, until the estates grow tired of the matter, and
something [from] Denmark, God knows when it takes a resolution. That will be the war in these parts. His Highness's most
humble, most faithful servant I am certainly not, as a bishopric of Halberstadt can, and at Vienna besides; but this
alone the fruit, and indeed the greatest, which we could have from Halberstadt; for what of it will profit me, I must
leave it at that and live at home. So I regret our state, that it should be possible to help him, but that partly the
estates do not cooperate enough against the enemy, especially in these parts [...], partly that we ourselves will not
take up the means we still have with enough resolution and magnanimity. To get through with connivance and clemency
alone is impossible: arms must make respect. [...] one believes we wish no peace, but to weaken one estate after
another; for no one who has the way believes more. And we have [...] the estates [...] with Hungary and this common
weal, to give no further testimony, but in the end it will have to be: the safety of the people is the supreme [?law].
And not we ourselves judged the Circle, but as His Imperial Majesty decides, let it go as God wills; otherwise the
Circle and we will go [?lost] together. I remain. Hamburg, 10 February 1639. [monogram and signature]
