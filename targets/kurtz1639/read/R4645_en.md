# R4645, Kurz von Senftenau to Trauttmansdorff, Hamburg, 13 Feb 1639

[Salutation not read.]

Count Penz tells me [?it was] the beginning of the future conjunction that the King should give up the mediation and
[?denounce] this to the Crown of Sweden, and moreover that while the negotiations are delayed the one and the other
would happen, he [?also] would not do it. So the rupture of the negotiations is here the beginning of the
conjunction. I leave it undecided; our counsels stay with what I wrote lately. I have [?said] often [...]: if one does
not grasp by now that nothing can or will come of the negotiations, then I do not have the capacity to argue it
further; consequently [...] persuaded the King [...] good offices can be the hope of the congress, which [is the hope]
of peace; by the very admission of the confederation war has been declared on him; we are therefore now clearly
[?shut out] of the negotiations. That the King can easily do the reckoning: the choice, if he does not move, must
come sooner or later. I will write him one more letter; afterwards the days will plead for the man. For God's sake,
let one [?show] the Nuncio one's teeth, [and] the absurdities and the will to break off; [...], nothing happens. Your
Grace may believe me, it would help much towards progress. I am now thinking of a way to forestall [?that] the Emperor
and the Empire [should] rely on no conjunction; we will still defend ourselves, even alone. In what a labyrinth the
King now is, and how he [?is] stuck in it, Your Grace sees; let him come out of it as he will.

Whatever Salvius promised to found the King's mediation with his distinctions, without which [?the King] would not
undertake the mediation, are pure lies, so often foretold to the King by me. The King's mediation [...] fails in
respect of all those things on the French side that are necessary for the congress, and on which the sum of the
negotiations already depends here too. Which therefore [...]

[...] Oxenstierna does not again push through the prolongation of the alliance in Sweden, to which the realm commits
itself for only one more year. And I will send my thoughts shortly, but to Your Grace alone, with a pantalonata [a
buffoonery]. The Duke is thus [...] as at Vienna in good [...]: [...] appears. I commend myself [...].

Hamburg, 13 Feb. 1639. [Signature.]
