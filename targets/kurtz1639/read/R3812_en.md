# R3812, Kurz von Senftenau to Trauttmansdorff, Hamburg, 3 Feb 1639

Highborn Lord Count, gracious Lord.

My last of 27 January is at length; I must describe the illness, so that one may know how to direct the remedies
to it. [?The soldiery], since under the accommodation of the six regiments and the Circle diet [the matter] rests on the
following: the first is explained by Salis's letter sent to me, the second now rests on the outcome of the Circle
diet. As for Salis, I do not know whether the [?orders] have reached him, eventualiter, to take quarters in the
Halberstadt country, and that it may be settled against a future grant; the lack of means and the good inclination of
the land are not to be taken away before the Circle's resolution; so after the Circle's resolution it will go [...]
[?harder], for they will, e concluso, have the Circle resist then, as also nothing but a particular resistance. Banér
[...] the Circle [...] advance statim, and will in the end capitulate with Duke Georg with his army for bread from the
guns [Stuckbrott]. Halberstadt will be Banér's prey and the whole chapter will go to ruin. Duke Georg will take still
less trouble to resist the enemy there than in the land of Lüneburg. If Salis can do nothing per forza, it is called
[...] est sine viribus ira. But the Circle without neutrality and the counsels therein [...] to hold, is with others as
in one part; that the Circle [...] itself, the other estates may look sour at it or not, they will in the end let it
stand.

But where Salis is to retire on a further [...] of the enemy, whether he is in any case to join the Saxons, how the
regiments of the artillery are to be helped: on that he will receive [?sufficient] orders here or elsewhere. Perhaps there may
also be a way to dissimulate everything and in time to appear with a sufficient corps; but I hold to my opinion that
this disunited Circle should and must be held quocunque [?modo]; otherwise the chapter is lost and ruined, with the enemy's
[?now] [...] and [?without] any service [...] for Your Imperial Majesty; His Majesty has shown so gracious a trust in
Duke Georg, so that [...] letter and assurance that Banér should not cross, let the Imperial army, kept away, march
into the Hereditary Lands, so that no one take it ill if His Imperial Majesty no longer relies for himself and the
Holy Empire on others, but only on [?his] own arms. Fama bella geruntur. Which is to say, we had run away from the
Circle and dare not show ourselves here. Though we shall indeed see [it] come back, the counsels have less care and
more room. His Highness will, God willing, not be [?offended] by this, that [it] come to the benefit of His Imperial Majesty and the
common weal, what otherwise the enemy certainly takes to himself and with it recovers, the strength of which he [?would
turn] into [?Bo]hemia and Austria; that is the [?right] way to come to Erfurt. And if Duke Georg acts otherwise against
himself on all sides [...] on and over the Elbe [...] safety and respect with friend and foe rest only in arms, and we
can come to peace only through them, if they are rightly governed; otherwise nothing; also these regain credit, there is
nothing at all to hope for in any negotiation.

And so much for the six regiments. Whereby it will have to be judged whether Halberstadt beside the other
[?designated] places and the regiments are to be let go to ruin together, and [...] of the estates, and that each
[?in particulari] wishes to be spared with the ruin of the whole Roman Empire until the war is carried on, whereby we
shall soon, soon be lost: that for the first.

For the second, and as far as the Circle diet is concerned, I must in God's name await its resolution. I would gladly,
if it were possible, transfer such [...] to Stade, so that I need not await, with all the daily letters and bravado to
Banér, an assembly of strange heads present there. I fear we shall [...] thalers ever to get, and lose a million of respect;
it shall not fail on my part. I have nevertheless brought it so far that they again [...] with little of my
contentment, that I [...] the Brunswick envoys [...] with twenty horse to Lüneburg [...]. I shall also come there.
Einbeck and so [...] [?companies of] horse on the Aue [...] an army.

As to the negotium pacis, I refer to my letter sent to His Imperial Majesty; ultimam et extremam negativam, I fear; I will send it on next time.
When I believed there was some intention of peace, I took great pains to reconcile His Imperial Majesty's dignity and
interest with the pretensions of the Imperial estates and the Crown of Sweden in such a way that no one was
approached too closely ante congressum, but above all the Imperial [...] preserved; for also in Sweden [...] was content
with that. But I see that there is no intention of peace at all, or as little as with the French envoys; so I have
found that all my trouble is in vain, and I assure Your Grace that if we were to give them today [...] half [?Pomerania],
grant the general amnesty, and the enemy [were] beyond the Elbe, we would not thereby obtain peace with Sweden. I say:
as the enemy now stands, and before he is driven back into Pomerania, they care nothing for the word "highborn" or
"illustrissimi", and if one were to show them the words nondum reconciliatos and the safe-conduct, still, so long as
they do not feel the war in Sweden, they will never be brought ad congressum. Laborand[um] per Brandenburg that one may
include [it], and to Nuremberg, by whatever way, to [...] the tractatus ad particulares. Therefore [I hold] it well and
good to keep what [we have]; if not, we must defend ourselves as long as God gives us strength.

I had indeed hoped that His Imperial Majesty's desiderium pacis, now inculcated with gentle effort on the world, would
be much more [?useful] to the furtherance of that intention; but arms prevail, and the loss of Breisach has taken the
effect from all these counsels at this end; and whether it will stay so, the Elbe [will be] again in our hands. Between
Denmark and Sweden there are great disgusti, of that I am sure. There is no lack of terriculaments with the King and the
two estates [sic] of England, France and Sweden. Nor, in my view, does the King lack the inclination to break, [...]
but as long as he does not see our posture, I think the Council of the Realm will always advise him to wait for the
work, and even if he wished to treat with me super teloniae [the Sound tolls], item how and in what way to assist the Empire against Sweden,
in summa to assist the Empire. So I am empowered in nothing, and before such full power comes, summer is at hand. If
he looks at his own and his kingdom's futura pericula, he should offer us the conjunction. If we, considering the
situation, [made] him the offers [...]. He will now be waiting to see how he will resolve on the Electors' College's
letter. Meanwhile the Councillors of the Realm are together to deliberate on everything.

My negotiation has rested per [...] tractatus, to purify the obligation given to His Imperial Majesty. This I have
established ex propria regis confessione: his word, he confessed, he had engaged; he confesses that what was asked of
the King [...] by His Imperial Majesty was granted; [?his] word is there, that Warnemünde is to be [?demolished]. I know
that the enemy is not to cross the Elbe; but this the Crown of Sweden, per Salvium and Banér, has [...]; the word given
to the King ratione safe-conduct [...] because of Warnemünde, though I wrote it to Your Lordship alone, [?that] the
matter is accommodated, no [?demolition], no restitution has followed. Indeed with Banér it was flatly refused, and
from the Crown no resolution has been obtained bis dato. The enemy is all too [far] over the Elbe; if this does not
[?move] the King, we can with good reason take up other means ad tractatus; but until then the matter had to be
brought [so far].

First of all, Your Grace's letter de dato 12 January I received safely yesterday. I send you with it the list of the
letters I have had sent to [...]; of the Circle diet I have written more than 3 miles, of the enclosed for Salis, as I
thought they should be styled, in simili what I had Salis reminded of through Herr von Puchheim and Mandelsloh; of
Damer more than I like. With less [...] Count Gallas [...] Gallassiana [...] concurrunt, that the army has gone to
ruin [?ex] mala directione; sed et ille audiendus est. [...] all the sweat is in vain [...], if what a winter scrapes
together with our blood goes to ruin in one summer mala propositi, the thing [...] over here, and will not last three
years more. It must be found where the fault lies, how [...] play at the rest, and our affairs need strong resolution,
[?aliquae] etiam [...] Friedlandicis; otherwise we shall soon be finished.

With the Oldenburg men it is half [done], so that it is nothing, for we shall come too late, and it seems much that
they would rather bring them to the Emperor than to the Palatine or Damer. If Banér is hindered in his march, which
Your Lordship can best know, he is not to bring [...] into Mecklenburg. Now they say he is going to East Frisia, is in
Meissen, now that he will stay by the Elbe; but he is still at Lauenburg for now.

As to the disbanding of the Danish troops, which [...] writes, I have been able to learn nothing from Count Penz,
except that the Holstein estates had granted a sum of money for their upkeep. I communicated to Count [...] the letter of Salvius
about Horn's [matter], as also my answer, [and asked] his thoughts, what he held of it; [he] judges it is nothing but an
invention to come to particular tractatus with me and to make Denmark [?jealous], even so far as if Sweden wanted to
negotiate apart with [?us] and then make war on Denmark, da nos [...] natura esse [?suspicacissimos], especially
towards Sweden. The Councillors of the Realm were to deliberate the matter in earnest with His Majesty, and perhaps
because of the naming of the day and Salvius's term so far kept, entirely acceptable resolutions would follow for him
and for Sweden; [...] follows here, waiting, aims at counsel. He has [shown] me the day before yesterday's letter from
the Danish [...] course, in quibus fit mentio, that a Swedish servant is coming out to the Danish resident and brings word of
strong threats from both against Denmark; thereupon, because of that, [he] asks the King [...] to excuse Arnim for
taking up so zealously, and perhaps without effect, the cause of his servants still held in Sweden. Addit, the Swedes
had let it be put to the King that he should then say what his recompense might be. Respond[it] the treaties [...]
give; so far he has had no answer to this. I am [...] well assured that the King, like the Councillors of the Realm,
inclines more to His Imperial Majesty than to others; if the King [...] considers the obligation given to His Imperial
Majesty and what has been negotiated about it in this time, I do not see how he comes out of it with reputation
without the congress or a rottura. But how long one sword will keep the other in its sheath is unknown to me. So I am
awaiting Count Penz and still in suspenso whether to speak with Salvius or not; I find very many considerations in it
[...], because salus per syndicos Hamburgenses regi non acceptis ne[...] et nullam spem [...] cuius emolumentum. To Herr von
Schwarzenberg, however, I write as the enclosure says. If we have a principium in [...] tractatus in puncto
recompensae, then de modo how to set it going [...] [?meanwhile] the matter [...] is to be commended to God. Ad Salvium
nunquam deerit accessus, if indeed no other way is to be found. I remain Your Grace's [...]

himself assured of the Emperor's grace, and he would rather be where the pepper grows than have an ungracious Emperor
in Germany; he went on, then, and reported that he had held the oppositum to the English ambassador, and how little they
can rely on Sweden, how [...] now also to build on France; Sweden has no [...] res and will be able to seek its
interest by peace; France [...] will strike in for peace because of the Palatinate. The Pope [is] nevertheless
[?interpositor]; France, Spain and [...] are devoted to His Imperial Majesty; [...] accommodate the matter, and the
Palatine will gain nothing by it. His omnibus Anglicum Legatum respondisse fateri se utrumque: the Queen of England,
however, would take up the cause to the utmost, send out money and troops [...], since the Scots have now handed the
King a quite humble petition to come to terms, and [would] keep ten thousand men on foot for the King in Germany. Propalatim.
