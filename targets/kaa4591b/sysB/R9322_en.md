## Translation

It cannot be said, most illustrious prince and my most respected lord, how welcome to [D] were the counsels of Your
Most Illustrious Lordship, for which [F] gives Your Most Illustrious Lordship great thanks; and in all things His
Majesty has followed the counsels of Your Lordship, for His Majesty has written to Duke [?Frederick], captain of the
Roman Empire, [?all] that Your Lordship [?advised]. And let Your Most Illustrious Lordship know tha[t] [F] is inclined
and ready for all things whatsoever [?may] regard the [h]onour, dignity and advantage of Your Lordship, and counts you
among his first and dearest friends.

This is the news: the Emperor of the Turks, having done the ninth service [X], not so much because of the winter,
although this too somewhat harmed our affairs, but more because of the plague, which raged wonderfully in the
Emperor's army, the Emperor was forced to return to his dominions; where, in this [?his] return, he left to the Royal
Majesty the kingdom of H[u]ngary with all that belongs to it; he left fifty guns and one thousand hundredweight of
powder; besides, he has ordered as many troops as will be necessary, for since we can have troops from nowhere else,
we must use those we can, especially as we [?have] the Emperor himself so favourable to us, who wishes to hold [D] in
no other place than in the place of his brother, which he has now sufficiently declared in giving this kingdom to His
Majesty without any tribute, without any conditions. And now, indeed, all this time the Emperor was waiting for [?],
who challenged the Emperor to battle, promising that he would come to meet him; but unless in the meantime there is
concord between [D] and [X], he said that he would come next summer, but somewhat earlier and with far other
preparation, to attack the dominions of [X], than he had come to preserve [them], to join hands with him; whereupon,
just as Your Most Illustrious Lordship has now suffered nothing [...] in us his, so neither in fu[?]ture will you have
anything to fear, nor anyone else, provided they have not given help to [X]. Of me, indeed, let Your Most Illustrious
Lordship be persuaded that I am and wish to be your [?new] servant, Your Most Illustri[?]ous Lordship's, to whom I
commend myself most diligently and from the heart. Buda, the twenty-fourth of November in the year one thousand five
hundred and twenty-nine. This is the copy of the letter to the most illustrious lord Frederick, captain of the Empire.
