# Dinteville to [the Duke of Nevers], Langres, 4 July 1592 (BnF fr. 3621 f. 130, no. 116): English translation

⟨…because I have had word today from several quarters that the enemy [?] intends to come back and⟩ carry it, […] it
were not provided for, which makes me send them back there with powder. ⟨You will also judge, if you please,
Monseigneur, that⟩ I am left alone here with the townspeople, and ⟨that the [army] … of Lorraine [?] is strengthening
itself with what was on the Strasbourg side,⟩ and, if it turned back on me, it would be hard for me to keep this place
in the obedience in which [?it] is. ⟨The gentlemen of the town most humbly beg the King and you to station two hundred
horse and two hundred foot there…⟩

⟨…There are more than five hundred horse of enemy garrison, and the country is […] under contributions on wh[at …]⟩ if
the troops are not stationed here, besides the danger the town runs, not a penny of taille will be raised. On the other
hand, one sees the inside so well built that the outside is no longer the only thing to fear; the troops will serve as
much to keep them to their duty as to defend the town. The King and you will excuse me if I [?say it in a word]: that
[?], if it does not please His Majesty to provide for it, [I ask] to withdraw from it, since there is life and honour to
be lost ⟨as [?] he. I owe it to His Majesty…⟩
