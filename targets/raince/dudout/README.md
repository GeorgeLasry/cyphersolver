# Raince, 20 Nov 1526: p. 105 completed and p. 106 read (Brice Dudout, 28 Oct 2026)

Write-up: https://bdudout.github.io/europe-chiffree/en/documents.html#raince-1526

## What is new

- **p. 106** (Gallica view 55, left page) is a whole page in cipher (35 lines) that continues the 20 November letter
  after "il auroit tousiours le moyen non" at the foot of p. 105; it ends with the plain postscript about "la bulle du
  monitoire contre le cardinal Colonne". It is not in this folder's notes or readings, which stop at p. 105.
- **p. 105, lower 21 lines**: completed. Compared letter for letter with `trans/residue1526_reading.md`, the two
  readings agree on 95.6 %; ours fills most of its holes (e.g. "habandoner", "et des", "s en trouvoit", "le
  trouueroit", "veu que", "l inconvenient et peril auquel il se trouvoit", "rompu", "voulant", "afin", "rompant").
  We had seen trans/residue1526_reading.md before reading p. 105, so this is not a blind comparison.

## Reading

First published decipherment of p. 106, and first complete reading of the lower part of p. 105, to our knowledge. 56 lines, about 610 words. Read: about 98 % of words (about 95 % certain, about 3 % probable {x}); about 2 % remain unresolved ‹…›: nearly all are word signs missing from the table (‹ℓ°› three times, ‹⊞› twice, ‹ϖ›, ‹⋒›, ‹⫝›, ‹C› twice), whose sense is sometimes clear (‹⊞› = France, ‹ϖ› = Madame) without being certain. The lower part of p. 105 had been read in part by Daniel Bourdeau (5 October 2026, classed “decoded, not read”); our two readings agree on 95.6 % of letters.

The microfilm is sharp at full resolution (IIIF `full/max`): read at about 3x on crops of half a line, nearly every
sign resolves; the 13 % of unresolved glyphs in the earlier pass seem to come from the 2x token crops rather than from
the film.

## Values beyond the table, and nulls

- The unresolved word signs. The known table gives only three word signs (con, the Emperor, the Pope); the letter uses others. ‹ℓ°› (a cursive ℓ with a small ring), three times, stands for the one against whom France should “break” and who would otherwise “seek to do more harm” to the King: the Emperor or his party, most likely, but the table’s sign for the Emperor is a different one. ‹⊞› (three small squares), twice, reads best as “France” (“answer him from ‹⊞›”; “in ‹⊞› it could be said”); ‹ϖ› reads best as “Madame”, Louise of Savoy (“to the said lord and to ‹ϖ›”, taken up by “the said lord and lady”); ‹⋒› (“in the presence of the ‹⋒›”) is someone in the Pope’s entourage; ‹⫝› follows “his house and of the”; ‹C› (a large C enclosing “rc”) twice closes a reported sentence, perhaps “etc.”.
- Values found beyond the table. The 6-shaped sign is x (“l’experience”, “exposer”, “expressement”); a barred circle with a rising stem is g (“gaingné”, “besongne”); a dash followed by a circle is r (a form in the table, “verra”); a circle with two bars and a stem with two crossbars are nulls; a plain × is f in “effect” and “afin”. The circle on a straight stem, given as d by the table, often reads l (“le”, “plus”).

## Check held out

Method: (1) Satoshi Tomokiyo’s table (2020) as re-measured by Daniel Bourdeau, frozen before reading (SHA-256 9f29cbc9…); no value of the table was changed during the reading, and the extra values found are listed in the notes; (2) held-out check: on the letter of 9 June 1526 to the King (same volume, p. 33, wholly in cipher), whose contemporary plain copy is on p. 17, three lines read blind with the table alone give exactly the text of the copy (“le faict de sa commission estoit tout autant pour vostre service comme pour celuy du pape et que son intention…”); (3) sign-by-sign reading on the full-resolution Gallica images (read at that scale, the microfilm is sharp); (4) the lower part of p. 105, already read in part by Daniel Bourdeau, was known to us: our two readings agree on 95.6 % of letters, and our differences fill most of his gaps; p. 106 had been read by no one.

Prior work, checked on 28 October 2026. Daniel Bourdeau’s catalogue (no. 5) lists the letter; his dossier reads the lower part of p. 105 in part and does not mention p. 106. Tomokiyo publishes the table without text. Mignet (1886), Bourrilly (1901) and Pastor print other Raince letters; Molini (Documenti di storia italiana, 1836) only catalogues the volume; the Négociations diplomatiques de la France avec la Toscane do not contain this letter.
