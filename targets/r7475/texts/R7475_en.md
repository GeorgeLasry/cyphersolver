# R7475, pp. 29-30, an adviser to Hetman Adam Mikołaj Sieniawski, c. 1707: English translation

Although I do not believe the Warsaw gazettes regarding the acceptance of the mediation of the Bishop of Chełmno and
the giving of passports by Your Lordship for his coming to Warsaw, nevertheless, fearing that the doctors of both laws
may, under the guise of friendship, lead Your Lordship and the Commonwealth into irreparable danger, I set out some
reflections out of pure love for the public good and for Your Lordship's person.

First, let Your Lordship be more than certain that this character was given to the Bishop of Chełmno by Stanisław in
secret, not only without the approval but even without the knowledge of the King of Sweden, specifically to deceive
and mislead our party, and principally Your Lordship, so that under the guise of negotiations the army may be drawn
away; and even if every favourable thing were promised to Your Lordship, they would not keep to it in the smallest
matter, after the example of the princes Wiśniowiecki, with the King of Sweden's protest coming afterwards by virtue
of the Treaty of Warsaw, in which it is expressly written that amnesty may not be granted to any of ours without his
knowledge.

Consequently, whatever we concluded with the Bishop as plenipotentiary of Stanisław alone would all come to nothing;
and therefore, as a first step, I would think it best, before accepting the mediation and giving passports, to put
the Bishop off preliminarily with this: that he should first justify his character to Your Lordship by copies of the
instruments and show a plenipotentiary power from both principals [?in] [?secus]; this treaty would be of no use,
except to alienate the one we expect, and also foreign courts, to the discredit[…] of Muscovy and of our friends, who,
by the rivalry innate in our nation, would at once strive one ahead of another to […] favours at the new court; and
so Your Lordship would be deserted, [?I am not mistaken], by [?not] all, sooner than the treaty would be concluded.

If I saw greater forces on that side, or at least a head present for it, there might still be some pretext for
accepting this mediation; but since we are, by the grace of God, […] in forces, [and] of the head [?asich] we have
certain news that the King of Sweden will march on Muscovy; [?so] [?za a] you would be risking this dangerous hazard,
particularly before the outcome of the battle between the Swedes and Muscovy, and also the real evacuation of our hope
and of so many other means which the conjunctures themselves may bring in time. For in a case favourable to Muscovy we
could be unfortunate, [and] suffer much harm from our vain haste, both from the Muscovite and from his allies, who
will always find his following among so many malcontents, apart from our own persons, even if we all agreed with Your
Lordship to go over to that side.

Finally, I swear to Your Lordship that in this opinion of mine I have no interest of my own, for I am above all
persuaded that Your Lordship would have to want to conclude this treaty with no one excluded; and even if we were
excluded, means will still not be lacking to [?save] private persons, once the public good is safe, for which one must
strive above all, in good time, and with everyone together, and not singly, carrying things through to the end by
the legitimate and public way, not by recesses and oblique channels; which, God grant, may not lead Your Lordship and
us into a labyrinth with no way out, and to the final ruin of the fatherland.
