# R1143, Beatrice of Aragon to Eleonora, Duchess of Ferrara, Buda, 2 May 1486: English translation

Most Illustrious and Most Excellent Duchess, our dearest Lady and sister. Through Benedicto de Leyra, our courier, who
lately passed through there coming from Spain, we have received three letters from Your Most Illustrious Ladyship, with
a copy of a letter written to you by Pitigliano Dabisbal, by which we have been made certain of some good news, all
favourable to our father's Majesty. We shall therefore answer by this letter all the necessary parts of the said
letters; and first we say that the contents of the letter of the Magnificent Messer Francesco Fontana, which Your Ladyship
sent us in cipher, have been most welcome to us; and the original letter not having yet reached the hands of His Majesty
our Lord King and consort, we have had read to him everything that the said Messer Francesco writes, and together with
His Majesty we have taken great pleasure that Don Ippolito your son is so loved by the Lord Duke of Milan and the Duke of
Bari, because we touch with our hands that there they are working for the opposite of what the venerable Abbot of Forlì,
ambassador of Cardinal Ascanio, reported here, saying that out of regard for the said lords, and especially the Duke of
Bari, it would have been necessary for the Most Illustrious Lord Duke your consort to give way to the said Cardinal
Ascanio. So, this letter being written now, we understand that the said Duke of Bari most prudently is moved to wish
rather to plead for your said son, especially since he has more need of benefices than the said Lord Cardinal, than for
his said brother. Nevertheless Your Ladyship may be sure that once the grace of the said archbishopric has been granted
by the said Lord King our consort to the said Most Illustrious Don Ippolito, he would not change to do otherwise at the
request of anyone in the world; and so that Your Ladyship may be more secure of it, we have sent you the letters of the
donation by Donato our courier. It remains only that on their side instance be made at the court of Rome to obtain the
confirmation, because from here, by the said Lord King our husband and by us, most pressing letters have been written to
our ambassador in Rome to solicit and procure the confirmation for the said Don Ippolito our nephew.

Although the part in which Your Ladyship writes to us of the indisposition of the said Most Illustrious Don Ippolito
displeased us, yet because you say he will soon be restored, and also this courier Benedicto has returned from there and
tells us he saw him well and riding, we have taken incredible pleasure in it, because we know that he will soon be able
to come here, where we await him with the greatest desire in the world. And by now Bartholomeo Bresciano, whom Your
Ladyship was expecting, must be there, and will have explained to you everything that was entrusted to him by the said
Lord King our husband and by us; and although he carried some commissions of ours about the people we wished to be sent
with our said nephew, yet afterwards another came to our mind, which is that we should like Your Ladyship to send with
him some woman who knows his nature and habits, and who could look after him should he, God forbid, be unwell; and of
this we beg you as much as we know and can.

What you tell us — that in the war made against that state two castles on the borders of the kingdom were taken from
you, and that now, these having been deposited in the power of the state of Milan, the Lord Ludovico, your son-in-law, had
sent you the countersigns to have them restored to you, etc. — has been so very welcome to us, and we have taken from it
the pleasure we took from the conquest of Vienna; and it pleased us all the more since, as you write, the said castles
matter much for the preservation of your state; and we also hope that, given the very great justice Your Lordships have
in what was taken from you, all will soon be restored to you.

We know that the daily prospering of affairs in favour of our father's Majesty proceeds from divine providence, which
will not suffer such iniquity and malice of rebel barons, who have plotted without cause; [and we trust] that our Lord
God, as a just judge, will give such victory to the said Lord King our father that he will punish the said barons and all
his enemies, and that the ambition and malice of the Pope and of the cardinals will not go unpunished; and for our part
we shall not fail to do everything with our husband that he may send the necessary auxiliary troops. And so far His
Majesty has had an advance for six months given to six hundred horse, men-at-arms and light horse, and to six hundred
foot, all of which troops have set off by way of Senj to embark and cross to Apulia in the service of our father's
Majesty, who wrote that he needed that number of men and no more, and that they should be sent to that province; and
later, if more men are needed, as many will be sent as he wants. We most earnestly beg Your Ladyship always to share any
news you have with us, for one could not believe how much pleasure we take in it; and at present we have taken the
greatest pleasure in the world in the said copy from Bisbal.

We also beg you to press in every way that that Andriolo come here, so that some good conclusion may be reached in that
business for which he is to come here, and that he be sent as soon as possible; and the said Lord Duke your consort and
Your Ladyship could not give our husband greater pleasure, for he is very desirous to hear the said Andriolo, so that
order may be given for the execution, because his coming is desired.

The veils that Your Ladyship has sent us were so delicate and beautiful that they pleased us exceedingly; and we give you
infinite thanks for them, and for love of you we mean to wear them, to do them the honour they deserve; and we commend
ourselves to you. Given in our castle of Buda, 2 May 1486.

Your Ladyship's obedient sister, the Queen of Hungary.

To the Most Illustrious Lady Eleonora of Aragon, Duchess of Ferrara etc., Lady and most honoured sister. Ferrara.
