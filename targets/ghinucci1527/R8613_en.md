# R8613 = BL Cotton Vesp. C IV f. 363: Ghinucci to Wolsey, duplicate of 22 July 1527: English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `R8613_reading.md`, the reading of the key decode of the cipher passages
(`r8613_reading.txt`) with the clear text around them. Text in ⟨ ⟩ was in cipher. Same holes: `[…]` unread cipher
tokens, `[?word]` a doubtful word, `[word]` a word supplied that no figure carries. "D.V.R." is Dominatio Vestra
Reverendissima, Your Most Reverend Lordship (Wolsey).

## Translation

⟨The Imperialists⟩ seem above all ⟨to wish to have […], ho[…]⟩ to take care and in some way to hope that by some
means ⟨they may draw the King's Majesty to themselves⟩, so that at least ⟨they may separate him from the King of the
French; and as long as⟩ he has persuaded himself that he can hope for this, ⟨I believe they⟩ think of nothing ⟨less⟩
than ⟨of peace⟩. And if he offered ⟨them richer conditions⟩, although for a certain show of honesty ⟨they say in words
that they want nothing else⟩. I well believe that, if ⟨this hope did not feed them, richer conditions having been
offered them […]⟩, it could be hoped well enough ⟨that […] they would come to [?peace]⟩. And much more could be
hoped, in my judgement, if ⟨they did not hold it for certain that the friendship and union between the King's Majesty
[and] the King of the French will not last long⟩.

⟨The French⟩ seem much, ⟨if not too much, to desire […]⟩. And they strive as much as they can, when they meet ⟨with
the Emperor's councillors⟩, since they hold the [?discussion] divided, that some ⟨handle [?be left] by which⟩ they
themselves ⟨or we⟩ can always ⟨return to the talks; and they often press us⟩ to insist particularly on this ⟨with the
Emperor's councillors⟩ as if on Our own account, showing quite openly ⟨that they⟩ greatly ⟨desire that the treaty⟩
be ⟨not⟩ altogether ⟨excluded⟩. From this ⟨excessive desire⟩ I believe comes the reason why, ⟨through Avemaria⟩, in
the past days ⟨the Emperor⟩ told Us ⟨that three millions had been offered⟩, if only that be true. I for my part gave
little credence to this matter, until ⟨Tarbes⟩, wishing to clear it up, rather confirmed it in my judgement: ⟨having
[found] the messenger N., a friar, in his house, he wanted⟩ him to say before Us what he had been instructed on this.
⟨He, however⟩, beginning, as it seemed to me, not from the beginning but from the end ⟨of his commission, said that
de Lautrec, at the said friar's departure⟩, had told him that ⟨nothing was to be spared for the liberation of the
sons, and that he should offer even up to a million⟩.

But since, from what I have heard, ⟨less than a million⟩ had never before been ⟨offered⟩, but rather ⟨[…] a good deal
more⟩, it seems in no way likely that ⟨Lautrec⟩ was dealing about one ⟨million⟩ only, but rather about ⟨a million⟩
beyond what had been ⟨offered⟩; which also seems borne out well enough, since at that time they perhaps did not yet
⟨count on the King's Majesty for themselves⟩. Some likelihood also seems to be given to what ⟨the Emperor⟩ hinted to
Us, namely that ⟨a long delay⟩ had been asked for paying those ⟨three millions, and that the payment⟩ be divided into
several ⟨[…] instalments⟩, which perhaps ⟨will suit the King of the French⟩ better. Whatever it be, I do not doubt
that Your Most Reverend Lordship will have certain light on the truth.

Serious men think that ⟨the man who is being sent by the Emperor to the Pope⟩ has no commission at all ⟨concerning
his liberation, and that this […] is put about to stop the [?mouths] of those who speak ill⟩, here as elsewhere,
⟨of the Pope's detention⟩.

I have understood from a serious man that ⟨it is spread […] by th[?ese men]⟩ that Your Most Reverend Lordship is
coming into France to see to it, among other things, that ⟨the English and the Gallican Church be separated from the
Roman⟩, not indeed ⟨during the Pope's captivity⟩ and to [?help] ⟨his liberation⟩, as is said, ⟨[…] but for a
perpetual division⟩.
