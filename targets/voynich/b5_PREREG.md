# b5 pre-registration: generator built to pass the battery (written 28 Oct 2026, before any b5 code or score)

Question. Can a 15th-century-executable procedure with few free parameters reproduce Voynichese across the
b3 battery, and does a meaningless-text procedure or a verbose cipher of real text do it better? This file is
committed before either generator exists. Anything below changed later is listed in a dated "Deviations"
section at the end, never edited in place.

## Text and battery

- Real text: ZL3b-n, locus P, parsed by the corrected `parse_ivtff.py` (28 Oct 2026: `<->` = word break,
  `{..}` groups kept). Everything as in b3: same `a4.load_zl`, same skeleton (pages, lines, line lengths).
- Battery: the 37 items of `b3_bakeoff.BATTERY_TEXT`, computed by `b3_bakeoff.battery`, unchanged.
- Match rule: b3's rule unchanged (tol = max(10 % of real, autocopist seed spread), real values from the
  re-run on the corrected parse). Full and short (10,053-token) batteries both reported.

## Fit items and held-out items

- **Fit set (17 items): groups a, b, c, f** (character entropies, word length, vocabulary, glyph slots).
  Free parameters may be tuned only against these, on the real text's values.
- **Held-out set (20 items): groups d, e, g, h, i, j, k** (adjacency, self-citation, line position, long
  range, predictability, page level, drift). Never looked at while tuning. The tuning script computes only
  fit-set items; the held-out items are computed once, by a separate scoring script, after the parameters are
  frozen in a committed file `b5_params.json`.

## Candidates

Both get at most **6 free continuous parameters**, plus discrete design choices that are fixed in this file.
Neither may read Voynich word types or token frequencies. Allowed inputs are the glyph inventory, the
hand-built glyph grammar already in `a4_selfcitation.py` (SIMILAR, FINAL_SUB, PREFIX_OK, `valid`), the page
skeleton, and each page's scribe, Currier language and section (IVTFF $H, $L, $I): a real scribe knew which
section and hand they were in.

**G (meaningless): drifting-source autocopist.** Timm & Schinner's self-citation procedure (`a4.Autocopist`)
with three changes fixed now: (1) a per-section seed line, generated by the same morph operations from Timm's
initial line, at each change of section or scribe; (2) source choice biased towards the current page and
section rather than the whole document; (3) the morph probabilities drift linearly within each scribe's run
of pages (the A-to-B regime change as a change of habit). Free parameters: reuse-unchanged probability,
page-locality weight, section-locality weight, drift rate, morph-size mean, line-initial gallows rate.

**C (meaningful): verbose slot cipher with a nomenclator.** Latin plaintext with topic structure (Pliny,
Naturalis historia, books ordered by the manuscript's sections: herbal → books 20-27, astronomical →
book 2, balneological → book 31, pharmaceutical → books 28-32, recipes → books 20-27 in a second pass).
Each plaintext unit (letter bigram or single letter, chosen by greedy left-to-right parse against a table)
becomes one glyph group drawn from a slot table (prefix, gallows/bench, suffix) under `valid`. One table per
Currier language (the scribe chooses the table). A nomenclator of frequent Latin words becomes whole tokens.
Free parameters: nomenclator size, share of single-letter units, homophone count per unit, table overlap
between A and B, null-group rate, line-final group rate.

## Outcomes

- Primary: held-out matches out of 20, full battery, mean over seeds 1-5, with the range.
- Calibration: the same held-out count for `voynich_A` and `voynich_B` against the whole text (how much the real
  text disagrees with itself). A candidate **reproduces** the held-out structure if its mean is at least the lower
  of the two.
- Secondary: short battery; fit-set matches (expected high by construction, reported, not interpreted).

## What each result would mean (written now)

- G reproduces, C does not: meaningless text is sufficient for everything the battery measures; the cipher
  account then needs a better design, not a different verdict. Does not show the text is meaningless.
- C reproduces, G does not: a verbose cipher of real Latin is sufficient; the meaningless account loses its
  main advantage (economy). Does not show the text has meaning.
- Both reproduce: the battery cannot separate the two; report which needs fewer parameters.
- Neither: report which held-out items fail for both; those become the target for the next round.

None of the four outcomes settles meaning. The site page will say so.
