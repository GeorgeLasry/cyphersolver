# AGS Estado leg. 8 no. 150, the three governors of Castile (Adrian of Utrecht, the Admiral, the Constable) to Charles V, Vitoria, 30 Dec 1521: English translation

Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty,

After the other general letter that goes with this one was written, which is a duplicate of another that we sent to
Your Majesty some days ago, two substantial matters have arisen of which to inform you. The first is the death of the
King of Portugal, for which we have felt the grief that is due, both for what the Queen of Portugal, Your Majesty's
sister, loses and will feel, and because it seems to us that the death of the said King has happened at a
[?juncture] that does not [suf] Your Majesty's affairs; for through the friendship and kinship you had with him,
everything touching that quarter was so secure, and he showed himself so good a friend and kinsman of Your Majesty.
And now, with this young man who succeeds as King in that realm, and since it is said he has about him some who do
not leave his ears idle, we do not know what attitude he will choose to show; for, as Your Majesty must already know,
the Kings of Portugal have an ancient alliance with those of France, and we believe the French will not be slow to
pursue by that route what serves their purpose. To remedy this, and to try to continue Your Majesty's kinship and
friendship with the said King, and to visit and console the Queen as is right and as she needs, we have resolved to
send from here a prelate or a lightly equipped gentleman; and since we believe Your Majesty will be pleased to do the
same from there, we beg you, before the King of France takes the lead, to send to the said Portugal a person who is
kin to your house, to show him the sorrow you feel at the death of the King his father, so dear to you and so close a
kinsman, and to ask him to be pleased to succeed to the love, kinship and alliance that used to exist between Your
Majesty and his father, offering him that on Your Majesty's part all this will be kept up with great goodwill, and
whatever more Your Majesty may command; and the same person will visit and console on your behalf the Queen, for
which she has [?great] [?grief]. And since Your Majesty will consider and provide the other things that the case
requires, there is no need to write more about it.

The other matter is that some days ago, with the [?bad] fortune they have had at sea, hulks (urcas) [xub] [yo]as have
put in to the seas of Spain: one of them at San Sebastián, which is detained and kept very securely where they come
in from the cove of Guipúzcoa, where it now is; and the other two at Laredo, for whose detention [ren] [nod] to
procure there; but we do not know whether they will let themselves be detained, because at present there is no force
there to compel them. Those that can be embargoed, our purpose is to keep sequestered, both the ships and the goods
that come in them, until we have consulted Your Majesty. The question is this: although they come under Your
Majesty's safe-conduct, a copy of which will go with this despatch, it has seemed to those of the [?Council] who were
here, and also to those of the Royal Council who are at Burgos, whom we informed of it, that the whole basis of the
safe-conduct depends on the letters of marque and reprisal; and that, notwithstanding that it does say the fleet of
war is not understood to alter the safe-conduct, it is still understood to depend on that and to extend to the
suspension of the letters of reprisal and no further; that it cannot reasonably be believed that Your Majesty granted
the safe-conduct, or meant that, in case [xac] were at war against him, their hulks should go safely, making war and
peace at their will; and that if he were to come, as we are told he is coming, and [xac] and the French are to join
the [sif], and Your Majesty in the war being waged in [?Italy], this should be held good prize; and that if [xac] are
neutral with Your Majesty, they should be won over with [tib]... or that Your Majesty should take advantage of this
in the considerations of state that are held with them. I beg Your Majesty to have the circumstances that depend on
this looked into, and to write to us at once what you are pleased that we should do, so that it may be done as is
right. And besides the above, it seems to us well that Your Majesty should know that, in case they come to act in aid
or company of the said French and it is presumed that these hulks would go there, we believe that the French would
take them by fair means or foul to use them in their fleet, if the war between [Lag] and France is to go on; for which
the said hulks would not be worth much, nor the French fleet, which they are already beginning to make very large, as
we are told today. And since we inform Your Majesty of what we can learn, send us [xit] word of the decision on all of
it.

After the above was written we learned that the hulks at Laredo have set sail, so that Your Majesty has to decide
only about the hulk at San Sebastián, which still remains and will be kept securely until Your Majesty orders
otherwise. From Vitoria, 30 December 1521.

Your most humble servant. — The Cardinal of Tortosa. — The Admiral. — The Constable.
