# ff. 149v–150v, Forget to Villeroy, April 1586: English translation

Translated from the reading (`reading_f150.md`), which follows the contemporary clear copy on ff. 155v–156r.
`(?)` marks a word read with doubt, `[…]` an unread word.

## ff. 149v–150r

As was very needful, since(?) he had settled the surrender of Castets in a way that would not, in my opinion, have
been found good. For in the capitulation of Castets itself he had included those of the opposing party, granting
them twelve thousand écus for the demolition of [the captain's] house, which was like a public penance; that they
might peacefully enjoy their property without changing religion, which was to break the edict outright; and then
granting them a general amnesty. It was settled as you will have seen from the despatch. And the capitulation of the
place, as surrendered(?) and signed(?), is only in the form it ought to have been, and is no more than a verbal
promise, which goes against his most honest pretext. But if there was a fault in the substance, there was more in
the form: this capitulation had been so rushed during my absence that it was as good as settled without the advice
and consultation of the Marshal, who was rightly very angry. And it being true(?) that the Sieur du Mayne [Mayenne]
had parted from him so badly over it, the said Marshal had resolved to go back(?) and was leaving for Bordeaux.
Nevertheless I went back and forth(?) between the one and the other so much that, the capitulations having been
reworked as I tell you, what concerned them was very well kept(?), and I can assure you they are now on very good
terms; and if the […] that they did not part over a company nor in friendship(?); for I assure you I know no reason

## f. 150v

why they should do so, unless it is that both listen too readily to their own people, who on each side want
nothing to do with the other.

As for the above, I wanted to write you the true story of it, so that if it is spoken of over there, as may happen,
you are well informed of the truth of the matter, which, thank God, passed off quietly enough, although there was
every sign of the contrary. I had not expected to see any more ruin(?) in this army; but in this I think I have
somewhat earned my wages(?), as perhaps both of them will admit to you.
