# f. 143v, ll. 26–35, Mayenne to Henri III, April 1586: English

Translation of `reading_f143v_tail.md`, with the same holes. It begins inside the sentence where the f. 155r copy
breaks off.

## The cipher, from the copy's break

… since we have from him this undertaking not to bear arms for his party for six months, where he [l. 26] could have
done [?much more] harm; [l. 27] and I have great hope of drawing him away from it [l. 28]
altogether. In doing so, I hope to be able to [?get] good service [l. 29] from him, and through his means and
example to draw away other [l. 30] men and places of consequence.

That is what [l. 31] moved me to enter into this treaty more willingly.

[l. 32] On which Your Majesty will, if it please you, let me know your [l. 33] intention. I have left the whole
of it, except the sum to which [l. 34] I have pledged my word, subject [l. 35] to the good pleasure of […].

(*Pour beaucoup davantaige*: "by a good deal more"; the f. 155v copy has *pour beaucoup davantage*. The closing figures stand where the copy has *Vostre Majesté*, "Your Majesty", but
they do not spell it.)

## The clear text that follows (outside the cipher)

Yesterday(?) the Marshal had those who were inside the said place of Castets come out, and has already(?) […]
begun to raze(?) it, to which the people of the country contribute so willingly that it will soon be done. I expect
the said Marshal here in two days with all his(?) troops(?). Meanwhile I have begun today to have this place
invested, of which the enemy are very b[…] …

Docket of the copy (f. 155v): *M.r du Mayne*, April 1586; the day may be the 10th.
