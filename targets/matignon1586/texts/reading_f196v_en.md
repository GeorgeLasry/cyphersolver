# f. 196v, Mayenne's camp to Henri III, before Monségur, May 1586: English

## The siege

[f. 196r ends: *However, because the town is large and hard to besiege, it has been necessary to dig*]
[l. 1] trenches in several places. And being [l. 2] short of pioneers, the work has [l. 3] been the longer for it.

I believe that the day after tomorrow we shall [l. 4] begin to batter it with a battery of fifteen cannon, at
which [l. 5] it seems likely they will be taken aback, not being [l. 6] used to seeing places besieged in [l. 7] this
country with such an equipage.

Nevertheless, so far they [l. 8] make every show of wishing to [l. 9] defend themselves, so as not to suffer the
shame that the King of Navarre made [l. 10] the man who commanded in Sainte- [l. 11] Bazeille suffer, whom it is
certain he has had [l. 12] put in prison.

And if it is said that he means [l. 13] to do to him *pas* […] (the sentence breaks off: *faire pas* and then the
next sentence; perhaps *faire passer [le pas]*, "put to death", but the figures give only *pas*).

## Navarre and Turenne

We have word that he is to leave [l. 14] within a few days from [99] to go into [l. 15] Saintonge, and they
put it about among themselves that it is to [l. 16] gather […] of his friends, and from there to go to meet
[l. 17] his reiters.

The lord Viscount of Turenne is [l. 18] still at Nérac, from where there is no talk of his leaving; and it is
[l. 19] the common opinion that the charge of this whole [l. 20] province is to remain with him [l. 21] during the
absence of the said lord King of Navarre.

## The army's needs

Your Majesty has been informed [l. 22] by the earlier despatches, and still more [l. 23] [?p]articularly by the
sieur de [?Sénégal], [l. 24] of the needs of this army, as much for money as for [65], [l. 25] which grow worse every
day, so much that they can hardly [l. 26] be felt to grow any more, for they have almost [l. 27] reached the
extremity.

To which Your Majesty will provide, if it [l. 28] please you, because these [?could not] be remedied in an instant,
[l. 29] the evils which are [?afterwards] irreparable.

We await [l. 30] by the first despatch certainty about the [l. 31] sending of the third month['s pay], the waiting
for which is [l. 32] very costly to you, as Your Majesty will have learned from the last [l. 33] despatch.

And as it is also very necessary to make known [l. 34] promptly your will on the proposal [l. 35] which will be
made to you by [?Seval], concerning what is to be [l. 36] undertaken on leaving this siege.

## The clear close

*Sire, I most humbly pray the Creator to give you, in perfect health, a very long and very happy life. From the camp
before Monségur, the […] day of May 1586.*

*Your most humble, most obedient and most faithful —*

(The signature is not on the page.)

Notes. *Partement du troisième mois*: literally "the departure of the third month", i.e. the sending of the third
month's money for the army (the English adds "pay"). *Dont l'attente vous est bien chère*: "the waiting for which
costs you dear". *Que les ne se sent plus guères augmenter*: the cipher has *les* where *elles* is wanted; read as
"that they can hardly be felt to grow any more". *[?Seval]*: perhaps the sieur de Bosseval named in the clear
opening on f. 196r (the cipher gives only *seval*). *[99]*: the place Navarre is leaving from; *[65]*: a second
kind of supply beside money (provisions?).
