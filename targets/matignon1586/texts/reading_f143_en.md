# ff. 143r–v, Mayenne to Henri III, April 1586: English translation

Translated from the reading (`reading_f143.md`), which follows the contemporary clear copy on f. 155r. `(?)` marks
a word of the copy read with doubt; `…` where the copy breaks off.

## f. 143r

[The captain of Castets] having let it be understood that he wished to leave the King of Navarre, with whom I know
he is very discontented; and having considered that I have always heard him held to be the best man of command he
has, and that it would be no small favour to [Navarre's] affairs to keep him, I have treated with him. [I did so]
in consideration of a promise he has made me not to bear arms for his party for six months, and of some particular
service he has promised to do you in that time; and also because in truth he agreed to almost everything we wanted
for the surrender of his place, which was still strong enough to hold us at this siege had they chosen to hold out
obstinately a while longer. That would have cost a great deal of expense, and still more of time, which is very
dear to us, because I would have had to cross the river to get there and then cross back to come here. For all
these considerations, and for the first more than the others, I have verbally promised him

to enjoy his property, or permission to sell it; and moreover to intercede with Your Majesty to have him granted a
pardon for all his past offences. I most humbly beg Your Majesty to confirm this, and to believe that I have
settled none of it except subject to your good pleasure. Yet I would not have gone so far in this treaty had I not
known that it was enough to come near(?) a great gain, with the risk of little loss(?). For the condition of leave
to sell his property is as nothing, since he can make no use of it: almost all his said property, even the said
place of Castets, comes from his wife's side and is entailed on his children. As for the pardon, it cannot be valid
