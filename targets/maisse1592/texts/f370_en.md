# f. 370, Henri IV to Maisse, S. Denis, 3 Nov 1592: the cipher passage, English

Translation of `f370_reading.md` (the figures of f. 370r-v decoded with Tomokiyo's key and aligned with the Brienne 13
fol. 102v-103v clear copy). Modern spelling; no holes in the reading.

## Translation

Cardinal de Gondi will have passed near you, some days ago already; he will have told you my news and the state in
which he left things here, and that he would soon be followed by the marquis de Pisany, who is going to the Pope in the
name of the Catholics of this kingdom who serve me. I believe this will have made you change your decision to leave
and come home, as your last letter showed you meant to do; for you have judged well enough that at this juncture my
affairs over there must not be left without your presence, and I am so sure of your devotion to my service that you
would not want to fail me at the moment when your staying there is most necessary and useful. The continual
difficulties that attend my affairs have not allowed the said marquis to leave as soon as I would have wished: he set
out only ten or twelve days ago, somewhat unwell, which may delay the speed he could and would otherwise have made.
Lambert has gone with him, sent by me to return to the Levant. I am sending you everything he carries, so that you may
see it and add to it or cut it back as you think fit, to give the sieur de Brèves better instructions for the charge I
am giving him; I wanted it to pass through your good judgement, for the experience and knowledge you have of affairs
over there. I only hope that the despatch arrives safely, since I expect that by means of it the matters touching my
service may begin to recover some shape there and be handled with a little more dignity and respect.

Meanwhile I was very glad of the warnings you gave the English ambassador and the said sieur de Brèves, both about the
particular approach your last two letters mention, so that they may watch it and prevent anything being done there to
my prejudice, and to stir up the advances begun against the house of Austria, which I know the King of Spain resents
almost as much, as an obstacle and setback to his designs, as if they were in his own lands. And since it is against
me that his ambition overflows, I have reason to wish and seek that every means by which he could harm me be cut off
from him. I think you will have heard of the war the sieur de Lesdiguières has opened on the side of Piedmont. That is
reason enough to invite our friends to help him, if they wish to see themselves avenged on one who would not spare
them if he had as much means to harm them as he has the will. You will do adroitly whatever good offices you can to
encourage them in this. For the rest, we shall see what the Pope resolves concerning this kingdom on the despatches
sent him from here, after which he will have no more occasion for the dissimulation that, I have been told, he was
forced to practise until some way opened for him to show his goodwill towards the preservation of this Crown.
