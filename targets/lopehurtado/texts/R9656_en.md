# R9656 — Lope Hurtado to Charles V, Rome, 22 November 1522 — English

Translation of `R9656_reading.md`, with the same holes. Passages in cipher in the original are in *italics*.

S. C. Majesty,

The Archbishop of Bari did not write the Pope news *of the coming of the French*, nor to anyone but me; as far as I have
been able to understand, everything was referred to the man of his whom he is to send. The letters were brought by a
courier who came with the [?valante]; *the Pope does not [?want] […] the coming of the French.* I have asked *[?His
Holiness] in […] if they come […] what the Pope will do*, so that I could write his answer to Your Majesty; he has told
me that in everything he can reach of the Pope's will he will not fail Your Majesty, nor will the Secretary in what he
can, and that Your Majesty should have no doubt of it. *[…] has told me* that he has heard the Pope say that if the
French come it will be to their harm. His Beatitude cannot fail Your Majesty; what the Pope answered the Duke in this
matter Your Majesty will see by his letter. Nothing *[?moves] the Pope; they are […] […] for peace; he does not [?believe]
that he has […] of the […] to lose […] the state, if not of no [?more] than money, which is the thing in the world that
most […] [30].* His Holiness says that he is Your Majesty's. When the Archbishop's servant has come, I shall at once
inform Your Majesty *of what he [?brings]*.

*And how [?so] they [?will take] it.* Since I wrote to Your Majesty, they say that Rome is much better, at which the Pope
is very content. The Pope [has given] the galleys to [?Gaspar] Mollete, a Catalan; he died eight days ago. The Duke has
begged him to give them to [?Alcayca] or Figueroa; the Pope answered me that Your Majesty does not know these men, and
that they want them for themselves by importunity. It would be well for Your Majesty to write to His Holiness to give
them to one of them; I believe he will not fill them before the summer.

He has ordered me to write again to Your Majesty *about the matter of [?Ostia]*, and His Holiness has told me to write to
Your Majesty at length. As to Don Juan, let Your Majesty give satisfaction, *for the Pope is very suspicious that he was
ordered from over there to do what he has done.* May Our Lord keep and increase Your Majesty's most royal person and
estate. From Rome, 22 November 1522.
