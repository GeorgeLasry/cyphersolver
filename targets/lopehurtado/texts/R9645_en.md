# R9645 — Lope Hurtado to Charles V, Rome, [November 1522] — English

Translation of `R9645_reading.md`, with the same holes. Passages in cipher in the original are in *italics*.

After the post left, His Holiness told me *that he had reached a settlement with the Duke of Ferrara, and three
conditions of the capitulation […]:* the money *of the fief that Pope Alexander [?remitted] to him at the [?marriage] of
his [?daughter]; the Duke* to be bound to serve the Church with a hundred men-at-arms for half a year when it needs
them; *and that he may not confederate with [?any] [?prince] or [?lordship] except with His Holiness and with Your
Majesty; that he may not harbour in his state disservants […] nor of Your Majesty.* His Holiness [?reminded] us of the
other conditions; he says they are to bring them tomorrow signed *by the said Duke*, and he will send the copy to Your
Majesty. His Holiness gives him no more than what he now holds.

He has told me *that he will [?not do] […] to Your Majesty; that […] what they [?wish], but that the [?camp] does not
[?come]; that he cannot [?do]* what he would like, *and [?they come] […] that the […] of the Duke of […]* comes upon […].

*And […] and that the Duke, with […] men dismissed*, the captain and some men-at-arms, and that Your Majesty's men *[?treat]
their […] badly* in their quarters; and although some come upon *and […]* with plenty of men, they think it is done by
the servants *of Your Majesty […] him […] of […], because he [?will do] what Your Majesty asks.* I have informed the Duke
of Sessa, *and made [?great] […] […] to the Pope; and I have told His Holiness not to think that a servant of Your
Majesty would have* such daring. *His Holiness is [?very] […] that all of the Duke of […].* It seemed to me that Your
Majesty should be told, because this is the second news that has come to the Pope today, and the first I made known
to the Duke; I believe he will have written to Your Majesty, so that you may send orders as best serves you.

Likewise Bernardino de [?Aviño] has written from London of the [date] of last month; *he says that the King of England
had come to terms with the Scots and that they had thrown out the Duke of Albany*, and that he was waiting for the King,
who had gone on a pilgrimage, and that on his return the Cardinal had told him he would dispatch him. By the reckoning
made here, Bernardino will be late. The Pope has been much pleased with the agreement with Scotland, which has been a
knife for the French. Here they comfort themselves much *by saying that the English and Your Majesty's men did nothing*,
and that they left *discontented*.
