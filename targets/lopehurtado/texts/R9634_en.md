S. C. Majesty,

Since I wrote to Your Majesty from here, illness has not left me until now, when I am well, and I leave tomorrow, God
willing, for Rome, where His Holiness is. What I have been told from there I do not write to Your Majesty, because I take
it for certain that Don Juan Manuel will send a post informing Your Majesty of the affairs of His Holiness and of your
service.

Sire, as I have been ill, many kinds of people have come to see me, men of war and others, and the people here, though
they are not, know everything in the world, because they go about in it from birth; and some have told me, sensible and
honourable men, *that because […] are so […] of Your Majesty and of the King of England, wishing to destroy the King of
France, […] […] the war through [?Provence], which is the weakest land he has, and where Your Majesty's army […] can

they will put themselves in pledges for it, if *[…] that by […] Your Majesty put an army, not […] and Lyon and part of
