# R9650: Lope Hurtado de Mendoza (Rome) to Charles V, November 1522: English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of the text in `read_r9650.md`: the clerk's contemporary clear version of

## Translation

Yesterday a post came from the Archbishop of Bari; he writes to me from the [23rd] to the [1st] of this month. He says
that within six days he would send one of his men here, by whom he would give full news; he says he would then send
another to Your Majesty. For this reason I do not send my letter to Your Majesty.

In substance he tells me that **the French are resolved to come into Italy, and are sending a great sum of money to
Lyon**, and that it is necessary to see to the defence, and that the Pope should do what he can, since more is at
stake for him than for anyone.

And I have told His Holiness that this warning has come to me from **Hieronymo Adorno**, from some letters he has
taken out of love for Your Majesty; and, for what he owes, let him not set himself against this; for the service of
God and the remedy of the Church and of his state he ought to consider what he has to do without further delay,
because afterwards there will be no time.

This I told him before he saw the Archbishop's [letter]. He writes to me not to think that another would give
warning; he takes it so lukewarmly that not every good is to be hoped for. In all he has told me that he would look
into it, and would speak to me afterwards. I shall work to learn what the Archbishop writes, and shall see His
Holiness's answer; and I shall then inform Your Majesty, for now it cannot be, because the Duke has written to me that
the post leaves today and that I should not hold it back. I have informed him of this, and **Don Juan Manuel** and the
**Viceroy of Naples**. I believe the Duke will write to Your Majesty his opinion and what he thinks of doing.

I have written to the Archbishop in accordance with what Your Majesty has commanded me, and he tells me, on his
coming, that he wanted to make the [peace] fitting [for] the Pope, if the French come; and the Pope dissimulates so as
not to make himself a party, but to keep his world there. Let Your Majesty see whether it will be better that another
should go than the Archbishop, if he is there; he would always give warning. If he comes here, I hold it certain that
he must have a place near the Pope better than the others, after [?…], and they will press His Holiness …
