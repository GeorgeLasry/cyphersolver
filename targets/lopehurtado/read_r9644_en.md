# R9644 (and its duplicate R9648): Lope Hurtado de Mendoza (Rome) to Charles V, 1 November 1522: English translation

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of the text in `read_r9644.md`: the contemporary clear version of the
despatch (RAH Salazar 9/26 ff. 241-242), written out in lettered sections A, B, C keyed to the cipher; the ciphered
leaves of R9644 and of the duplicate R9648 carry the same text. Same marks: `[x]` letters or a word expanded or
supplied by the transcriber, `…` a break between leaves. Spanish.

## Translation

**[A]** It has been very well that Your Majesty forewarns His Holiness of what is fitting for your service, because,
although he may not do what he is obliged to, he may not excuse himself afterwards by saying that Your Majesty did not
have him forewarned, so that he did not know whether what he was doing was wrong.

Likewise it was well to write at length, because His Holiness had such passion that he did not recognise what Don
Juan had served him, and [?] held his person against me. And if Your Majesty had not written from there, he would have
thought that Don Juan and the **Cardinal de' Medici** had arranged [now] what they were to tell him, if Your Majesty's
letter had found them here.

I have asked His Holiness what he thinks of the Duke, and he told me he was content, which he was not with Don Juan,
as everyone says.

What Your Majesty says is very well, that whatever is to be done with His Holiness's servants he should know of
first; but until Your Majesty gives them what you are pleased to, if anything were said to him he would think it was
so as not to give them anything, and would say he did not want them to take it, and the servants would remain
discontented, as they say they are; because, as **Monsieur de La Chaulx** and I spoke to him of it so long ago, they
have already lost hope.

I have written to Your Majesty of his character, and Monsieur de La Chaulx knows it; and now I send him a memorandum
so that he may make a report to Your Majesty. The **chamberlain Pedro** is the principal one, and more regard is to be
had for him than for any; and he is beyond comparison, and the others good, if they were not corrupted by being given
[gifts]. When Your Majesty decides to grant them the favour, it will be well to tell the Pope …

… that Your Majesty gives them that for the labours they had in Spain, and so that they may take more care to serve
His Holiness; I [think that], as Your Majesty will be pleased, the Pope will approve it, which I believe he would not
do now, as I have said.

Since the Duke left, I have spoken to the Pope of what seemed to me to be Your Majesty's service. His will could not
be better as regards what concerns Your Majesty; as for his deeds, no servant of Your Majesty is satisfied with them.
He gives as excuse for what he leaves undone the obligation he has to procure peace, being pope, and the infidels
being so powerful and his need so great; and [says] that Your Majesty has no [such need] that he should show himself
as he is obliged to; for if Your Highness had it, he would not fail you for anything. And those who take him for
French may see whether he fills his offices, his fortresses and the service of his house with Frenchmen or with
Castilians.

His Holiness, as I think, is waiting for what the Archbishop of Bari will do, and not to spend anything if it could
be helped, for he is so close-fisted that never was man more so. Until the Archbishop comes, I think it will be better
not to press him much.

**[B]** With the Archbishop of **Cosenza** His Holiness deals on some matters, the other being present, for without
him there is nothing; and with him, as regards things here, he is lost, and I think he will be slow to win him back.
The Archbishop is ill content; he says that, were it not that they would not take him in anywhere for leaving here, he
would be gone, because he sees in the Pope some things that do not please him; and since he has no part [in affairs],
he does not want it said that he is [even] ill with His Holiness. And he is a good servant of Your Majesty, and you
ought to order him to serve.

**[C]** I have learned from **Cisterer** that he is a good servant of Your Majesty, and that he can serve, that he has
the cipher; that His Holiness gives signs of peace, and that the **King of France** was asking for a safe-conduct to
send an ambassador to His Holiness, and that the Pope is not of a mind that he should come.

I asked the Pope what news he had from France; he told me that the Archbishop had sent him Your Majesty's letter, and
that he was waiting concerning the matter of **England**; that he greatly desired peace, because of the infidels and
because of Your Majesty's need, since you come out of the war honoured and with advantage. I told His Holiness that
Your Majesty always wanted it, and the **King of England** too, the King of France doing what is just.

I have written to the Archbishop of Bari that he ought to come, since the Pope has left it to his will, and that
meanwhile he should serve him, putting to him some need from there. Let His Holiness come to terms with Your Majesty
and **Venice** and his own as soon as he can. I have written this in accordance with what Your Majesty commanded me.
May Your Majesty have me informed whether it has been well [done], and what I am to write to him from now on; for I
hold him a good servant of Your Majesty, and I think he will not fail in what he is obliged to. I also found a letter
here, although it is old, of the 6th of September: I send it to Your Majesty. Today the Pope is despatching a post
with the answer to the Archbishop for him.

I spoke to His Holiness of what Your Majesty wrote me of **Prince Don Enrique**. He said that of his grandfather he
knows nothing, and that the man has not come back; he deceived His Holiness. It would have been better to have
arrested him, since with his master there can be no good settlement, because of the drawbacks Your Majesty mentions.

Likewise I spoke to His Holiness of those of the council; he answered me that he was not content with the bishop,
and that of the letters they were writing to Your Majesty in Flanders they had shown him the copy in Castile; that he
knew no more, and that he wished all of them well.
