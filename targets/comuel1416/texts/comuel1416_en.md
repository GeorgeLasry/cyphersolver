# Pedro Comuel to Francesc Climent, 27 June 1416: English

Translation of `comuel1416_reading.md` (Puig y Puig 1920, app. CXXXV). The passages Puig deciphered are in _italics_.
Names modernised here only. Holes: none.

## Translation

Most reverend father and most dread lord. With due commendation first made: after Guillem Ortolà left I wrote to Your
Reverend Paternity by Pedro Soriano how our lord had written to you that you, who know the business, should see in
what state things remain at the court of the King of Castile, whether you ought to go back there or come to this
kingdom, since he is content with what Your Paternity decides on it. He warns you of one thing, of which, it seems
from your letters, you are already well warned: that you will have to bear many great dangers for the faith of
Christ and the service of the Pope, perhaps greater and harder than he wrote to you and you to His Holiness.
Therefore put on the armour of God and prepare yourself, as he not only believes but is certain you will, to bear
whatever and whatsoever may come or befall. He says that this is the time in which God wills that those who are good
be made manifest; for he knows that you care little for temporal goods or the honours of the world, would not
willingly trade them for the glory of paradise, nor break your loyalty for any danger that might befall you.
Monsignor repeated these words to me so many times that I can hardly set out His Holiness's mind; and though he knew
that I had written to you, I wished to write again. He also said and ordered me to write what follows: that you
should not strike such terror into yourself that you throw yourself to the ground, since many prelates resisted
strongly in many things and few granted letters on the revenues of the Camera to be raised by commissaries, and those
afterwards had recourse to His Holiness asking absolution, rehabilitations, dispensations and even new provisions of
their dignities, and I was present at at least two of them. He trusts in God that you will act as you have always been
used to act well, will make up the failings of others who act less well, strengthen the weak and timid, and carry off
the crown. Item, because I had not written, he ordered me to write to you _that although Don Bartolomé lost [his]
goods, he is still not fit to govern the church of Zaragoza; he also says that you should let those young men of
Vera and the other return to their studies and not trouble them; item, that you keep Juan Ugueti, who is good,
commended; that you write or send someone so that the Pope is informed of your mind and of the state of affairs,
etc._ And may God direct Your Paternity in your doings, to whom I commend myself. _Don Gil [Sánchez Muñoz] is at
Barcelona for the bishop of Valencia and does wonders, they say, and rouses the prelates who are asleep, and
undeceives them plainly: you see what a man does who dares to speak._ Written 27 June. P., your servant. — To the
reverend father in Christ and my most dread lord, the lord Archbishop of Zaragoza.
