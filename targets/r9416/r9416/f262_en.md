# R9416 f. 262: English (from f262_reading.md, same holes)

## Translation

Gracious lord: after I, on the way, on the way, on the way
to [X] [...], I have, through a [?trusted]
friend under the Count of Schaumburg, [...]
[...] first that [the news] comes up here from Vienna [?warned] that for me
to get through was impossible, so much is it, not only
in Austria but also in Styria, occupied [with troops],
and the day before yesterday a [?messenger] was laid low, so that
the rumour went that I should learn it. Now I cannot, because of
Minckwitz and other dangers, go through Silesia
and [?Poland] either. But since [X] is of considerable
standing and [⊼] in the end intends to
overrun with the Italian and Spanish foot-
soldiers, who are to march through Styria with one [force] and along the
Danube with the other force into Hungary:
therefore my simple view is that Your Princely
Grace should have dispatched Bartlen von [?Eschlkam] there
to give a good report of everything; besides,
I would also write what is needful; for [⊼]
gives out [that] the Turks are up to four thousand strong;
they have set out under cover of his embassy, to
escort [it] through [the] lands(?); they spare neither friend nor
foe. Yet it rests with Your Princely
Grace's pleasure. [...] Gracious lord, since in this
place I cannot [get] through by [?way] and it is otherwise not safe to
stay, I have resolved to make a journey to [...],
to carry further certain dealings begun through Herr [...],
and also to claim my outstanding
pension of seven years. I therefore ask
Your Princely Grace, in part payment of
my salary, to appoint me with fifty gulden at Straubing
as Oberrichter, in the form [...] Your Princely
Grace may think good, for I
otherwise lack the means to live; I also [ask for] a letter of
recommendation from Your Princely Grace or,
where that is not convenient, from his embassy.
To consider my long service, for which neither the innkeepers'
[?hills] nor the times have been able to give me anything,
I humbly commend myself to Your Princely Grace
in all of this.
Dated Tuesday after St James.
