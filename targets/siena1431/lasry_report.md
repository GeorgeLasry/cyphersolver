Ciphertext
Both letters open with a clear Latin salutation and continue in cipher to the end: no. 6 has 15 lines (about
1,000 ciphertext elements), no. 24 38 lines (about 2,560), some 3,570 elements in all.
Key
36 letter signs, 7 nulls and 18 word signs in the key. Homophonic substitution with nulls and a nomenclature:
four signs for each of a, e, i, o and u/v and one for every other letter (h, k and x included), seven nulls, and
eighteen word signs for persons, peoples and places (five of them used here: Lucca, Piccinino, Duca di
Milano, Viniziani, Fiorentini).

No. 24 (R4812)
From: unsigned; the same hand and cipher as no. 6
To: the Signori and Capitano del Popolo of Siena (address on the dorse)
Place: not named ('qua a Lucca')
Date: undated — about 1431–1432: the King of Hungary (Sigismund, in Italy 1431–1433), the Council of

No. 6 (19 February) is written from the Milanese side of the war over Lucca. The lords of la Mirandola have
written back to Lucca. A man has been with the Venetians and settled his accounts, with three thousand
ducats still owed to him; someone close to Count Luigi is believed to have taken service with the Venetians.
Men-at-arms are on offer for Siena. On the 14th Piccinino began to send four hundred men and is opening
the road to Lucca; the companies are being called up by trumpet and will leave within the month if the
weather improves. News also comes through Milan: the chancellor of Antonio Petrucci has returned from
there, and the chancellor at Milan and Piccinino are both writing to Siena. The postscript reports that
someone will not come to terms with the Duke of Milan because he asks too much.
No. 24 is an unsigned report from the same war, probably a little later. The writer is ready to help Lucca,
Piccinino appears to be on the move, and troops are being made ready. Ambassadors of Camerino are about
to leave, Lucchese ambassadors are present, and the Florentines are mentioned. The towns of the country
have been ordered to bring provisions. Lucca is reported confident that the king (Sigismund) will come to
terms, and that no relief will be needed. The marquis of Mantua has come to terms with the Duke of Milan,
and so has Taliano (probably the condottiere Taliano Furlano). The letter also names Duke Albert (of
Austria?), the King of Hungary and the Council of Basel, and reports that the Emperor will not come. A
company has stopped ninety miles away, and the writer will report what follows.
Historical context and dating
The letters belong to the war of Florence against Lucca (1429–1433), in which Siena and the duke of Milan
supported Lucca and Milan's captain Niccolò Piccinino fought for it. Antonio di Cecco Rosso Petrucci (1400–
1471), named in both letters, won the duke to the Lucca enterprise in 1430 and in 1431 was capitano del
popolo of Siena, then 'commissario generale' of the duke [DBI, s.v. Petrucci, Antonio]. No. 6 speaks of
Piccinino's men and the road to Lucca; no. 24 of helping Lucca, of the Florentines, of the King of Hungary
(Sigismund, in Italy 1431–1433), of the Council of Basel (opened 1431) and of the marquis of Mantua (a title
Sigismund created in 1432–1433): so about 1431–1432, and no. 6, dated 19 February, is probably February
1431 or 1432 (in Sienese style, with the year beginning on 25 March, written 1430 or 1431). Meister prints
three Sienese keys of the 1430s from the same office, with the same repertoire of signs and other values
[Meister, Die Anfänge der modernen diplomatischen Geheimschrift, 1902, pp. 50–53; cf. Somogyi, Verbum
2016/1–2, pp. 195–217].
