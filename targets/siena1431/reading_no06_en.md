# Concistoro 2308 no. 6 (R4795), Cristofano to the Signori of Siena, 19 February [c. 1431-38]: English translation

Magnificent and powerful lords, my most singular lords, after humble commendation. ‹b·› [I] wrote by Biageo, as much as
was needed. Pensolare ‹teaite› the news to you ‹iao›, how the lords of Mirandola have also written to Lucca that, through
‹kanaa·not›, chancellor, and of Petrolino da ‹rver›mo, he has been with the Venetians and settled his accounts; and
there remains [for him] to have three thousand ducats, of which they give half at present, and they return to him all
his things; and, given [that], she hoped ‹aa› of those of Count Luigi; and he believes for certain that he is settled
with the Venetians, for which reason one must take good heed; [if] you have need of men-at-arms, some valiant men would
be found here. And on the fourteenth of this month Piccinino began to send four hundred to make and open the road to go
to Lucca; and he has sent the trumpeters round the companies, that they be here within this week; and if the weather is
ever good again, they will leave within this whole month. As for which, the chancellor of messer Antonio Petrucci came
back from Milan without having done anything ‹lado› and Bartalomeo, and the reason why I was with Piccinino, and he
wrote anew; in the way that was asked, yesterday he sends. Now the chancellor is at Milan, and Piccinino writes to you
there about the affairs of Vincenzo da Camerino; it is laid on me, now command it. And given on the nineteenth day of
February. Cristofano de Mont‹vccia› ‹·› commends himself. And the ea will not come to terms with the Duke of Milan,
because he wants too many things.
