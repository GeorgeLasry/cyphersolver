# Spagna 423/11 (DECODE R188), Rome, 22 December 1736. English translation of 423-11_reading.md

With particular kindness His Holiness has praised the exactness with which Your Lordship's letter of the 19th of last
month is framed.
On the point of the claimed right of patronage, orders have been given to draw up the Briefs addressed to the persons
you suggest.
Although one would like to suppose that Mons. the Inquisitor, above all after the news mentioned at the end of your said
ciphers, will not fail in his proper duty, nonetheless the examination of the books in question is being hastened, so
that the judgement formed of them here may serve him as a spur to fulfil his obligation with more courage.
The blameworthy, irregular conduct of Mons. the Governor of the Council calls for special consideration, and the
question whether it is fitting and he ought to be held to account for it will not be left unexamined, since one must
foresee and weigh the circumstances and consequences of a step of such a kind.
I conveyed by the last post to Your Lordship His Holiness's views, should there ever be a thought of naming him
Cardinal, and that he, in view of so many bonds that bind him to the defence of the Holy See and of ecclesiastical
jurisdiction, not only fails to answer fully to his proper duty but even shows himself its enemy.
I must now add that, even should the case arise, with the necessary number of hats, that His Holiness could think of a
promotion of the crowns' candidates while the present rupture with Spain lasts, he would either suspend it altogether
or would not make the Spanish cardinal, since he would fear meeting universal disapproval by creating a cardinal on the
nomination of a Court from which he is at present receiving so many insults and injuries, without having given it the
least, not even an apparent, pretext: something that will perhaps find no example in history.
This reflection, handled well by Your Lordship with the necessary delicacy as a particular doubt of your own, but a
well-founded one, with whoever may hope to be the one named by His Majesty, ought to engage him to exert himself with
all his efficacy and credit so that the many just reasons His Holiness has to be embittered may cease.
His Holiness also wishes to be exactly informed how the Secretary of the royal patronage, being a layman, bears the
name of Abbot, and who holds the said hereditary abbey of his house.
Lastly, should the outrages of that Court go on, since His Holiness may find himself obliged to suspend the many graces
of the Cruzada, millones and excusado, he wishes to hear confidentially from Your Lordship what impression and effect
such a resolution could produce in those kingdoms.
