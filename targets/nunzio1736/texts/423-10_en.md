The very grave bitterness His Holiness felt on hearing the content of the decree that has been issued has grown further
on learning from Your Lordship's ciphers of the 12th of last month that one of the two [?reasons] that prompted its
publication arose from the representations that [?benefices] of Spain were being provided in the Dataria after the
all-too-well-known interruption of commerce.
The origin of this new grief of mine lies in seeing with what haste they rush upon false reports to resolutions so unreasonable
and of such consequence.
I should think that by now they would be convinced there how distorted and false the accounts of the riots and of
the Ostia affair have been, especially as to the substantial and principal circumstances of both, [?since] [..] from
such insincere reports all the [?current] irregularities and outrages against the Courts of Spain and of Naples take
their fatal beginning.
On a supposition of fact evidently false, that pensions in the Kingdom of Naples were being assigned through
figureheads, one of the most unjust cedulas issued against the pensions of the Court of Naples was likewise founded;
and now, on Your Lordship's report, on the many other false representations that [?benefices] of Spain are dispatched in
the Dataria, they rush to the publication of a decree of so weighty a nature.
His Holiness wishes you to employ all your skill and efficacy to set this palpable truth in its full light: that the
Court has been taken by surprise and deceived on so many points [58] of fact, about which it ought itself to have become
clear, and consequently that, after such a doubled experience, so grave outrages should be remedied and more caution
used in giving credit to whoever, from malice or from too much haste, without examining and [?verifying] the facts, comes
forward with groundless representations and reports.
Note, however, that all this is pointed out only to make known the material error and deceit of the false assumptions;
while for the rest His Holiness intends to be at full liberty to exercise his right in all the [?cases] in which it
shall seem fitting to him to exercise it, there being no one who can prevent him; nor, by now observing these false
reports, does he mean to tie his hands or to refrain from acting with all [..] liberty, granting any grace as was
done in the times of other such ruptures.
Mons. Molina is certainly not taking the right road to earn and obtain the hat.
Even if His Majesty were to nominate him, and if well-founded reports then came that he had been to the prejudice of
the Holy See, it would not be the first time that the Holy See had refused to accept a nomination, signifying to the princes
to change it.
