Two letters written by Your Lordship, dated the [1960] and [1985] of last month, were received [?this] week. The news
contained in those [letters] has greatly increased the bitterness in my mind, above all on seeing that the one who, by his
character and profession, would be more obliged than the others to enlighten Their Majesties [1335] has so large a part
in the deception they are under.
With his eyes turned to the Most High and his first trust placed in Him alone, His Holiness is seriously considering
the course he must hold in circumstances so thorny, which touch so directly not only his dignity but his pastoral zeal.
Meanwhile let Your Lordship try every road to enlighten that Court; the skilful bearing you keep in showing some trust
in Mons. Molina himself is approved, [1200] he may do, if possible, less harm to the [?business].
His Holiness will take great care not to rest the affairs of the Holy See on him, to which he shows himself in so many
ways so hostile; it is moreover quite new that he should have conceived the idea that His Holiness could ever make use
of a serving minister of a Court to treat the interests of the Holy See with that same Court.
