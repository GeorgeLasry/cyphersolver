Savary de Brèves:
a decipherment revisited

A two-image diplomatic document and a closer reading of the short cipher in Marie de Médicis’s letter of 10 November 1610.

George Lasry deciphered the additional document in 2022 using CTTS (CrypTool Transcriber and Solver). The transcription, key and decryption were improved with Opus 5.5 in October 2026. Codex prepared this edition, translated the visible text, compared the scans and checked the mechanical key application.

Brice Dudout, working with Claude and Codex, deciphered the Marie de Médicis passages in October 2026; Satoshi Tomokiyo contributed key identifications and checks. Daniel Bourdeau published and checked that work in the breves1610 entry on 20 October. This is a focused improvement to that reading, informed by Lasry’s two-image document, not a claim to a new first solution of the November letter.

A focused contribution

A repeat sign explains the second “s”

The longer document shows that the sign labelled s- in CTTS repeats the preceding plaintext element. In the November passage, S + repeat gives the second s of recognoissant. This resolves one of the uncertainties recorded in Bourdeau’s check of Dudout’s reading.

The revised November reading:

« que Sa Sainteté, recognoissant leur arogance et sentant sa timidité »

“that His Holiness, recognizing their arrogance and sensing his own timidity”

Dudout’s arogance is retained. The C/B discrepancy in the archive alphabet remains a separate question. This is an improved reading with additional evidence, not a new first decipherment.

Below, the French edition and English translation cover only what is visible in the supplied images. Brackets mark uncertain readings, missing text and editorial restorations. The two longer images belong to one document; its exact date and shelfmark remain unverified.

  • La Hale, Le Cormier, La Cheville, Pindare, Gris de Lin, Saint Paul, Saint Félix, Le Bateur and L’Assembleur are retained as written names or apparent aliases; no historical identities are asserted.

  • Word division, accents and punctuation are editorial. Icy normalizes the literal idy; alliance / contractée / entendre normalize imperfect cipher spelling or transcription. The visible damage after a- is supplied as [dou] in a[dou]cir.

Reading notes
  • Dudout’s preferred word arogance is retained (modern French arrogance); the superseded CTTS croyance is not the reading in this edition. The current CTTS labels are 3 d- j v x f a, → A R O G A N CE.
  • The two cipher lines read literally: QUE SA SAINTETE RE C O G N O I / S [repeat S] A N T LEUR A R O G A N CE ET S EN T A N T S A T I M I D I T E. Punctuation and word boundaries are editorial.
  • The cross-document improvement is the repeat sign: the second s of recognoissant is an operator, supported by the longer document’s l’alarme, intelligence, aage, puisnée, necessaire and brouillons. It need not occupy an S cell in the archive alphabet.
  • The C assigned to CTTS b in recognoissant is retained from Lasry’s supplied key. That label occurs only in this excerpt; the two longer images do not independently settle Bourdeau’s B/C key-table conflict.
  • His Holiness renders bare 4, whose archive-key wording is “Le pape Sa Sainteté”. LEUR is the supplied value of 41̄; Dudout printed leurs. The cleartext continuation is read ne les pressera, rather than the supplied edit ne leur pressera.
  • The first two cleartext lines remain partly uncertain. They are marked rather than reconstructed from unseen text.
