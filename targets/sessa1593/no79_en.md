# fr. 3983 no. 79, f. 162: the Duque de Sessa to Diego de Ibarra, Rome, 11 March 1593: English translation

… to see the slanders they raise against everyone, I believe it will have been to the purpose to show the […] to His
Holiness [4̈9] so as to [dir] inform the legate particularly of his intention. I am sorry for the lord Duke [pal] of
Feria […] […] […] […] who have to deal with persons so ambitious […] […] constant; although we who live in Rome do not
wish to have experience of the one or of the other; and if for a notice that does not last four days nor passes to the
successors such extraordinary [mal^] are done, I do not wonder that […] difficulties are offered in the election of a
lord. With all that, it is a business so […] important to Christendom […] that I hope He will [?favour] it [mil] in such
a way that […] […] […] is fitting; that among the princes who follow the [xom] today [there are] various pretensions,
and little [vu], and each one claims for [xor] himself, I am not astonished [as]; I cannot help doing it, at least that
there should not be ministers in agreement and united in the […]sion of those of the blood, and undeceived that they
cannot trust any of them. To the purpose, it is worth seeing in particular how embarked the Duke has remained through
the letter of [Des]portes. I said yesterday to my purpose that I took it to him, that it is [rili] of the [xom] he had
shown him some letters of the Duke in which he wrote to him that so [ʃo] he owed gratitude to the [mos] [86T] of
Desportes; still it had grieved him that he had dealt with their persons with His Holiness, because he knew [mil] that
he had no ambition at all, but that his aim [ʃer] was to wish that the election of a King be made, whichever, although
for the preservation of religion […] […] […] that [has a] kingdom and [is] powerful to defend it; and that [h̊o] he should
assure His Holiness of it on his behalf; and [ho] told the firmness in which they stood, not to give […] […] […] to the
peace that he proposed […] […] […] […] that because [they are] matters in which it was fitting that His Holiness should
open his eyes wide and that they should not deceive him …
