# R9963: Count of Cifuentes, Rome, to Charles V, 3 September 1534 — English

Translation of `R9963_reading.md` (same holes). Rests on the letter's clear text and the contemporary decipherment on f. 54r.

## Translation

Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty.

On the first of this month I wrote to Your Majesty and gave you notice of His Holiness's illness and of the other matters at hand. His Holiness's improvement has not gone forward, because he cannot eat anything of substance except washed bread and some sops in wine; now he drinks water with sugar. The continuous fever is more evident than until now; it rises at night, though not as much as it used to. The physicians have little hope for his life.

These Italians are such negotiators that, with His Holiness as he is, unable to attend to any business of his own or anyone else's, the Duke of Ferrara sent his ambassador to tell me that, since he now has some improvement, he asked me to speak to His Holiness so that he would decide whether or not he would extend the term of [?the] eighteen months. And in this connection he told me that a month ago the French ambassador who resides here told him that whenever he wished he would work to settle the said duke with His Holiness, and he says he gave no sign; and afterwards, as he was leaving, he told him again that he would settle it, giving to understand that he had some word from His Holiness; but that he was telling me what had happened, not because the duke wants it to be handled by any other means than Your Majesty's. To this I answered in general words, telling him of Your Majesty's goodwill towards the said duke and the efforts you had ordered me to make in this matter, and that he could well see that no business was being handled, His Holiness being in the state he was in.

The said ambassador also told me that the French ambassador had told him that the Landgrave and the Duke of Saxony were coming to [?meet] the King of France to work out a means of agreement with Your Majesty. Here the contrary is said: that the Landgrave was raising troops through the Count of Nassau. A person who deals with His Holiness's affairs of state told me [?that] Your Majesty was engaged in no agreement with France, because Nassau was going to Flanders and would pass through the court of the King of France; and it is said that the said […] was going home because he was not in good health in Spain, and that his road lay through the French court. And the rest as Your Majesty commands me.

After writing the above I received Your Majesty's letter of the 13th of last month, to which there is nothing to answer, praying our Lord to keep and increase the life and imperial estate of Your Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty, as your good servants and retainers desire. Rome, 3 September 1534.

Kisses the royal feet and hands of Your Sacred Caesarean Catholic Majesty, [?the Count of Cifuentes].
