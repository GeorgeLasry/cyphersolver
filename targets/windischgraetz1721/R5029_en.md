## Translation

Brussels, 18 November 1721.

High-born Count.

From Your Excellency's letter of 1 November, safely received, for which I most humbly give thanks, I gather among other things that Eugene is not at all [?well-disposed] towards me, because the Emperor himself advised [?him] against making an excuse.

If I am supported from there even a little, the advice is very good, but otherwise not; [205] is probably also holding [?with] the Prince under hand.

For he writes to me in that way, quite absurdly, when he was told that we had visited each other, and I [?answer] him, as the enclosed copy shows, which I send only in true, most dutiful brotherly confidence; but since [205] will surely mention it to no one, I most humbly ask you likewise to say nothing of it to anyone, not even to Althann, unless it should come to my justification.

In this way I make enemies through no fault of mine, but blessed are they who suffer for justice's sake.

I believe that with [205] it is "one hand washes the other"; of this and the rest there would be strange things to say, but I will not play the prosecutor.

On the matter of the [Ostend] Company it is almost unbelievable how not only the [151] but everyone else loses the love for Eugene, even the Emperor, [?more] every day, seeing that after the courier at last, after so long, arrived [?with] the Prince's plan in [?Vienna], Eugene has [?not] let it [?be opened] for over two months now, so that the time passes and the whole business gets stuck for these reasons, while the Emperor looks on quite calmly.

In this way I could hardly keep the people patient any longer.

The English and also the [?French] want to go home, and might then [?spread] little to the credit of our [?court] and [204] everywhere.

[102] please, once more, for love of the fatherland and the public good, to make the last attempt.

If nothing comes of it, I can still less sustain the business: thy destruction is of thyself, O Israel.

I shall succumb, the Prince triumph, but the Emperor will [168] lose the Company and the [181] [206] were, as the outcome will soon show.

My services, however, which Your Excellency could in any case lay before the highest place as your enlightened judgement sees fit, will always remain true marks of my most dutiful loyalty and zeal; if they are not recognised, virtue is still to be praised for its own sake.

The Congress should now open soon, because the [?Spanish] courier come back from Madrid has brought Pozobueno in London the order, at the exchange of the respective renunciations concerning the titles, to set up exactly such a reserving article as the Count of Windischgrätz (those are the very words) made with the Marquis Beretti Landi at The Hague at the time of the [?Spanish] accession; which has much consoled me, and I now see nothing more that could hinder the opening of the Congress.

God grant that Miosch's promise has a good effect; otherwise Wertheimer will not hold on any longer.

That you yourself now advise me [?not] to think of a [?Breuner] succession is all the more welcome to me, as I have made it a point of honour never to accept it, chiefly, among many other reasons, so that it should [?not] be said: the Prince has [206] me in order to [?have] his services, which would be shameful, and what was done, done only for [?Her] Emperor [?have].

But I beg Your Excellency to be so gracious as to tell me His Imperial Majesty's true disposition towards my humble self, so that I may know with confidence what to hope for, how I am to recover from my great expenses, and whether I might see myself supported against the very considerable enmities raised against me.

This will spur me to undertake the heavy business of the Congress all the more zealously.

Hope sustains, encourages and increases.

I remain with all consideration, h. C.
