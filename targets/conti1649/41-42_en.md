# Conti's letters, BnF fr. 3854 nos. 41-42 (Paris, 26 and 27 March 1649): English translation

## No. 41, f. 113r-v, Paris, 26 March 1649

So that Monsieur [Le] Monsieur de Noirmoutier may know the state of things positively, as they are going at Saint-
Germain, he will be informed that in the conference they are insisting strongly on the interests of the Parlement of
Normandy, with which the Parlement of Paris is so closely joined. That yesterday the First President and President de
Mesmes declared that if the Parlement of Normandy were not satisfied, they would break off. The interests of the said
Parlement are great, which makes one believe in a rupture.

That they will also insist strongly on the exclusion of the Cardinal, the deputies of the generals having had express
orders to do so. That they also demand positively, and without any possibility of yielding on it, that Monsieur de
Longueville shall negotiate with Monsieur de Tenerande [person unidentified].

That on these articles a rupture is expected, of which one would speak more positively if His Highness did not act with
some dependence. That the Parlement has indeed granted the truce, but with positive orders not to continue the
conference after the four days have expired, which is the time the prolongation is to last, and that the deputies will
come back; and since within that time things cannot probably be settled, this gives grounds to believe that the
conference will break off.

That people were much surprised at the withdrawal of the Archduke from Pont-à-Vert and the abandonment of the river
crossings, Monsieur the Marshal du Plessis having only middling forces, and Erlach advancing with so few men. That this
withdrawal has turned out very badly and done very great harm to affairs; but that, there being no danger in retaking a
crossing on the river, this would give warmth to affairs again and put everything back in good order, which is
absolutely expected of His Imperial Highness.

That on Sunday or Monday we shall not fail to give you word of the final resolution of things, which we hope will be such
as can be wished, there being little light on an accommodation; but that, in case one were made, it will not be without
making every effort to draw from it assurances of the general peace.

The provinces are in this state: Monsieur de Longueville has ten [or six] thousand men. That Toulouse has issued the
decree. That Bordeaux is going to issue it. That Provence is in arms, with Poitou, Périgord, Quercy, Limousin, and many
places in Brittany, and the people [?] better disposed than ever.

## No. 42, f. 115r, Paris, 27 March 1649

Since the letter I wrote you yesterday, I thought I should inform you that today a decree was issued in the Parlement
ordering its deputies to join those of His Highness the Prince of Conti in insisting on demanding the removal of
Cardinal Mazarin. After that no one doubts the rupture any more. You know well enough what measures are to be taken on
that. Conclude nothing, however, until you have the last word; but if it comes, put yourself in a position to facilitate
what follows from it. Armand de Bourbon.
