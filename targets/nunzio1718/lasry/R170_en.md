# R170, the Secretariat of State to the nuncio in Madrid, Rome, 9 June 1718 (Spagna 364D/17, ff. 322r-324v): English translation

## Translation

Following what I wrote to Your Most Illustrious Lordship under the 31st of last month by the extraordinary courier sent
to you there by Cardinal Acquaviva, I send you by this express the Brief suspending the well-known Indults granted at
other times, and then renewed from time to time by this Holy See to the Kings of Spain. The mere reading of the Brief
itself, besides all the very strong reasons [?so] amply set out to you both in my despatch of the 2nd of April last and
in the last one of the 31st of May, will serve as sufficient instruction for Your Most Illustrious Lordship, so as to
understand well that His Holiness could not excuse himself from coming promptly to such a step, and will put you in a
position to justify it also to others. I therefore think it superfluous to enlarge further on this, because I could
only repeat the same things to you.

Only I cannot refrain from begging you to take up again my despatches from the beginning of August last year, to observe
that, although His Holiness carries out the suspension of the said indults only now, it is not that he had not
determined on it and believed it necessary ever since the certain news reached him of the horrid failure done to him by
Spain in sending to Sardinia, and consequently indirectly in favour of the Infidels, those naval forces which had been
promised and destined against them; so as by this step to put beyond all calumny the honour of his sacred person, torn
in so many ways, as all Europe knows, and to fulfil those parts which the Apostolic ministry required of him in so grave
a danger to Christendom. We never expressed it openly, however, in the confidence that that Court would at last give
some reparation for the most grave offence done to the Pope; since this can no longer be hoped for, as the same designs
are seen being pursued there, no less harmful to Christendom for the hindrance they bring to the continuation of the war
against the Turk, His Holiness has not believed he could defer any longer the use of the most vigorous remedies, by
suspending for Spain at least those graces which were granted her by the Holy See solely with a view to promoting the
war against the Infidels effectively; which are besides so strictly bound to this precise condition that the Catholic
Kings themselves, being well aware of it, have several times humbly had recourse to the Supreme Pontiffs to be absolved
from the guilt they believed they had incurred by having turned the sums received from them not only to a contrary use,
but even to a use merely different from that prescribed in the Indults themselves, as is clearly gathered from several
Briefs of Clement VIII and Innocent X, of which I send you the copies annexed.

[...] the Brief, then, on parchment, which is sent you for every need in three copies, printed from the same; and as
many others likewise printed are sent as there are archbishops and bishops both of Spain and of the Indies, care having
been taken to enclose them in the added packets addressed to the [?said] prelates, so that Your Most Illustrious
Lordship, without this trouble, may deliver them to them more quickly and secretly, beginning with those addressed to
the bishops most remote from the Court, and sending the others for the Indies to those ports from which the departure of
the usual ships for those parts is to take place soonest. For the rest, since the prompt execution of this order
matters supremely to His Holiness, so that its publication may follow in time to be able to produce some fruit, such as
making Spain desist from further prejudice to the public cause of Christendom, His Holiness leaves you no discretion in
it to interpret or suspend the Pontifical mind; nay, His Holiness wishes that you, carrying it out without delay from
whatever place you may be in, send the courier back to us immediately and at once with the news of having fully carried
out this most urgent commission.
