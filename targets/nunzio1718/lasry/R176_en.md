# R176, the Secretariat of State to the nuncio in Madrid, Rome, 19 July 1718, second copy (Spagna 364D/23, ff. 358r-360r): English translation

## Translation

Before the arrival of the express which Your Most Illustrious Lordship sent back to me, Cardinal Acquaviva, following a
courier from that Court that had reached him earlier, had told some friend of his of the complaints being made there
because the Pope had ordered Your Most Illustrious Lordship to close the Nunciature without even giving the King account
of it; and moreover that, His Majesty's piety [?having] tried to persuade you to suspend it, it had not succeeded; and
that therefore, Your Most Illustrious Lordship being stripped of your character by His Holiness, it had not seemed to
befit the King's dignity to consent that you remain any longer in his kingdoms. His Holiness learned at once what the
said Cardinal was saying; and because the orders given to Your Most Illustrious Lordship being entirely different from
and opposed to what His Eminence asserted, such an event was never to be imagined, he saw to it that the Cardinal
learned at once that no such orders had ever been given to you. From this gather what and how great was Our Lord's
astonishment on reading your despatch brought to me with the return of our said express, in which Your Most Illustrious
Lordship confirms to us what Cardinal Acquaviva had asserted. I assure you that Our Lord cannot yet bring himself to
believe that you proceeded to a step that has no precedent, and to which the Holy See has always been so averse that it
has never interrupted, let alone abandoned, the use of that authority which it freely exercises in the Catholic
kingdoms through its ministers; nor have the Nunciatures ever been closed except by sheer force and violence.

A[lso] the [4416] Cardinal Acquaviva has not so far shown that he has any order to discuss the present pending matters;
if he does, we shall se[e].
