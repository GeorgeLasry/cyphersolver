# R154, the Secretariat of State to the nuncio in Madrid, Rome, 11 January 1718 (Spagna 364D/1)

## Translation

Our Lord's troubles grow on hearing that the horrid calumny is gaining ever more strength, that the Vicar of Christ was
an accomplice in the baneful wrong done by the King of Spain to Christendom and to His Holiness, and on seeing its most
pernicious effects. The expulsion of the nuncio from Naples and the total abolition of every vestige of Apostolic
authority in that kingdom has been followed by the nuncio in Vienna being forbidden access to the court and all dealings
with those ministers, with much more threatened if the Pope does not refute the said calumny with deeds. From this
little, Your Most Illustrious Lordship may gather the state in which His Holiness finds himself.

It was hoped that the piety of the King there, recognising the justice of the complaints and the fairness of His
Holiness's suggestions, would resolve to restore Sardinia and to put things back as they were; but His Majesty has not
even deigned to answer the Brief of 25 August in which His Holiness made most pressing requests to him for it. On the
contrary, one hears from every side that nothing else is thought of there but massing troops, building ships and
preparing to continue the war, of which the letters from Barcelona, Alicante and Cadiz are informing the whole world.

Among so many misfortunes it is most painful to His Holiness that Your Most Illustrious Lordship does not write a word
about so many preparations, which, having alarmed all the other ministers, cannot possibly be unknown to you alone; and
that instead of hearing from you some fruit of the Pope's fatherly remonstrances for the restitution of Sardinia, one
hears from others that every measure is being taken there for new conquests. Such a thought wounds Our Lord's heart,
all the more because your silence extends also to all the other more important matters of your ministry.

Nothing has been learned so far as to whether the Concordat, in the form set out in the Brief of ratification, has been
confirmed by the King. Nothing as to whether His Majesty has been absolved from the censures incurred, by virtue of the
faculties given for it to Father Daubenton. Nothing as to whether, and how, he has made the agreed composition with the
royal ministers over the spolia and the revenues of vacant sees unduly collected. Nothing as to whether the new Agent of
the Churches has been elected as usual, and when he is to come and reside at this court. Nothing, finally, about the
enormous violation of ecclesiastical immunity that occurred in the incidents already reported at Valencia and Toledo.
The Pope is in substance reduced to learning only from the public gazettes, or from private persons, news that so
closely concerns the ministry of the Apostolic Nuncio.

What reason you can offer in your defence I do not know. I know well that such a silence keeps His Holiness in
distress, and that, being in the dark about the necessary news, he does not know how to regulate his conduct in such
grave circumstances.

Two things chiefly give currency to the shameful rumours spread against the Pontiff's good faith. The first, that the
Brief written to the Catholic King on 25 August has not been presented to the King. The second, that the Briefs
concerning the two well-known subsidies on the ecclesiastical property of the Indies and of Spain are, at least
indirectly, being put into effect.

As to the first: why have you not forcefully represented, and had represented, to His Majesty the duty he was under,
and is more and more under, to reply to the said Brief, particularly in the present circumstances, in which not doing so
deprives the Pope of a most powerful weapon for confounding so many slanderers and defending his honour, so grievously
outraged?

As to the second, it is incomprehensible that you cannot obtain the return of the said two Briefs to our hands, since,
being unenforceable by their nature, they do not change it by remaining in the King's hands, nor do they become in any
way enforceable; indeed, if His Majesty wished to make any use of them, His Holiness could not in conscience refrain
from expressly revoking them; and even if the Pope wished at another time to favour His Majesty with similar subsidies,
new Briefs would have to be issued. How then can this reasonably be denied to the Pope? When the Briefs being in the
King's hands does him no good, while their not being in ours does us essential harm, because we lack an incontestable
document with which to show the world that these Briefs are in reality not being executed. If at least you had sent us
authentic attestations of this from the bishops and chapters of Spain, or at least from the Archbishop and Chapter of
the metropolitan church of Toledo, we would have some means of proving the truth of a fact otherwise so disputed; but
these too are lacking.

What then must Your Most Illustrious Lordship do? You must weigh well the unhappy state to which Our Lord is reduced,
and be persuaded that if your efforts do not succeed in obtaining there the means to free him from it, His Holiness
will not be able to avoid resorting to those he judges necessary to erase from men's minds the shameful suspicions
conceived against the Vicar of Christ.
