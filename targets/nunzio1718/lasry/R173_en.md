# R173, the Secretariat of State to the nuncio in Madrid, Rome, 19 July 1718 (Spagna 364D/20)

## Translation

Before the arrival of the express that Your Most Illustrious Lordship sent back to us, Cardinal Acquaviva, following a
courier from [?that] court that had reached him earlier, had told some friend of his of the complaints being made there
because the Pope had ordered Your Most Illustrious Lordship to close the nunciature without even informing the King;
and that, although His Majesty's piety had tried to persuade you to suspend it, it had not succeeded, and that
therefore, Your Most Illustrious Lordship having been stripped of your character by His Holiness, the King had not
thought it proper to consent to your remaining any longer in his kingdoms.

His Holiness learned at once what the said Cardinal was saying; and because the orders given to Your Most Illustrious
Lordship were entirely different from, and opposed to, what His Eminence asserted, and he could never have imagined
such an outcome, he saw to it that the Cardinal learned at once that no such orders had ever been given to Your Most
Illustrious Lordship. You may therefore judge what and how great His Holiness's astonishment was on reading your
despatch, brought to us on the return of our said express, in which Your Most Illustrious Lordship confirms what
Cardinal Acquaviva had asserted.

I assure you that His Holiness cannot yet bring himself to believe that you have proceeded to a step that has no
precedent, and which the Holy See has always so resisted that it has never suspended, let alone abandoned, the exercise
of the authority it freely exercises in the Catholic kingdoms through its ministers; nor have the nunciatures ever been
closed except by sheer force and violence.
