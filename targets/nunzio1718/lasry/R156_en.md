# R156, the Secretariat of State to the nuncio in Madrid, Rome, 8 February 1718 (Spagna 364D/3, ff. 47r-51r): English translation

## Translation

I wrote to Your Most Illustrious Lordship on the 25th of last month the reasons which obliged Our Lord to suspend for
Cardinal Alberoni the grace of the translation to the Church of Seville until His Holiness should be fully satisfied
on them. Now I must inform you that Cardinal Acquaviva, to whom Our Lord took care to make known the indispensable
necessity of such a suspension, not only through me but also by himself, not showing himself satisfied with it,
claimed to move Our Lord from it by setting out his reasons, compiled in the writing of which a copy is sent to you
for no other purpose than to show there their lack of substance and the strangeness of the maxims with which His
Eminence upholds the alleged rights of the King. I think it superfluous to point them out to Your Most Illustrious
Lordship, because the mere reading of these sheets will suffice to make you know the poison they contain, and in
particular where it is impudently asserted that the provision of the bishopric of Vich, in the person of him who by
Apostolic authority is the true and only Bishop of that Church, is void and destitute of any lawful title, and who
has been recognised as such for six years and more, during which he has enjoyed peaceful possession of it without any
disturbance or contradiction from the lay power; since no Catholic can say, except by manifest error, that the
institution of bishops depends, or can depend, on any authority other than that given by Christ to His Church.

As Your Most Illustrious Lordship may well believe, the aforesaid writing made no impression on Our Lord's mind,
capable only of embittering him and turning him away from granting what in substance is nothing but a pure and simple
grace. But the matter has not stopped there; for Cardinal Acquaviva, having flattered himself without any foundation
that Our Lord was to put off the Consistory until the return of the answers from there, on hearing that His Holiness
could not delay it, because the Church of Nanking was to be proposed in it, since otherwise the Bulls could not reach
Portugal before the ships bound for China sailed from Lisbon, has added to the strange propositions put forward in the
said sheet various threats of ruptures and protests; as if the right the Catholic Kings have to present persons for
the Churches of Spain extended to translations from one Church to another, and as if the Pope could not, without
injury to the royal prerogatives, defer carrying it out for one or even more Consistories, as has been the custom in
every Catholic country and with all princes alike, not only in translations, where there is neither nomination nor
presentation, but also in the simple provisions of Churches, where both are admitted. The Pope, though he could do so,
does not refuse to make the said translation, but defers it until he is reassured about the scandalous innovations
that occurred after the intention of it had already been given to Cardinal Acquaviva, and is consequently in a
position to make it without burdening his own conscience and without setting a pernicious example.

Whether this most moderate conduct deserves to be answered with sentiments such as are expressed in the aforesaid
writing, and with the public declarations for which Cardinal Acquaviva is preparing, even calling the other princes
into the cause, as appears from the tenor of the note written by His Eminence to Monsignor the [?Secretary] of the
Consistory, of which I also add a copy, I leave to anyone who has some sense of justice and equity. I know well that,
although Our Lord is by now accustomed, in eighteen years of a most thorny pontificate, to see turned against this Holy
See for the most part those very persons whom it has most distinctly benefited, he can nevertheless never imagine that
Cardinal Alberoni, whose promotion cost Our Lord so much, should wish to follow their example by upholding the
position taken by Cardinal Acquaviva without any colour, let alone title, of reason, and should not rather try with
his authority and prudence to remove those obstacles which alone delay his obtaining the desired translation. To this
object all Your Most Illustrious Lordship's efforts must tend, and I do not doubt that you will employ them there with
all the greatest effectiveness.

After writing thus far, Cardinal Acquaviva has repeated his instances to Our Lord in a note, that he would deign to
have the Consistory already fixed for next Friday put off. His Holiness would perhaps have been induced to oblige him,
if the proposal of the Church of Nanking did not suffer the least delay; but let Your Most Illustrious Lordship know
that, even if it is proposed on Friday, the Bulls will barely reach Lisbon in time. Consider what it would be if a
longer delay were interposed. Our Lord, since he has not been able to console that King with the erection of new
bishoprics in China, although the petitions and instances have been very long and very pressing, cannot delay any
further for him, after repeated engagements, the provision of the one already erected; all the more as, His Majesty
being, alas, much displeased with us on this head, and [?therefore] already beginning to call into doubt the sending
of the ships to the Levant this year, which is yet the only help we can hope for, Our Lord does not wish to give him a
new reason to deny Christendom so necessary a succour, and himself to be answerable before the whole world for so
grave a common prejudice. For the rest, I say again to Your Most Illustrious Lordship that His Holiness is ready to
propose the Church of Seville as soon as he is satisfied on the points you know of.

Besides the great and grave outrages against ecclesiastical authority committed by the lay power in that kingdom, of
which, without having had any light from Your Most Illustrious Lordship, I have gone on expressing to you one by one
Our Lord's most just complaints, we have had word here that the Royal Ministers, not content with having expelled the
Vicar of Valencia with the well-known violence, have even gone so far as to appoint on their own authority the Bishop
of Burgos as administrator of that Church. Our Lord heard this with horror and wonder together, all the more since
Your Most Illustrious Lordship does not say a word to me of such an excess, and much less of having procured the
necessary remedy for it, since Our Lord can in no way suffer, without failing in his own duty, that anyone should
meddle in the administration and government of a Church without being furnished with ...
