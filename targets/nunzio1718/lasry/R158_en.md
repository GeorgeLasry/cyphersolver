# R158, the Secretariat of State to the nuncio in Madrid, Rome, 16 February 1718 (Spagna 364D/5, ff. 63r-66v): English translation

## Translation

Your Most Illustrious Lordship will recognise from the enclosed sheets the conduct held by Cardinal Acquaviva because
Our Lord has, not refused, but merely deferred the proposal of the Church of Seville, and the steps taken by Our Lord's
ministers for the pure and simple [?preservation] of the incontestable reasons that support the Holy See and His
Holiness in this business. Three things, besides the protest, have justly increased the bitterness of Our Lord, who
hopes that they too can in no way be approved by the King.

The first appears from the sheet marked A. It is enough to read it to agree on the strange claim of Monsignor Herrera,
as if the Royal Ministers should be free to defend, in whatever manner they please, the alleged and unfounded royal
prerogatives so as to burden the Pope, while Our Lord's ministers, on the contrary, were prevented from upholding
their own very strong and irrefutable reasons with those acts that are proper to preserve them. To answer protests is
a defence granted to everyone, and an unshaken usage among all nations, practised also by Cardinal Corradini in 1710,
when he was Auditor, as appears from the sheet marked letter B; and it is indeed a grace and favour that the Pope
allowed the one made on behalf of Cardinal Acquaviva to be received by his own ministers in the present case, in which,
since no prejudice to the King's rights was implied, as I wrote at length to Your Most Illustrious Lordship with the
last despatch, it could have been rejected without any wrong or injury.

The second regards the four reasons adduced in the act of protest which is sent marked letter C, made by Monsignor
Herrera in the name of Cardinal Acquaviva to Our Lord's Monsignor Auditor, and then executed in the Chancery and
registered in that tribunal. Four are brought forward. It is false, in the first place, that the proposals of
Churches to which princes have the right of nomination or presentation have never been deferred; and there are many
examples of it, it being enough to see the acts of the Consistory to be convinced. As for translations, what is
asserted is so false that in earlier pontificates their causes were examined in the Consistorial Congregation, where,
when they were not canonical, the translations were rejected; and if for some time now the rigour formerly practised
about verifying the said causes has been partly relaxed, which however has not always been so, it is not for that
reason that they have been admitted as a matter of course and almost blindly, where not only were the requisites
prescribed by the sacred Canons lacking, but circumstances concurred wholly opposed to the granting of the grace
requested, as in our case. Secondly, it is not understood to what prejudice of the royal right this deferment tends,
which depends on the will of that Court; for, if the Pope is satisfied on the points you know of, or at least about
the expulsion of the Bishop of Vich and the administration of the revenues of the mensa of Tarragona, he is disposed,
notwithstanding the conduct held here by the Royal Ministers, to cut short all delay. As for the scandal adduced in
the third place, it would rather arise in Spain from seeing that the Pope, without any regard to the well-known and
most grave innovations, so injurious to all ecclesiastical authority, and before obtaining the due reparation for
them, granted those graces which he would hardly grant even when the lay power did not abuse but respected the
ecclesiastical, and did not meddle in those things which by every right belong to it alone. Lastly, the fourth reason
is sufficiently answered in the counter-protest which is sent marked letter D, likewise registered by Monsignor Auditor
in the acts of the Chancery; to which I need only add that Our Lord intends to dispose of the fruits of the Church of
Seville during this time of vacancy in such a way that no one can complain that he has applied them to a use contrary
to the sacred Canons, much less that he has wished to fatten the Apostolic Camera with them.

The third concerns the publication which Cardinal Acquaviva has made of the said protest, even in print, and also
with a translation into Italian, as appears from the annexed copy which I send you. The Pope was truly surprised at
such a step, it seeming to him that he had proceeded in a way that should not be answered with such publicity. His
Eminence knows with how much bitterness, and only compelled by his own duty, Our Lord suspended the proposal of the
Church of Seville. He knows that His Holiness deferred the Consistory until Friday to await the ordinary post from
Spain, hoping to be able to have with it some news that would have put him in a position to proceed to the said
proposal. He knows besides the disposition in which His Holiness was to delay the Consistory, although three
examinations of bishops had preceded it, had not the provision of the Church of Nanking forced him, for the reasons
already written in the last cipher. On the said publication, the resolutions His Holiness will take for the
safeguard of his reasons and those of the Holy See are not known to me. I know well the very great prejudice that has
come to Our Lord, even in his own esteem and dignity with foreign nations, from the too cautious and circumspect
measures observed solely so as not to displease the King of Spain over the publication of the dreadful failure done
to him last year; and I also know that there is in Our Lord's power a quantity of notes written in Cardinal
Acquaviva's own hand, with which, and with one in particular of the fifth of August, he assured Our Lord that the
ships had sailed and were on their way to help the Christian fleet in the Levant. All these items of news and
considerations, which are indicated to Your Most Illustrious Lordship for your instruction and to make timely use of
them, make the Pope hope that the Catholic King, knowing Our Lord's most upright intentions, will be willing to take
in good part the indispensable necessity in which His Holiness has been of having to suspend the proposal of the said
Church, and will move him to supply the Pope with the means by which his royal instances may be satisfied.
