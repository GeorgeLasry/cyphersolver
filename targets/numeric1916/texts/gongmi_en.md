# 共密 (gongmi): English

Brice Dudout's translations from his Wenmi page (bdudout.github.io/wenmi/en/gongmi.html, CC BY 4.0), copied 23 Oct 2026 and
adjusted where the b21 reading differs (noted per telegram). Holes as in `gongmi_reading.md`.

### N17

President Li has taken office and the general situation is beginning to settle. In my humble view, we must first calm people’s minds and remove coercion. For the most important and urgent matters, please consult secretly with Jiwu, Zu’an and the others, and quickly ask Mr Li to carry out: 1. restore the old Provisional Constitution and the Parliament; 2. issue no amnesty and no order dropping prosecutions; 3. make Duan and the others resign and appoint anew a provisional premier, without readmitting cabinet members who helped Yuan’s rebellion, then, once Parliament meets, form a regular cabinet anew; 4. transfer Wang Zhanyuan and appoint as governor a commander of the people’s army holding strong forces; 5. invite the leading men of the people’s party to Beijing to consult on everything. Moreover, the Japanese authorities have decided not to lend to the Beijing government unless North and South are unified. So Mr Li should not be constrained. For information. — Xing. The 10th.

### N18

Your telegram of the 10th received. If Li and Duan sincerely support the Republic, the people’s party’s demands can naturally be accommodated, and it will not be hard to bring the State onto the proper path of the rule of law. To reach this goal, armed force is needed as support. I fear that for now we cannot let things take their course. The return home will be decided according to circumstances. — Xing. The 11th.

### N32

The old Provisional Constitution can be hoped to be restored; Liang’s faction is favourable. The leading men of Beijing and Shanghai have no hard-line position. Zhongshan has wired his troops to cease fighting and asked your opinion by wire; the reply has already appeared in the press and he will certainly publish it: be careful. Ge, Yin and Huang have wired repeatedly; you must reply. […]

### N33

Feng is very ambiguous. Another telegram from Beijing: Duan and the others have decided that each province will send three men to Beijing to discuss the Constitution. The Shanghai politicians long knew Liang’s schemes: [to] the Constitution, obtain the cabinet in exchange, with Duan as president. Huang Qun has arrived in Beijing; […] Duan has changed; the Shanghai members of Parliament will meet at once to oppose it. If the old Constitution is not restored, we fear fighting. Watch quietly for now. […]

### N35

Beijing is summoning delegates of the provinces to discuss the Constitution, intending to destroy the Constitution and the Parliament. Let our colleagues […] quickly choose a safe place and convene by themselves, and find a way to persuade the independent provinces not to send delegates. The salt revenue has still not been handed over; at present […] is not free. Duan seems to be taking up Yuan’s old tricks; Parliament should deliberate […], and the diplomatic representatives of the people’s army should say […], asking the foreigners to wait until true unification before handing over [the funds]. — Xing.
