# R993, Balbases to Fuenmayor, 28 Dec 1677 (cipher): English translation

I am informed by the contents of Your Lordship's first letter of the state in which, in other more recent ones, you told
me those affairs stood, and of the purpose with which Monsieur de [?Ponctuo], Councillor of State of Denmark, had
arrived in that town, to settle some treaties with the House of Brunswick; on which point Your Lordship reasons with all
the sound considerations of your great understanding. I truly find it most delicate and very problematic whether it
suits us to take an interest in its being carried out or to hinder it, for on both sides objections of the greatest
consequence will arise; and so I would not know what else to tell Your Lordship, except that if it cannot properly be
prevented, indifference will be necessary, and it will be advisable to keep gaining ground by always going in
uniformity with the Imperial ministers; because the present state of things, and the turn that those of England are
beginning to take — that King having decided to assemble his Parliament for the 25th of next month — might oblige them
to change their appearance, and consequently their measures for the future. Meanwhile we pass our time at this post
without any news, so I have nothing to add for Your Lordship, except that news arrived today that the town of Stettin
was about to capitulate, and that the Swedes were thinking of abandoning the whole of the rest of Pomerania; which, if it
proves true, will also change many of Your Lordship's measures in those parts.
