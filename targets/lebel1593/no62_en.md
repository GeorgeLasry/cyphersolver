## Translation

I have learned since my last that the discontent which the Duke of Mayenne had with Monsieur has been remedied, he
having had satisfaction given him for what was owed to him, and the sum was larger than I wrote before; they have
[?offered] him forty thousand écus and had assignations given him, both in [?Flanders] and in Spain, up to the cash
amount of two hundred and sixty thousand écus. It was thought that, after so many remonstrances made to the
[?servants] of the King of Spain about the impossibility of his pretensions to this [crown?] … the [?kingdom] be for
[?him], the Infanta and the prince, that he would have resolved to have some other of his kin attain it; but his
ministers continue more than ever in their opinion, the Duke of Feria bringing a doctor [?who] wants to dispute the
[?crown] on points of law, which is openly mocked over here, where it is also remarked that the King of Spain is very
[?ill] advised to show at this time, and in the face of the Estates, an army smaller than any [?that] has yet come,
and the money shorter than ever. [?I have] learned that Mayenne's captains, seeing little prospect of being able to
have the [?crown] given to the said Duke of Mayenne, or to make it fall to the [?house] of Bourbon, will try to have a
truce proposed. [?] will leave very soon [?to] [go] [?to see] Your Highness on Mayenne's behalf. I believe it will be
for Your Highness's service to give him every satisfaction and to make much of him. And truly, without his assistance
I would have found myself more hindered than ever [?in this] negotiation. The Duke of Mayenne and he are making great
offers.
