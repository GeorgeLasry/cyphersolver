## f. 194r

On the 13th of this month I wrote to Your Highness, and I am still awaiting with good devotion Your Highness's
commands, having received none since my departure, and even to hear that mine of the 25th of last month arrived
safely, in which I repeated to you the principal points of what was contained in my letters of the 5th and 15th of
January, which I believe were held up at Nevers. And in which I also told you that the lord has not made the credit
over here that he wanted people to believe. He is very short of means. He has, in truth, lords and [?friends] among
them. And he goes, as I believe, in good faith, having addressed himself to Your Highness because he foresaw that, if
the first designs of the King of Spain could not succeed, Your Highness could with more reason than any other prince
hope for much from this occasion, and that, making himself as it were the principal conductor of this negotiation, he
could not but [?receive] from it much [?advantage], from now on and in the future, every good and advancement for
himself and his people; so that I considered that what Your Highness would be willing to have given him from now on
would be well employed. I told you the same with regard to his companion and the [?président] Jeannin, but that, as
for putting great sums into [?their hands], I was not of that opinion; yet I have always thought, as I still do, that
it is very requisite that Your Highness should have money brought at once to [?Paris], to do your service with it at
need, without, however, the said lord knowing of it, who would not cease to [?press] for its disbursement. The
[?Sieur] Zamet would be very suitable if he were willing to take charge of it. He is to come here very soon; I shall
try

## f. 194v

to discover his intention. For the rest, I shall tell Your Highness that it seems that [?God] is guiding affairs to
the end which every good servant of Your Highness desires. The designs of the King of Spain for the Infanta, the
prince(s) and himself are full of every impossibility. The favours I receive from the Duke of Feria and from the
[?son] Tassis make me hope that, the said designs of the King of Spain being unable to succeed, it [Spain] may be
resolved to do favour to Your Highness. And I have even learned that the said [?Commander] Tassis told a person that
he believes it will have to come to that. The Cardinal Pellevé too let slip a word in passing that comes near it, and
I am sure that Your Highness has not forgotten to have the required offices done with the Pope and [?the Legate / the
Cardinals], being able to assure Your Highness that if the Pope, seeing that the designs of the King of Spain cannot
succeed, inclined to do favour to Your Highness, you could not wish to have over here a better lord than the said
Cardinal Pellevé will be to you, [?as I have] let Your Highness know by my other letters, as also that the procurators
of the country of Provence and the Court of Parlement itself have written here to their deputies to beg Monsieur de
Mayenne to give the government of the said country to Monsieur de Vaudémont; to which the said lord would not resolve
until he knows Your Highness's will. The bearer and solicitor of the said letters is garre(?) well known, I believe,
to Your Highness; he is of this town and lived a very long time at Marseille. Since I have been over here he has
already made two journeys into Provence, and went back there not long ago. He always passes through [?Lyon]; if
[?Monsieur de Nemours] were willing, [he could] make Your Highness a present of him. It seems to me that he would be
very well lodged in the castle of Miolans. I am waiting from hour to hour for

## f. 195r

[the m]an of the Friend, and by the same way some notable help, and leave to be able to make my way to Your Highness
as soon as the assembly of the Estates separates, following the very humble request I made of it before to Your
Highness, your service so requiring. The King of Navarre is resolving to harass and inconvenience this town more than
ever, and has commanded all the neighbouring garrisons to let no more victuals come into it, whereby the dearness,
which was extreme, will become unbearable to those who are not well provided with money for some months; which I beg
Your Highness very humbly to take into consideration, especially as I have been living on loans these two months and
can no longer hope for such accommodation. Monsieur de Mayenne is at the siege of Noyon, which he has battered with
fourteen pieces; news of the outcome is expected from hour to hour, and it is believed he will carry it. The King of
Navarre has ordered all the nobility of his party to mount promptly on horseback, and intends shortly to make himself
the strongest in the field. Monsieur de Mayenne, leaving the siege of Noyon, will come straight here to attend the
continuation of the Estates, when the Duke of Feria will come in to present his letters from His Majesty and make
known to the assembly the continuation of his good will, then will ask the said assembly to appoint some to treat
particularly with him; he is greatly astonished to find no one here [?devoted] to the [?greatness] of the King of
Spain. [?As soon as] Monsieur de Mayenne has come, he will despatch [?someone] to Your Highness, having so written to
me from the camp. By my first letters, which went astray, I advised Your Highness of a performance in the form of a
dialogue which was recited at the lodging of Monsieur the Legate after the feast he gave the Princes, Princesses and
other principal persons who were there. I send the text of it again to Monsieur Bruyset to send to Your Highness. ...
[kiss] your hands very humbly, and pray the Creator to give you all contentment in perfect health. At Paris, the 27th
of March 1593. (Postscript, left margin:) It is held that, when Monsieur de Mayenne comes back here, Monsieur de Guise
will command the army. Signed: most humble and most obedient and most faithful subject and servant [Lebel].
