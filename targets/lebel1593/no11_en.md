## Translation

… I shall only tell you by this letter that I have learned, and from a good quarter, that the Duke of Mayenne is
from day to day coming back to the opinion of [?claiming] the [?crown] for himself, principally because of this fancy
[given him] by letters from Rome, in which he is written that the Pope said it would be very ill [?gone], if, being
able to [?have] the crown for himself, he gave it to another. Your Highness sees well what likelihood there is that
this second(?) design, or to put it better, leave, can take effect. Failing that, he would greatly desire the regency
of the state, raising several difficulties on all the subjects who may be proposed, hoping that time, giving him the
means, by some happy successes of his actions, to regain as much credit among the French as he has lost through the
setbacks this party has received since the charge and administration of the state was committed to him, he could
then more easily obtain what he will at present find [?not] a difficulty, but an impossibility. The second design will
be much altered by the resolution which the Pope and the King of Spain have taken to want the election of a king,
desired with all affection by the good Catholics. To which, au(?)… he would see himself forced to consent, he will try
to favour the one from whom he can, not hope for, but in effect receive the most advantage: the King of Spain to
present everything to him, four millions; the Cardinal of Bourbon to seek and solicit him for everything, promising
him to marry his daughter, and all the honours and goods he may wish to ask, saying that with him the principal
Catholics who follow the party of the King of Navarre will come over to the Union. This overture and proposal is
favoured at all events by two of the three whom the Duke of Mayenne has chosen for his secret council in all this,
who are the [Monsieur] of [?Lyon], Villeroy, and the third, who is the président Jeannin, will easily let himself be
brought round to it. Your Highness sees well the difficulties and oppositions to which this overture will be subject.
I shall lose no time in doing, (by interposing myself?), the offices required in the service of Your Highness, of whom
I shall not ask pardon … since the matter in question and the humour of this nation have it … as I am sure I have
already obtained for myself from Your Highness.
