# Henri IV to Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Kassel, 1602-1609 (Rommel 1840): English translation of the readings

Translation (Claude Opus 5.5, 23 Oct 2026) of `letters/READINGS.md`, sections 1-7: the readings of the ciphered
passages, from the decodes of Rommel's 1840 printed transcription, with the clear text round them as Rommel prints it.
As in the reading, the ciphered passages are **bold**; `…` marks where the quoted text breaks off in clear; `[word]` a
word supplied where the print is faulty or a table entry is unlisted; `[?word]` a doubtful word; `[?]` and the
bracketed notes (unresolved groups, clear passages summarised) are kept. The "controls" read alongside (the
Landgrave's own letters and a letter deciphered in 1846) are not translated here.

## 1. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 22 November 1602 (Rommel p. 84)

… So I delay the despatch no longer except to await the **advice which you have promised me to be on it, and
especially on the matter of the diet, and to know whether it is to take place, what is to be treated there, what
proposal I am to make there for the public and the particular interest of my allies and o[thers], [fol]lowing the
discussions we [had] together; and also to know whether I should have my said ambassador go on into Saxony and
Denmark, with your good counsel on the [19, overbar: ?memorandum] and instruction I am to give him.** For I have taken
such assurance of and confidence in your friendship and prudence that I wish hereafter …

## 2. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 12 January 1603 (Rommel p. 99)

… **[what] can [be done] without prejudice to our allies,** on which I shall then be awaiting your good advice with
good devotion. Since the Emperor will not appear at the diet, I consider that nothing of importance will be treated
there, **and principally not the election of a King of the Romans,** of which I expect you will inform me, as of what
will happen in the assembly of the Estates of Poland. [Brandenburg and Prussia, in clear.] **But the duke [de
Bouillon] has not written me a single word in his letter about the coming here of the Administrator [of Strasbourg],
nor about the matter of the bishopric of Stras[bourg], so that I do not know how he will have taken this journey, nor
in what way he embraces the cause of the said Administrator;** I pray you to let me know what you have learned of it.

## 3. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 27 December 1604 (Rommel pp. 211-214)

… what you have been pleased to refer to me about it. **[The duke of … (98 with overbar, not in Rommel's table)] makes
every show of bearing me affection. I assure you also that I hold their friendship in great esteem; my brother the
Duke of Lorraine is the go-between in it.**

[Sending an embassy to the German princes:] But two reasons have prevented me: one is **the bad understanding I have
observed among the Protestant [?princes], which I feared would make the said visit unfruitful;** and the other **the
discontent and distaste which some of them have given me by their actions,** principally since **they gave credit to
the talk of the Duke of Bouillon and favoured his cause with less respect and consideration for my person than I
hoped from them,** as I have sometimes written to you …

The fire of the revolt of Upper Hungary was soon put out, but **its sequel and consequence will be great, and may
spread with time to the neighbourhood.** The King of England is sending the Duke of Lennox to me, and his admiral to
Spain, to maintain himself in good friendship on all sides: **a counsel which I have not heard that he finds [good]**
with time, but **he must put it to the proof before he believes it.**

I am informed that **the United Provinces have as much courage to defend themselves and continue the war as ever,**
and I will believe, this being so, that **the means will not fail them, provided as they are; they are sending
deputies to England; they hope they will be better received and favoured than the Spaniards have promised
themselves, for the s[aid] King does not wish to lose their friendship,** as I have been told. **The said provinces**
were lately visited **by a new [name, 9 groups unresolved]**, by whose good qualities they were much edified. And he
is indeed virtuous. I hear **that he gave them some hope of arming in their favour next year.** In which I pray you
**to encourage and assist him,** if you find the occasion.

**I am with the Spaniards as usual, discovering daily some new intrigue forged on their part to my disadvantage;
but** God will preserve me from it. I have brought to justice the Count of Auvergne, his father-in-law and his sister,
who **are charged and accused of having dealt with them further than they ought,** and you shall be informed of what
follows …

## 4. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 28 April 1605 (Rommel p. 230)

## 5. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Paris, 22 December 1605 (Rommel pp. 269-272)

[After the Mérargues affair and the attempt on the King:] … and even on my part, **it must be considered that all
these accidents befell me almost at the same time as the conspiracy in England. It seems too that they were forged on
the same anvil.** The ambassador of Spain wanted to make me believe that I had done his master an injury in detaining
his secretary, who had been caught in the weaving of his treason, **so accustomed are the ministers of that nation to
se[d]uce and corrupt the subjects of the princes with whom [they] reside.** I am assured that the King of Great
Britain, my good brother, has as much reason to complain of it as I, **yet he covers and dissimulates it better.** But
I cannot believe that for this **his affairs prosper** the more; which it would be needful **that all who love him
should point out to him,** as **I have done by an ambassador whom** I sent to him expressly for this purpose … likewise
I have commended to him **the United Provinces in the extreme need they have** at present of being **assisted and
reinforced by their allies, to restore** next year **the reputation of their arms.** But, my cousin, as you have **a
notable interest in their preservation, together with the other princes of Germany,** I pray you to work so that
they are likewise **succoured from your side, with both money and troops,** following the hope that some **of the said
princes have given them,** and you will do a very good work, **very useful to the common cause, and which will be**
very agreeable [to me]; praying you to let me know what I am to hope for from it. For my part, I have resolved **to
continue to them next year the same assistance in money and troops that I have given them this year; it is very
considerable and not less than two million livres; but if with that they are not succoured from elsewhere, it will
be impossible for them to resist their enemies, for these are gathering money and forces from all sides to attack
them more vigorously and powerfully than they have ever [done].** Be then **the author,** my cousin, **of having them
receive the said succour, and let it be as promptly as can be done, so that they may take the field among the first
in the spring, for he who can forestall his opponent in that will be greatly advantaged by it.** For the rest, **let
me know what the Emperor intends to treat in the diet of the Empire which he has summoned, and what** you hope **from
the negotiations for peace with [the Turk] and from the agreement of the Hungarians with the Emperor, as also from
the continuation of the siege of the city of Brunswick, in which I regret to see [the Duke of Brunswick] engaged.** I
pray God … Written at Paris, the 22nd day of December 1605.

## 6. Henri IV to the Landgrave, Fontainebleau, 20 May 1606 (Rommel pp. 307-314)

… to share it with the princes of Germany of that condition, **[7 groups unresolved] that things are still in great
confusion,** because **the Emperor does not change his conduct, and he is very badly assisted and [served] in his
affairs, principally on the side of** Hungary, where neither peace nor war is made as it should be … [p. 308 in clear:
the Turks, the Austrian nobility in arms, the archdukes at Vienna, the Imperial diet] … on all sides. **The Spaniards
will do what they can to have a King of the Romans elected from the House of Austria who is entirely at their
devotion, having** learned that the Duke of Feria … [in clear: Feria sent to the diet to promote the election, to
propose the pacification of the Netherlands, and if the States refuse, to engage the Empire to make war on them] …
**which are two proposals of great consequence, and in which all Christendom has no less interest [than] Germany
itself. That is why I consider it necessary that all the kings and princes who must be jealous of the growth and
prosperity of the Spanish power should advise in good time and take counsel together on what is fitting to do to
prevent the said Spaniards from making the affairs and resolutions of the said diet square with their wishes. For my
part, I shall always contribute to it very willingly what depends on me and is becoming for me to do, principally
when I recognise that the others can do the like, and above all the princes of [Holland: ?Germany], being no less
desirous than the kings my predecessors have been to favour and second the good intentions of the ancient allies and
friends of this crown on similar occasions, as on all others that may arise, for [the] preservation of German
liberty, without however expecting any other advantage from it than to advance the good of the common cause and the
public security; for which I shall be very glad not only that you inform those whom you judge worthy of it, but also
that you give me advice and counsel on what I can and must [do] now, both to assure them of my good will and to
advance its effects.**

I have been informed that the King of England is with good right very ill satisfied with the last refusal [of Owen
and Baldwin, in clear] … **[a run of letters and signs the 1840 typesetter could not render] I have at least … I have
not heard that the King of England breaks with the said Spaniards for this just offence, so much does he desire to
live in neutrality and peace; besides which I know that the said Spaniards omit nothing to do towards him and towards
his servants to compensate for and make him forget this discontent, having had several very specious offers and
overtures made to him anew expressly for this purpose. Nevertheless, as he is prudent and well counselled, I hope also
that he will take good care not to let himself be drawn by their inducements and persuasions, which have no other aim
than to abuse and circumvent those [to whom] they address them, so as to be able to [do] their business the better in
the Low Countries, where they intend to make a great effort this year, principally on the side of the Rhine and of
Friesland, having for this purpose doubled their army and awaiting only** the return to Brussels of the Marquis
Spinola … Which it will be very difficult for the said States of the United Provinces to resist, **for as they are
much weaker than the others, they will also be forced to put themselves on the defensive,** which will be **a
wretched condition for them, and one whose outcomes are too [?greatly] to be feared in a state composed of and governed
by several heads,** as is **the republic of the said provinces,** than in another. That is why **all who are joined in
interest with them must think well on it,** so as **to set their hand to it** before **their weakness and exhaustion**
become greater; **otherwise the remedies will come too late. You have [?seen] what I** wrote to you before on this
subject, praying you **to favour their affairs and suits with the princes of Germany,** of which I understand very
little effect has come. But the affairs and enterprises of the gentlemen of the said States, both on the side of the
East Indies and on the coast of Portugal, are prospering greatly, to the great prejudice of those of the said
Spaniards; **yet I doubt that this good fortune will last for them if that on land [?fails] them** and **their peoples
grow weary of bearing the burden of the war.** I shall write you nothing of the dispute between the Pope and the
Venetians, except that **I know the Spaniards will do what they can to take advantage of it,** as **they have always
done** on similar occasions. That is why, on my side, I shall do all I can to settle it as soon as possible … Written
at Fontainebleau, the 20th day of May 1606.

## 7. Henri IV to the Landgrave, 24 January 1609 (Rommel p. 391)

… **the ministers of the Pope and of the King of Spain** will make every kind of effort and diligence to reconcile
the Archduke Matthias with the Emperor …
