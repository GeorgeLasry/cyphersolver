# NARA bulk-scan triage, slice E: cipher-word pass (RG 165, RG 65) and the remaining letter-code files (27 Oct 2026)

Brief: `research/nara_reels/TRIAGE_BRIEF.md` and `research/archive_sweep_2026-10/BRIEF.md`. Every row was checked against
catalogue.json 628-680, the candidates and READMEs of `nara_triage_a`-`_d`, `nara_military`, `nara_state` and `nara_catalog`,
and run through `known.py`. No bulk_scan.py process was started, restarted or stopped. Nothing was committed.

Output: `candidates.json` (22 rows, generated by `rows.py`). Most rows rest on MID index cards. The MID correspondence files
the cards cite (RG 165 entry 65, files 4131-, 61-, 9140-, 10020-, PF) are **not digitised**, so most rows have solvability 1
and their `verify` step is a College Park order. Two rows (Soviet Baltic Fleet 1928 and the Swiss consul 1917) rest on
digitised film. All of them were read from NARA's OCR; no page image was viewed.

## Method

1. `triage.py` loads `rg_165_words.jsonl` (3,743 lines) and `rg_65_words.jsonl` (592 lines). Lines are deduplicated on
   (objectId, word); there were no exact repeats in this pass.
2. `tiers.py` splits all 4,335 snippets into reading tiers, collapsing identical card summaries (the same entry repeated
   on cross-reference cards):
   - `tier1.txt` (145): a failure phrase ('could not', 'unable to', 'undeciphered', 'impossible', 'no key' ...) within 150
     characters of cipher vocabulary. I read all of these myself.
   - `tier2.txt` (1,186 distinct, 1,216 frames): a submission or seizure phrase near cipher vocabulary ('fwds', 'for
     decipherment', 'intercepted', 'found on', 'letter in code' ...).
   - `rest.txt` (2,559 distinct, 2,974 frames): everything else.
3. Tier 2 (3 chunks) and rest (4 chunks) were each read in full by a sub-agent with fixed keep/drop rules. Their verdicts
   are in `read_tier2_{1,2,3}.txt` and `read_rest_{1..4}.txt`: one line per kept item (#N refers to the tier file) and a
   drop tally. The strongest items were then cross-checked against each other and the shortlist checked by me.
4. Items were fetched with `research/nara_reels/reels.py fetch`, one request per item, 4 s apart (`fetch1.log`), and read
   with `show.py <naId> grep|frame`. The items: BI case files on M1085 rolls 436, 375 (two parts), 379, 760, 308, 316,
   305, 597 (two parts), 607, 307, 377; M1024 rolls 335, 217 and 270; M1443 roll 19; M1513 roll 47.
5. The letter-code files were read line by line (see the coverage table).

`tier*.txt`, `rest*.txt` and `fetch1.log` are regenerable and git-ignored (`.gitignore` here). The `read_*.txt` files are
the reading record.

## Best leads

1. **Revolutionary War letter in code, probably by a British officer** (MID 4131-829, card 25 July 1934). Its owner sent
   it for decipherment in 1934 and the card records no answer. It is the only pre-1900 cipher in the pass. Class C until
   the file is seen.
2. **Obregón ↔ Mexican consul Enrique D. Ruiz (San Antonio), code telegrams, June 1921** (61-988). The photostats are
   'not translated'. MI-8 had broken several Mexican government codes in 1917-19, so the code may belong to a known family
   (class B).
3. **Rueckheim/Hinsch/Cronholm code letters, 1917** (9140-2235, 9140-668/42). These are from the Black Tom sabotage
   network and the Swedish channel in Mexico, and the papers passed through the 1932 German-claims files. No decipherment
   is recorded on the cards, and none was found in print.
4. **Hindu–German conspiracy**: Heramba Lal Gupta's cipher letter (10020-87) and a telegram printed as 'not as yet
   satisfactorily deciphered' (M1085 roll 760 fr. 0286). Manly read other letters in this family.
5. **The 'Gulick message' signed Honytre**, still unsolved at Riverbank in Oct 1917 (10020-69). This is a separate file
   from the Tampico message (entry 674).

Also rowed: Nauen code message that MI-8 called indecipherable (4131-532, 1919); the 'Frank James' treasure document
that SIS could not solve (4131-788, 1931); German legation telegrams from Central America and La Paz, 1940-41 (10987-718,
4131-B-16, 4131-B-13); the Visser courier's code message with its key (9140-103, Feb 1917; class A because the key was
seized with it); the Pfeiffer, Kracker, Belmforth, Castellanos, Loya, Becer and Bettman letters and the Selfridge Field
postcard (each explicitly unread, or with no result on the card); Kovacs, Valparaíso (10020-115); the Tismera message
(10270-81); the Soviet Baltic Fleet cipher groups of Aug 1928 (M1443 roll 19 fr. 0431-0432, ciphertext in OCR, two short
messages, difficulty 5); and the Swiss-consul message at San Francisco, OG 3337 (Weiskopf could not decode a single
message).

## Dead ends checked on the film (read at the time, or not cipher)

- **Ononion/Aronion letter, Boston 1917** (BI 71891, M1085 roll 436 fr. 2700-2707): 'submitted to experts in 225
  languages'. It was then deciphered: M1024 roll 335 fr. 0038-0040 (10369-1/-3) bills the War College for 'the deciphering
  of the letter sent ... by the Food Administration', $35 'to the code man for a week's work'. The writer was an
  Armenian who could not write English.
- **Zadkovitch postcard to Istria** (OG 31601): decipherment recorded on card 10020-106 (M1194 roll 176 fr. 0379).
  OG 31619 (Fabyan asks for the original 'cipher letter' for a further attempt) has no subject on the film; it was left
  as a further lead.
- **Goodrich Lockhart telegram** (BI 31949, roll 375 fr. 1118-1123): a commercial code, with the decode interlined.
- **Bucharest attaché telegram 1916** (M1024 roll 217 fr. 0111-0113): US War cipher, 'Deciphered by Index Bu.'.
- **Taihoku → Tokyo consular messages, Feb 1938** (M1513 roll 47 fr. 1103-1104): US State Department code-word groups,
  filed with their translated content.
- **Fargo enclosure 1917** (M1024 roll 270 fr. 1034-1038): an abbreviated troop-strength list, not a cipher.
- **Portland 'morocco leather book' 1917** (BI 3416, roll 308): the War Department asked for the book; no copy is on the
  film.
- **Gregory/Miami 'espionage plot' 1918** (roll 316 fr. 0824-0837): the exhibits are transcribed. They are
  theosophical/Sanskrit names ('Kartukama, Caraki, Upamakaganspati'), not a cipher.
- Riverbank's 'Voynich ... War Department cipher' (BI 33354, 1917): this is the Voynich MS, a famous target, so it is
  excluded.
- Many 1917-19 postal-censor letters were deciphered by Manly/Agostini and found harmless (love letters, Yiddish,
  chess). So were the Mexican government code telegrams of 1917-18 (10531, 10640, 9700 series; 4131-21/-22/-23/-26).
  These are listed in the read files.

## Not reached / blockers

- **The MID correspondence files are not digitised.** Every M1194/M912/M1474 card lead needs an RG 165 entry 65 pull at
  College Park. Most rows here depend on that.
- Riverbank's monthly 'undeciphered messages' reports (10020-64) would list everything MI-8/Riverbank failed on in 1917.
  They are the single most useful file to order.
- No page images were viewed. The Soviet 1928 groups and the BI letters were read from OCR only.
- The M1024 Guatemala 'Japanese attaché' radio item (267092615) is digitised but was not fetched.
- Prior-art web checks were run for the Gupta letter, the 'Frank James' document and the Hinsch/Cronholm letters. None
  turned up a decipherment.
