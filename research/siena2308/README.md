# Siena, Concistoro 2308: the cipher file of the Sienese government (collection index)

Archivio di Stato di Siena, Archivio del Concistoro 2308, "Cifrari e lettere cifrate": fasc. 1 "Cifrari" (the filed
keys, DECODE R4746-R4789) and fasc. 2 "Lettere in cifra" (the letters, DECODE R4790-R4814, plus R1858). This folder is
the collection index, not an entry: each key has its own entry under `targets/`, following George Lasry's consolidated
report "The cipher documents of Concistoro 2308, fasc. 2" (17 Oct 2026, sent by email to Daniel Bourdeau), which asks
for one entry per key. Site index page: `docs/siena1421.html` (URL kept from the former bundle entry).

Until 18 Oct 2026 this was the entry `targets/siena1421/` (catalogue 336, "the unread pieces"). It was split into the
entries below and moved here; its profile was deleted (a survey has none). Its transcriptions moved to the entries they
belong to; the shared solvers, the transcribed filed keys, the fasc. 1 key index and the access notes stay here.

## Disagreements between the report and this repository

- **No. 8**: Lasry lists it as unsolved, a link with the memorandum above "possible but speculative"; this project's 24
  Sept inventory had it "read at the time". Aligned sign by sign on 18 Oct 2026: the memorandum's paragraphs 5-7 are the
  slip's decipherment (`targets/siena8/NOTES.md`). Put to him.
- **No. 12**: DECODE R1858 is marked "Decrypted" with no document attached; this project took that status on trust on
  24 Sept. Lasry's reading of 17 Oct is the first decipherment found (`targets/siena12/NOTES.md`).
- **No. 5?** (R4794): the 24 Sept inventory gave no. 5 to R4791 (a clear letter, pencil "1460?") and read the slip's
  pencil number as an erased 4 or 5; Lasry calls the slip "No. 5?".
- **Nos. 22-23**: the inventory numbered R4810 "22-23"; Lasry lists R4810 among the cleartext letters and R4811 as no. 23.

## Access: the whole filza is on DECODE (found 24 Sept 2026)

The 23 Sept scan (`research/oldest/scan_2026-09-23/italy.md`) had only the Yale Ilardi reel 1503 (13 key frames). DECODE
holds the whole busta, photographed in the ASSi reading room (owner 32, created 2020-11-24 and 2023):

- **R4746-R4789 = fasc. 1 "Cifrari"** (cover "Concistoro 2308, Fasc. 1, Pezzi 46", divider sheets "Secolo XV" /
  "Secolo XVI"), DECODE names `Concistoro_2308_01` ... `_43`, record type key (R4787 = type 3, 10 pp.). R4761 is
  not Siena (a Kurtz 1639 record in the id gap); R4783 has no image.
- **R4790-R4814 = fasc. 2 "Lettere in cifra"** (cover "Concistoro 2308, Fasc. 2, Pezzi 25" on R4790 p. 3),
  DECODE names `Concistoro_2308_44` ... `_68`, all dated "1500-1599" (a placeholder), status N/A, no transcription
  or decryption attached.
- **R1858 "Siena keys and ciphers"** (8 pp., status "Decrypted" but no document attached): photos of key sheets
  (the 1454 Milan key, "Cifra cum frate Bernardino", the 1475 key, R4747's slips) and a second photo of no. 12.

Meister (1902, pp. 50-51) counted 29 pieces and dated no. 1 to 1421, no. 4 to 1456, no. 11 to 1478, no. 17 to 1528;
these anchors hold, so the pencil numbers are his numbering. The fascicle cover says 25 pieces.

## Solver calibration (24 Sept 2026)

`homsolve2.py` breaks a synthetic Italian homophonic cipher of the same size (2,673 tokens, 58 signs, 3% nulls)
perfectly (-1.74/token). The real nos. 6/24 and 20/23 stayed at -2.9/token under every variant tried (Italian, Latin,
Catalan, Spanish; f-groups merged; 'p p' merged or dropped; crossed or superscripted signs as nulls; null-aware
homsolve3). George Lasry later recovered both keys (nos. 6/24: R4755; nos. 20/23: siena2023), so the failure was in the
transcriptions and the systems (nomenclator signs, look-alike signs merged or split), not in the solver's reach.
