# Leads closed by the printed-edition check (28 Oct 2026)

These catalogue rows were removed: the letter is already printed deciphered.

- **1141** Charles I (Newport) to Ormonde, copy of a cipher letter. Thomas Carte, A Collection of Original Letters and Papers, concerning the Affairs of England, from the year 1641 to 1660, found among the Duke of Ormonde's Papers (1739), vol. I pp. 185-186: 'The King's Letter inclosed. Newport, in the Isle of Wight, Oct. 28, 1648. Ormonde, Lest you might be misled by false reports ...' (obey my Wife's commands; do not obey any command of mine until I send word I am free; be not startled at my great concessions concerning Ireland, they will come to nothing). Enclosed in a letter whose writer refers to 'these inclosed in cypher'. Plaintext printed, no figure string given. Northumberland ZSW/554/1 (copy of cipher letter, 28 Oct 1648) is this letter; closed as printed. Side lead Richmond to Rupert 15 Aug 1644: Warburton vol. I appendix calendar lists 'Richmond, Boconnock, to Prince Rupert, Aug 15 - does not believe in plots at Court against Prince Rupert; letter in cipher relates to' and vol. III p.35 says three Richmond letters are 'almost unintelligible from ciphers'; the text is not printed (Digby's Boconnock 15 Aug 1644 letter is, III pp.13-14), so that one is not_found.
- **1188** Henri IV → Sillery on his resolve to recover Calais, partly ciphered. Berger de Xivrey & Guadet (ed.), Recueil des lettres missives de Henri IV, t. VIII (Supplément, 1872), pp. 685-691: Henri IV to Sillery, Saint-Germain-en-Laye, 13 Dec 1597 (signed Neufville); the ciphered passage is printed in clear with the remark '(Passage en chiffres.)' (p. 686) Plaintext for the whole letter including the ciphered passage; no cipher string. Closed as a reading target. Date and Calais/Cardinal Albert/Père général content match the entry.
- **1174** Prince de Condé's negotiations with General Pichegru and the Coalition agents, ciphered letters with decipherment tables. Chantilly Ms 1967 = série Z t. XXXIV (Z 34). Its letters (Montgaillard, Fauche-Borel, Demougé, Mme de Reich, pp. 1-264) are cited item by item and used in deciphered form in Caudrillier, La trahison de Pichegru et les intrigues royalistes dans l'Est avant fructidor (Paris 1908); many were printed earlier in Correspondance trouvée dans les fourgons du général Klinglin (Paris, an VI, Réal's deciphered edition); Pichegru's musical-note billets survive only as decipherments (Caudrillier, introduction p. xiii) Caudrillier says the Strasbourg correspondents' and Demougé's 1796 letters were sent in cipher, deciphered by Mme de Reich and the abbé Jœglé, and 'le déchiffrement de Strasbourg' agrees with Réal's printed text; the volume's front tables are that decipherment. Cipher strings are not printed in Caudrillier. Read at the time and in print, not an open cipher target. Daudet (Revue des Deux Mondes 1901) and Chantilly Ms 1988 (musical scores) not separately checked; Caudrillier says Wickham took the musical originals and only translations survive.
- **1190** Landgrave Maurice of Hesse-Cassel → Henri IV on the duc de Bouillon, partly ciphered. Rommel (ed.), Correspondance inédite de Henri IV, roi de France et de Navarre, avec Maurice-le-Savant, landgrave de Hesse (Paris 1840), pp. 297-299: Maurice to Henri IV, Cassel, 28 Mar 1606 (French; regret over the Bouillon affair, Hungary, Brunswick, birth of a daughter) Full plaintext printed; no cipher string and no editor's cipher note on this letter (nearby letters of 12 and 15 Mar 1606 carry notes that the originals were in chiffres). Print gives the year as '1600' (typo for 1606). Closed.
