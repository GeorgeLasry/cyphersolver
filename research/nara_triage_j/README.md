# NARA bulk_all triage, slice J: RG 24, RG 29, RG 64 (27 Oct 2026)

Brief: `research/nara_reels/TRIAGE_BRIEF.md` and `research/archive_sweep_2026-10/BRIEF.md`. Scope: the three groups left out of
slice I because they were still scanning (`reviewed.txt`). Per-group counts and conclusions in `coverage.md`. Nothing committed;
catalogue.json, merge.py and docs/ not edited.

Output: `candidates.json`, 1 row, generated by `rows.py`. Checked with `known.py` (M1486, Russian Consulate, Olarovsky, bigram key:
no hits) and by grepping catalogue.json and the `nara_*` folders for the naId and roll.

## Method

1. `slim.py` streamed the multi-GB `out/rg_<n>_all.jsonl` files (rg_24 2.3 GB, rg_29 6.6 GB, rg_64 2.3 GB) line by line into
   `out/rg_<n>_slim.jsonl` (all desc/phrase/word/letters/caps hits plus the top numeric frame per 60 items) and
   `out/rg_<n>_slim_items.tsv` (numeric-frame counts per item). `review_j.py` is `nara_triage_i/review_i.py` run on the slim files;
   its output is `review_rg_24.txt`, `review_rg_29.txt`, `review_rg_64.txt`.
2. RG 64: all 17 desc titles; all 126 phrase hits other than plain "in cipher/in cypher" (undeciphered, could not be
   deciphered/decoded, not decoded, key lost ...) read one by one; the 1,179 "in cipher/in cypher" hits grouped by microfilm
   publication (M997 122, M274 110, M1065 102, M580 98, M316 68, M336 60, M632 34, M353 34, T724 33 ...) and sampled; top items of
   `rg_64_slim_items.tsv` checked by title. For leads, the full `rg_64_all.jsonl` was grepped for the item naId (stream, not loaded).
3. Network: 10 NARA requests in all, spaced by `reels.get` (4 s) or 5 s, no 403/429. `reels.fetch` of 571170362 (record + 2 OCR
   pages) and 574876562 (record + 1 OCR page); `nara_triage_i/rec.py` for 26025477, 335686412, 335684856; two frame images
   (M1486-0083-0703, -0751), kept in the session scratchpad, not in the repo. One web search (M1486 cipher material).

## Row

1. **Russian Foreign Ministry cipher circulars to the Imperial Russian Consulate General, New York, 1888-1910** (RG 64, M1486
   roll 83, target 2 "Cables and Coded Communications, 1893-1910", naId 571170362, frames 0692-1550). About 20 circulars from the
   First Department in 2-digit bigram-key groups (1891 No. 3902 and 1895 No. 5078 run to 300+ groups), a 5-digit secret circular
   No. 864 to all missions, the consul's own cipher replies (1895), 5-digit cables of 1908 and 1917. Some circulars have the
   consulate's letter-by-letter worksheets on the following frames; several show none. The same roll carries the instructions and
   worked plaintext/cipher examples for bigram keys No. 381 (1893) and No. 389 (1900), the circular introducing No. 400 (1905),
   additions to dictionary key No. 402 (1910), lozungi to recipher key No. 448 "Lambda" (1915), rules on keeping keys, the Naval
   Ministry cipher No. 383 and Naval key No. 394 (instructions, ship-name and place lists, dictionary), and decode tables
   ("Razbornaya tablitsa No. 404"). Class A, importance 3, solvability 4, difficulty 3.

## Key leads (no row)

- The naval and MFA keys on M1486 roll 83 (above) are themselves key material for any Russian naval/diplomatic traffic of
  1893-1915 elsewhere (e.g. intercepts in British or French files).
- Carl Lokke subject file "Official Ciphers - Livingston 1801-1804" (naId 26025477): loose sheets, container 3, not digitised.
  Lokke was a National Archives staff member; this is an archivist's subject file, probably notes or copies on Robert R.
  Livingston's Paris code (the family covered by `targets/armstrong`). Worth a scan request only as key evidence for the
  Livingston/Monroe/Armstrong code; no ciphertext expected.

## Existing catalogue entry updated by this slice (not a new row)

- **Catalogue 658 (`lima1917`, German Minister at Lima ↔ Santiago/Buenos Aires/Quito, 1917)** was recorded as "blocked on access
  (file not scanned)". The file is scanned: RG 59 decimal file 862.20223 is filmed on **M336 roll 64** (RG 64 copy, item naId
  574876562), frames c. 0048-0207 and later. The OCR shows the US Legation's relays of the German code telegrams (5-figure groups
  under c. 24000 with high-figure groups, plus a letter code "dobombobet fesafbefys ..." between Quito and Lima, f.0104), the
  Department's re-sends "For Bell from Harrison" to London (f.0049-0050, 0053-0054, 0069, 0103, 0129-0130, 0132, 0204), London's
  replies with the substance (f.0101: "Second undecipherable ... Fourth undecipherable"; f.0207 a verbatim English text), the
  Lima despatch of 6 Jun 1917 (f.0070-0072) and Minister Handley's of 9 Oct 1917 (f.0205: "in a code I could not decipher").
  F.0845 (Valparaiso, June 1918): two Groothoff (Iquique) commercial telegrams in an altered ABC code. The main session should
  update the lima1917 notes rather than add a row.

## Dead ends (counts)

- RG 64 phrase hits, 1,305 in all: 1,179 "in cipher/in cypher" are State Department telegram forms and despatch boilerplate
  ("telegram sent in cipher", "confirming my cablegram in cipher") with the clear text filed, Signal Service weather reports
  "received in cipher" (M997 War Department annual reports), cable-tariff schedules, and narrative mentions; none is a filed
  undecoded text. The 126 others: garbled own-government telegrams later repeated or decoded (Mexico, Honduras, Liberia, Persia,
  Russia 1918 "your 177 undeciphered at vital point", Lausanne 1923, Kirk 1939 without cipher tables, Lisbon strip cipher 1941);
  Navy Fleet Problems I-XXI (M964) exercise intercepts "not decoded" (own training traffic); "Key lost" = Key West OCR (Army
  morning reports, naturalisation papers, Coast Guard) or literal lost door keys; mentions of foreign codes not filed (Peking
  1925 Bolshevik gunboat radio, China 1925 arms-ship cablegrams, Turkish Red radio 1922, Tinoco government cables 1918, Weisgerber
  code, Mihailovich name, Japanese paragraph "could not be deciphered" = untranslated language); "not deciphered" for illegible
  handwriting, fish ages, signatures. Only the Lima German traffic (above) is real filed ciphertext, already catalogued.
- RG 64 desc: Alien Property Custodian code books (accession dossier 335684856, NARA paperwork, not digitised; commercial codes);
  captured German cryptography records accession dossier (335686412, paperwork for RG 242 NN-373-168, not digitised); Purple
  machine photo; Confederate cipher disc loan exhibit 1966; ANSI/SAE "group coded recording" and bar-code standards; Montreal and
  Vancouver Russian consulate rolls (military service of Russian subjects; no cipher frames); American Samoa coded subject files
  (filing codes); employee language-skill cards.
- RG 64 numeric frames (31,788 items with counts): top items are War Department annual-report tables, Chief of Engineers indexes,
  Peary tide readings, Treasury First Auditor accounts, land-office cash entries, Army morning reports, Indian census rolls,
  Haiti/Sweden/Denmark financial files (838.51 etc.), Russian consulate financial reports, certificates of origin and money-
  transfer lists. Only M1486 roll 83 target 2 holds cipher. Other Russian consulate rolls checked by grep for MFA circular
  frames (San Francisco incoming letters 1876-81): circulars in clear.
- RG 24 (Bureau of Naval Personnel): 28 desc (American civilian internees code book in POW records = WWII casualty/status code
  book; personnel files of men surnamed Cypher), 41 phrase (deck logs: "key lost/missing" in 1957-78 logbooks, numeric positions
  and watch entries), caps/frames = logbooks. No row.
- RG 29 (Census): 184 desc and 22 phrase are 1880-1900 Soundex index cards where "Undeciphered" stands for an illegible name,
  1950 enumeration-district descriptions, and territory punch-card code keys (1950 tabulation codes). No row.
