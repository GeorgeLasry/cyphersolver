French Manuscripts – Wall, Cynthia 
Sundberg. Post-1500 French 
Manuscripts in the Newberry Library 
 
The following inventory lists alphabetically by main entry all post-1500 (as distinguished from 
“medieval,” which have their own inventories) “French” manuscripts in the Newberry Library. The 
parameters for this inventory are quite wide: included are all manuscripts in the Case, Wing, and Ayer 
collections which are either by a French author, located in France, written in French, and/or 
concerning French affairs. For example, the reader will find, besides manuscript items of Bonaparte, 
Richelieu, Saint-Saëns, and Voltaire, a wide assortment of French Canadian documents, letters of 
French explorers or missionaries in North and South America, airs (with French titles) by Chopin and 
Tcherepnin, calligraphy specimens by Jean de Beauchesne used by Princess Elizabeth, and a 
sixteenth-century copy of the history of France by Andrea Cambini. The inventory also includes 
facsimiles and reproductions. If the description of an Ayer manuscript, for example, says “photostat,” 
or the call number of a Wing manuscript contains “fac,” the item in our collection is a reproduction of 
an original located in another library. (The main catalog entry will normally give the location of the 
original.) 
This list was gathered from the shelf lists of Case (including Cutter, lower case, fixed location, and 
Library of Congress call numbers), Wing (including Case Wing, Cutter, Library of Congress, and 
MS-fac), and the Ayer manuscript catalog (entire). Many of these entries are abbreviated, and the 
reader is advised to check the main catalog entry for more information. For additional manuscript 
material of (late) nineteenth- and twentieth-century French composers, artists, authors, etc., the reader 
should also check the Modern Manuscript collections. The online catalog may offer additional 
entries. 
C. S. Wall 
June 1991
