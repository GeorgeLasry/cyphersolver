# NARA bulk_all triage, slice I: record groups not reviewed in slice H (27 Oct 2026)

Brief: `research/nara_reels/TRIAGE_BRIEF.md` and `research/archive_sweep_2026-10/BRIEF.md`. Input: `research/nara_reels/out/<rg>_all.jsonl`
from `bulk_all.py`. Scope: every group with a non-empty `_all.jsonl` that is not in `nara_triage_h/reviewed.txt`, except
rg_24, rg_29 and rg_64 (still being scanned). 62 groups, listed in `reviewed.txt`; per-group counts and conclusions in
`coverage.md`. The scanner process was not touched. Nothing committed; catalogue.json, merge.py and docs/ not edited.

Output: `candidates.json`, 4 rows, generated by `rows.py`. All four were checked with `known.py` (no hits) and by grepping
catalogue.json and the `nara_*` sweep folders for the naId, file number and names.

## Method

1. `review_i.py <rg> [--frames N] [--words N]` (adapted from `nara_triage_h/review.py` for the very large groups): every `desc`
   title; every `phrase` snippet with telegraph-blank and form boilerplate filtered ("errors in cipher or obscure messages",
   "if liable to be intercepted ... in cypher", "in cipher mode", cipher-machine terms); `word` hits summarised by series and
   match word, then the stronger matches printed (decipher*, cryptogram, code letter/message, secret/invisible ink), one per
   item; frame/letters/caps items capped to the N with most hits, one solid frame each. Agency groups were read in full at
   this level; for the rest (and the huge rg_85, rg_69, rg_26, rg_27, rg_28, rg_499, rg_146, rg_260, rg_21) desc, phrase and
   strong word hits plus the top frame items were read.
2. `rec.py <naId>` fetches a catalogue record through the NARA proxy (via `nara_reels/reels.get`, 4 s spacing). OCR of five
   items was fetched with `reels.fetch` and grepped: 3854702 (RG 111 cipher wheel 1865), 26777512 and 26585046 (RG 256 rolls
   461 and 193), 119604188 (RG 85 M1144 roll 34); records for 2668864, 57347664, 57339093. About ten requests in all, no
   403/429. No image downloaded.

## Rows

1. **Cipher samples submitted to the Chief Signal Officer, 1861-69** (RG 111, series naId 2668864, 4 in., not digitised).
   "Samples and translations of ciphers" and proposals for new systems; any sample without its translation is an unread
   Civil War cipher. Importance 2, solvability 1.
2. **Carl Reissman cipher letters, 1917** (RG 60, DOJ file 9-16-12-1683; record slip in naId 288646108). Letters in cipher
   held by a detained alien enemy, Northern Illinois. Importance 2, solvability 1.
3. **Edmond DeSalm Horstmar code message, 1918** (RG 60, DOJ file 9-16-12-3169; slip in naId 288515611). Code message found
   on his person, South Carolina, sent to O'Brian and Bielaski. Importance 2, solvability 1.
4. **Letter in cypher from Leavenworth, 1916** (RG 75, BIA CCF 100295-14-211; index naId 312695146). Jack Downing,
   Cherokee Nation. Importance 1, solvability 1.

All four are known only from a description or index card; the ciphertexts are in non-digitised files.

## Leads the caller named, and what they turned out to be

- **RG 111 2668864**: kept (row 1). The RG 111 hit "Substitution Cipher Wheel" (naId 3854702, 1865, digitised) is Sgt. A. W.
  Holman's reconstruction of the Confederate signal code, forwarded by Lt. L. A. Dillingham on 28 Feb 1865, so it is read.
  RG 111 "Intercepted Letters" (2668866) was already covered and is not re-added.
- **RG 256 Peace Commission "undeciphered" groups**: dropped. The 75 phrase hits are garbles in the Commission's own code and
  cipher traffic (Bandholtz in Budapest, Belgrade, Hoover's relief cables: "not decipherable stop repeat"), resent in clear or
  in another code. The Bandholtz enciphered telegrams on roll 193 have their clear text on the same frames. The one real
  undecodable message (Close to Grew, 16 Apr 1919, roll 461 f. 459) was addressed to "Wilson" and handed to the British
  as theirs; its ciphertext is not filed. The Turkish Fourth Army telegram (roll 541), the Albanian courier letter (Inquiry
  roll 29, "we succeeded in finding the key") and the Mannerheim name cipher (roll 497) were read at the time.
- **RG 238 Waneck file**: dropped. Bureff's raids stole papers from the British Embassy (Sofia) that "could not be decoded";
  the papers are not in the interrogation file. Likewise Dernbach (coded messages of a captured agent at Brest, no key), not
  filed.

## Weak leads not made into rows

- RG 80 M1067 index (Navy secret correspondence, 1930s-41): "CODE submitted for deciphering" (E. P. Johnson, 31 Dec 1940,
  A6-3(1)), "CODE message fwded for examination" (G. B. Anderson, 1936). Private submissions, likely amateur systems.
- RG 60 slips: "copy telegram ... unable to decipher" (1920 code telegrams, DOJ's own code); Hintze-Bernstorff code messages
  1917 sent by State for translation (German diplomatic traffic, solved by MI-8 era work); "Luxburg messages" (published).
- RG 407 CIC 441: coded message of the All Kyushu Cultural Association, June 1948, "not deciphered by this headquarters",
  original forwarded to higher headquarters (not in the file; Japanese, out of the European-classical scope).
- RG 241 patent drawings: R. T. Oney "Cipher Code and Apparatus", C. G. Burke "Cryptography", A. Berghold "Devices for Secret
  Writing": inventors' systems, published with their patents.
- RG 85 M1144 (Philadelphia Chinese Exclusion case files): a coaching letter read by route transposition (roll 43 f. 627,
  read in the file); roll 34 "written in code" is an inspector's description of coaching practice, not ciphertext.

## Dead ends (counts)

- Telegraph-blank and message-form boilerplate dominate the phrase hits: RG 75 M234 (40+), RG 48, RG 407 (300+ "in cypher"
  forms), RG 260 (OMGUS cable forms), RG 85, RG 146.
- "Not decipherable" for illegible handwriting, serial numbers, tags and stones: RG 21, 49, 69, 79, 85, 92, 260, 386.
- Surnames Cypher/Cyphers/Cifra: RG 21, 49, 75, 125, 127, 153.
- Frame hits in every group are tables (census, wage, weather, fish, ship drawings, dockets, currency serials, statistics).
- Post-1950 or own crypto administration: RG 127 (Vietnam), 263, 330, 342, 499, 260 (1950 cryptographic messages).
- Already known: Lincoln conspirators' cipher exhibits (RG 153), Bopp 1917 (RG 21), Venona (RG 263), Pearl Harbor
  intercepts (RG 80).

## Not reached

rg_24, rg_29, rg_64 (still scanning). For the largest groups only desc, phrase, strong word hits and the top frame items
per kind were read; a frame-by-frame numeric-code pass (as for RG 59 in earlier slices) was not done, since none of these
groups holds diplomatic correspondence. No non-digitised series was ordered.
